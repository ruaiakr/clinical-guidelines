<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Traumatic rupture of eardrum - Adult/Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hearing loss, tinnitus (ringing in ears), earache</li>
<li>+ history of:
<ul>
<li>blow to ear e.g. slap, fall from water skis, diving</li>
<li>rapid change in ear pressure e.g. air travel, scuba diving, blast/explosion</li>
<li>object poked in ear e.g. cotton tipped applicator</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>circumstances + mechanism of injury e.g. any water exposure</li>
<li>date/time of injury + when first noticed symptoms</li>
<li>vertigo/dizziness, hearing loss, tinnitus, light-headedness, other symptoms/injuries</li>
<li>prior history of ear infections/problems</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If blow to head, assess for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Head-injuries">Head injuries</a>, including base of skull fracture</li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Do otoscopy - note location + size of perforation, any discharge/pus:
<ul>
<li>do NOT syringe ears with water or remove any blood in canal - can delay healing</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If hearing loss, use tuning fork test to determine type (if able):<sup>1,3</sup>
<ul>
<li>activate fork by striking firmly on your bent elbow. Place vibrating fork firmly on the midpoint of the patient’s skull, or central forehead. Is sound heard better in normal ear (sensorineural hearing loss), or ear with ruptured eardrum (conductive hearing loss)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If vertigo/dizziness seek urgent medical advice</li>
<li>Consult MA/NP who may advise:
<ul>
<li>chloramphenicol ear drops if ‘dirty’ injury (e.g. in water) or discharge/infection</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise to keep ear dry until healed or cotton wool in ear with Vaseline® over top while showering.</li>
<li>Avoid swimming in the sea or in contaminated water.</li>
<li>Most perforations will heal without treatment within 2 months<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>If related to assault/Domestic and family violence, offer support/referral as per local policies. Also consider <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-protection">Child protection</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to be reviewed in 2–3 days, see MA/NP at next clinic + review 2 weekly until healed if asymptomatic, or sooner if concerned e.g. infection</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MA/NP may refer to ENT specialist if not healed after 3 months</li>
</ul>
