<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Foreign body in ear or nose - Adult/Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Foreign body (FB) in ear or nose e.g. insect, bead, pebble, plastic toy, food</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Urgently consult MA/NP for ENT advice.
<ul>
<li>suspected <a href="/docs/Emergency/Button-battery-ingestioninsertion">Button battery</a>, or paired magnets (in nose) - time critical emergency:
<ul>
<li>urgent removal needed. If suspected, but not visualised, x-ray needed to rule out. If no on-site x-ray arrange urgent evacuation</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>bleeding or airway issues if FB to nose</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>ALERT</strong> button batteries require urgent removal to prevent necrosis of surrounding tissue
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history and examine ears/nose</li>
<li>Identify exact location, shape and composition of FB</li>
<li>If possible, get assistant to help, especially if child (in addition to caregiver)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Foreign body in the Ear (external auditory canal.)
<ul>
<li>Examine ear canal with otoscope or any available light source</li>
<li>If there’s a foreign body</li>
<li>Do ear Flushing (you can try flushing at maximum of 4-5)</li>
<li>If the FB successfully removed then check the other ear.</li>
<li>If the FB not successfully removed then consult the MA/NP</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> if the INSECT make sure to kill it first by using some drops of oil or Lignocaine, then can flush.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="ear-flushing">How to do Ear Flushing</h2>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Method</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>The patient must sit with the ear facing the light and you must be able to see the entrance to the ear canal clearly.</li>
<li>Fill the syringe with warm water and squirt it hard a few times back into the water container to check that it works well and the tip does not come off.</li>
<li>Gently pull the pinna backward away from the head.</li>
<li>Place the tip of the syringe just inside the canal and squirt the water into the ear canal.</li>
<li>Water should be directed around the foreign body. The water will then be behind the foreign body and should push it out.</li>
<li>Catch the water that comes out in a bowl. Check to see what has come out of the ear canal.</li>
<li>Examine the ear canal with an otoscope after every five syringes.</li>
<li>Once the foreign body has come out of the ear canal you should be able to see the ear drum.</li>
<li>Check both ears.</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-note-panel">
<strong>NOTE</strong>
<ul>
<li>Remember: the water used to syringe the ear MUST be the same as body temperature — 37°C, warm but comfortable on the hand.
<ul>
<li>If you use cold or hot water for ear flushing, the patient may feel dizzy, nausea or vomiting.</li>
<li>If the patient complains of dizziness, stop syringing and make the patient comfortable with support.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>DO NOT direct the water straight onto the foreign body.
<ul>
<li>This will push the foreign body further into the ear canal.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>DO NOT SYRINGE if the ear is dry and if you know or suspect there is a perforation in the ear drum. Refer these patients.
<ul>
<li>Syringing a dry perforated ear may lead to suppurative otitis media.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If you see any trauma to the ear canal after removal of the foreign body:
<ul>
<li>Fill the ear canal with eardrops.</li>
<li>Give the patient eardrops to take home and use four times each day.</li>
<li>Check the ear again after two days.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Attention</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do not syringe ear if there is pain in the ear or recent trauma.</li>
<li>Always look in the ear before syringing. If any pain — stop and look again.</li>
<li>If CSOM — syringe with diluted povidone-iodine 10%. See <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Chronic-suppurative-otitis-media">Chronic suppurative otitis media (CSOM)</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ear FB removed - advise to be reviewed:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>if any symptoms arise ie hearing loss, discharge, marked pain, facial nerve paralysis or dizziness/feeling off balance. Consult MA/NP ± ENT referral</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Nose FB removed - advise to be reviewed:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>if any symptoms arise ie fever, purulent discharge, nosebleed, facial pain or swelling. Consult MO/NP ± ENT referral</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If FB not successfully removed</li>
<li>As above.</li>
</ul>
