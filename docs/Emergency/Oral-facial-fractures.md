<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Oral facial fractures</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">assess, provide initial management, and appropriately refer patients presenting</p>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Facial fractures are increasingly common presentations in primary healthcare. Prompt recognition and appropriate management are critical to prevent permanent functional, cosmetic, or vision-threatening complications. General Principles</li>
<li>Follow Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) protocols for all trauma cases.</li>
<li>Conduct a full visual assessment in any facial trauma.</li>
<li>Identify red flags requiring immediate referral.</li>
<li>Provide early pain management and wound care as appropriate.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>To assist Medical Assistants and Nurses in the primary care setting to recognize, with oral facial fractures.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Nasal Fractures: Often resulting from interpersonal violence, nasal fractures are the most common facial fractures, accounting for up to 58.6% of all facial fractures. Symptoms include nasal deformity, epistaxis, and periorbital bruising. ​ www.racgp.org.au/afp/2012/april/maxillofacial-trauma/?utm_source=chatgpt. com</li>
<li>Mandibular Fractures: These may present with pain, swelling, malocclusion, and limited mouth opening. They can result from blunt or penetrating trauma and may destabilize the airway. www.entnet.org/wp-content/uploads/files/Trauma- Chapter-5.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com</li>
<li>Zygomatic Fractures: Symptoms include periorbital swelling, infraorbital nerve paraesthesia, and restricted eye movement.​</li>
<li>Orbital Fractures: Signs include diplopia, reduced visual acuity, and orbital rim tenderness.​</li>
<li>Frontal Bone Fractures: Present with forehead deformity, possible CSF rhinorrhoea, and periorbital bruising.​</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Airway Management: Ensure airway patency, especially in cases of mandibular fractures that may compromise the airway. https://link.springer.com/ chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-1346-6_48?utm_source=chatgpt.com</li>
<li>Hemostasis: Control bleeding using direct pressure, nasal packing, or hemostatic agents as appropriate.​</li>
<li>Analgesia: Administer appropriate pain relief, considering the severity of the injury.​</li>
<li>Antibiotics: Prophylactic antibiotics may be indicated in cases of open fractures or significant soft tissue involvement. https://tsaco.bmj.com/content/9/1/ e001304?utm_source=chatgpt.com</li>

</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical Assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>History: Obtain details of the mechanism of injury, time of injury, and any associated symptoms.​</li>
<li>Examination: Perform a thorough head and neck examination, assessing for deformities, swelling, tenderness, and neurological deficits.​</li>
<li>Functional Assessment: Evaluate the patient’s ability to open and close the mouth, chew, and speak.​</li>
<li>Neurological Examination: Assess cranial nerve function, particularly the trigeminal nerve, for sensory deficits.​</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Conservative Management: Indicated for non-displaced fractures without significant functional impairment. Includes analgesia, soft diet, and follow- up.​ www.rch.org.au/trauma-service/manual/maxillofacial-injury/?utm_ source=chatgpt.com</li>
<li>Surgical Intervention: Required for displaced fractures, fractures with airway compromise, or those causing significant functional impairment.​ www.slideshare. net/slideshow/maxillofacial-trauma-19339123/19339</li>
<li>Referral: Refer to oral and maxillofacial specialists for definitive management. https://metronorth.health.qld.gov.au/specialist_service/refer-your-patient/oral- maxillo-facial/facial-fractures-lacerations?utm_source=chatgpt.com</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow-Up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitoring: Regular follow-up to assess healing, manage complications, and address any functional issues.​</li>
<li>Rehabilitation: May include physiotherapy for jaw mobility and speech therapy if necessary.​</li>
<li>Psychological Support: Provide support for patients dealing with the psychological impact of facial trauma.​</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral / Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Urgent Referral: For patients with airway compromise, significant bleeding, or complex fractures.​</li>
<li>Routine Referral: For non-displaced fractures without functional impairment.​</li>
<li>Specialist Consultation: Consult with oral and maxillofacial surgeons for definitive management. ​www.westernhealth.org.au/HealthProfessionals/Referrals/ Documents/ASC%20Access%20and%20Referral%20Guideline_Oral% Maxillofacial%20Surgery.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com</li>
</ul>
