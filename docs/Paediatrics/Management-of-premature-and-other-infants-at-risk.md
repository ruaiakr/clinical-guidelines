<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Management of premature and other infants at risk (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">(Because of Size and Age or High-Risk Pregnancy)</p>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>Note:</strong> Consult a doctor at the central hospital regarding transfer (and care pending transfer) of all twins, premature, and low-birth weight infants. Also consult about any infant who is having problems.
</div>

<p>The following summary provides information about problems certain high-risk infants may have.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="infants-at-risk-size-age">Infants at Risk Because of Size and Age</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>A gestational age examination allows the health worker to assess neurologic and physical maturity in order to classify the infant according to age, sex, and size.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="multiple-pregnancy">Infants of Mothers with Multiple Pregnancy</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Higher risk of premature labour, abruptio placentae and intrapartum haemorrhage.</li>
<li>One or more infants may be compromised in fetal life.</li>
<li>Infant risks include intrauterine growth retardation (IUGR), twin-to-twin transfusion, and other complications.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="rh-sensitised">Infants of Rh-Sensitised Mothers</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>These mothers are Rh-negative with a prior Rh-positive infant and no Rhogam administration.</li>
<li>Infants need prompt oxygen and may require intubation.</li>
<li>Risks include congestive heart failure, acidosis, hypoglycaemia, rapidly rising bilirubin; often critical condition.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="diabetic-mothers">Infants of Diabetic Mothers</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Risk of birth trauma, intracranial bleeding, and/or asphyxia.</li>
<li>Hypoglycaemia within first 6 hours or later.</li>
<li>Higher risk of respiratory distress syndrome, congenital anomalies, hyperbilirubinaemia, and hypocalcaemia.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="polyhydramnios">Infants of Mothers with Polyhydramnios</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Often have fetal anomalies, including GI tract and CNS defects.</li>
<li>Other complications: premature rupture of membranes, cord prolapse, premature labour, and small for gestational age.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="hypertension-of-pregnancy">Infants of Mothers with Hypertension of Pregnancy</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Common complications include prematurity and IUGR.</li>
<li>Increased risk of abruptio placentae.</li>
<li>High magnesium levels can cause hypotonia.</li>
<li>Infants may need resuscitation and close monitoring for respiratory distress, hypoglycaemia, or CNS issues.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="placental-bleeding">Infants of Mothers with Placental Bleeding in the Third Trimester</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Premature delivery is likely.</li>
<li>Immediate treatment for shock/asphyxia may be needed.</li>
<li>Resuscitation and blood volume restoration may be required.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="preterm-infants">Preterm Infants</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Born before 37 weeks' gestation.</li>
<li>Higher risk of illness and death; the earlier the birth, the greater the risk.</li>
<li>Common problems: respiratory and cardiac issues, hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia, infections, GI or brain bleeding.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="postterm-infants">Postterm Infants</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Risk of in utero death, asphyxia, meconium aspiration, CNS damage, hypoglycaemia, and lung bleeding.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="lga-infants">Large for Gestational Age Infants (LGA)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Above the 90th percentile in size.</li>
<li>May have congenital heart disease, bone fractures, cephalhaematoma, nerve injuries, subdural haematomas.</li>
<li>Prone to hypoglycaemia.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="sga-infants">Small for Gestational Age Infants (SGA)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Below 10th percentile in size.</li>
<li>Causes: malnutrition, maternal infection, diabetes, hypertension, smoking.</li>
<li>Often thin, wasted, with little subcutaneous tissue.</li>
<li>Common issues: meconium aspiration, asphyxia, hypoglycaemia, congenital anomalies.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="summary-problems">Summary of Problems of Preterm or Low Birthweight Babies</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Irregular respirations, apnoea, and asphyxia</li>
<li>Poor temperature control</li>
<li>Difficulty with swallowing and sucking</li>
<li>Higher infection risk</li>
<li>Fragile capillaries leading to haemorrhage</li>
<li>Common jaundice — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Neonatal-jaundice">Neonatal jaundice</a></li>
<li>Anaemia and rickets due to low iron/vitamin D — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Anaemia">Anaemia</a></li>
<li>Kidney immaturity causing salt retention and oedema</li>
<li>Slower weight gain: immature babies double weight in 1–3 months</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="rules-of-care">Rules of Care for Preterm or Low Birthweight Babies</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Maintain utmost cleanliness</li>
<li>Handle minimally</li>
<li>Keep cot/crib warm and wrap in calico</li>
<li>Place between mother’s breasts for warmth</li>
<li>Feed carefully to avoid aspiration</li>
<li>Keep room sunny to prevent jaundice</li>
<li>Administer iron and multivitamin drops if transfer delayed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="after-delivery">After Delivery</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Gently clear airway with mucus extractor</li>
<li>Provide oxygen as needed (limit to 1 L/min)</li>
<li>Delay cord clamping to allow placental blood transfer</li>
<li>Wrap baby in warmed blanket; elevate head</li>
<li>Use incubator if available (target temp: 33°C)</li>
<li>Administer Vitamin K 1 mg IM</li>
<li>If pethidine given to mother within 4 hrs, give 0.02 mg (1 mL) neonatal naloxone IM</li>
</ul>
<p>See also <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Neonatal-care">Neonatal care – newborn at birth</a>.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="conservation-of-energy">Conservation of Energy</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Cluster care every 3 hours (temp, feeds, nappy change, position)</li>
<li>Treat cyanosis promptly</li>
<li>Keep baby flat during feed; elevate head 30° after feeding for 15 mins</li>
<li>Do not bathe</li>
<li>Gently clean vernix with liquid paraffin</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="feeding">Feeding</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start within 3 hours of birth to prevent hypoglycaemia</li>
<li>Use NGT for expressed breast milk if unable to suck</li>
<li>Feed every 4 hours (day and night)</li>
<li>Aspirate stomach before feed and check residual volume</li>
<li>Encourage breastfeeding as soon as baby can suck</li>
</ul>
