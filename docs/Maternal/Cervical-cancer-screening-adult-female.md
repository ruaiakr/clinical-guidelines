# Cervical cancer screening adult female (Kiribati Original Protocol)

4.	 Management
A. Outpatient (mild to moderate PID):
•	 Start treatment immediately:

–
Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose (1 g if >150 kg body weight)
PLUS

–
Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 14 days
PLUS

–
Metronidazole 500 mg orally twice daily for 14 days
(Metronidazole covers anaerobes and should be included unless
contraindicated)
B. Inpatient (severe PID or complications): Admit if:
•	 Surgical emergency not excluded (e.g. appendicitis)
•	 Tubo-ovarian abscess
•	 Severe illness (fever, vomiting, significant pain)
•	 Pregnancy
•	 No improvement after 48–72 hours of outpatient therapy
Recommended regimen:
•	 IV Ceftriaxone 1–2 g daily
PLUS
•	 Doxycycline 100 mg orally or IV every 12 hours
•	 ± Metronidazole 500 mg IV every 8 hours
•	 Continue IV antibiotics until 24–48 hours after improvement, then complete 14
days with oral doxycycline ± metronidazole.
5.	 Follow up
•	 Reassess clinically within 48–72 hours to confirm response to treatment
•	 Counsel patient to abstain from sex until treatment completed and partner(s)
treated
•	 Test of cure not routinely needed unless symptoms persist or M. genitalium
involved
•	 Retest for STIs in 3 months
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to doctor or hospital if suspected abscess, pregnancy, or no response to
treatment
7.	 Partner Notification and Treatment
•	 All sexual partners from the last 60 days should be evaluated and treated for
Chlamydia and Gonorrhoea, regardless of symptoms
•	 Advise abstinence until all partners have been treated

Cervical cancer screening adult female
(Kiribati Original Protocol)
Recommend 1–5
•	 Cervical cancer is preventable through regular screening and early treatment of
precancerous lesions
•	 Screen all eligible women aged 30–49 years, or earlier if HIV-positive
•	 Visual Inspection with Acetic Acid (VIA) is the recommended screening method
•	 HPV vaccination and safe sexual practices reduce cervical cancer risk
•	 Encourage community education and reduce stigma around screening
Background  1–5
•	 Cervical cancer is the fourth most common cancer among women globally, but
highly preventable
•	 In Kiribati, cervical cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related deaths in women
•	 Most cases are linked to persistent infection with high-risk human papillomavirus
(HPV) types
•	 Screening allows early detection of precancerous changes and timely treatment
•	 VIA is a simple, low-cost screening method suitable for use in low-resource
settings
1.
May present with
(Screening may be initiated even if asymptomatic)
•	 Woman aged 30–49 years, or sexually active woman requesting screening
•	 History of multiple sexual partners, early sexual debut, or STI
•	 HIV-positive status (start screening earlier and repeat more frequently)
•	 No prior cervical cancer screening
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Ensure privacy and informed consent
•	 Offer VIA screening if trained personnel and supplies are available
•	 Refer if VIA not available or provider not trained
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Confirm age and sexual history
•	 Ask about HIV status (if known), previous screening, and HPV vaccination
•	 Assess for symptoms of invasive cancer (e.g. abnormal bleeding, discharge)
•	 Conduct a pelvic exam and VIA (if trained and available)
•	 Document findings on screening record

4.	 Management
If VIA negative:
•	 Reassure patient
•	 Advise next VIA in 3–5 years (or sooner if HIV-positive)
•	 Provide HPV vaccination advice if eligible
If VIA positive (precancerous lesion suspected):
•	 Offer cryotherapy (if available and eligible)
•	 Refer to gynaecology if not eligible for cryotherapy or no cryotherapy available
•	 Counsel on follow-up and possible further treatment
If invasive cancer suspected:
•	 Refer urgently to gynaecology or surgical/oncology care
•	 Provide emotional support and ensure follow-up
5.	 Follow up
•	 VIA-negative: repeat screening in 3–5 years (or every 3 years if HIV-positive)
•	 VIA-positive and treated: follow-up in 1 year
•	 VIA-positive and referred: ensure referral completed and tracked
•	 Monitor program performance and coverage rates
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 Refer to:
•	 Gynaecology or surgical specialist for:

–
Suspected invasive cancer

–
VIA-positive and not suitable for cryotherapy

–
Recurrence or treatment failure

–
HIV clinic if not already in care
•	 MHMS to advise on referral pathways and eligibility for public sector HPV
vaccination programs
