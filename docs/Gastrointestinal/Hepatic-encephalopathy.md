<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Hepatic encephalopathy – Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hepatic encephalopathy (HE) is a spectrum of potentially reversible neuropsychiatric abnormalities seen in patients with liver dysfunction, particularly those with cirrhosis. It results from accumulation of toxins, such as ammonia, that are normally cleared by the liver.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Common features of hepatic encephalopathy include:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Disturbance of diurnal sleep pattern (insomnia or hypersomnia)</li>
<li>Loss of balance and falls</li>
<li>Advanced neurologic dysfunctions such as asterixis (flapping tremor) and hyperactive reflexes</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Grading hepatic encephalopathy:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Grade I: changes in behaviour, disordered sleep</li>
<li>Grade II: lethargy, moderate confusion</li>
<li>Grade III: marked confusion (stupor), incoherent speech, sleeping but arousable</li>
<li>Grade IV: coma, unresponsive to pain</li>
</ul>
<p>Precipitating factors to evaluate for include:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Gastrointestinal bleeding (e.g. varices)</li>
<li>Infection (e.g. SBP, UTI)</li>
<li>Kidney failure</li>
<li>Electrolyte imbalance (especially hypokalaemia)</li>
<li>Hypoxia</li>
<li>Metabolic abnormalities (e.g. alkalosis, hypoglycaemia)</li>
<li>Constipation</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sleep disturbance</li>
<li>Personality or behaviour changes</li>
<li>Difficulty concentrating</li>
<li>Confusion or disorientation</li>
<li>Slurred speech or tremor</li>
<li>Drowsiness or coma</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help – inform MO</li>
<li>Triage patient, determine vital signs and assess severity using grading system
<ul>
<li>Grade I may be managed as an outpatient</li>
<li>Grades II–IV require admission for treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Identify and treat any precipitating factors such as sepsis, bleeding, or dehydration</li>
<li>Determine the underlying cause of cirrhosis (e.g. hepatitis B, alcohol)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess mental status and grade severity of encephalopathy</li>
<li>Check for signs of infection, bleeding, or constipation</li>
<li>Review medications and fluid balance</li>
<li>Screen for hepatitis B if not already known</li>
<li>Examine for signs of chronic liver disease</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<p><strong>Supportive care:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Provide appropriate nutritional support – no need to restrict protein intake</li>
<li>Avoid dehydration; ensure adequate fluid intake</li>
<li>Maintain a safe environment – implement fall precautions</li>
<li>If agitated, consider non-pharmacological restraint options before sedation</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Nutritional support:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Maintain energy and protein intake</li>
<li>Recommend small meals throughout the day</li>
<li>Offer a late-night snack of complex carbohydrates (e.g. bananas, potatoes, nuts)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Drug therapy:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start lactulose 30–45 mL PO every 1–2 hours until 2–3 soft stools/day</li>
<li>Once improving, reduce to 30–45 mL PO every 6–8 hours to maintain 2–3 soft stools/day</li>
<li>Continue lactulose for maintenance if recurrent encephalopathy occurs</li>
<li>If sepsis is suspected (e.g. fever, suspected SBP or UTI):
<ul>
<li>Start Ampicillin 1g IV q6h + Gentamicin 240 mg IV OD for 5–7 days</li>
<li>Adjust duration based on clinical response</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>If HBsAg-positive:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Facilitate linkage to care with Hepatitis Program</li>
<li>Initiate antiviral therapy with Tenofovir once the patient is clinically stable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor stool output and mental status daily</li>
<li>Reassess for any recurrent precipitating factors</li>
<li>Educate patient and family about early signs of encephalopathy and importance of adherence to therapy</li>
<li>Encourage long-term follow-up with Hepatitis Program for HBV-positive patients</li>
<li>Consider long-term use of lactulose in recurrent cases</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / consultation</h2>
<p>Refer to medical officer or hospital if:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Encephalopathy is Grade II or above</li>
<li>Sepsis or gastrointestinal bleeding is suspected</li>
<li>Mental status does not improve with therapy</li>
<li>Patient is HBsAg-positive and requires evaluation for antiviral treatment</li>
</ul>
