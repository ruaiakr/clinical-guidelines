<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Uterine Inversion (Kiribati Protocol)</h1>

<p><strong>Acute uterine inversion</strong></p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommended</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Acute uterine inversion is a life-threatening obstetric emergency where the uterus turns inside out, usually following delivery of the baby. It may occur due to inappropriate cord traction on a fundal placenta, particularly in a relaxed (flaccid) uterus without adequate counter-pressure, or it may occur spontaneously.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Uterine inversion causes severe haemorrhage and profound shock, which is often greater than expected for the visible blood loss. This is due to both haemorrhage and neurogenic shock from traction on pelvic structures. Immediate recognition and rapid correction are essential to restore uterine anatomy, control bleeding, and prevent maternal death.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Severe postpartum bleeding</li>
<li>Sudden onset of <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">shock</a> (pallor, hypotension, tachycardia)</li>
<li>Collapse or altered level of consciousness</li>
<li>Severe lower abdominal or pelvic pain</li>
<li>Uterus not palpable in the abdomen</li>
<li>Mass felt or seen in the vagina or protruding from the introitus</li>
<li>Placenta may still be attached</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help immediately</li>
<li>Call hospital and obstetrician urgently</li>
<li>Begin resuscitation immediately (ABC approach):
<ul>
<li>Ensure airway is clear</li>
<li>Support breathing</li>
<li>Give oxygen if available</li>
<li>Establish large-bore IV access</li>
<li>Start rapid IV fluid resuscitation</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Order blood for transfusion if haemorrhage present</li>
<li>DO NOT remove placenta if still attached</li>
<li>Stop oxytocin infusion immediately (if running)</li>
<li>Prepare for immediate manual reduction</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess vital signs (shock severity)</li>
<li>Estimate blood loss</li>
<li>Palpate abdomen (uterus absent from normal position)</li>
<li>Identify inverted uterus (vaginal or external mass)</li>
<li>Assess whether placenta is attached</li>
<li>Monitor level of consciousness</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="manual-reduction">4.1 Manual reduction (FIRST-LINE – perform immediately)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Wear sterile gloves</li>
<li>Place the flat palm of the hand against the inverted uterine fundus</li>
<li>Push the uterus upward through the cervix toward its normal anatomical position</li>
<li>Use the other hand externally to support the uterus if required</li>
<li>Apply steady, continuous upward pressure (do not use forceful or jerking movements)</li>
</ul>

</figure>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Maintain pressure continuously for several minutes</li>
<li>Reduction usually occurs with sustained upward pressure</li>
</ul>

<figure class="pccm-flowchart-figure">

<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Figure: Complete reduction</p>
</figure>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="after-manual-reduction">4.2 After successful manual reduction</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start Oxytocin infusion:
<ul>
<li>40 units in 1 L normal saline</li>
<li>Run at approximately 250 mL/hour</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do NOT remove hand from the uterus until a firm uterine contraction is felt</li>
<li>Continue IV fluids</li>
<li>Deliver placenta only when signs of separation are present</li>
<li>If placenta does not deliver spontaneously:
<ul>
<li>Refer for removal in theatre</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="manual-reduction-fails">4.3 If manual reduction fails (SECOND-LINE MANAGEMENT)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult obstetrician immediately</li>
<li>Attempt hydrostatic reduction (if trained and equipment available)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Hydrostatic reduction technique</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Place the woman in deep Trendelenburg position
<ul>
<li>Lower her head approximately 0.5 metres below the level of the perineum</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Infuse 500–1000 mL of warm normal saline into the posterior vaginal fornix:
<ul>
<li>Use IV administration set</li>
<li>Hold fluid bag 1–2 metres above the patient</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Ensure assistant firmly seals the vaginal orifice to maintain pressure</li>
</ul>

<figure class="pccm-flowchart-figure">
<img src="/img/obstetric-gynecological/uterine-inversion-hydrostatic-setup.png" alt="Hydrostatic reduction setup: saline infusion with hand sealing the vaginal orifice" />
<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Figure: Position for hydrostatic reduction / saline infusion setup</p>
</figure>

<figure class="pccm-flowchart-figure">
<img src="/img/obstetric-gynecological/uterine-inversion-hydrostatic-diagram.png" alt="Cross-section diagram of hydrostatic reduction showing fluid pressure returning the inverted uterus to normal position" />
<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Figure: Hand blocking vaginal orifice / hydrostatic reduction of inverted uterus</p>
</figure>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>The fluid distends the posterior fornix and cervix</li>
<li>This increases the circumference of the cervical ring</li>
<li>This relieves constriction and allows the uterus to return to normal position</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="hydrostatic-successful">4.4 If hydrostatic reduction successful</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Proceed as per post-reduction management above</li>
<li>Continue monitoring closely</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="reduction-unsuccessful">4.5 If reduction unsuccessful</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>This is a surgical emergency</li>
<li>Requires urgent laparotomy</li>
<li>Refer immediately to hospital</li>
<li>Alert obstetrician urgently</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor vital signs closely (shock, bleeding)</li>
<li>Monitor for postpartum haemorrhage</li>
<li>Observe for reinversion</li>
<li>Provide:
<ul>
<li>Antibiotics (if available)</li>
<li>Analgesia</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Continue uterine monitoring:
<ul>
<li>Frequent checks for tone and bleeding</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Document:
<ul>
<li>Time of inversion</li>
<li>Time of reduction</li>
<li>All interventions</li>
<li>Patient response</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral and consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>This is a life-threatening emergency – urgent referral required in ALL cases</li>
<li>Contact hospital and obstetrician immediately</li>
<li>Continue resuscitation during transfer</li>
<li>Ensure skilled escort if available</li>
</ul>
</div>
