<h1 class="pccm-page-title">History and physical examination - Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Paediatric presentation</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Pay particular attention to history from parent/carer</li>
<li>Regardless of the time (day or night) or circumstances, reassure the parent/carer has done the right thing bringing the child in - for any concern</li>
<li>If you have concerns for the safety and wellbeing of a child, see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-protection">Child protection</a></li>
<li>Consult MO for all children &lt; 3 months of age</li>
<li>Consider serious illness if:
<ul>
<li>infant &lt; 3 months with T ≥ 38</li>
<li>child 3–6 months with T ≥ 39</li>
<li>rigors, low T &lt; 36</li>
<li>unexplained pain/restlessness</li>
<li>drowsiness/decreased activity</li>
<li>looks sick/toxic</li>
<li>not waking easily</li>
<li>poor feeding in infants</li>
<li>nasal flaring, grunting, indrawing of chest</li>
<li>dry mucous membranes, reduced skin turgor</li>
<li>not responding normally to social cues</li>
<li>reduced urine output (no urine for ≥ 12 hours)</li>
<li>cool extremities</li>
<li>pale/mottled/ashen/blue skin, lips or tongue</li>
<li>weak, high pitched or continuous cry</li>
<li>&gt; 5 watery diarrhoea in 24 hours</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Small children, especially young babies, get sick very quickly</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="sepsis-and-button-battery">Sepsis and button battery considerations - all children</h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="think-sepsis">Think could this be sepsis if:<sup>2</sup></h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Signs of infection, including history/evidence of fever or hypothermia</li>
<li><strong>PLUS ANY</strong> of the following
<ul>
<li>Looks sick/toxic</li>
<li>Parental and/or clinician concern</li>
<li>Immunocompromised</li>
<li>Altered behaviour or ↓ level of consciousness</li>
<li>Age &lt; 3 months</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If any of the above, screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>Be button battery aware</strong>
<ul class="pccm-content-list" style="margin-bottom:0;">
<li>Consider <a href="/docs/Emergency/Button-battery-ingestioninsertion">Button battery</a> ingestion if any of the following:
<ul>
<li>Battery missing, seen to be playing with battery (may deny ingestion)</li>
<li>Gagging, gulp, cough or choking episode</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Or, non-specific symptoms eg:
<ul>
<li>Unexplained partial food refusal, poor feeding - may still take soft food/fluids</li>
<li>Drooling or regurgitation</li>
<li>Croup like cough</li>
<li>Chest pain or grunting (may be due to chest pain in pre-verbal child)</li>
<li>Fever/vomiting/signs of infection without clear focus</li>
<li>Upper GI bleeding - melaena (black stools), vomiting blood (may mimic a nose bleed)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If not managed urgently, a swallowed button battery can burn a hole through the oesophagus into the aorta and cause fatal haemorrhage</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="step-1-history">Step 1: Obtain history of the presenting concern/problem<sup>9</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history in conjunction with examining the child</li>
<li>In a sick child do a full assessment of all systems</li>
<li>The history is the most powerful tool for identifying the diagnosis in most cases</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="step-3-physical-examination">Step 3: Do physical examination<sup>9</sup></h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="in-a-sick-child">In a sick child</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>A thorough and complete examination is required</li>
<li>All of the child’s clothing will need to be removed at some stage</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="in-a-child-who-is-not-sick">In a child who is not sick</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Examine the relevant system first + proceed to further examination as guided by the history + your findings</li>
<li>This is particularly important when examining children who often present with generalized signs and symptoms</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="tips-for-examining-children">Tips for examining children</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use distraction techniques</li>
<li>May be best done with the child on the carer's knee</li>
<li>If the child is irritable perform the examination opportunistically i.e. do what you can when you can</li>
<li>Leave the most disruptive parts until last eg ears + throat</li>
</ul>

<ul>
<li>compare against most recent weights</li>
<li>plot on growth charts appropriate for age + gender</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If appropriate, also measure:<sup>12</sup>
<ul>
<li>length if &lt; 2 years or height if &gt; 2 years + able to stand</li>
<li>head circumference if &lt; 2 years, or if indicated in older child</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Hydration</td>
<td class="pccm-row-content">

<li>Any weight loss</li>
<li>Eyes - normal or sunken. Tears absent or present</li>
<li>Mouth and tongue - wet or dry</li>

</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Skin</td>
<td class="pccm-row-content">
<ul>
<li>Always check the whole body, particularly in a sick child</li>
<li>Inspect for:
<ul>
<li>rash - non-blanching, petechiae, purpura</li>
<li>colour - unusually pale, mottled or cyanotic</li>
<li>bruising, unexplained or unusual marks</li>
<li>signs of infection - redness, swelling or tenderness</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Skin lesions or sores:
<ul>
<li>colour, shape, size, location, distribution on body</li>
<li>exudate eg clear, pus, bloody</li>
<li>any family members/close contact with similar lesions</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Any palpable/tender lymph nodes in the neck, axilla and groin</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Cardiovascular system</td>
<td class="pccm-row-content">
<ul>
<li>Inspect skin colour:
<ul>
<li>pink, white, grey mottling. Compare the trunk with the limbs</li>
<li>any oedema - check hands, feet, shins, lower legs, face</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Palpate/feel:
<ul>
<li>skin temperature - hot, warm, cool, cold, sweating. Compare the trunk with limbs</li>
<li>peripheral pulses - weak or strong</li>
<li>peripheral perfusion - ‘blanch’ the skin on a finger or toe for 5 seconds. Time how long it takes for the colour to return</li>
<li>central perfusion - as per peripheral perfusion, but blanch the skin over the sternum with your thumb</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If trained in auscultation listen to heart sounds</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Respiratory system</td>
<td class="pccm-row-content">
<ul>
<li>Most information is gained through inspection</li>
<li>Inspect anterior/posterior chest for:
<ul>
<li>equal chest movement</li>
<li>↑ WOB - use of accessory muscles, rib retraction/recession; nasal flaring; head bobbing</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If age appropriate, can child talk continuously, only in words/sentences/unable to talk at all</li>
<li>Measure RR over 1 minute - rhythm, depth and effort of breathing</li>
<li>Listen for extra noises - cough ± sputum, wheeze, stridor, grunt, snore, hoarse speech/cry</li>
<li>Auscultate air entry in both lung fields:
<ul>
<li>equal, adequate, decreased or absent</li>
<li>wheeze or crackles - on inspiration or expiration</li>
<li>note: transmitted sounds from the upper respiratory tract are very common in children and may mask other signs</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Will the child lie flat</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Gastrointestinal/reproductive systems</td>
<td class="pccm-row-content">
<ul>
<li>Inspect for:
<ul>
<li>scars, abdominal distension, hernias, bruising or other discolouration, prominent veins, obvious masses</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Auscultate bowel sounds - present or absent</li>
<li>Palpate abdomen - if pain, palpate with extra care:
<ul>
<li>soft or firm</li>
<li>any obvious masses</li>
<li>tender to touch - identify which abdominal quadrant and exact area</li>
<li>any guarding/rigidity - even when the child is relaxed</li>
<li>any rebound tenderness - press down and take your hand away very quickly - is the pain greater when you do this</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Percuss and feel for bladder</li>
<li>Check the testes in boys - are they both in the scrotum:
<ul>
<li>any redness, swelling or tenderness</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Nervous system</td>
<td class="pccm-row-content">
<ul>
<li>A brief assessment is all that is needed</li>
<li>Assess orientation to time, place and person if appropriate for child’s age:
<ul>
<li>ask - name, age, location, time, date, year</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Pupils - size, symmetry, reaction to light</li>
<li>Assess asymmetry of tone and power - compare each side of the face and limbs</li>
<li>If indicated, test touch sensation using cotton wool</li>
<li>Test finger nose coordination. If possible, observe child walking, looking around and using hands</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Musculoskeletal system</td>
<td class="pccm-row-content">
<ul>
<li>Any pain in limbs, joints or muscles</li>
<li>Check for range of motion in limbs, joints and muscles - active and passive</li>
<li>Any redness, pain, swelling, heat or laceration over or near a joint(s)</li>
<li>Observe gait</li>
<li>Consider <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-rheumatic-fever">ARF</a>, and <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acutely-swollenpainful-joint">Swollen/painful joint - child</a></li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td class="pccm-row-label">Ears, nose and throat</td>
