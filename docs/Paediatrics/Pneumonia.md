<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Pneumonia - Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever, cough</li>
<li>Looks unwell</li>
<li>↑ RR</li>
<li>↑ WOB - use of accessory muscles eg:
<ul>
<li>tracheal tug</li>
<li>subcostal and intercostal recession (indrawing between or under the ribs)</li>
<li>infants may have nasal flaring, grunting, head bobbing, episodes of apnoea</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
<li>Rapidly assess severity as per table below</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Also consider other diagnoses eg <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Bronchiolitis">Bronchiolitis</a>, <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Upper-respiratory-tract-infection">URTI</a></li>
<li>Get rapid history, including:
<ul>
<li>rapid breathing, apnoea</li>
<li>cough</li>
<li>pleuritic pain (sharp chest pain) in older child</li>
<li>abdominal pain<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>fever</li>
<li>feeding - normal or reduced intake/ability to feed</li>
<li>times passed urine/number of wet nappies in last 24 hours</li>
<li>vomiting, diarrhoea</li>
<li>recent infections eg bronchiolitis</li>
<li>recent travel</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>listen to chest for any crackles, wheeze</li>
<li>Hydration assessment - child (see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Gastroenteritisdehydration">Gastroenteritis/dehydration</a>)</li>
<li>check:
<ul>
<li>ENT</li>
<li>skin for rash</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Assess for risk factors for severe pneumonia eg:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>born &lt; 37 weeks</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
<li>underlying conditions eg chronic lung disease, cardiopulmonary disease, cancer</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO in all cases, urgently if moderate–severe or &lt; 3 months of age:
<ul>
<li>if &lt; 2 months treat as per <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a><sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="moderate-severe-pneumonia">Moderate–severe pneumonia<sup>2</sup></h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Arrange urgent evacuation<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>If unable to maintain SpO<sub>2</sub> ≥ 95% consult MO urgently</li>
<li>Monitor closely until evacuation:
<ul>
<li>vital signs + WOB</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>MO may order:
<ul>
<li>oral amoxicillin or IV/IM antibiotics:
<ul>
<li>if allergy to penicillins - MO will advise</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If not tolerating oral fluids, MO may consider NG or IV fluids</li>
<li>Offer paracetamol for discomfort of fever or pain. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="mild-pneumonia">Mild pneumonia</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MO may order:
<ul>
<li>oral amoxicillin:
<ul>
<li>if allergy to penicillins - MO will advise</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If mild with risk factors for severe pneumonia, further observation/hospitalizations may be required<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Consider sending home in consultation with MO if:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>normal RR + HR</li>
<li>able to maintain SpO<sub>2</sub> ≥ 95% in room air</li>
<li>feeding adequately</li>
<li>parent/carer can safely manage infant at home eg consider time of day, parent/carer understanding of condition, access to clinic</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Provide Advice to parents/carers before sending home</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> May cause rash, diarrhoea, nausea or thrush</p>

<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a><sup>1,4</sup></p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Advice to parents/carers<sup>2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Most children usually improve within 1–2 days of starting antibiotics, although they may cough for a few days/weeks</li>
<li>Bring your child back if you are worried or if any:
<ul>
<li>vomiting and are unable to drink much</li>
<li>difficulty breathing</li>
<li>very sleepy, becomes pale or sweaty, or begins to look blue in the skin</li>
<li>pauses between breaths</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Make sure your child is getting enough fluids. Smaller feeds given more often may help</li>
<li>Paracetamol may help if child looks uncomfortable</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated advise to be reviewed next day, or earlier if concerned:
<ul>
<li>consult MO if not improving</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise to see MO at next clinic</li>
</ul>
