<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Testicular/scrotal pain - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Testicular torsion</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Testicular torsion is an emergency.</strong> Surgery is required ≤ 6 hours to avoid permanent harm, even if the pain has been present &gt; 6 hours</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Pain in the testes/scrotum ± swelling/lump, nausea + vomiting</li>
<li>Abdominal pain only</li>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> if recent testicular injury assume testicular torsion until proven otherwise</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Rapidly assess against table below:
<ul>
<li>the presence or absence of a single sign cannot exclude testicular torsion +</li>
<li>keep in mind patient may be reluctant to volunteer symptoms due to embarrassment and reluctance to be examined<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Contact MO urgently in all cases of testicular/scrotal pain</li>
<li>Offer analgesia ± antiemetic. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a>, <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Nausea-and-vomiting">Nausea and vomiting</a></li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="assessment-of-testicular-pain">Assessment of testicular pain<sup>1,2</sup></h2>

<td><strong>Onset and location</strong></td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Sudden onset of severe unilateral scrotal pain + pain at rest</li>

<li>Gradual onset of pain and swelling over a few days</li>
<li>Usually 1 side</li>
</ul>

<td><strong>Other symptoms</strong></td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Nausea, vomiting, tachycardia, dizziness ± fever<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Dysuria, frequency, smelly urine</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Examination</strong></td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Absent cremasteric reflex*</li>
<li>Testis - abnormal position, horizontal lie on standing and high riding</li>
<li>Scrotal skin changes - red or darkening</li>
<li>Tender to palpate globally</li>
<li>Thickened spermatic cord</li>
</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Intact cremasteric reflex*</li>
<li>Red, hot, swollen testis in normal position</li>
<li>Tender epididymis (tubular structure found at back of testes, running in a sagittal plane)</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Also check</strong></td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Urinalysis - leucocytes does not exclude testicular torsion</li>

</ul>
</td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Urinalysis - NAD does not exclude EDO</li>
<li>Risk factors eg recent urethral surgery, history of viral infection eg mumps; sexually active<sup>4</sup></li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>

<p>*<strong>Cremasteric reflex</strong> - pinch or stroke the skin of the upper thigh. The testis on the same side should elevate via contraction of the muscle</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>5</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO urgently in all cases who will arrange urgent retrieval + referral to surgical team:
<ul>
<li>even if the pain has been present &gt; 6 hours</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Monitor closely:
<ul>
<li>vital signs + pain</li>
<li>re-examine scrotum if patient deteriorates, check for any changes<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Keep nil by mouth</li>
<li>Need urgent doppler scan of the scrotum – cannot be done at the outer island service setting</li>
<li>Consider <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-protection">Child protection</a>, if scrotal trauma/bruising, especially in infants or where the cause is unclear<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Need urgent referral so discuss with surgent for urgent advice and opinion.</li>
</ul>
