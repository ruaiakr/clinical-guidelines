<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Chemical burn to eye - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Immediate irrigation is critical to avoiding permanent damage + blindness.<sup>1-3</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>History of substance in eye(s) ±
<ul>
<li>Pain</li>
<li>weeping, swelling of the lids or conjunctiva</li>
<li>↓ vision</li>
<li>Photophobia</li>
<li>Foreign body sensation</li>
<li>Red eye</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Irrigate eye(s) with 1–2 L of sodium chloride 0.9% for ≥ 30 minutes. Use:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>IV bag with giving set fully open or<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>tap water/shower if nothing else available<sup>1,3</sup></li>
<li>do not examine eye(s) until this has been done</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Lie patient down, hold tubing 3–5 cm above eye surface, ask patient to:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>open eyelids as wide as possible - may need to hold eyelid open</li>
<li>look left, right, up + down while irrigating</li>
<li>if only one eye, tilt head away from unaffected eye</li>
<li>remove any foreign bodies - evert upper/lower eyelids + sweep with a soaked cotton tip<sup>1,3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Seek MO advice re pain relief</li>
<li>Offer analgesia if eye drops do not provide adequate pain relief. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a>.</li>
<li>Urgently contact MO</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get rapid history while irrigating:
<ul>
<li>when/how did it happen - if explosion may have other injuries<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>what was the chemical and active ingredients if possible. Note: alkalis burn deeper + require more irrigation eg lime, oven cleaner<sup>1-3</sup></li>
<li>any first aid, how soon after<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li>photophobia</li>
<li>pain in or around the eye</li>
<li>vision changes</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do vital signs?</li>
<li>After 30 minutes of irrigation:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>use litmus paper to check pH, touch the paper on inside lower eyelid</li>
<li>pH should be between 6.5–7.4</li>
<li>if not - keep irrigating + contact MO</li>
<li>use Morgan Lens® if skilled</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If pH 6.5–7.4 check:
<ul>
<li>VA + stain with fluorescein</li>
<li>outer aspects of eye + lids for abnormalities:<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li>cornea clear or cloudy, can you see iris details<sup>2</sup> — cloudy may indicate severe burn<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>evert lids again - any retained chemical<sup>2,3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>check surrounding skin</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO urgently, who will advise:
<ul>
<li>further management ± evacuation for ophthalmology review</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As per MO/ophthalmologist</li>
</ul>
