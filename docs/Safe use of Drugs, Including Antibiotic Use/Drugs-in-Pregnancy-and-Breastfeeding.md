<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Drugs in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding</h1>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always ask women of childbearing age about pregnancy before prescribing any drugs.</li>
</ul>

<p>During pregnancy, some drugs given to a mother can harm the developing baby. The most dangerous period for possible damage to the fetus is during the first 12 weeks of pregnancy. Certain drugs can also affect the baby via breastmilk.</p>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Give as few drugs as possible during pregnancy and breastfeeding and always check to make sure the drugs are safe to use.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="calculating-liquid-doses-when-working-with-medications">Calculating Liquid Doses When Working with Medications</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>To calculate the volume of liquid medication required:</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Dose you want (mg) × Volume on hand (ml) ÷ Dose on hand (mg) = Volume to administer (ml)</strong></p>
<p><em>Example:</em></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If the order is for 250 mg, and the medication comes as 500 mg in 2 ml:</li>
<li>(250 mg) × (2 ml) ÷ (500 mg) = 1 ml</li>
</ul>
<p>See also <a href="/docs/Safe%20use%20of%20Drugs,%20Including%20Antibiotic%20Use/Calculating-Liquid-Doses-When-Working-with-Medications">Calculating liquid doses when working with medications</a>.</p>
