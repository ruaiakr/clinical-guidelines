# Scabies – Adult/Child

Scabies – Adult/Child
Recommend
Treat scabies promptly to prevent secondary infections, which can lead to serious
complications such as acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (APSGN) and
acute rheumatic fever (ARF).
Background
Scabies is caused by infestation with the Sarcoptes scabiei mite and spreads
through close physical contact. It causes intense itching and skin lesions. Crusted
(Norwegian) scabies is a highly contagious form with a large mite burden. Dog
scabies does not affect humans.
For further details and photos, refer to the National Healthy Skin Guideline
at:https://infectiousdiseases.telethonkids.org.au/resources/skin-guidelines/
1.
May present with
Scabies
•	 Small bumps/papules, blisters ± visible burrows
•	 Very itchy, worse at night
•	 Common sites: finger/toe webbing, wrists, elbows, armpits, waistline, abdomen,
breasts, buttocks, thighs, genitals
•	 Infants: pustules on palms and soles; may be widespread and include head/neck
Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies
•	 Scaling and crusting, may not be itchy
•	 Creamy colour crusts, may affect large areas
•	 Common sites: buttocks, elbows, arms, palms, soles
•	 May resemble tinea, psoriasis, eczema, or dermatitis
•	 Highly contagious
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Not applicable

Honey-coloured crusts, pus-filled lesions, redness or weeping
•	 If crusted scabies suspected:

–
Take skin scraping for microscopy

–
Ask about household contacts and living conditions

Additional management
•	 If infected scabies, treat at same time – see Impetigo
•	 MO may order topical corticosteroid cream if itch persists after treatment
Scabies Advice
•	 Apply cream in clinic if needed
•	 After shower, apply to whole body (except face), including:

–
Between fingers/toes, soles of feet, under nails (use nail brush), genitalia,
behind ears

–
All creases (e.g. elbows, knees)
•	 Leave on overnight. Reapply if washed off.
•	 Infants &lt;6 months: cover hands after application; leave on for 8 hours
•	 Repeat treatment in 7 days
Household advice
•	 Treat all close contacts at same time – even if no symptoms
•	 Wash clothes/bedding the next morning; dry in the sun
•	 Keep children home from school/childcare until day after first treatment
•	 Itching can persist 2–4 weeks after treatment
•	 If persistent or worsening, consult MO
Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies
•	 Consult MO – may require hospitalisation and infectious disease specialist input
•	 Treat with:

–
Ivermectin (oral)

–
PLUS Benzyl benzoate or Lyclear®
•	 Wash crusts the next day with sponge before reapplying treatment
Household/environmental measures
•	 Wash clothes, sheets, and towels in hot water daily or seal in plastic for 8 days
•	 Vacuum furniture, floors, and cars
•	 Treat all contacts and monitor for reinfestation
