<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Urticaria, allergic rhinitis - Adult</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Hives, hay fever</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Hives (urticaria)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Itchy rash - blotches or raised red lumps:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>vary in size from pinhead to dinner plate. When first appear, can look like mosquito bites</li>
<li>usually disappear within minutes to hours in one spot, but may come and go</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>± angio-oedema - deeper swellings mostly affects face and lips:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>often bigger, last longer and may itch less ± hurt or burn</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>± symptoms of viral infection - most common cause of hives in children<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> if hives occur within 1–2 hours of exposure (eg food, medicines, stings) and disappear within 6–8 hours, is likely an allergic reaction. Localised hives may be a contact allergy<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Hay fever (allergic rhinitis)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Clear rhinorrhoea, sneezing, nasal blockage, nasal itch<sup>3,4</sup></li>
<li>Darkened circles around eyes ± watery, red eyes<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Itchy throat, frequent need to clear throat<sup>3</sup></li>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> symptoms can be confused with recent URTI</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If hives, check for ANY signs of anaphylaxis. If any treat urgently as per <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a></li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<p><strong>Signs of Anaphylaxis - any ONE of the following:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Difficult/noisy breathing</li>
<li>Swelling of tongue</li>
<li>Swelling/tightness in throat</li>
<li>Difficulty talking ± hoarse voice</li>
<li>Wheeze or persistent cough</li>
<li>Persistent dizziness or collapse</li>
<li>Pale and floppy (young children)</li>
<li>Abdominal pain, vomiting</li>
<li>Suspect Foreign body in nose, in child with any nasal occlusion, facial swelling ± pain, or smelly, purulent or blood-stained nasal discharge<sup>3,6</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>onset/duration of symptoms</li>
<li>known allergies/triggers eg food, exercise, pollen, dust mites, mould</li>
<li>previous episodes, treatment, was it effective<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>eczema, asthma<sup>6</sup></li>
<li>if hives:
<ul>
<li>foods and medications consumed several hours before the reaction<sup>6</sup></li>
<li>possible stings or bites<sup>6</sup></li>
<li>symptoms of a viral infection eg fever, malaise</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if hay fever:
<ul>
<li>are symptoms there year round, seasonal or come and go</li>
<li>is it troublesome for patient/impacting on sleep, sport, work etc</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>vital signs</li>
<li>if hives - check skin, describe lesions, localised/widespread</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If angio-oedema (see above), consult MO</li>
<li>Advise patient to avoid/minimise exposure allergens (if known)<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>Give antihistamine eg cetirizine (effective for hives and hay fever)</li>
<li>If hives advise:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>keep cool, wear loose clothing</li>
<li>avoid aggravating factors eg alcohol, excessive heat, spicy foods<sup>7</sup></li>
<li>do not take aspirin or other NSAIDs - can make symptoms worse</li>
<li>most hives will resolve within a couple of weeks without treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If hay fever:<sup>7</sup>
<ul>
<li>if watery, red eyes, also see Allergic conjunctivitis, for eye drops</li>
<li>advise to see MO at next clinic if:
<ul>
<li>severe/troublesome for patient, or has pre-existing asthma<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>note: hay fever can co-exist with asthma</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if allergic to pollens, advise may be at risk of thunderstorm asthma. Stay indoors in thunderstorms. Also see <a href="https://www.allergy.org.au/patients/asthma-and-allergy/thunderstorm-asthma">https://www.allergy.org.au/patients/asthma-and-allergy/thunderstorm-asthma</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
