<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Congestive cardiac failure - Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>CHF is a clinical syndrome that may develop slowly over months or years and is due to the heart’s inability to pump enough blood to meet the body’s needs.</li>
<li>It is vital to identify and manage both the underlying cause and any precipitating factors.</li>
<li>Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment can improve quality of life and reduce morbidity and mortality.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Congestive Heart Failure (CHF) is a condition caused by structural or functional abnormalities of the heart leading to impaired cardiac output. It is not a disease itself, but a syndrome resulting from other cardiac or systemic conditions.</p>
<p>CHF is commonly due to conditions such as ischaemic heart disease, valvular heart disease, hypertension, hyperthyroidism, or cardiomyopathy. Less common causes include congenital heart disease, infective endocarditis, myocarditis, cor pulmonale, and pericardial disease. In many cases, CHF is in a compensated state until decompensated by precipitating factors.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Shortness of breath on exertion or at rest</li>
<li>Orthopnoea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea</li>
<li>Fatigue or weakness</li>
<li>Peripheral oedema</li>
<li>Elevated jugular venous pressure</li>
<li>Hepatomegaly or ascites</li>
<li>Weight gain from fluid retention</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management If severe cardiac failure:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sit patient upright</li>
<li>Give oxygen (1–3 L/min if available)</li>
<li>Administer Frusemide 40 mg IM or IV slowly over 10 minutes</li>
<li>Contact medical officer urgently</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Take history of cardiac disease, hypertension, medications, salt/fluid intake, adherence</li>
<li>Examine for signs of fluid overload, arrhythmia, murmurs, infection</li>
<li>Investigations (if available):
<ul>
<li>CBC</li>
<li>Electrolytes and urea</li>
<li>ECG</li>
<li>Chest X-ray (if accessible)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Non-pharmacological:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Salt restriction (no added salt)</li>
<li>Weight reduction if overweight</li>
<li>Fluid restriction only if dilutional hyponatraemia (limit to 1.5 L/day)</li>
<li>Daily weights before breakfast with a target weight loss of 0.5–1 kg/day. If target weight loss not achieved it indicates inadequate diuresis and may push up frusemide dose if BP &gt;90/60.</li>
<li>Educate on medication adherence and monitoring symptoms</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Pharmacological (mild to moderate heart failure):</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Frusemide 40–80 mg orally daily</li>
<li>Enalapril 2.5 mg daily, increasing gradually to a max of 20 mg daily
<ul>
<li>Monitor for hypotension, especially in elderly, dehydrated, or hyponatraemic patients</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If still symptomatic, add:
<ul>
<li>Digoxin 0.0625–0.5 mg orally daily (adjust dose based on age and renal function)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Other medications (if available):</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Spironolactone, thiazide diuretics</li>
<li>Beta-blockers (carvedilol, metoprolol, bisoprolol)</li>
<li>Isosorbide dinitrate</li>
<li>Hydralazine</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Precipitating factors</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Poor medication adherence</li>
<li>Excess salt or fluid intake</li>
<li>NSAIDs, steroids, antidepressants, verapamil</li>
<li>Infections, especially respiratory</li>
<li>Anaemia, hyperthyroidism, uncontrolled hypertension</li>
<li>Arrhythmias (especially atrial fibrillation)</li>
<li>Acute myocardial infarction</li>
<li>Pregnancy</li>
<li>Overexertion</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor clinical status, weight, adherence, side effects</li>
<li>Adjust medications as needed</li>
<li>Reinforce lifestyle modifications</li>
<li>Ensure follow-up with medical officer</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral / Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always consult a medical officer when managing CHF</li>
<li>Refer urgently if:
<ul>
<li>No response to treatment</li>
<li>Worsening symptoms</li>
<li>Suspected underlying cause needing further investigation</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
