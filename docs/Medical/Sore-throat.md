# Sore throat – (Pharyngitis, tonsillitis) - Adult/Child

Sore throat – (Pharyngitis, tonsillitis) -
Adult/Child
Recommendation
•	 Treat sore throats with antibiotics promptly in people living in rural + remote
settings and in others who are at high risk of Acute rheumatic Fever and Acute
post streptococcal glomerulonephritis.
1.
May present with
•	 Sore throat ± fever
•	 Difficulty swallowing, not eating/drinking as much, croaky voice
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Look for symptoms of airway obstruction/compromise or deep neck space
infection eg peritonsillar abscess (quinsy), including:
•	 muffled voice, stridor, trismus (unable to open mouth)
•	 drooling, neck swelling/twisting, posturing ie sitting forward, neck extension
•	 severe neck pain, respiratory distress
•	 If any of above, contact MA, admit to start iv antibiotic, secure airway and for
urgent retrieval
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Get history, including:

–
cough, fever, rash

–
feeding - normal or reduced intake, difficulty swallowing

–
number of times passed urine/wet nappies in last 24 hours

–
other symptoms eg diarrhoea, vomiting, headache, malaise, runny nose, red
eyes, hoarse voice, joint pains

–
past history - ARF or RHD, immunocompromised, immunisation status
•	 Do physical examination, including:

–
chest - listen for crackles/wheeze, air entry

–
throat/mouth:

–
redness, swelling/pus on tonsils

–
ulcers/vesicles, strawberry tongue

–
greyish or green membrane - may indicate diphtheria

–
ears

–
palpate neck for enlarged/tender lymph nodes

–
look for rash + signs of APSGN, and ARF
•	 A runny nose or a hoarse voice is suggestive of a viral infection and does not
require antibiotic therapy.
•	 Sore throat in the absence of a runny nose or a hoarse voice is suggestive of a
bacterial infection may need antibiotics treatment.

Sore Throat: Is the Patient at High Risk of ARF (Acute Rheumatic Fever)?
Consider patient at high risk of ARF
if ANY of the following apply:
•	 Living in an ARF-endemic area
•	 Māori or Pacific Islander person
•	 Existing RHD, or history of ARF/
RHD and aged &lt; 40 years
Important Notes
•	 If on secondary prophylaxis for ARF and needs more antibiotics, confirm last
dose.
•	 If &lt; 7 days since last dose, do not give extra dose.
•	 The next secondary dose is due in 28 days unless otherwise reset.
Symptomatic Treatment
•	 Use paracetamol or ibuprofen for relief of pain and fever
•	 Throat lozenges may be helpful for adolescents and adults
•	 Encourage adequate fluid intake – prefer water, avoid sugary drinks
•	 Advise to rest and avoid exposure to smoke
Also Consider High Risk If:
•	 Family or household has recent
ARF/RHD Overcrowding (≥
2 people/bedroom) or low
socioeconomic status
•	 Migrant/refugee from a low- or
middle-income country
•	 Give 1 dose of IM benzathine
benzylpenicillin (Bicillin L-A®)
•	 If IM not possible, give oral
phenoxymethylpenicillin and
explain the importance of
completing the full course
•	 Mild allergy (e.g. rash): Give
cefalexin
•	 Severe allergy (e.g. anaphylaxis):
Give azithromycin
•	 Do a throat swab (MCS) first
•	 Give antibiotics immediately
Antibiotic Selection
Do NOT give antibiotics routinely
