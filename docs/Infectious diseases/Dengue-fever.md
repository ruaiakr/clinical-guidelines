<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Dengue fever</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Prevention involves controlling mosquito populations and reducing mosquito breeding sites.</li>
<li>Symptomatic treatment is the primary approach, with monitoring for warning signs of severe dengue, such as bleeding and shock.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Dengue fever is a viral illness transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes, primarily Aedes aegypti. It is endemic in tropical and subtropical areas. Dengue is characterized by high fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain, muscle and joint pain, and rash.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>High fever</li>
<li>Severe headache</li>
<li>Retro-orbital pain (pain behind the eyes)</li>
<li>Muscle and joint pains ("breakbone fever")</li>
<li>Rash (can appear 3-4 days after the fever)</li>
<li>Nausea and vomiting</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Symptomatic treatment: Paracetamol for fever, hydration, and rest.</li>
<li>Monitor for warning signs of severe dengue (e.g., bleeding, shock, organ failure). See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check for signs of dehydration and bleeding (e.g., petechiae, bleeding gums).</li>
<li>Measure blood pressure, heart rate, and temperature.</li>
<li>Assess for warning signs of severe dengue such as sudden abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, or bleeding.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Provide supportive care: Rest, fluids, and paracetamol for fever.</li>
<li>Monitor for signs of severe dengue.</li>
<li>If signs of severe dengue develop, initiate appropriate treatment for shock, bleeding, or organ failure. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a> and <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow-up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Follow-up within 24-48 hours to assess clinical improvement or worsening symptoms.</li>
<li>Immediate follow-up if warning signs of severe dengue appear.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to a medical officer immediately if signs of severe dengue such as shock, bleeding, or organ dysfunction are observed.</li>
</ul>
