<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Pleural effusion - Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<p>Pleural effusion is a potentially serious condition that may indicate infection, heart failure, or malignancy. Evaluation of the underlying cause and referral for definitive management is essential. If an underlying lung infection is suspected, treat empirically for pneumonia. Provide oxygen support if SpO<sub>2</sub> is less than 94%.</p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Pleural effusion is the collection of fluid in the pleural space surrounding the lungs. It commonly occurs due to tuberculosis, congestive heart failure, malignancy, or bacterial infections. Symptoms can be subtle but may include cough, shortness of breath, and chest discomfort. Diagnosis requires clinical suspicion and confirmation via chest examination or imaging.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Shortness of breath, especially on exertion</li>
<li>Chest heaviness or discomfort</li>
<li>Cough, which may be dry or productive</li>
<li>Fever or night sweats, especially if due to infection or TB</li>
<li>General fatigue</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess airway and breathing</li>
<li>Support the patient in a comfortable sitting position</li>
<li>Administer oxygen if SpO<sub>2</sub> is less than 94%</li>
<li>Take history:
<ul>
<li>Duration of symptoms</li>
<li>History of TB, heart failure, or cancer</li>
<li>Night sweats, weight loss, recent illness</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Physical examination:
<ul>
<li>Reduced chest movement on affected side</li>
<li>Dullness to percussion over fluid</li>
<li>Decreased or absent breath sounds</li>
<li>Reduced vocal fremitus</li>
<li>Possible tracheal deviation in large effusions</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check for associated signs:
<ul>
<li>Lymphadenopathy</li>
<li>Peripheral oedema (suggestive of heart failure)</li>
<li>Signs of systemic disease</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Perform full respiratory and cardiovascular exam</li>
<li>Assess vital signs</li>
<li>If signs of infection are present (e.g. fever, purulent sputum, crackles), treat empirically for <a href="/docs/Cardiovascular%20and%20respiratory%20disorders/Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> while awaiting referral</li>
<li>Consider differential diagnoses: pneumonia, TB, cancer, heart failure</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO in all suspected cases</li>
<li>Refer to hospital or doctor for further assessment and possible thoracocentesis</li>
<li>Do not attempt aspiration or drainage in PHC setting without training and supervision</li>
<li>Provide oxygen therapy if SpO<sub>2</sub> &lt; 94%</li>
<li>If pneumonia is suspected, start empiric antibiotic treatment as per <a href="/docs/Cardiovascular%20and%20respiratory%20disorders/Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> protocol</li>
<li>Supportive care: monitoring and symptom relief</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review at next clinic visit if already referred</li>
<li>Monitor for worsening breathlessness or new symptoms</li>
<li>Support adherence to TB or heart failure treatment if initiated</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer urgently if:
<ul>
<li>Moderate to large effusion suspected</li>
<li>Respiratory distress</li>
<li>Suspected TB or malignancy</li>
<li>No clear cause found</li>
<li>Patient fails to respond to initial antibiotic treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
