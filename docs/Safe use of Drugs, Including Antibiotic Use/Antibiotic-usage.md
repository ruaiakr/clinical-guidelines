Asprin and Paracetamol
Aspirin and paracetamol are commonly used to treat fevers and to reduce pain.
Health care workers should not automatically give drugs to reduce temperature
to all children with fever. First the cause of the fever should be determined, then
appropriate treatment given. If a child’s fever lasts beyond 2 days or reappears
within 14 days and the cause cannot be determined, the nurse or medical assistant
should consult the doctor about the fever.
USE OF PARACETAMOL
Paracetamol is given to reduce the discomfort of children with high fevers only (>
38.5°C)."Treat the child, not the thermometer."
USE OF PARACETAMOL


 Paracetamol is the drug of choice for fever in children. The dose is 15 mg/kg
body weight—see drug reference tables


 Paracetamol is TOXIC TO THE LIVER if given in too high doses or if the liver is
inefficient or unable to metabolize it because of inflammation or disease (such as
hepatitis or cirrhosis)


 Paracetamol can also cause kidney damage if given in too high doses


 Use paracetamol only when needed. Like all drugs it has side effects and
potential TOXIC EFFECTS
WHY WE AVOID USING ASPIRIN IN CHILDREN


 Aspirin use in children has many bad effects, compared to those of paracetamol


 Aspirin irritates the stomach and increases the RISK OF ULCER, HAEMORRHAGE
and PERFORATION


 Aspirin also increases the RISK OF BLEEDING


 Aspirin has also been linked to REYE’S SYNDROME. Reye’s syndrome, a disease
associated with liver, brain and neurological problems, has been linked to the use
of aspirin in children with viral infections

5 For these reasons, we use paracetamol, not aspirin in children and adolescents

DRUGS WHICH CAN BE HEPATOTOXIC (damaging to the liver)
The following drugs can potentially cause liver damage and must therefore be
used WITH CAUTION and ONLY WHEN INDICATED. Many people in Kiribati have
liver disease, hepatitis, cirrhosis and liver cancer. Persons with liver inflammation or
damage or disease may not have any signs or symptoms until late in the disease,
therefore we must be very careful in using drugs which can damage the liver.


 The drugs listed below are NOT GIVEN TO PATIENTS WITH LIVER DISEASE


 Aspirin, Brufen and Indocid are avoided in patients with liver problems because
they increase the risk of bleeding

Antibiotic usage
Standard guidelines are used in prescribing antibiotics. Antibiotics are chosen
on the basis of known or likely organisms.For example, most respiratory and skin
infections are caused by gram positive organisms. Penicillin is quite effective against
most gram positive organisms. Genitourinary infections are usually caused by gram
negative organisms. Amoxicillin and Cotrimoxazole are effective against many gram
negative organisms. Some antibiotics are “broad-spectrum,” they are effective
against both gram positive and gram negative organisms. Amoxicillin, erythromycin
and tetracycline are broad spectrum antibiotics. Before treating with antibiotics,
consider the cause of the infection. Viral infections should not be treated with
antibiotics.
OVERUSAGE OF ANTIBIOTICS is very dangerous for a number of reasons.
Overusage is costly, can result in dangerous allergic reactions and also results in
bacteria becoming resistant to many antibiotics. As bacteria become resistant,
certain antibiotics are no longer effective, resulting in possible death of the patient,
and eventual costly use of more expensive and more toxic antibiotics.
Antibiotics are also chosen on the basis of safety, effectiveness, few or minimal side
effects, and low cost. Many antibiotics, because of possible fetal anomalies are not
given in pregnancy; the safest antibiotic in pregnancy is penicillin or amoxycillin.
The dose of antibiotic varies according to age, weight, renal and liver function and
severity of infection. Serious infections can result in death if too low of an antibiotic
dose is given. Antibiotic doses that are too low also can result in bacteria continuing
to grow and becoming resistant to the antibiotic. Certain antibiotics have strict dose
ranges however, such as Gentamycin, because of toxic side effects.

The route of administration often depends on the severity of the infection. Serious,
life-threatening infections require IV antibiotics. When a patient is seriously ill, the
body is very busy fighting the infection, blood supply to the gut and muscles may
not be sufficient for drug absorption.
Duration of therapy depends on the nature of the infection and the response
to therapy. Always explain to patients the importance of taking the full course
of antibiotic. Prolonged courses, however, are costly, wasteful and may lead to
side effects. Certain infections do require long courses of antibiotics—such as
tuberculosis and osteomyelitis.
