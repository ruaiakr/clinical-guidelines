<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acute rheumatic fever (ARF) - Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>ARF should always be considered in patients with sore ± swollen joint(s) in populations at high risk of ARF, RHD or Group A Strep infections</li>
<li>Aim to prevent ARF by promoting:
<ul>
<li>early treatment of <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Impetigo-Child-Adult">Impetigo</a>, (skin sores) and <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Sore-throat">Sore throat</a></li>
<li>community control of <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Scabies">scabies</a> and skin sores</li>
<li>regular washing + hand hygiene, especially children</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>ARF:
<ul>
<li>is an auto-immune response to an untreated infection with Group A Strep in the throat + the skin. It affects the heart, joints, skin + the nervous system</li>
<li>difficult to diagnose. Correct diagnosis is important so that people who do have ARF are correctly managed + those who do not have it avoid unnecessary treatment</li>
<li>peaks in children aged 5–14 years + adults 15–24, reducing substantially with age. Is rare &gt; 35 years</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>High risk populations:
<ul>
<li>People living in rural + remote areas</li>
<li>previous history of ARF/RHD and aged &lt; 40</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Symptoms may evolve over several weeks</li>
<li>Can be very subtle eg joint pain or unexplained fever</li>
<li>Sore ± swollen joint - most common symptom (arthritis or arthralgia):
<ul>
<li>swollen hot joint with pain on movement</li>
<li>usually asymmetrical and migratory - 1 joint becoming inflamed as another subsides</li>
<li>may involve 1 or multiple joints</li>
<li>large joints usually affected - especially knees and ankles</li>
<li>usually extremely painful - often out of proportion with clinical signs</li>
<li>problems weight-bearing or walking unaided</li>
<li>joints may be painful but not swollen</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Fever ≥ 38 - common</li>
<li>Carditis:
<ul>
<li>heart murmur</li>
<li>may be signs of heart failure eg:
<ul>
<li>↑ RR, ↑ HR (resting)</li>
<li>crackles in base of lungs, pulmonary oedema</li>
<li>↑ JVP, oedema in feet/lower legs</li>
<li>puffy face, enlarged liver</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Sydenham chorea - a mood and movement disorder:
<ul>
<li>jerky, uncoordinated movement
<ul>
<li>especially affects hands, feet, tongue, face</li>
<li>disappears during sleep</li>
<li>may affect 1 side of the body only</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>relatives and teachers may describe them as ‘jumpy kids’</li>
<li>strongly associated with carditis</li>
<li>note: Sydenham chorea can develop after other symptoms have resolved and can be used solely to diagnose ARF</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Rare - subcutaneous nodules:
<ul>
<li>crops of small round painless nodules over elbows, wrists, knees, ankles, Achilles tendon, occiput and vertebrae</li>
<li>highly specific symptom of ARF; strongly associated with carditis</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Extremely rare - erythema marginatum:
<ul>
<li>circular patterns of bright pink macules or papules on trunk and proximal extremities</li>
<li>difficult to detect in dark skinned people, but highly specific for ARF</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Look for signs of carditis/heart failure. If present urgently contact MO:
<ul>
<li>+ strict bed rest</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about symptoms, in particular:
<ul>
<li>pain or swelling in limb(s) or joint(s)</li>
<li>jerky/uncoordinated movements</li>
<li>current or recent fever</li>
<li>measures taken to treat symptoms:
<ul>
<li>have they tried ibuprofen for joint pain; how effective</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>history from a relative or teacher eg strange movements</li>
<li>recent history of sore throat, painful joint(s) or skin infections, and if treated</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Get past history, including:
<ul>
<li>past episodes of ARF or previous symptoms suggesting ARF</li>
<li>family history of ARF/RHD</li>
<li>history of benzathine benzylpenicillin (Bicillin LA®) injections for ARF/RHD:
<ul>
<li>have any injections been missed</li>
<li>if unsure contact RHD control program</li>
<li>current medicines</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>vital signs - note any fever</li>
<li>inspect and palpate joints for:
<ul>
<li>swelling, tenderness and mobility</li>
<li>does the pain seem out of proportion to the joint signs</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>inspect:
<ul>
<li>skin for old or infected sores</li>
<li>throat for redness</li>
<li>for any jerky movements of the face, tongue, trunk and limbs</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>auscultate the heart if skilled - listen for a murmur/abnormal sounds eg whooshing sound</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO in all cases</li>
<li>See:
<ul>
<li>ARF Diagnosis calculator <a href="https://www.rhdaustralia.org.au/apps" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.rhdaustralia.org.au/apps</a></li>
<li>Suspected acute rheumatic fever clinical pathway</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If monoarthritis (inflammation of 1 joint) consider Septic arthritis, until proven otherwise</li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a>
<ul>
<li>give paracetamol. Withhold NSAIDs (eg ibuprofen) until diagnosis confirmed - can cause joint symptoms to disappear complicating the diagnosis</li>
<li>if severe pain consult MO</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>MO may:
<ul>
<li>arrange evacuation for physician/cardiology review, echo and for diagnosis</li>
<li>hospitalisation should occur within 24–72 hours after the onset of symptoms, even if symptoms resolve</li>
<li>note: thorough investigations for alternative diagnoses should always be undertaken eg septic arthritis, disseminated gonococcal infection, gout, innocent murmur, congenital heart disease</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If ARF possible/suspected give:
<ul>
<li>benzathine benzylpenicillin</li>
<li>if allergic give:
<ul>
<li>azithromycin if anaphylaxis or immediate reaction to penicillins</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> May cause diarrhoea, nausea and pain at injection site</p>
