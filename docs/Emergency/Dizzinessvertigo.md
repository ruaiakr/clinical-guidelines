<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Dizziness/vertigo - Adult</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Dizziness and vertigo are common symptoms, with many causes. Aim to identify serious causes</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background1</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Dizziness common causes - postural hypotension, arrhythmias, syncope, anaemia, hypoglycaemia, hypercalcaemia, “vitamin B<sup>12</sup>, medicine side e ect, anxiety,2 hyperventilation<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Vertigo - can be central (brain stem or cerebellum) or peripheral (inner ear) related</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Dizziness, faintness or feeling like body or surroundings are moving eg spinning</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>1,2</sup>,4,5</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check for Red flags:<ul><li>if any, get rapid history and contact MO urgently. Suspect Stroke</li><li>MO may consider retrieval for CT/MRI ± referral to neurologist Red flags</li></ul></li>
<li>Facial weakness/numbness</li>
<li>Arm ± leg weakness, ataxia</li>
<li>Speech di#culty/slurred</li>
<li>Visual eld loss, double vision</li>
<li>Difficulty swallowing</li>
<li>Sensory loss eg in 1 side of body</li>
<li>Confusion</li>
<li>Headache</li>
<li>New sudden unilateral hearing loss with 1st onset of continuous dizziness/vertigo</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup>,4,5</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history of dizziness/vertigo, including:2<ul><li>timing - episodic or continuous; gradual or sudden onset</li><li>when occurred, are episodes in severity or frequency<sup>4</sup></li><li>duration of episodes - seconds/minutes/hours/days</li><li>character:<ul><li>vertigo - spinning, rocking or tilting<sup>2</sup></li><li>dizziness - o balance, giddiness (light headed), faint (presyncope)</li></ul></li><li>spontaneous or triggered by eg head movement, sitting, standing, rolling over, tipping head back, lying down, loud noises, anxiety/stress</li></ul></li>
<li>Any associated symptoms eg:<ul><li>nausea, retching/vomiting, sweating<sup>2</sup></li><li>new hearing loss - 1 or both ears, fluctuating/continuous</li><li>tinnitus - noise or ringing in ears OR ear discharge/pain or ‘full’ sensation of ear</li><li>vision issues eg bouncing, bobbing or jerking of still objects (oscillopsia)<sup>6</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Get past history - including migraines, Ménière’s disease, current medicines</li>
<li>Any recent:<ul><li>change of medicine eg for BP</li><li>illness eg URTI</li><li>ear trauma or infection</li><li>head trauma - even minor</li><li>alcohol or drug use, exposure to toxin(s)</li><li>diving or other pressure change (that may cause barotrauma)</li></ul></li>
<li>Do physical examination + assess:<ul><li>vital signs + lying and standing BP - does BP drop when standing, how much</li><li>BGL, ECG</li><li>eyes - any nystagmus - quick jerky eye movements (vertical or horizontal), either spontaneous or only when looking in a speci c direction</li><li>Examine ears</li><li>observe patient’s ability to stand and walk. Are they unsteady If no red flags, consider additional assessments (as below) in consultation with MO</li></ul></li>
<li>Dix-Hallpike test if:1,2,5,7<ul><li>vertigo is episodic +</li><li>is only triggered by speci c head movement +</li><li>lasts only seconds +</li><li>nystagmus not occurring spontaneously +</li><li>no other symptoms</li></ul></li>
<li>HINTS plus exam if:<ul><li>vertigo has continued for hours/days + nystagmus occurring spontaneously<sup>3-5</sup>,8 DIX-HALLPIKE TEST<sup>4</sup> SEE VIDEO HTTPS://WWW.YOUTUBE.COM/WATCH?V=LXD-LGQIX-S OFFER TO SHOW PATIENT VIDEO 1ST, SO THEY KNOW WHAT TO EXPECT. WARN PATIENT IT CAN PROVOKE VERTIGO Get patient to sit at the edge of the bed. Turn their head with your hands 30–45o to 1 side Patient to keep eyes open + focus on a stable point. Then quickly assist patient to lie supine and hyperextend the neck 30–45o below level of bed, while still holding head. Look for nystagmus If no nystagmus seen, return to seated position, then test the other side If patient gets nystagmus and reproduction of positional symptoms, test is +ve for Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV)<sup>1</sup> - a common (not harmful) cause of vertigo due to disturbance of tiny crystals in inner ear<sup>1</sup> HINTS PLUS EXAM<sup>2,4</sup>,5 SEE VIDEO HTTPS://COLLECTIONS.LIB.UTAH.EDU/ARK:/87278/S6BP04F<sup>0</sup> CAN ASSIST IN DETERMINING IF VERTIGO HAS A CENTRAL OR PERIPHERAL CAUSE. DISCUSS FINDINGS WITH MO Head impulse test</li></ul></li>
<li>Sit opposite patient and ask them to stare at your nose</li>
<li>Hold the patient's head with both of your hands and turn head slowly side to side 10–20O then back to midpoint. Repeat but rapidly rotate the head to 1 side and pause. Patient's eyes should stay on the examiners nose with the rapid head turn</li>
<li>Repeat again but this time rapidly rotate to the opposite side</li>
<li>If eyes do a series of 'catch-up' movements to re-fixate on nose, test is +ve</li>
<li>Document the direction the head was rotated to when the 'catch-up' movement occurred - eg if catch-up movement was seen when head was quickly rotated to the patient's le', then document ‘+ve left head impulse test’ Nystagmus</li>
<li>Ask patient to follow your finger as you slowly move it right to left</li>
<li>Observe direction of the fast phase of nystagmus - horizontal, vertical, rotates, unidirectional or direction changing</li>
<li>Does nystagmus stop if patient stares at object Test of skew</li>
<li>Ask patient to look straight ahead. Cover 1 of their eyes with your hand. Then, quickly move your hand to cover the other eye</li>
<li>During this process, observe uncovered eye for any vertical/ diagonal corrective movement (if present, test is +ve) Plus acute hearing loss</li>
<li>Rub fingers next to both ears and ask if new unilateral hearing loss. If new and 1st onset of acute continuous vertigo, this is a +ve for a central sign<sup>9</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management<sup>1,5</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider calling vertigo hotline Dial-a-Dizzy 1300 4 DIZZY, for advice (office hours)</li>
<li>If BPPV likely:<ul><li>offer to do Epley manoeuvre (see video for Dix-Hallpike test for instructions). It may relieve vertigo immediately</li><li>offer advice and exercises to help, as per fact sheet eg www.safercare.vic.gov. au/ best-practice-improvement/clinical-guidance/emergency/vertigo</li></ul></li>
<li>If already diagnosed with Ménière's disease and acute attack, offer prochlorperazine</li>
<li>In all other cases of dizziness or vertigo consult MO:<ul><li>+ if nausea/vomiting, offer prochlorperazine or promethazine</li></ul></li>
</ul>
