Fractured jaw - Adult/Child
Recommend
•	 Be alert to airway obstruction from bleeding or swelling
1.
May present with
•	 Trauma to face + pain, swelling, bleeding, deformity, drooling
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Do primary survey DRSCABCD as per Traumatic injuries,
•	 Assess against Criteria for early notification of trauma for interfacility transfer
(inside front cover)
•	 Consider need for c-spine immobilisation,
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Ask about mechanism of injury +

–
timing + events surrounding the injury

–
any witnesses, any first aid





•	 Do vital signs + secondary survey EFGHIJ as per Traumatic injuries,
•	 Assess as per Head injuries,
•	 Do full facial examination, including:

–
check for signs of fractured jaw:
	›
clenched teeth not ‘fitting together’ as usual/misaligned
	›
limited or painful mouth opening1
	›
unable to maintain bite on tongue depressor while twisted2
	›
flattening, swelling of cheeks
	›
difficulty swallowing2
•	 any other injuries eg:

–
mouth + tongue - lacerations, swelling

–
broken teeth - keep knocked out teeth for possible replacement. See Trauma
to teeth,

–
orbit - palpate for step defect, numbness - may indicate fracture

4.	 Management
•	 Consult DO/MO who may consider:

–
IV antibiotics, x-ray, nil by mouth
•	 evacuation/hospitalisation for surgical review
•	 Offer analgesia. See Acute pain,

–
consider ice packs + soft collar for comfort1
•	 Check tetanus vaccination status and give booster if indicated.1 See Tetanus
immunisation,
•	 Consider Domestic and family violence,
•	 Closely monitor until evacuation + frequent airway checks
5.	 Follow up
•	 As per MO/DO
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 Refer to Oral Surgeon
