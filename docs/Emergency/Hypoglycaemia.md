<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Hypoglycaemia - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul>
<li>Hypoglycaemia is a medical emergency. If left untreated it can cause convulsions, irreversible brain damage + death<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul>
<li>Most common cause in children is ketotic hypoglycaemia (KH), a physiological condition that is a variant of normal + expected in a fasting state. Most outgrow KH by mid–late primary school<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>For known diabetics, hypoglycaemia may be secondary to insulin use</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>2,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>BGL &lt; 4 adult, &lt; 3 child</li>
<li>Sweating, pale skin, shaking, palpitations, feeling anxious</li>
<li>Hunger, difficulty concentrating, confusion/inappropriate behaviour</li>
<li>Loss of consciousness, seizures</li>
<li>Child - drowsy, listless + lethargic<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do BGL - rapidly assess + manage concurrently - avoid delays</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>ALERT</strong> if hazardous alcohol use or severely malnourished<sup>3,4</sup>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Conscious patients:
<ul>
<li><strong>Children:</strong> a teaspoon of powdered sugar in a few ml of water or 50 ml of fruit juice, maternal or therapeutic milk or 10 ml/kg of 10% glucose by oral route or nasogastric tube</li>
<li><strong>Adults:</strong> 15 to 20 g of sugar (3 or 4 cubes) or sugar water, fruit juice, soda, etc.</li>
<li>Symptoms improve approximately 15 minutes after taking sugar by oral route</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Patients with impaired consciousness or prolonged convulsions:
<ul>
<li><strong>Children:</strong> 2 ml/kg of 10% glucose by slow IV (2 to 3 minutes)</li>
<li><strong>Adults:</strong> 1 ml/kg of 50% glucose by slow IV (3 to 5 minutes)</li>
<li>Neurological symptoms improve a few minutes after the injection</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check blood glucose after 15 minutes. If it is still low, re-administer glucose by IV route or sugar by oral route according to the patient’s clinical condition</li>
<li>If there is no clinical improvement, differential diagnoses should be considered: eg serious infection (meningitis, etc.), epilepsy, unintentional alcohol intoxication or adrenal insufficiency in children</li>
<li>In all cases, after stabilisation, give a meal or snack rich in complex carbohydrates and monitor the patients for a few hours</li>
<li>If patient does not return to full alertness after an episode of severe hypoglycaemia, monitor blood glucose levels regularly</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If known diabetic, ask about:<sup>6</sup>
<ul>
<li>antihyperglycaemics, insulin - type, total daily dose, adherence<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>recent alcohol<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>unstable glycaemic control, past hypoglycaemic events</li>
<li>do they have a diabetes management plan</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Look for other causes, ask about:<sup>3</sup>
<ul>
<li>severe vomiting, diarrhoea or fasting in last 3 days - most common cause in children:<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>if fasted - how long before becoming hypoglycaemic</li>
<li>stopping hyperglycaemia-inducing drugs eg glucocorticoids</li>
<li>drugs + alcohol misuse/withdrawal</li>
<li>vigorous ± prolonged exercise, exposure to heat</li>
<li>recent unexplained weight loss/gain</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If child without clear cause, ask about:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>difficulty to wake in the morning</li>
<li>fasting overnight, if so how long</li>
<li>recent protein meal, fruit or honey</li>
<li>potential ingestion eg insulin, metformin, beta-blockers, quinine, chloroquine, salicylates + valproate</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Get past history, including:<sup>3</sup>
<ul>
<li>cognitive or kidney impairment</li>
<li>liver disease<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>coeliac disease, bariatric surgery eg gastric sleeve/bypass</li>
<li>hypopituitarism, adrenal cortical failure</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>Hydration assessment - adult or child</li>
<li>weight</li>
<li>if child - urinalysis for ketones (if blood ketones not available)<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>pregnancy test if female of reproductive age</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor closely until evacuated:
<ul>
<li>BGL every 15 minutes until normal, then hourly or as per MO<sup>1,3</sup></li>
<li>hourly - vital signs, neurological observations + IV site for signs of extravasation<sup>1,3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If non-severe hypoglycaemia<sup>3</sup>
<ul>
<li>Check BGL 15 minutely until normal, then 1–2 hourly for 4 hours</li>
<li>Check diabetes management plan (if in place)</li>
<li>Consult MO prior to discharge</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to be reviewed the next day or sooner if recurrent hypoglycaemia - contact MO</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Discuss with MO for further management if necessary</li>
</ul>
