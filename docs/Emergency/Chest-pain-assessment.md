<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Chest pain assessment</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Cardiovascular emergencies</p>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chest pain assessment is time critical<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Use cardiac clinical pathways. Ensure they are readily available.2,3 In Qld see https://clinicalexcellence.qld.gov.au/resources/clinical-pathways/cardiac-clinical- pathways</li>
<li>ECG Flash is available in some Qld remote facilities https://qheps.health.qld.gov. au/caru/networks/cardiac/ecg-flash</li>
<li>Do not use gastrointestinal (GI) cocktails eg ‘pink lady’ (oral viscous lidocaine (lignocaine), antacid ± anticholinergic) to assist in ruling out coronary ischemia 4,5</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chest pain or discomfort</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Aim to identify Acute coronary syndrome or other life-threatening conditions<ul><li>Do rapid assessment:</li><li>general appearance</li><li>vital signs</li><li>allergies</li><li>ECG - to be reviewed by MO within 10 minutes of presentation:</li><li>if difficult to interpret, MO may send to Medical Specialist using ECG Flash if available</li></ul></li>
<li>Ask about the pain and symptoms:<ul><li>Site - central chest, left chest, epigastric, shoulder/back, jaw, neck, arm</li><li>Onset - when did it start, sudden or gradual, is it present now</li><li>Characteristics - discomfort, pressure, tightness, heaviness, cramping, burning, ache, sharp, dull, stabbing, fullness, squeezing, tearing, ripping</li><li>Radiation - to neck, jaw, shoulder, one or both arms, into hands and wrists, back</li><li>Associated symptoms:<ul><li>SOB</li><li>nausea, vomiting, sweating</li><li>dizziness/light-headedness, fainting</li><li>fever, cough with purulent or pink frothy sputum or blood</li></ul></li><li>Timing:<ul><li>constant or intermittent</li><li>ever had this pain before, how often does it occur, how long did it last</li></ul></li><li>Exacerbating or relieving factors:<ul><li>what brought on pain eg activity, foods, cold, stress, trauma</li><li>what makes it better/worse eg rest, medicines (GTN, antacids), eating, position changes, deep inspiration, movement, cough, laugh</li><li>any analgesia taken, effect</li></ul></li><li>Severity - scale of 0–10, with 0 being none and 10 being the worst</li><li>Use flowcharts on next page to help with differential diagnosis - note, these flowcharts do not include all causes of chest pain Aortic dissection (rare)<sup>6</sup> Contact MO urgently</li></ul></li>
<li> Sudden onset of severe chest ± back pain:<ul><li>sharp, ripping, tearing, stabbing</li><li>can radiate anywhere in chest or abdomen</li></ul></li>
<li> Pulse deficits ie ↓flow to peripheral vessels</li>
<li> Commonly associated with hypertension or connective tissue disorder</li>
<li> Fainting, ↓BP, shock</li>

<li> Other symptoms eg SOB, nausea/vomiting, sweating, unexplained fatigue, syncope/fainting, dizziness, SOB when lying down</li>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment<sup>1,6</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get past history, including prior:<ul><li>presentation/admission to hospital/clinic with chest pain within 28 days<sup>3</sup></li><li>cardiac investigations eg stress test, exercise ECG, coronary CT angiography</li><li>heart disease, previous myocardial infarction</li></ul></li>
<li>Ask about cardiovascular risk factors:3<ul><li>↑ cholesterol</li><li>hypertension, diabetes</li><li>smoking - cigarettes/day, years smoking, quit date</li><li>obesity - weight, BMI</li><li>family history</li><li>chronic kidney disease - stage</li></ul></li>
<li>Also ask about:<ul><li>current medicines + aspirin, anticoagulants</li><li>lung disease</li><li>cancer</li><li>alcohol, recreational drug use eg cocaine, amphetamines</li><li>diet</li><li>recent events eg:<ul><li>pregnancy, trauma, major surgery or medical procedures</li><li>periods of immobilisation, long-distance travel</li><li>illness, fever, malaise</li><li>strenuous physical activity</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:<ul><li>BP on both arms - if concern for aortic dissection</li><li>inspect and auscultate chest ± heart sounds if skilled</li><li>palpate abdomen. See Abdominal pain</li></ul></li>
<li>Chest x-ray may be ordered by MO to assist in differential diagnosis</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>For all patients with suspected cardiac causes of chest pain see ACS</li>
<li>Urgently contact MO if severe or life-threatening symptoms</li>
<li>Consult MO for all other presentations of chest pain</li>
</ul>
