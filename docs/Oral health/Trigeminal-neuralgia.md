<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Trigeminal neuralgia – Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">(Kiribati Original Protocol)</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Trigeminal neuralgia is a chronic pain condition involving the trigeminal nerve (5th cranial nerve).</li>
<li>It causes sudden, severe, sharp, or electric shock-like pain on one side of the face.</li>
<li>Often triggered by light touch, chewing, talking, wind or brushing teeth.</li>
<li>May be idiopathic or due to compression, multiple sclerosis (MS), post-herpetic neuralgia, or rarely a tumour.</li>
<li>In resource-limited settings, clinical diagnosis and first-line treatment should be initiated before considering imaging.</li>
<li>See also:
<ul>
<li>Better Health Channel – Trigeminal Neuralgia<br /><a href="https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/conditionsandtreatments/trigeminal-neuralgia">www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/conditionsandtreatments/trigeminal-neuralgia</a></li>
<li><a href="https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/trigeminal-neuralgia">https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/trigeminal-neuralgia</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always discuss with Medical Officer (MO) if trigeminal neuralgia is suspected. This condition is rare in children – consider alternative diagnoses in young patients.</li>
<li>Early treatment improves quality of life and reduces the risk of persistent pain and weight loss.</li>
<li>If recurrent or resistant to treatment, refer to visiting neurologist or dental surgeon if available.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Severe, sharp, stabbing or electric-shock pain in the cheek, jaw, teeth, or forehead.</li>
<li>Pain occurs on one side only.</li>
<li>Attacks last from a few seconds to 2 minutes, often in clusters.</li>
<li>Pain may be triggered by:
<ul>
<li>Chewing, swallowing, brushing teeth</li>
<li>Talking, touching the face, cold wind</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>No fever, swelling, rash or pus</li>
<li>No facial numbness or weakness (if present → consider another diagnosis)</li>
<li>Atypical or persistent pain, numbness or bilateral symptoms may suggest another condition (e.g. dental abscess, shingles, sinusitis, MS, tumour).</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Reassure patient and explain condition.</li>
<li>Start first-line medication only after MO review.</li>
<li>Avoid known triggers (e.g. cold wind, chewing on affected side).</li>
<li>Advise to return for review if:
<ul>
<li>Symptoms worsen or do not improve in 1–2 weeks</li>
<li>Facial numbness, weakness, or persistent pain occurs</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do not attempt dental extraction unless clear cause identified.</li>
<li>If available, refer to dental or visiting specialist clinic.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>History:</strong>
<ul>
<li>When did pain start? How long does each episode last?</li>
<li>Which part of the face is affected?</li>
<li>Any known triggers?</li>
<li>Pattern: how often, how intense?</li>
<li>Previous similar episodes?</li>
<li>Recent dental problems or facial infections?</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Examination:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Check vital signs</li>
<li>Inspect skin for rash or swelling (exclude herpes zoster)</li>
<li>Check cranial nerves: any weakness, droop, numbness?</li>
<li>Examine teeth and gums</li>
<li>Palpate face for tenderness or masses</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If neurological signs or persistent pain → MO may refer for imaging (if available)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>First-line medication (after MO review):
<ul>
<li><strong>Carbamazepine</strong>
<ul>
<li>Form: Tablet (100 mg)</li>
<li>Strength: 100 mg</li>
<li>Route: Oral</li>
<li>Dose: Start 100 mg twice daily; increase slowly every 3–7 days</li>
<li>Maximum: Usually 400–1200 mg/day in 2–3 divided doses</li>
<li>Duration: Review after 2 weeks</li>
<li>Contraindications: Hypersensitivity, bone marrow suppression</li>
<li>Safety notes: Monitor for drowsiness, dizziness, rash, low sodium, low white cell count. Do not stop suddenly.</li>
<li>Blood tests (if available): FBC, electrolytes, LFTs prior and during treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If carbamazepine is not tolerated: MO may consider, or amitriptyline if available</li>
<li>Non-medication care
<ul>
<li>Soft diet, avoid cold wind, cover face in wind</li>
<li>Offer counselling for chronic pain or mental health impact</li>
<li>Monitor for weight loss or sleep disruption</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review within 1–2 weeks to assess symptom control and side effects</li>
<li>If pain is well controlled, continue same dose</li>
<li>If no improvement or poor tolerance, discuss with MO</li>
<li>Ongoing review if long-term therapy is needed</li>
<li>Encourage return for reassessment if:
<ul>
<li>Pain worsens</li>
<li>New neurological symptoms develop</li>
<li>Unable to eat or sleep</li>
<li>Facial numbness or weakness appears</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to MO or specialist if:
<ul>
<li>Diagnosis uncertain or persistent pain</li>
<li>Numbness or facial weakness present</li>
<li>Recurrent episodes not responding to treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If available, refer to:
<ul>
<li>Dental services if suspect dental origin</li>
<li>ENT or neurology specialist (if visiting or via outreach)</li>
<li>Consider telehealth if imaging or specialist opinion needed</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If suspected secondary cause (MS, tumour, shingles) → refer to hospital</li>
</ul>
</div>
