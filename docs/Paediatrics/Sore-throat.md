<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Sore throat - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Pharyngitis, tonsillitis</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Treat sore throats with antibiotics promptly in people living in rural + remote settings and in others who are at high risk of <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-rheumatic-fever">ARF</a>, and <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-post-streptococcal-glomerulonephritis">APSGN</a></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sore throat</li>
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Difficulty swallowing</li>
<li>Not eating/drinking as much</li>
<li>Croaky voice</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Look for symptoms of airway obstruction/compromise or deep neck space infection eg quinsy, including:
<ul>
<li>muffled voice, stridor, trismus (unable to open mouth)</li>
<li>drooling, neck swelling, torticollis (twisting of neck)</li>
<li>severe neck pain, respiratory distress</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If any of above, urgently contact MO for urgent evacuation + airway management</li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>cough, fever, rash</li>
<li>feeding - normal or reduced intake, difficulty swallowing</li>
<li>times passed urine/number of wet nappies in last 24 hours</li>
<li>other symptoms eg diarrhoea, vomiting, headache, malaise, runny nose, red eyes, hoarse voice</li>
<li>past history of ARF, immunocompromised</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>chest - listen for crackles/wheeze, air entry</li>
<li>throat/mouth - redness, swelling/pus on tonsils, ulcers/vesicles</li>
<li>ears</li>
<li>palpate neck for enlarged/tender lymph nodes</li>
<li>look for rash + signs of <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-post-streptococcal-glomerulonephritis">APSGN</a>, and <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-rheumatic-fever">ARF</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Use table below for Differential diagnosis of sore throat</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO if:
<ul>
<li>&lt; 1 year of age</li>
<li>unable to drink/signs of dehydration</li>
<li>T ≥ 39</li>
<li>looks sick, not alert or interactive when T reduced</li>
<li>rash or signs of ARF/APSGN</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Use flowchart below to determine if antibiotics are indicated or not for ‘sore throat’</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="antibiotic-indications">Antibiotic indications for sore throat<sup>1</sup></h3>

<div class="pccm-flowchart" role="region" aria-label="Antibiotic indications for sore throat">
<div class="pccm-flow-stack">
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#f8e4c8; border:2px solid #c47a3a;">
<p class="pccm-flow-heading" style="color:#222;">Is patient at high risk of ARF. ANY of:</p>
<ul>
<li>Person living in rural or remote setting, or</li>
<li>Maori or Pacific Islander person, or</li>
<li>Existing RHD, or</li>
<li>History of ARF/RHD and aged &lt; 40</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">▼</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-split">
<div class="pccm-flow-branch">
<div class="pccm-flow-label" style="color:#c47a3a;">Yes <span>▼</span></div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#fff0dc; border:2px solid #e8a060;">
<ul>
<li>Do throat swab MCS first</li>
<li>Give antibiotics</li>
<li>Give IM <a href="#benzathine-benzylpenicillin">benzathine benzylpenicillin</a> (Bicillin LA®)</li>
<li>If IM injection not possible, give oral <a href="#phenoxymethylpenicillin">phenoxymethylpenicillin</a></li>
<li>If hypersensitive eg rash, give cefalexin</li>
<li>If anaphylaxis or immediate reaction give <a href="#azithromycin">azithromycin</a></li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-branch">
<div class="pccm-flow-label">No <span>▼</span></div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box pccm-flow-box--blue">
<p><strong>Do NOT give antibiotics</strong></p>
<p>If symptoms suggest Strep A sore throat/tonsillitis:</p>
<ul>
<li>take throat swab MCS</li>
<li>advise to be reviewed next day or sooner if worried/new symptoms develop eg rigors, dehydration</li>
<li>if not improving/getting worse in 3–7 days + swab confirms <em>S. pyogenes</em> consider oral phenoxymethylpenicillin</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-note-panel" style="margin-top:1.25rem;">
<p><strong>May be at high risk of ARF if</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Family or household recent history of ARF/RHD</li>
<li>Overcrowded living or low socioeconomic status</li>
<li>Migrant or refugee from low-middle income country</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> if oral antibiotics given - advise the importance of taking whole course to prevent ARF.</p>
<p>If on Secondary prophylaxis for ARF still needs more antibiotics, unless given ≤ 7 days prior<sup>1</sup></p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="symptomatic-treatment">Symptomatic treatment of sore throat<sup>5</sup></h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Paracetamol or ibuprofen for pain and fever. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
<li>Throat tablets for adolescents/adults<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Drink plenty of water, avoid sugary drinks; sip warm water/gargle with salt water</li>
<li>Keep away from smoke, get plenty of rest</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="benzathine-benzylpenicillin">BENZATHINE BENZYLPENICILLIN (BICILLIN LA®)</h3>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">6. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check results of throat MCS if taken</li>
<li>Advise to be reviewed the next day, or earlier if worried, symptoms worsen or new symptoms develop eg vomiting, dehydration, fever, rigors:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>contact MO if not improving</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If at high risk of ARF:
<ul>
<li>provide education on signs and symptoms of ARF and APSGN, which can occur about 10 days to 2 weeks after sore throat or skin sores</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
