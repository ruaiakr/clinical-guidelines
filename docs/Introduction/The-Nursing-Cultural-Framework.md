# The Nursing Cultural Framework (Abridged Version)

10 principles of nursing including standards are provided in the code.
These characteristics are fundamental to I-Kiribati cultural values and are
in harmony with each other.
The term ‘trust’ has been used deliberately as an entry-point to defining
the 10 principles. This is because the first step to achieving successful
partnerships of any kind, is in the patient trusting that the nurse has their
best interests at heart. This is the Kiribati way:
Principle 1: Kateimatoaon kamanoan kakawakii aoraki
Translation: Maintain patient trust by protecting their dignity.
Principle 2: Kateimatoaen te reitaki ae tamaroa rinanon te makuri;
Translation: Maintain patient trust by working in partnership
Principle 3: Kateimatoaan ao uarokoan makuri ni kuakua rinanon te katei
ao te aroaro.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by providing culturally responsive care
Principle 4: Kateimatoaan ao kamanoan rongorongoia ma tararuakiia
aoraki.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by ensuring privacy and confidentiality
Principle 5: Kateimatoaen ao kanakoroaon te kuakua ae oioi iaoiia ke
mairouii aoraki.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by the provision of patient-centred care
Principle 6: Kateimatoaan te tararua ae manoroai, nakoroai ao n rokoroi.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by providing safe and competent care
Principle 7: Kateimatoaan onimakinan te makuri ni kuakua ao n tararua
iroiiia Neeti.
Translation: Maintain patient trust in the nursing profession
Principle 8: Kateimatoaan onimakinan karinean inaomatan te aoraki.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by providing informed consent
Principle 9: Kateimatoaan karinean te reitaki ae tamaroa imarenaia Neeti
ao raoia ni beku.
Translation: Maintain patient trust through respectful relationships with
colleagues
Principle 10: Kateimatoaen te onimakinaki rinanon kakaraon te koaua, te
eti ao te riai.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by working with integrity and honesty

The Nursing Cultural Framework
(Abridged Version)
Four key objectives underpin the Cultural Framework:
1.	 Ara Katei / Culture
2.	 Aoraki ma aia utu / Patients as consumers of Health Services and their
family
3.	 Te bonnano / Unity
4.	 Karikirakean te rabakau n te mwakuri / Professional Development
Vision
A healthy and prosperous I-Kiribati through quality and responsive nursing
workforce founded on unique cultural needs and values.
Philosophy
The cultural framework will serve as the ‘roadmap’ and also a ‘key driver’
for I-Kiribati Nurses in ensuring optimal health and safety for patients
which is an inclusive term for (individuals, families and communities) as
well as the safety for the profession.
The Philosophy is grounded on I-Kiribati cultural needs and values; to
ensure public confidence and trust, nurses and/or midwives are required
to uphold a high standard of conduct or behaviour in their personal and
professional lives.

Maneaba: I-Kiribati Nursing Model of Care
Maneaba means accommodating and welcoming: A safe place to rest, to
meet or get together or take refuge.
Historically, the maneaba was considered a meeting place for villages/
island to congregate and make important decisions. Customarily, subject
to context e.g. church gatherings/tribal affiliations or individual status,
special seating allocations within the maneaba are adhered to accordingly.
Traditional beliefs suggests that the Maneaba had been ‘purposefully’ built
with a lowly-positioned roof to serve as a constant reminder to I-Kiribati
that on entering the dwelling, they must always bow or kneel to show
respect for their Unimwane & Unaine / Elders. This was also symbolic of
acknowledging their ancestral gods, prior to the arrival of Christianity on
their shores.
In present-day, although many members of the Kiribati community have
adapted to Western ideology, traditional values and beliefs are still
maintained. Hence, the symbolic gesture in the use of the Maneaba as a
cultural framework for nursing is befitting.
This is likened to other Polynesian conceptual frameworks where
traditional thatched or flax-woven shelters have been utilised
metaphorically to depict the significance of culture in their care delivery.
