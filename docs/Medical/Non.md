# Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) Assessment and management

Non-Communicable Disease (NCD)
Assessment and management
Recommend
•	 Assess all adult patients for common NCDs: obesity, hypertension, and diabetes.
•	 Use risk-based approach for prevention and treatment.
•	 Counsel patients on lifestyle changes and adherence to medications.
•	 Refer to hospital or physician when urgent criteria are met.
Background
•	 Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are a major public health concern in
Kiribati. The most prevalent NCDs include cardiovascular diseases, diabetes,
and associated metabolic conditions such as obesity and dyslipidemia.
Effective management requires a comprehensive approach including lifestyle
interventions, medication adherence, regular screening, and timely referral.
1.
May present with
•	 General fatigue or weakness
•	 Excess weight or central obesity
•	 Headaches, dizziness
•	 Blurred vision or visual changes
•	 Excessive thirst, hunger, urination
•	 Sores on feet or infections that heal slowly
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Check vital signs: BP, HR, weight, height (calculate BMI)
•	 Measure random or fasting blood glucose
•	 Ask about lifestyle, symptoms, family history
•	 If symptoms suggest urgent referral (e.g. BP >200/120 or blood glucose >30
mmol/L), refer immediately
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 BMI:

–
Overweight: 25.0–29.9

–
Obesity: ≥30

How to measure BMI and screen for obesity
MEASURE BODY MASS INDEX (BMI):
•	 Using the adult height board, take the client’s height in centimetres.
•	 Using the beam balance (weighing scale), take the client’s weight in kilograms.
•	 Use the BMI chart or the formula below to compute for the BMI
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)2     or
         = weight (kg) ÷ height (cm) ÷ height (cm) x 10,000
CLASSIFY
If the client’s BMI is equal to or above 30, the client is obese and is AT RISK.

4.	 Obesity
•	 Counsel on dietary changes, portion control, physical activity
•	 Monitor BMI and waist circumference
5.	 Management of Hypertension
•	 Start treatment within 4 weeks of diagnosis
•	 &lt;65 yrs: HCTZ 12.5–25 mg/day or Enalapril 5–20 mg/day
• ≥65 yrs: Amlodipine 2.5–10 mg/day or HCTZ
•	 Use combinations as needed (e.g. Enalapril + HCTZ)
•	 Avoid Atenolol in asthma; avoid HCTZ in gout

6.	 Management of Diabetes
•	 Metformin 500 mg bd with meals (max 1 g tid)
•	 Add Glibenclamide or Glipizide if uncontrolled
•	 If still uncontrolled, add insulin (Isophane 10U at night)
•	 Monitor fasting BGL daily, titrate as needed
Supportive Management
•	 Statins (Simvastatin/Atorvastatin) for Type 2 DM ≥40 yrs or TC ≥8 mmol/L
•	 Aspirin 75–100 mg if CVD risk ≥20% (no contraindications)
•	 Inspect feet every visit; perform microfilament test every 6 months
•	 Eye exam yearly; refer for vision changes
•	 Check urine protein yearly

MANAGEMENT OF HYPOGLYCAEMIA (EMERGENCY)
(PG. 48 OF PEN GUIDELINE.)
•	 Hypoglycaemia is blood glucose level of 4mmol/L or less
•	 The signs and symptoms of hypoglycaemia are:

–
Cold sweat

–
Tremor

–
Tingling in the lips or mouth

–
Hunger

–
Palpitations

–
Pounding headache

–
Feeling faint or hungry

–
Light headedness

–
Irritability

–
Confusion

–
Decreased or loss of consciousness
•	 If the patient is unconscious do not administer anything orally.  Seek medical
attention immediately.
•	 If the patient experiences symptoms of hypoglycaemia or a blood glucose level
of 4mmol/L or less and is conscious:
•	 Administer quick-acting sugar immediately.   Examples of quick-acting sugar
include:

–
2 teaspoons of sugar

–
2 teaspoons of honey or jam

–
1 glass of fruit juice

–
 ½ can of soft drink

–
6 jelly beans or soft lollies

–
1 ripe banana
•	 Recheck blood glucose level in 15 minutes, if available.
•	 If blood glucose level is ≤4mmol/L or the patient is not feeling better, give
quick-acting sugar again or give long acting carbohydrate or let the patient
have meal if it is due. Example of long acting carbohydrates include:

–
1 piece of fruit

–
Sandwich

–
Biscuits
•	 Let the patient rest.
•	 Identify the cause of hypoglycaemia, which may include:

–
Missing a meal, delayed meal or no carbohydrate in previous meal

–
Physical activity or exercise

–
Too much oral hypoglycaemic or insulin

–
Too much intake of alcohol or intake of alcohol on an empty stomach
•	 Advise the patient to carry quick-acting sugar with them at all times.
*Improving Diabetes Care, A Training Program for the Health Workers in Nauru.
World Diabetes Foundation Project, 2007.

MANAGEMENT OF DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS (EMERGENCY)
(PG. 47 OF PEN GUIDELINE.)
•	 Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is characterized by ketosis, hyperglycaemia and
acidosis. DKA occurs predominantly in Type 1 diabetic patients but can occur
in Type 2 diabetes.
•	 The common precipitating factors of DKA are omission of insulin, infection
or sepsis, drugs such as corticosteroids and recent trauma or acute coronary
event.
•	 Symptoms include:

–
Vomiting

–
Abdominal pain

–
Kussmaul’s breathing (initially rapid and shallow but as acidosis worsens,
breathing becomes deep and laboured)

–
Mental confusion progressing to coma

–
Dehydration

–
Ketotic breath
•	 Patients also notice rapid weight loss which can be a marker for dehydration. It
is necessary to test urine for moderate to large ketone bodies.
•	 Management should be undertaken urgently, and the most important
intervention is
FLUID REPLACEMENT
•	 Administer intravenous normal saline – first litre for 30 minutes, second later
for one hour, third litre for 2 hours and fourth later for 4 hours.
•	 Transfer patient to the hospital once condition is stabilized.
•	 In case that an IV access cannot be established, give a short-acting insulin e.g.
Actrapid IM at 8 units/hour. Insulin therapy can be started subsequently.
*RMI National Diabetes Guidelines 2016

THE RECOMMENDED GUIDELINES ON ANTI-PLATELET DRUG
(PG.43 OF THE PEN GUIDELINE)
All individuals with established CVD or CVD risk ≥20% should be given
low-dose Aspirin (start at 75mg – 100mg) if there are no contraindications
(e.g. hypersensitivity to aspirin, bleeding GI ulcers, haemorrhagic diathesis,
haemorrhoids, thrombocytopenia, ulcerative colitis etc.)

RECOMMENDED GUIDELINES ON LIPID-LOWERING DRUGS (STATINS)
(PG.42 OF THE PEN GUIDELINE)
All individuals with total cholesterol at or above 8 mmol/L (320 mg/dl) should
be advised to follow a lipid-lowering diet and given a statin (Simvastatin/
Atorvastatin, start at 10mg – 40mg, nocte) to lower the risk of cardiovascular
disease.
All other individuals need to be managed according to the cardiovascular risk (10-
year risk of cardiovascular event &lt;5%, 5 to &lt;10%, 10 to &lt;20%, ≥20%)
