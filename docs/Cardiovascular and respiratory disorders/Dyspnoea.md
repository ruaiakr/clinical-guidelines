<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Dyspnoea – Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<p>Dyspnoea is a serious clinical symptom that may be caused by cardiac or respiratory conditions. Early identification of the underlying cause is important for appropriate management. Sudden onset dyspnoea, especially in children, may indicate foreign body aspiration and requires urgent attention.</p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Dyspnoea is defined as an uncomfortable awareness of breathing or difficulty in breathing. It can result from disease of the lungs, heart, or nervous system. Common causes include <a href="/docs/Emergency/Acute-asthma">asthma</a>, <a href="/docs/Cardiovascular%20and%20respiratory%20disorders/Pneumonia">pneumonia</a>, <a href="/docs/Cardiovascular%20and%20respiratory%20disorders/Congestive-cardiac-failure">congestive heart failure</a>, <a href="/docs/Emergency/Pulmonary-embolism">pulmonary embolism</a>, <a href="/docs/Common%20medical%20conditions/Anemia">anaemia</a>, or <a href="/docs/Cardiovascular%20and%20respiratory%20disorders/Pleural-effusion">pleural effusion</a>. It is essential to distinguish between cardiac and respiratory origins of breathlessness for appropriate management.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Shortness of breath during activity or at rest</li>
<li>Chest tightness (may be mistaken for pain)</li>
<li>Waking at night gasping for breath (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea)</li>
<li>Needing multiple pillows to sleep (orthopnoea)</li>
<li>Wheeze, cough (dry or productive)</li>
<li>Fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance</li>
<li>Anxiety, rapid breathing, fast heartbeat</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess airway, breathing, and circulation</li>
<li>Provide oxygen if available (1–3 litres per minute)</li>
<li>Support patient in a sitting position</li>
<li>Ask about and document key symptoms and history:
<ul>
<li>Onset, duration, triggers (e.g. exercise, allergens)</li>
<li>History of heart disease or respiratory illness</li>
<li>Smoking or occupational exposures</li>
<li>Presence of cough, sputum (colour, amount)</li>
<li>Orthopnoea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do full physical examination:
<ul>
<li>Vital signs: RR, SpO<sub>2</sub>, HR, BP, temperature</li>
<li>Chest exam: wheeze, crackles, reduced breath sounds, dullness to percussion</li>
<li>Cardiac exam: murmur, elevated JVP, pedal oedema</li>
<li>General appearance and work of breathing</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Consult MO urgently if:
<ul>
<li>Acute severe distress</li>
<li>Hypoxia or cyanosis</li>
<li>Suspected cardiac failure or pulmonary embolism</li>
<li>No clear diagnosis after assessment</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Distinguish between likely cardiac and respiratory causes:
<ul>
<li><strong>Cardiac:</strong> history of heart failure, dry cough, white frothy sputum, orthopnoea, peripheral oedema, crepitations, improved with diuretics</li>
<li><strong>Respiratory:</strong> history of asthma or chronic lung disease, purulent sputum, wheeze, improved with bronchodilators</li>
<li><strong>PE:</strong> sudden onset, tachypnoea, tachycardia, immobility history</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Identify red flags:
<ul>
<li>Confusion, cyanosis, very low SpO<sub>2</sub>, inability to speak full sentences</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start initial treatment based on suspected cause (e.g. salbutamol for asthma, frusemide for heart failure)</li>
<li>Provide oxygen if desaturation is present</li>
<li>Consult MO for further management or referral</li>
<li>Prepare for evacuation if severe symptoms or uncertain diagnosis</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor vital signs regularly</li>
<li>Reassess respiratory status every 2–4 hours</li>
<li>Review in clinic once stable to confirm underlying diagnosis and ongoing treatment</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to medical officer for all moderate to severe cases or if diagnosis unclear</li>
<li>Evacuate if respiratory distress does not improve with initial treatment or if hypoxia persists</li>
</ul>
</div>
