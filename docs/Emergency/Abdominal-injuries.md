<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Abdominal injuries - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">patients1</p>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Missed abdominal injuries are a major cause of avoidable death in trauma</li>
<li>The absence of abdominal pain does not rule out the presence of significant abdominal injury<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Abdominal trauma<ul><li>Blunt abdominal injury<ul><li>Blunt abdominal trauma is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality among all age groups. Identification of serious intra-abdominal pathology is often challenging; many injuries may not manifest during the initial assessment and treatment period.</li></ul></li><li>Penetrating abdominal injury<ul><li>Penetrating abdominal trauma typically involves the violation of the abdominal cavity by a gunshot wound or stab wound. Blunt Abdominal Injury:</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>History taking and identifying the mechanism of abdominal injury is crucial in establishing the severity of the abdominal injury.</li>
<li>Two major life-threatening conditions result from blunt abdominal injury I. Internal bleeding from ruptured major vessels or organs II. Leakage from visceral content from perforated viscous – peritoneal contamination. Signs and symptoms The initial clinical assessment of patients with blunt abdominal trauma is often difficult and notably inaccurate. The most reliable signs and symptoms in alert patients are as follows:</li>
<li>Pain</li>
<li>Tenderness</li>
<li>Gastrointestinal hemorrhage</li>
<li>Hypovolemia</li>
<li>Evidence of peritoneal irritation (board like abdomen/rigid and positive rebound tenderness) If the patient had the above symptoms – warrant admission for close monitoring and to reassess regularly Management:</li>
<li>Admit</li>
<li>Do primary survey DRSCABCD as per ATLS</li>
<li>If hypotensive and tachycardia – insert 2 large IV cannulas</li>
<li>Resuscitate with IV fluid crytalloid fuids (give Boluses of fluid if hypotensive)</li>
<li>Insert IDC to monitor and assess the patients response to IVF resuscitation<ul><li>To maintain the volume of urine-output &gt; 30 mls/hour (Normal 1-2 mls/kg/ hour)</li></ul></li>
<li>Start IV antibiotic – triple antibiotic covering gram negative, gram postive and anaerobic bacteria :<ul><li>Ampiciliine 1gm every 6 hours (Ampicilline 50mg/kg in paediatric age group)</li><li>Gentamycin 240mg daily (Gentamycin 5mg/kg daily)</li><li>Flagyl 500mg every 8 hourly</li></ul></li>
<li>Need regular pain relieve<ul><li>Panadol / Diclofenac</li></ul></li>
<li>Need NGT if patient start developing abdominal distension</li>
<li>Discuss with MA/MO for further treatment and urgent referral Pregnant woman with abdominal trauma 2,5<ul><li>If &gt; 20 weeks pregnant (uterus at level of umbilicus) - baby can compress the vena cava causing hypotension if lying flat:<ul><li>lie on left side, or if spinal precautions/lying on back, elevate the right side 15–300 by placing a rolled towel/wedge under hip</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>Can lose a significant amount of blood before ↑ HR, hypotension/signs of shock occur</li>
<li>Blunt trauma (even minor) can cause placental abruption. See APH.</li>
<li>Consult Obstetric opinion<ul><li>Patient need thorough examination<ul><li>check for vaginal loss/bleeding</li><li>fetal heart rate (if skilled) + ask about baby’s movements (normal or decreased)</li><li>monitor for contractions</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>If ≥ 23 weeks pregnant, CTG (if available) for at least 4 hours</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated, advise to be reviewed the next day, or sooner if concerned:<ul><li>consult MO if ↑ pain, ↑ HR, ↑ T or any abdominal finding.</li></ul></li>
</ul>
