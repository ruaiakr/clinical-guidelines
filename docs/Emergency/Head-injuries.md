<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Head injuries - Adult</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider the possibility of non-accidental head trauma in all children presenting with mild to</li>
<li>moderate head injury</li>
<li>Do not do x-ray or USS in lieu of CT scan if skull fracture suspected in child</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If child, also see Head injury - emergency management in children https://www. childrens.health.qld.gov.au/qpec-statewide-guidelines</li>
<li>Head injury clinical pathways (Qld) https://clinicalexcellence.qld.gov.au/ resources/clinicalpathways/head-injury</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Trauma to head</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>1-4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do primary survey DRSCABCD as per Traumatic injuries, + initiate any lifesaving measures</li>
<li>C-spine immobilisation should always be applied in all cases of traumatic brain injury.</li>
<li>Assess against Criteria for early notification of trauma for interfacility transfer (inside front cover)</li>
<li>Do GCS, + neurological observations:<sup>5</sup><ul><li>pupil size + reaction to light</li><li>motor response in limbs</li><li>fontanelle in infant</li></ul></li>
<li>Do vital signs + BGL if altered LOC</li>
<li>Consult MO urgently if:<sup>2,4,6</sup><ul><li>GCS &lt;</li><li>seizure(s)</li><li>penetrating injury or suspected skull fracture (depressed/open)</li><li>basal skull fracture suspected - black eye with no damage around eyes, bleeding from ear(s),</li><li>clear fluid (CSF) from ear(s) or nose, bruising behind ear(s)<sup>1,3</sup></li><li>focal neurological deficit eg loss of sensation, paraesthesia</li></ul></li>
<li>signs of ↑ intra cranial pressure (ICP):<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>GCS decreasing</li>
<li>abnormal posture (decorticate/decerebrate)</li>
<li>abnormal pupil responses, pupil dilation (1 or both), upward gaze</li>
<li>↑BP + ↓ HR + breathing abnormalities - ‘Cushing’s triad’ (late sign)</li>
<li>may be just headache, vomiting, irritability, altered LOC ± Cushing’s triad</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment<sup>2,4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do secondary survey EFGHIJ as per Traumatic injuries, including examination of head</li>
<li>Get history, including:<sup>1-3</sup><ul><li>time/date of injury, witnessed or not</li><li>mechanism of injury/how it happened</li><li>use of alcohol/drugs</li><li>bleeding disorder/taking anticoagulants<sup>1,4</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Ask about symptoms. Any:<sup>1-4</sup><ul><li>loss of consciousness - when, how long for. If &lt; 2 years old, was it for ≥ 5 seconds<sup>2</sup></li><li>vomiting (amount), headache (severity)<sup>1</sup></li><li>seizure(s) or amnesia post injury - when/duration</li><li>double vision, ataxia, clumsiness, gait abnormality</li><li>abnormal behaviour (child/infant) eg drowsiness, agitation, restlessness, not interested in</li><li>things around them, not themselves (as reported by parent)<sup>2</sup></li><li>weakness/tingling in arms or legs</li></ul></li>
<li>Assess for Head injury risk factors as per tables on followingg pages HEAD INJURY RISK FACTORS - CHILD<sup>2</sup> LOW RISK ALL OF THE FOLLOWING INTERMEDIATE RISK NO HIGH RISK FEATURES + ≥ 1 OF THE FOLLOWING MAY NEED URGENT CT SCAN HIGH RISK ≥ 1 OF THE FOLLOWING URGENT CT SCAN</li>
<li>Well appearing child</li>
<li>GCS</li>
<li>No intermediate or high risk features present</li>
<li>Severe headache</li>
<li>Vomiting</li>
<li>Amnesia</li>
<li>Post-traumatic seizure</li>
<li>Altered mental status (drowsiness, agitation, repetitive questioning, slow verbal response)</li>
<li>Significant mechanism of injury including:<ul><li>fall from significant height (≥ 1 m if aged &lt; 2, or &gt; 1.5 m if ≥ 2 years)<sup>1</sup></li><li>following vehicle accident</li><li>high speed, ejected from vehicle or with others significantly injured in crash</li><li>pedestrian/cyclist impacted by car</li><li>impact from high speed object eg golf ball, ceiling fan</li></ul></li>
<li>GCS &lt;</li>
<li>Focal neurological deficit</li>
<li>Clinical suspicion of:<ul><li>basal skull fracture (raccoon eyes, blood behind eardrum, Battle’s sign, clear fluid leaking from ear or nose (CSF)</li><li>depressed skull fracture (boggy haematoma, palpable depressions)</li><li>penetrating injury</li><li>open skull fracture</li><li>large haematoma, laceration or bulging fontanelle in young child</li><li>non-accidental injury</li><li>extensive other injuries HEAD INJURY RISK FACTORS - ADULT<sup>2</sup> 'HIGH RISK' ANY OF THE FOLLOWING</li></ul></li>
<li>Age &gt; 65 years</li>
<li>Multi-system trauma</li>
<li>On anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy</li>
<li>Delayed presentation or re- presentation</li>
<li>Known coagulopathy eg liver disease, factor deficiency</li>
<li>Persistent abnormal level of alertness, behaviour and/or cognition</li>
<li>Known neurosurgery and/or neurological impairment</li>
<li>Multiple comorbidities or combination of worrying factors</li>
<li>Dangerous mechanism of injury URGENT CT SCAN IF BELOW<sup>6</sup></li>
<li>Clinical suspicion of skull fracture</li>
<li>Deterioration in GCS</li>
<li>Unwitnessed head injury</li>
<li>Focal neurological deficit</li>
<li>Loss of consciousness &gt; 5 minutes</li>
<li>Post traumatic seizure</li>
<li>Intoxicated (alcohol ± other drugs)</li>
<li>Anterograde or retrograde amnesia<ul><li>30 minutes</li></ul></li>
<li>Persistent vomiting/more than 1 episode<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Persistent GCS &lt; 15 at 2 hours post injury</li>
<li>Persistent severe headache</li>
<li>Skull fracture suspected or other clinical concern</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If drug or alcohol intoxicated at presentation, treat as if the neurological findings are due to the head injury until proven otherwise 1,3,4</li>
<li>If scalp laceration, control bleeding - can result in major blood loss:4<ul><li>Treat as a bleeding wound</li><li>Wash and pack the wound with gause</li><li>Cover the wound with gause</li><li>Use a grip bandage to wrap over the dressing</li><li>This will help enforce pressure on the wound and control bleeding.</li></ul></li>
<li>Consult Surgeon / MO / MA on all cases sustaining traumatic brain injury to stabilise and initiate treatment before referring cases to main hospital.</li>
<li>Patient with Moderate to Severe Traumatic brain injury<ul><li>Admit</li><li>monitor closely general condition: every 1 - 2 hours</li><li>GCS + neurological observations every 1 - 2 hours</li><li>vital signs – ½ hourly</li><li>IVC x 2 or Intraosseous</li><li>O<sup>2</sup> to maintain SpO<sup>2</sup> ≥ 95% (+ capnography if available)</li><li>BGL – three times a day</li><li>analgesia - only on MO orders<ul><li>Regular Paracetamol and NSAIDS injection to give</li><li>Morphine is contra-indicated in cases with head injury</li><li>Morphine : sedative and interfere with neuro-observation documentation</li></ul></li><li>Start IV antibiotic : as per antibiotic guideline<ul><li>Cloxacillin 25mg/kg every 6 hours if there is scalp wound or no wound but suspected fracture skull.</li></ul></li><li>Keep patient NBM until fully conscious</li><li>IVF – maintenance rate</li><li>Discuss for urgent referral</li></ul></li>
<li>if ↑ ICP:elevate head of bed 20–30 : Discuss with MO / MO<ul><li>support airway/ventilation</li><li>keep warm/avoid hypothermia</li><li>keep patient NBM</li><li>start on IV omeprazole</li><li>if seizures, manage as per Fitting</li><li>MA may consider:<ul><li>IV fluid bolus 10–20 mL/kg sodium chloride 0.9% to avoid hypovolaemia</li><li>Start prophylactic : Phenytoin loading dose and followed with maintenance (discuss with surgeon / anaesthetist)</li><li>Regular panadol / diclofenac</li></ul></li><li>Discuss for urgent referral</li></ul></li>
<li>If asymptomatic, fully awake and alert, have no neurological abnormalities + assessed as low risk</li>
<li>If baby &lt; 6 months, consult MO promptly<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>If child very low risk no need to monitor ie: 1</li>
<li>trivial injury (ground level fall, walking/running into stationary objects), no loss of consciousness, GCS 15 AND no signs or symptoms of head trauma other than an abrasion. Discharge with Head injury advice</li>
<li>Otherwise, in collaboration with MO, monitor neurological observations for 4–6 hours: 2,6<ul><li>1/2 hourly for 1st 2 hours post injury, then hourly for 2–6 hours/until discharged</li><li>if GCS falls ≥ 1 point for 30 minutes,3 symptoms develop/persist, or any abnormal observations, urgently consult MO</li></ul></li>
<li>Consider discharge ≥ 4–6 hours if: 1,2,6<ul><li>GCS remains at</li><li>neurological observations remain normal + no symptoms develop</li><li>no concerns of non-accidental injury in child</li><li>no concerns of alternative/concurrent illness</li><li>patient has responsible adult to observe them for 24 hours + can return promptly if needed</li><li>not under the influence of drugs or alcohol 1</li><li>Advice on RED FLAG head injury symptoms : Persistent headache, vomiting and sudden loss of consciousness – to report to the health center ASAP</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated advise to be reviewed the next day and at the next MO clinic (1–2 weeks)<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>If on anticoagulants/has bleeding disorder, advise follow up within 72 hours due to increased risk of intracranial haemorrhage<sup>6</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO as above</li>
<li>Needs surgical admission Head injury advice - give verbal and written advice<sup>7-9</sup></li>
<li>Adults and children should be observed by someone responsible for next hours</li>
<li>Give fact sheets eg:<ul><li>adult - Concussion/minor head injury without concussion https:// clinicalexcellence.qld.gov.au/resources/emergency-department-patient- information-sheets</li><li>child - Head injury care after discharge https://www.childrens.health.qld.gov. au/chq/information-for-families/fact-sheets/ General advice</li></ul></li>
<li>Rest quietly for next 48 hours</li>
<li>Eat a healthy diet, drink lots of fluids</li>
<li>Avoid activities that make symptoms worse</li>
<li>Use ice packs on swollen or painful areas</li>
<li>Use paracetamol for headache (not ibuprofen/aspirin)</li>
<li>Do not take sedatives/other medicines unless instructed</li>
<li>Do not drive for at least hours and only once you can concentrate properly</li>
<li>Return to exercise after a few days rest Concussion</li>
<li>Symptoms may include:<ul><li>mild headaches, which settle with rest/paracetamol</li><li>mild dizziness, feeling tired, difficulty paying attention or remembering things</li><li>feeling emotional or moody</li></ul></li>
<li>Symptoms usually settle over time. Return to clinic if you are worried Return to the clinic immediately if</li>
<li>Severe headache, especially if getting worse</li>
<li>Dizziness or unsteady walking</li>
<li>Nausea, vomiting or poor feeding (child) or frequent vomiting (adult)</li>
<li>Unexpected drowsiness or weakness</li>
<li>Disorientation or confusion</li>
<li>Restlessness (adult), irritability, continued crying or unusual agitation (child)</li>
<li>Slurred speech or double/blurred vision</li>
<li>Seizure (fit) or spasms of face or limbs</li>
<li>Difficult to wake up</li>
<li>If you or carer(s) are worried for any reason</li>
</ul>
