<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Anaphylaxis - Adult/Child</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Adrenaline (epinephrine) is first line treatment. Give without delay</li>
<li>Anaphylaxis is potentially life-threatening and must be treated as a medical emergency</li>
<li>Check for medic alert jewellery and accessories in emergency situations. May look like normal jewellery or other accessory eg key ring, USB stick, shoe tag, anklet, watch or tattoo</li>
<li>Fluid resuscitation is important if severe anaphylaxis with hypotension</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>ANY ONE of the following (onset can range from minutes to hours after exposure to a substance):<ul><li>difficult/noisy breathing</li><li>swelling of tongue</li><li>swelling/tightness in throat</li><li>difficulty talking ± hoarse voice</li><li>wheeze or persistent cough - usually sudden</li><li>persistent dizziness or collapse</li><li>pale and floppy (young children)</li><li>abdominal pain, vomiting - for insect sting or injected drug (medication) allergy</li></ul></li>
<li>CONSIDER anaphylaxis in ANY acute onset:<ul><li>illness with typical skin features - itchy rash or red/flushing ± angio-oedema (swelling) PLUS involvement of respiratory ± cardiovascular ± persistent severe GI symptoms</li><li>OR hypotension or bronchospasm or upper airway obstruction where anaphylaxis is considered possible, even if typical skin features are not present</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<p><strong>Also see flowchart inside back cover</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Remove allergen if still present - do not delay adrenaline (epinephrine) to do this:<ul><li>stop infusion of medicine/blood product</li><li>flick out insect stings; freeze tick with liquid nitrogen or ether containing spray</li></ul></li>
<li>Call for help</li>
<li>Lay patient flat. Do NOT allow to stand or walk - can result in fatal hypotension<ul><li>if breathing difficult allow to sit - with legs outstretched in front (not in chair):<ul><li>lay flat again if ↓BP or ↓LOC or confusion</li></ul></li><li>do not hold infants upright</li><li>if pregnant, put in left lateral position</li></ul></li>
<li>Give IM adrenaline (epinephrine) into OUTER MID-THIGH WITHOUT DELAY<ul><li>repeat adrenaline (epinephrine) every 5 minutes as needed</li><li>if known asthmatic with allergy give adrenaline (epinephrine) first, then asthma reliever if sudden breathing difficulty (wheeze, persistent cough or hoarse voice) - even if no skin symptoms</li></ul></li>
<li>Urgently consult MO</li>
</ul>

<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">ADRENALINE (EPINEPHRINE) — IM</p>
</figure>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>OR if using autoinjector eg Epipen®, Anapen® — follow device label instructions</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>When skills and equipment are available<sup>1</sup></strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do NOT allow patient to walk or stand, even if they appear to have recovered</li>
<li>Monitor:<ul><li>vital signs</li><li>for altered LOC and confusion. See GCS/AVPU</li></ul></li>
<li>O<sup>2</sup> 6–8 L/minute if:<ul><li>respiratory distress, ↓LOC, or needs repeat doses of adrenaline (epinephrine)</li><li>consider O<sup>2</sup> if asthmatic, or chronic respiratory/cardiovascular disease</li></ul></li>
<li>Airway support if needed:<ul><li>jaw thrust, oro/nasopharyngeal airway or bag-valve-mask with high flow O<sup>2</sup> will save most patients, even with airway swelling<sup>1</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Insert IVC if adult (14–16 G) or hypotensive child</li>
<li>If hypotensive:<ul><li>IV sodium chloride 0.9% 20 mL/kg rapidly</li><li>+ insert another IVC</li></ul></li>
<li>If inadequate response to 2–3 adrenaline (epinephrine) doses OR deterioration — MO may consider:<ul><li>adrenaline (epinephrine) infusion on advice of emergency medicine/critical care specialist</li></ul></li>
<li>If adrenaline (epinephrine) infusion NOT available or NOT effective MO may order for:<ul><li>upper airway obstruction:<ul><li>nebulised adrenaline (epinephrine) 5 mL (5 ampoules of 1:1,000)</li></ul></li><li>persistent hypotension/shock:<ul><li>sodium chloride 0.9% - max. 50 mL/kg in the first 30 minutes</li></ul></li><li>persistent wheeze:<ul><li>salbutamol 8–12 puffs (MDI) using a spacer OR 5 mg (NEB) as per Asthma</li><li>oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg (max. 50 mg) as per Asthma, OR</li><li>IV hydrocortisone 5 mg/kg (max. 200 mg)</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>If cardiac arrest:<ul><li>do prolonged CPR</li><li>PLUS aggressive fluid resuscitation AND IV adrenaline (epinephrine) bolus</li></ul></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get rapid history - from patient, relatives or friends:<ul><li>food, medicine, sting/bite, herbal medicines, other exposures in the previous 6–8 hours</li><li>known allergies and reaction</li><li>any previous episodes, treatments used and effect</li><li>current medications, use of an autoinjector eg EpiPen®</li></ul></li>
<li>Do physical examination:<ul><li>check affected body systems - skin changes, face, throat, breathing, HR, neurological state</li></ul></li>
<li>Monitor response to treatment - using simple palpable systolic BP:<ul><li>palpate radial or brachial pulse. Note pressure at which this disappears</li><li>may be more difficult in children</li><li>note: infants can stay pale after 2–3 doses of adrenaline (epinephrine), which can resolve without further doses. Use BP to guide response</li></ul></li>
<li>Monitor for signs of over treatment:<ul><li>hypertension or pulmonary oedema</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<p><strong>When stable</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor closely for at least 4 hours after last dose of adrenaline (epinephrine):<ul><li>relapse/protracted ± biphasic (two phase) reactions may occur</li></ul></li>
<li>BP, RR, HR, LOC - 15 minutely for 2 hours then hourly</li>
<li>Evacuation/hospitalisation may be required if:<ul><li>severe reaction eg required repeated doses of adrenaline (epinephrine) or IV resuscitation OR</li><li>severe/protracted anaphylaxis OR</li><li>has other concomitant illness eg asthma, arrhythmia OR</li><li>lives alone or remote from medical care OR</li><li>presents for medical care late in evening</li></ul></li>
<li>If not evacuated, continue to manage as per MO instructions</li>
<li>Document allergy in medical record</li>
<li>Patient advice prior to discharge: avoid being re-exposed to the allergic trigger</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated advise to be reviewed the next day, or earlier if concerned</li>
<li>Advise to see MO at next clinic</li>
</ul>

</ul>
