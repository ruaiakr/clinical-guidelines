<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Asprin and Paracetamol</h1>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Aspirin and paracetamol are commonly used to treat fevers and to reduce pain. Health care workers should not automatically give drugs to reduce temperature to all children with fever. First the cause of the fever should be determined, then appropriate treatment given. If a child’s fever lasts beyond 2 days or reappears within 14 days and the cause cannot be determined, the nurse or medical assistant should consult the doctor about the fever.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="use-of-paracetamol">USE OF PARACETAMOL</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Paracetamol is given to reduce the discomfort of children with high fevers only (&gt; 38.5°C). "Treat the child, not the thermometer."</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="use-of-paracetamol-points">USE OF PARACETAMOL</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Paracetamol is the drug of choice for fever in children. The dose is 15 mg/kg body weight—see <a href="/docs/Safe%20use%20of%20Drugs,%20Including%20Antibiotic%20Use/Drug-Dosage-Tables#paracetamol">drug reference tables</a></li>
<li>Paracetamol is <strong>TOXIC TO THE LIVER</strong> if given in too high doses or if the liver is inefficient or unable to metabolize it because of inflammation or disease (such as hepatitis or cirrhosis)</li>
<li>Paracetamol can also cause kidney damage if given in too high doses</li>
<li>Use paracetamol only when needed. Like all drugs it has side effects and potential <strong>TOXIC EFFECTS</strong></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="why-we-avoid-using-aspirin-in-children">WHY WE AVOID USING ASPIRIN IN CHILDREN</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Aspirin use in children has many bad effects, compared to those of paracetamol</li>
<li>Aspirin irritates the stomach and increases the <strong>RISK OF ULCER, HAEMORRHAGE</strong> and <strong>PERFORATION</strong></li>
<li>Aspirin also increases the <strong>RISK OF BLEEDING</strong></li>
<li>Aspirin has also been linked to <strong>REYE’S SYNDROME</strong>. Reye’s syndrome, a disease associated with liver, brain and neurological problems, has been linked to the use of aspirin in children with viral infections</li>
<li>For these reasons, we use paracetamol, not aspirin in children and adolescents</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="drugs-which-can-be-hepatotoxic">DRUGS WHICH CAN BE HEPATOTOXIC (damaging to the liver)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>The following drugs can potentially cause liver damage and must therefore be used <strong>WITH CAUTION</strong> and <strong>ONLY WHEN INDICATED</strong>. Many people in Kiribati have liver disease, hepatitis, cirrhosis and liver cancer. Persons with liver inflammation or damage or disease may not have any signs or symptoms until late in the disease, therefore we must be very careful in using drugs which can damage the liver.</li>
<li>The drugs listed below are <strong>NOT GIVEN TO PATIENTS WITH LIVER DISEASE</strong></li>
<li>Aspirin, Brufen and Indocid are avoided in patients with liver problems because they increase the risk of bleeding</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Hepatotoxic Drugs Include:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sulphonimides (Cotrimoxazole)</li>
<li>Erythromycin</li>
<li>Chlorpromazine</li>
<li>Aspirin</li>
<li>Brufen</li>
<li>Griseofulvin</li>
<li>Indomethacine</li>
<li>Paracetamol</li>
<li>Chloramphenicol</li>
<li>Rifampicin</li>
</ul>
