# Congestive cardiac failure - Adult

Cardiovascular and
respiratory disorders

Congestive cardiac failure - Adult
Recommend
•	 CHF is a clinical syndrome that may develop slowly over months or years and is
due to the heart's inability to pump enough blood to meet the body’s needs.
•	 It is vital to identify and manage both the underlying cause and any precipitating
factors.
•	 Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment can improve quality of life and reduce
morbidity and mortality.
Background
Congestive Heart Failure (CHF) is a condition caused by structural or functional
abnormalities of the heart leading to impaired cardiac output. It is not a disease
itself, but a syndrome resulting from other cardiac or systemic conditions.
CHF is commonly due to conditions such as ischaemic heart disease, valvular heart
disease, hypertension, hyperthyroidism, or cardiomyopathy. Less common causes
include congenital heart disease, infective endocarditis, myocarditis, cor pulmonale,
and pericardial disease. In many cases, CHF is in a compensated state until
decompensated by precipitating factors.
1.
May present with
•	 Shortness of breath on exertion or at rest
•	 Orthopnoea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea
•	 Fatigue or weakness
•	 Peripheral oedema
•	 Elevated jugular venous pressure
•	 Hepatomegaly or ascites
•	 Weight gain from fluid retention
2.	 Immediate management If severe cardiac failure:
•	 Sit patient upright
•	 Give oxygen (1–3 L/min if available)
•	 Administer Frusemide 40 mg IM or IV slowly over 10 minutes
•	 Contact medical officer urgently

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Take history of cardiac disease, hypertension, medications, salt/fluid intake,
adherence
•	 Examine for signs of fluid overload, arrhythmia, murmurs, infection
•	 Investigations (if available):

–
CBC

–
Electrolytes and urea

–
ECG

–
Chest X-ray (if accessible)
4.	 Management
Non-pharmacological:
•	 Salt restriction (no added salt)
•	 Weight reduction if overweight
•	 Fluid restriction only if dilutional hyponatraemia (limit to 1.5 L/day)
•	 Daily weights before breakfast with a target weight loss of 05.-1kg/day. If target
weight loss not achieved it indicates inadequate diuresis and may push up
frusemide dose if BP >90/60.
•	 Educate on medication adherence and monitoring symptoms
Pharmacological (mild to moderate heart failure):
•	 Frusemide 40–80 mg orally daily
•	 Enalapril 2.5 mg daily, increasing gradually to a max of 20 mg daily

–
Monitor for hypotension, especially in elderly, dehydrated, or hyponatraemic
patients
•	 If still symptomatic, add:

–
Digoxin 0.0625–0.5 mg orally daily (adjust dose based on age and renal
function)
Other medications (if available):
•	 Spironolactone, thiazide diuretics
•	 Beta-blockers (carvedilol, metoprolol, bisoprolol)
•	 Isosorbide dinitrate
•	 Hydralazine
5. Precipitating factors
•	 Poor medication adherence
•	 Excess salt or fluid intake
•	 NSAIDs, steroids, antidepressants, verapamil
•	 Infections, especially respiratory
•	 Anaemia, hyperthyroidism, uncontrolled hypertension
•	 Arrhythmias (especially atrial fibrillation)
