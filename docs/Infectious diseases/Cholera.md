<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Cholera</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Rehydration is the cornerstone of treatment. Oral rehydration solutions (ORS) should be the first line of therapy for most cases.</li>
<li>Antibiotics (e.g., doxycycline) may be indicated in severe cases to shorten the duration of diarrhoea.</li>
<li>Prevention involves improved sanitation, access to clean water, and vaccination in endemic areas.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Cholera is an acute diarrheal disease caused by the bacterium Vibrio cholerae. It is typically spread through contaminated water or food, particularly in areas with poor sanitation. Cholera can lead to severe dehydration and death if untreated.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Acute watery diarrhoea</li>
<li>Vomiting</li>
<li>Dehydration (dry mouth, sunken eyes, skin tenting)</li>
<li>Muscle cramps</li>
<li>Shock in severe cases</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Immediate rehydration with oral rehydration salts (ORS).</li>
<li>If severe dehydration or shock is suspected, IV fluids are required. See <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Gastroenteritisdehydration">Gastroenteritis/dehydration</a> and <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess for dehydration (mild, moderate, or severe). See <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Gastroenteritisdehydration">Gastroenteritis/dehydration</a>.</li>
<li>Measure vital signs, especially blood pressure and pulse.</li>
<li>Monitor for signs of shock (rapid pulse, low BP, cold extremities). See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a> and <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Rehydrate with oral rehydration solution (ORS) in mild cases.</li>
<li>For severe dehydration or shock, initiate IV fluids and closely monitor the patient's status.</li>
<li>Administer doxycycline or another appropriate antibiotic for cholera treatment.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow-up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Follow-up within 24-48 hours to monitor rehydration and recovery.</li>
<li>Check for signs of complications like electrolyte imbalances.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to a medical officer if the patient does not respond to initial management or if there are signs of severe dehydration or shock.</li>
</ul>
