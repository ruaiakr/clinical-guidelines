Tuberculosis (including Pediatric TB)
Background
Tuberculosis (TB) is a bacterial infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It
primarily affects the lungs but can also affect other parts of the body. Pediatric TB
is of particular concern due to the potential for severe disease and transmission in
close-contact settings.
Recommendations
•	 TB should be diagnosed early through sputum tests and chest X-rays.
•	 Multidrug therapy (MDT) is required for effective treatment.
•	 Screening for latent TB is important in high-risk populations.
1.
May present with
•	 Chronic cough lasting more than 3 weeks
•	 Unexplained weight loss and fatigue
•	 Fever, night sweats
•	 In children, failure to thrive and cough
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Initiate first-line anti-TB drugs (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, and
ethambutol).
•	 Provide supportive care, including hydration and nutritional support.
•	 Isolate the patient to reduce the risk of transmission.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Take a detailed history of potential exposure to TB, especially in children with
close family members or known cases.
•	 Assess for signs of complications such as pleural effusion or miliary TB.
•	 Conduct chest X-ray and sputum tests to confirm the diagnosis.
4.	 Management
•	 Start the appropriate MDT regimen as recommended by local health authorities.
•	 Ensure regular monitoring for side effects of treatment, such as hepatotoxicity or
neuropathy.
•	 Provide nutritional support to maintain strength and immunity.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Follow-up every 1-2 months during treatment to monitor for side effects and
efficacy.
•	 Ensure completion of the full course of treatment to prevent resistance.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to a TB specialist if complications arise or if the patient is non-compliant
with treatment.
