<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Meningococcal disease – Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Acute meningococcal disease may present as severe sepsis with a progressive non-blanching petechial/purpuric rash, or meningitis with or without a rash</li>
<li>Rarer presentations include septic arthritis, pneumonia, pharyngitis and occult bacteremia</li>
<li>There are 13 serogroups of Neisseria meningitis (the cause of meningococcal disease)</li>
</ul>
<div class="pccm-alert-panel" style="margin-top:0.75rem;">
<strong>ISOLATION:</strong> Meningococcal disease is spread from person to person by respiratory droplets; therefore, patients should be isolated with droplet precautions.
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Rapid onset (&lt;12hrs) of headache, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, sore throat and coryza</li>
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Infantas may have reduced feeds, irritability</li>
<li>Leg pain and myalgia</li>
<li>Late signs (&gt;12hrs)
<ul>
<li>Altered conscious state</li>
<li>Neck stiffness headache, photophobia, bulging fontanelle</li>
<li>Non-blanching rash: petechiae/purpura</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-note-panel">
<p><strong>NOTE:</strong> a blanching rash does not exclude meningococcal disease (can initially be macular or maculopapular)</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use the ABCD approach
<ul>
<li>A: Maintain airway</li>
<li>B: Provide oxygen if respiratory distress</li>
<li>C: Ensure vascular access and give fluids if needed (2/3 IV maintenance)</li>
<li>D: Manage seizures and check blood glucose</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Treat fever with paracetamol</li>
<li>Arrange immediate referral to hospital</li>
<li>Begin antibiotics without delay</li>
<li>If in SHOCK – bolus 20mls/kg n/s or Hartmann’s, can repeat bolus 2x</li>
<li>PATIENT SHOULD BE ISOLATED FOR 24 HOURS AFTER ADMINISTRATION OF ANTIBIOTICS</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, capillary refill</li>
<li>Assess general condition and consciousness</li>
<li>Check for signs of raised intracranial pressure</li>
<li>Examine fontanelle in infants</li>
<li>Perform a brief screening physical exam</li>
<li>Doctor will assess for focal neurological signs and plan investigations</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">5. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer immediately to hospital</li>
<li>Consult doctor for lumbar puncture and investigations (FBC, blood culture, LP)</li>
<li>Discuss TB testing if weight loss, chronic cough, or failure to thrive</li>
</ul>
