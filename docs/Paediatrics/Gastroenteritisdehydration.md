<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Gastroenteritis/dehydration - Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Diarrhoea ± vomiting</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1-3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Rehydration is the most important part of management eg with oral rehydration solution (ORS)</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1-3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Most gastroenteritis is viral, self-limiting and resolves without specific treatment</li>
<li>Antibiotics are of no benefit in most cases and may exacerbate diarrhoea</li>
<li>Also see Gastroenteritis - emergency management in children<br /><a href="https://www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/qpec-statewide-guidelines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/qpec-statewide-guidelines/</a></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Related topics</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Giardiasis">Giardiasis</a></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sudden onset of diarrhoea ± vomiting, fever or abdominal pain/distension</li>
<li>Lethargy or altered level of consciousness</li>
<li>Irritability</li>
<li>Dehydration</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Assess hydration. Note: if in doubt, manage as if dehydration falls into the more severe category<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>If any signs of shock:
<ul>
<li>contact MO urgently</li>
<li>treat as per <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a> Also consider <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1-4</sup></h2>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Consider differential diagnoses<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Look for signs of: <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Urinary-tract-infection">UTI</a>, <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Meningitis">meningitis</a>, <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Pneumonia">pneumonia</a>, otitis media, appendicitis, <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Intussusception">intussusception</a>, bowel obstruction</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>ALERT</strong> consider <a href="/docs/Emergency/Button-battery-ingestioninsertion">Button battery</a>, ingestion in all children with vomiting
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>gastrointestinal symptoms:
<ul>
<li>date/time of onset, frequency, is there blood ± mucous in stools, bile stained or green vomit, location and severity of abdominal pain</li>
<li>times passed urine/number of wet nappies in last 24 hours</li>
<li>how many drinks/breastfeeds in last 24 hours</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>other symptoms:
<ul>
<li>fever, rash, headache</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>known illness in contacts</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>weight - bare weight if &lt; 2 years</li>
<li>urinalysis - ketones, signs of UTI</li>
<li>BGL</li>
<li>check + palpate:
<ul>
<li>abdomen for distension, guarding, rigidity</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do stool MCS + PCR if:
<ul>
<li>blood ± mucous in stool</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
<li>recent travel overseas</li>
<li>diarrhoea &gt; 7 days</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Look for Risk factors for dehydration and Red flags</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-two-col-grid">
<div class="pccm-red-flags">
<div class="pccm-red-flags-banner">RISK FACTORS FOR DEHYDRATION<sup>1</sup></div>
<div class="pccm-red-flags-body">
<ul>
<li>&lt; 1 year, preterm and &lt; 6 months</li>
<li>Low birth weight, failure to thrive</li>
<li>&gt; 5 diarrhoeal stools in 24 hours</li>
<li>Stopped breastfeeding during illness</li>
<li>Signs of malnutrition</li>
<li>Immunocompromised</li>
<li>Underlying chronic conditions</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<div class="pccm-red-flags">
<div class="pccm-red-flags-banner">RED FLAGS<sup>1</sup></div>
<div class="pccm-red-flags-body">
<ul>
<li>Severe or localized abdominal pain</li>
<li>Abdominal distension</li>
<li>Isolated vomiting</li>
<li>Bilious (green) vomiting</li>
<li>Blood in stool or vomit</li>
<li>Child appears very unwell or is very drowsy</li>
<li>T &gt; 39 or 38 if &lt; 3 months<sup>5</sup></li>
<li>Headache</li>
<li>Rash</li>
<li>Persistent diarrhoea &gt; 10 days</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>1-4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO/MA if:
<ul>
<li>&lt; 3 months</li>
<li>risk factors for dehydration</li>
<li>red flags</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>See <a href="#hydration-management">Hydration management flowchart</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-a">Plan A: Treat Diarrhoea at Home</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Counsel the mother on the 4 Rules of Home Treatment: Give extra fluid, give zinc supplements, continue feeding, and when to return.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-a-extra-fluid">1. Give extra fluid (as much as the child will take)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Tell the mother:
<ul>
<li>Breastfeed frequently and for longer at each feed</li>
<li>If the infant is exclusively breastfed, give ORS in addition to breastmilk</li>
<li>If the child is not exclusively breastfed, give one or more of the following:
<ul>
<li>ORS solution</li>
<li>Food-based fluids (such as soup, rice water, yoghurt drinks)</li>
<li>Clean water</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>It is especially important to give ORS at home when:
<ul>
<li>The child has been treated with Plan B or Plan C during this visit</li>
<li>The child cannot return to a clinic if the diarrhoea gets worse</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Teach the mother how to mix and give ORS</li>
<li>Give the mother 2 packets of ORS to use at home</li>
<li>Show the mother how much fluid to give in addition to usual intake:</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Fluid intake</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Up to 2 years: 50–100 mL after each loose stool and between stools</li>
<li>2 years or more: 100–200 mL after each loose stool and between stools</li>
</ul>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Tell the mother:
<ul>
<li>Give frequent small sips from a cup</li>
<li>If the child vomits, wait 10 minutes, then continue more slowly</li>
<li>Continue giving extra fluid until the diarrhoea stops</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-a-zinc">2. Give zinc supplements</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Tell the mother how much zinc to give:
<ul>
<li>Up to 6 months: ½ tablet daily for 14 days</li>
<li>6 months or more: 1 tablet daily for 14 days</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Show the mother how to give zinc:
<ul>
<li>Infants: dissolve the tablet in a small amount of expressed breastmilk, ORS, or clean water, and give with a spoon</li>
<li>Older children: tablets can be chewed or dissolved in a small amount of clean water</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Remind the mother to give zinc for the full 14 days</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-a-feeding">3. Continue feeding</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continue breastfeeding</li>
<li>Continue usual diet appropriate for age</li>
<li>Encourage regular feeding</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-a-return">4. When to return immediately</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise the mother to return to the clinic if the child develops:
<ul>
<li>Signs of dehydration</li>
<li>Drinking poorly or unable to drink</li>
<li>Vomiting everything</li>
<li>Blood in stool</li>
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Child becomes more unwell</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-b">Plan B: Treat Some Dehydration with ORS</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Give in clinic the recommended amount of ORS over a 4-hour period.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-b-amount">1. Determine amount of ORS to give during first 4 hours</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use the table below as a guide:</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap" role="region" aria-label="ORS amount over 4 hours">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<thead>
<tr>
<th>AGE</th>
<th>WEIGHT</th>
<th>ORS (ML OVER 4 HOURS)</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">UP TO 4 MONTHS</td><td>&lt; 6kg</td><td>200-400 mL</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">4 TO 12 MONTHS</td><td>6-&lt;10kg</td><td>400-700 mL</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">12 MONTHS TO 2 YEARS</td><td>10-&lt;12kg</td><td>700-900 mL</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">2 TO 5 YEARS</td><td>12-19kg</td><td>900-1400 mL</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use the child’s age only when weight is not known</li>
<li>The approximate amount of ORS (mL) can also be calculated as: <strong>75 mL × body weight (kg)</strong></li>
<li>If the child wants more ORS than shown, give more</li>
<li>For infants under 6 months who are not breastfed, also give 100–200 mL clean water during this period</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-b-show">2. Show the mother how to give ORS solution</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Give frequent small sips from a cup</li>
<li>If the child vomits, wait 10 minutes, then continue more slowly</li>
<li>Continue breastfeeding whenever the child wants</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-b-after-4h">3. After 4 hours</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Reassess the child and classify dehydration</li>
<li>Select the appropriate plan to continue treatment (Plan A, B, or C)</li>
<li>Begin feeding the child in clinic</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-b-leave-early">4. If the mother must leave before completing treatment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Show how to prepare ORS solution at home</li>
<li>Show how much ORS to give to complete the 4-hour treatment at home</li>
<li>Give enough ORS packets to complete rehydration</li>
<li>Also give 2 packets as recommended in Plan A</li>
<li>Explain the 4 Rules of Home Treatment:
<ol>
<li>Give extra fluid (see Plan A)</li>
<li>Give zinc supplements</li>
<li>Continue feeding</li>
<li>When to return</li>
</ol>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="plan-c">Plan C: Treat Severe Dehydration Quickly</h2>
<p>Follow the arrows. If answer is "yes", go across. If "no", go down.</p>

<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Plan C: Treat Severe Dehydration Quickly</p>

<div class="pccm-flowchart" role="region" aria-label="Plan C treat severe dehydration flowchart searchable text">
<div class="pccm-flow-stack">

<div class="pccm-flow-box pccm-flow-box--blue">
<p><strong>Can you give intravenous (IV) fluid immediately?</strong></p>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">Yes →</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#fff3e0; border:2px solid #e8a060;">
<p>Start IV fluid immediately. If the child can drink, give ORS by mouth while the drip is set up. Give 100 mL/kg Ringer's Lactate Solution (or, if not available, normal saline), divided as follows:</p>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap" style="margin:0.75rem 0; overflow-x:auto;">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<thead>
<tr><th>AGE</th><th>FIRST GIVE 30ML/KG IN:</th><th>THEN GIVE 70ML/KG IN:</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Infants (under 12 months)</td><td>1 hour*</td><td>5 hours</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Children (12 months up to 5 years)</td><td>30 minutes*</td><td>2 ½ hours</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>
<p>*Repeat once if radial pulse is still very weak or not detectable</p>
<ul>
<li>Reassess the child every 1-2 hours. If hydration status is not improving, give the IV drip more rapidly.</li>
<li>Also give ORS (about 5mL/kg/hour) as soon as the child can drink: usually after 3-4 hours (infants) or 1-2 hours (children).</li>
<li>Reassess an infant after 6 hours and a child after 3 hours. Classify dehydration. Then choose the appropriate plan (A, B, or C) to continue treatment.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">No ↓</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box pccm-flow-box--blue">
<p><strong>Is IV treatment available nearby (within 30 minutes)?</strong></p>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">Yes →</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#fff3e0; border:2px solid #e8a060;">
<ul>
<li>Refer URGENTLY to hospital for IV treatment</li>
<li>If the child can drink, provide the mother with ORS solution and show her how to give frequent sips during the trip.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">No ↓</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box pccm-flow-box--blue">
<p><strong>Are you trained to use a naso-gastric (NG) tube for rehydration?</strong></p>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">Yes → / or if child can drink →</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#fff3e0; border:2px solid #e8a060;">
<ul>
<li>Start rehydration by tube (or mouth) with ORS solution: give 20mL/kg/hour for 6 hours (total of 120mL/kg).</li>
<li>Reassess the child every 1-2 hours:
<ul>
<li>If there is repeated vomiting or increasing abdominal distension, give the fluid more slowly.</li>
<li>If hydration status is not improving after 3 hours, send the child for IV therapy.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>After 6 hours, reassess the child. Classify dehydration. Then choose the appropriate plan (A, B, or C) to continue treatment.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">No (cannot drink) ↓</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#fff3e0; border:2px solid #e8a060;">
<p><strong>Refer URGENTLY to hospital for IV or NG Treatment</strong></p>
</div>

<div class="pccm-note-panel" style="margin-top:1rem;">
<p><strong>Note:</strong> If possible, observe the child at least 6 hours after rehydration to be sure the mother can maintain hydration giving the child ORS solution by mouth.</p>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="hydration-management">Hydration management<sup>1</sup></h2>

<figure class="pccm-flowchart-figure">
<img src="/img/paediatrics/hydration-management-flowchart.png" alt="Hydration management flowchart for mild or no dehydration versus clinically dehydrated" />
<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Hydration management</p>
</figure>

<div class="pccm-two-col-grid">
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">If mild or no dehydration</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Encourage fluid intake eg:
<ul>
<li>ORS (preferred)</li>
<li>diluted apple juice</li>
<li>breast milk/formula</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Inappropriate fluids:
<ul>
<li>soft drink</li>
<li>cordial</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Mild or at risk of dehydration?</strong>
<ul>
<li>No → Consider sending home with Advice to parents/carers</li>
<li>Yes → Observe 1–4 hours — Give oral fluids 0.5 mL/kg every 5 minutes ±
<ul>
<li>Is child maintaining hydration?
<ul>
<li>Yes → Consider sending home with Advice to parents/carers</li>
<li>No → Contact MO</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel" style="background:#fff8ef;">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">If clinically dehydrated</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO urgently</li>
<li>If able give ORS:
<ul>
<li>small amounts 0.5 mL/kg every 5 minutes with syringe/cup</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If severe and &gt; 6 months rapidly rehydrate via NG/IV:
<ul>
<li>50 mL/kg over 4 hours</li>
<li>age ≤ 2 years - ORS via NG</li>
<li>age &gt; 2 years - sodium chloride 0.9% + glucose 5% IV or ORS via NG</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If severe and &lt; 6 months give slower:
<ul>
<li>50 mL/kg over 8–12 hours</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Responding to treatment?</strong>
<ul>
<li>Yes → Consult MO: consider sending home</li>
<li>No → Contact MO urgently — Ongoing rehydration as per MO — Evacuation/hospitalisation</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel" style="background:#fff8ef; margin-top:1rem;">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Advice to parents/carers<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Wash your hands frequently</li>
<li>Symptoms usually resolve within 1–2 days, stools can remain loose for 1–2 weeks</li>
<li>Bring your child back if you are worried or if:
<ul>
<li>develops tummy pain, headache, rash, high fever</li>
<li>vomit turns green</li>
<li>you notice blood in stool or vomit</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Keep your child drinking fluids like, breast milk or formula, watered down apple juice. Give small sips often using syringe, spoon or cup. Avoid sugary drinks like soft drink and cordial</li>
<li>Offer food if they want it and start gradually with plain pasta, rice or potato, dry toast or plain biscuits. Avoid fatty and sugary foods</li>
<li>Do not give anti-diarrhoeal medicines to infants or children</li>
<li>Keep your child away from others until no loose stools in a 24 hour period and they are well<sup>6</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to be reviewed the following day or earlier if parent/carer is concerned</li>
<li>Contact MO if symptoms worsen or persist after 2–3 days, or diarrhoea persists &gt; 10 days</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
</ul>
</div>
