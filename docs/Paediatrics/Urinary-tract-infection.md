# Urinary tract infection (UTI) - Child

4.	 Management
•	 Fever management:

–
Give Paracetamol 10–15 mg/kg oral every 4–6 hours
	›
Syrup (120 mg/5 ml) or tablets (100 mg or 500 mg) depending on weight/
age

–
Lower temperature with tepid sponging or wet towel
•	 Prevent recurrence:

–
For high-risk children (seizure >30 mins or recurrent febrile seizures), start
Phenobarbitone 3–5 mg/kg orally at night as a single dose
	›
Continue for at least 1 month after last seizure
	›
Taper gradually before stopping—do not stop suddenly
	›
Always consult a doctor before stopping
•	 Treat the underlying infection (e.g. URTI, otitis media)
5.	 Follow up
•	 Review response to treatment and monitor for recurrence
•	 Educate caregiver on fever management and seizure first aid
•	 Monitor adherence to prophylactic treatment if started
•	 Discuss medication tapering plan with doctor if seizure-free for 1 month
6.	 Referral / Consultation
•	 Always consult a doctor before:

–
Stopping prophylactic medication

–
If seizure lasts >10 minutes or recurs

–
If signs of meningitis or unclear diagnosis

Background
•	 Diagnosing a UTI in young children can be challenging as symptoms are non-
specific1
1.
May present with1
•	 Fever
•	 Unwell - looks sick, irritable
•	 Poor feeding, nausea, vomiting
•	 Poor weight gain
•	 Urinary symptoms - frequency, dysuria, haematuria, smelly urine
•	 Abdominal or loin pain
•	 Changes to continence in older child

•	 Do vital signs - note BP1
•	 Screen for Sepsis
