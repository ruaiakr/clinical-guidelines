# Hepatitis A – Adult/Child

Hepatitis A – Adult/Child
Recommend1
•	 Acute hepatitis A is a notifiable condition
•	 Vaccinate according to the National Immunisation Program schedule, and
advise on avoidance of risk factors (e.g. untreated water, poor hand hygiene,
unsafe food preparation)
•	 Perform contact tracing to identify and monitor exposed individuals
Background1
•	 Hepatitis A virus (HAV) is common in Kiribati
•	 HAV is transmitted by the faecal–oral route, typically from contaminated food or
water, and less commonly via sexual contact (oral–anal practices)
•	 Incubation period: 2–7 weeks (average 28–30 days)
•	 HAV infection is self-limiting, with illness usually resolving within 6 months. Does
not cause chronic disease
•	 HAV is excreted in stools 2 weeks before symptoms appear, and up to 1 week
after the onset of jaundice
•	 Two cases in a household or community setting constitute an outbreak
•	 Clinical severity varies:

–
Mild illness (1–2 weeks)

–
Severe disabling illness (lasting several months)
•	 Relapsing hepatitis A (up to one year) can occur; severity increases with age
•	 Mother-to-child transmission of HAV is not typical, but all pregnant women
should be screened and managed for hepatitis B, HIV, and syphilis, regardless of
suspected hepatitis A
May present with1,2
•	 Often asymptomatic, especially in infants and young children
•	 If symptomatic:

–
Fever, malaise, nausea, abdominal discomfort

–
Loss of appetite

–
Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes)

–
Dark urine, pale stools

–
Itchy skin (pruritis)
•	 Laboratory findings:

–
Detection of anti-hepatitis A IgM, in the absence of recent vaccination (if
available)

–
Detection of HAV by nucleic acid testing (if available)

1.
Immediate management
•	 Acute infection is self-limiting and can be managed on an outpatient-basis
•	 If in-patient care is required, it may involve supportive therapy
•	 Counselling of patient and immediate family must be provided to ensure
awareness, support and prevention of transmission
2.	 Clinical assessment1
•	 Obtain comprehensive history, including:

–
Contact with known hepatitis A case

–
Water and sanitation conditions at home or workplace

–
Travel history

–
Medications and occupation (especially food handling, healthcare, teaching)

–
Immunisation history
•	 Perform standard observations (Q-ADDS/CEWT or local early warning tools)
•	 Physical examination – look for jaundice, dehydration, abdominal tenderness
•	 Take bloods for:

–
Hepatitis A, B, and C serology

–
Liver function tests (LFTs)

–
INR
•	 For antenatal patients:

–
Conduct point-of-care screening for hepatitis B, HIV, and syphilis using
WHO-approved tests (e.g. Determine™ HBsAg2)
3.	 Management1,2
•	 Consult Medical Officer (MO) on all occasions when hepatitis A is suspected or
jaundice is present
•	 Consult urgently if INR ≥ 1.5, or if signs of liver failure (e.g. confusion, bleeding,
prolonged prothrombin time)
•	 Treatment of hepatitis A is supportive and symptomatic:

–
Hospitalisation rarely required

–
Bed rest advised if jaundiced
•	 During the infectious period (7 days after jaundice or dark urine begins, or
2 weeks after initial symptoms), advise the patient not to:

–
Donate blood

–
Drink alcohol or take paracetamol

–
Prepare or handle food for others

–
Have sex

–
Attend school, childcare, work

–
Provide personal care to others

–
Share food, drinks, cigarettes, drug-injection equipment
•	 Patient and household education:

–
Emphasise handwashing and hygiene

–
Avoid shared food preparation

•	 If pregnant and HBsAg positive:

–
Start antiviral prophylaxis at 28 weeks gestation, or earlier if clinically
indicated (see Hepatitis B section)

–
Women from outer islands should begin treatment during antenatal contact
and remain on treatment after delivery

–
Continue treatment for at least 6 weeks postpartum, to reduce risk of flares –
use is safe during breastfeeding

–
Ensure timely hepatitis B birth dose vaccine is provided – give within 24
hours of delivery
4.	 Follow up1
•	 Advise patient to return within 24 hours for clinical review
•	 Ensure Public Health notification has occurred
•	 For pregnant women, confirm linkage to antenatal follow-up and treatment
continuation postpartum if on hepatitis B therapy
5.	 Referral/consultation
•	 Consult MO for all confirmed or suspected cases
•	 Notify Public Health – hepatitis A is a notifiable disease
•	 Liaise with Public Health Unit for:

–
Contact tracing

–
Immunisation of close household contacts

–
Monitoring of antenatal screening coverage and treatment compliance in
pregnant women

–
Outbreak response in community settings
