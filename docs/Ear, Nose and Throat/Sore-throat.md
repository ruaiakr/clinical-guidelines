<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Sore throat – (Pharyngitis, tonsillitis) - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommendation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Treat sore throats with antibiotics promptly in people living in rural + remote settings and in others who are at high risk of <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-rheumatic-fever">Acute rheumatic Fever</a> and <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-post-streptococcal-glomerulonephritis">Acute post streptococcal glomerulonephritis</a>.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sore throat ± fever</li>
<li>Difficulty swallowing, not eating/drinking as much, croaky voice</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Look for symptoms of airway obstruction/compromise or deep neck space infection eg peritonsillar abscess (quinsy), including:</li>
<li>muffled voice, stridor, trismus (unable to open mouth)</li>
<li>drooling, neck swelling/twisting, posturing ie sitting forward, neck extension</li>
<li>severe neck pain, respiratory distress</li>
<li>If any of above, contact MA, admit to start iv antibiotic, secure airway and for urgent retrieval</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>cough, fever, rash</li>
<li>feeding - normal or reduced intake, difficulty swallowing</li>
<li>number of times passed urine/wet nappies in last 24 hours</li>
<li>other symptoms eg diarrhoea, vomiting, headache, malaise, runny nose, red eyes, hoarse voice, joint pains</li>
<li>past history - ARF or RHD, immunocompromised, immunisation status</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>chest - listen for crackles/wheeze, air entry</li>
<li>throat/mouth:
<ul>
<li>redness, swelling/pus on tonsils</li>
<li>ulcers/vesicles, strawberry tongue</li>
<li>greyish or green membrane - may indicate diphtheria</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>ears</li>
<li>palpate neck for enlarged/tender lymph nodes</li>
<li>look for rash + signs of APSGN, and ARF</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>A runny nose or a hoarse voice is suggestive of a viral infection and does not require antibiotic therapy.</li>
<li>Sore throat in the absence of a runny nose or a hoarse voice is suggestive of a bacterial infection may need antibiotics treatment.</li>
<li>Use table below for differential diagnosis of sore throat</li>
</ul>

<p>Non-infectious causes may include: Allergic rhinitis, smoke exposure, dry air, cancer, or reflux.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Supportive care
<ul>
<li>paracetamol: children: 10-15 mg/kg orally every 4-6 hours when required, maximum 75 mg/kg/day; adults: 500-1000 mg orally every 4-6 hours when required, maximum 4000 mg/ day</li>
<li>Get plenty rest.</li>
<li>Drink a lot of water</li>
<li>Collect throat swab.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>antibiotics are not usually indicated but if throat swab confirmed group A Streptococcus (GAS) or history of rheumatic fever</li>
<li>Consult admit and start IV antibiotic if:
<ul>
<li>&lt; 12 month</li>
<li>unable to drink/signs of dehydration</li>
<li>T ≥ 39</li>
<li>looks sick, not alert/interactive when ↓T</li>
<li>rash or signs of ARF or APSGN</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
<li>suspected diphtheria</li>
<li>severe throat pain</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Use flowchart below to determine if antibiotics are indicated or not for ‘sore throat’</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="arf-risk">Sore Throat: Is the Patient at High Risk of ARF (Acute Rheumatic Fever)?</h2>

<div class="pccm-flowchart" role="region" aria-label="Sore throat ARF high risk antibiotic decision flowchart">
<div class="pccm-flow-stack">
<div class="pccm-flow-split">
<div class="pccm-flow-branch">
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#f8e4c8; border:2px solid #c47a3a;">
<p class="pccm-flow-heading" style="color:#222;">Consider patient at high risk of ARF if ANY of the following apply:</p>
<ul>
<li>Living in an ARF-endemic area</li>
<li>Māori or Pacific Islander person</li>
<li>Existing RHD, or history of ARF/RHD and aged &lt; 40 years</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-branch">
<div class="pccm-flow-box pccm-flow-box--outline" style="border-color:#e8a060; background:#fff8ef;">
<p class="pccm-flow-heading" style="color:#222;">Also Consider High Risk If:</p>
<ul>
<li>Family or household has recent ARF/RHD</li>
<li>Overcrowding (≥ 2 people/bedroom) or low socioeconomic status</li>
<li>Migrant/refugee from a low- or middle-income country</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">▼</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-split">
<div class="pccm-flow-branch">
<div class="pccm-flow-label" style="color:#c47a3a;">Yes <span>▼</span></div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#f8e4c8; border:2px solid #c47a3a;">
<ul>
<li><strong>Do a throat swab (MCS) first</strong></li>
<li><strong>Give antibiotics immediately</strong></li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-arrow">▼</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box" style="background:#fff0dc; border:2px solid #e8a060;">
<p class="pccm-flow-heading pccm-flow-heading--center" style="color:#222;">Antibiotic Selection</p>
<ul>
<li>Give 1 dose of IM <a href="#benzathine-benzylpenicillin">benzathine benzylpenicillin</a> (Bicillin L-A®)</li>
<li>If IM not possible, give oral <a href="#phenoxymethylpenicillin">phenoxymethylpenicillin</a> and explain the importance of completing the full course</li>
<li><strong>Mild allergy (e.g. rash):</strong> Give cefalexin</li>
<li><strong>Severe allergy (e.g. anaphylaxis):</strong> Give azithromycin</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<div class="pccm-flow-branch">
<div class="pccm-flow-label">No <span>▼</span></div>
<div class="pccm-flow-box pccm-flow-box--blue">
<p><strong>Do NOT give antibiotics routinely</strong></p>
<p>If symptoms suggest Strep A sore throat/tonsillitis:</p>
<ul>
<li>Take throat swab (MCS or PoCT)</li>
<li>Review next day or earlier if concerned (e.g. new symptoms, rigors, dehydration)</li>
<li>If not improving in 3–7 days and swab confirms Strep A, consider phenoxymethylpenicillin</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-note-panel" style="margin-top:1.25rem; border-color:#e8a060;">
<p><strong>Important Notes</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>If on <a href="/docs/Common%20medical%20conditions/Secondary-prophylaxis-for-acute-rheumatic-fever">secondary prophylaxis for ARF</a> and needs more antibiotics, confirm last dose.</li>
<li>If &lt; 7 days since last dose, <strong>do not give extra dose.</strong></li>
<li>The next secondary dose is due in 28 days unless otherwise reset.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Symptomatic Treatment</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Use paracetamol or ibuprofen for relief of pain and fever</li>
<li>Throat lozenges may be helpful for adolescents and adults</li>
<li>Encourage adequate fluid intake – prefer water, avoid sugary drinks</li>
<li>Advise to rest and avoid exposure to smoke</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="benzathine-benzylpenicillin">BENZATHINE BENZYLPENICILLIN (BICILLIN L-A®)</h2>
<div class="pccm-drug-box">
<div class="pccm-drug-header">
<span class="pccm-drug-name">BENZATHINE BENZYLPENICILLIN (BICILLIN L-A®)</span>
<span class="pccm-drug-level">L2</span>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap" style="margin:0; overflow-x:auto;">
<table class="pccm-drug-table">
<tr><th>FORM</th><th>STRENGTH</th><th>ROUTE</th><th>DOSE</th><th>DURATION</th></tr>

<td>1.2 million units / 2.3 mL</td>
<td>IM</td>
<td>&lt; 10 kg: 450,000 units (0.9 mL)<br />10–&lt;20 kg: 600,000 units (1.2 mL)<br />≥ 20 kg: 1.2 million units (2.3 mL)</td>
<td>Single dose (stat)<br />Administer slowly over 2–3 minutes</td>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p>Preferred sites: Ventrogluteal or outer mid-thigh (not deltoid)</p>
<p>See Managing injection pain</p>
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> May cause diarrhoea, nausea, pain at injection site</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Severe/immediate allergy to penicillin</p>
<p><strong>Cross-reactivity:</strong> Possible with cephalosporins and carbapenems</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a></p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check results of throat MCS if taken</li>
<li>Advise to be reviewed the next day, or earlier if worried, symptoms worsen or new symptoms develop eg vomiting, dehydration, fever, rigors:<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>If at high risk of ARF, or previous ARF or RHD:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>provide education on signs + symptoms of ARF and APSGN, which can occur about 10–14 days after sore throat or skin sores</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If patient is not responding to treatment.</li>
<li>If shows any signs of airway obstruction.</li>
</ul>
