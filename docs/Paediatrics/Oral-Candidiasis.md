# Oral Candidiasis (Thrush) – Child

Oral Candidiasis (Thrush) – Child
Background
•	 Oral candidiasis (thrush) is a fungal infection caused by Candida albicans. It
commonly occurs in young children, particularly infants, and may be associated
with poor immune function, chronic illness, or recent antibiotic use. It can be
passed on by contact with contaminated items, from the mother (especially if
she has a fungal breast infection), or from the child’s own fungal nappy rash.
Recommendations
•	 Treat with oral nystatin suspension and discontinue antibiotics as soon as
clinically appropriate. Treat concurrent fungal nappy rash with nystatin cream.
Maintain breastfeeding and promote good hygiene.
1.
May present with
•	 No symptoms (mild cases)
•	 Pain when feeding or refusal to feed (severe cases)
•	 Diarrhoea
•	 White patches on lips, gums, tongue, or palate
•	 Red, inflamed base when patches are wiped off
2.	 Immediate management
•	 None required unless child is critically unwell or unable to feed
•	 If very unwell or malnourished, consult doctor urgently
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess general appearance for signs of malnutrition or chronic illness
•	 Examine lips, gums, tongue, palate, and pharynx
