# Gastroenteritis/dehydration - Adult Diarrhoea ± vomiting

Gastroenteritis/dehydration - Adult
Diarrhoea ± vomiting
Background1
•	 Most acute diarrhoea is viral and resolves within 24–48 hours without specific
treatment
•	 Antibiotics are of no benefit in most cases and may exacerbate diarrhoea
1.
May present with1
•	 Sudden onset of diarrhoea ± vomiting, fever, abdominal pain, nausea, chills
•	 Lethargy, dehydration
2.	 Immediate management1
•	 Do vital signs + Hydration assessment
•	 If severe dehydration:

–
contact MO urgently

–
continue to manage as per Shock
•	 Screen for Sepsis

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Get history, including:1

–
gastrointestinal symptoms:
	›
date/time of onset, frequency + amounts, is there blood ± mucous in stools,
bile stained or green vomit, location and severity of abdominal pain
	›
note: if > 24 hours of vomiting without diarrhoea, consider other causes.
See Nausea and vomiting

–
fever, rash, headache, light headedness/dizziness, mobility

–
known illness in contacts

–
recent overseas travel, antibiotic use or hospital admission

–
immunocompromised

–
medications eg aperients
•	 Do physical examination, including:

–
BGL + ketones - if  consider DKA

–
i-STAT - UE, lactate

–
urinalysis - ketones

–
Abdominal examination
•	 Do stool MCS + multiplex PCR + C.difficile if:1

–
diarrhoea > 7 days

–
severe symptoms eg blood in stools, high fever, HR, abdominal tenderness
or severe pain

–
immunocompromised

–
recent overseas travel (C.difficile not required)
4.	 Management1
•	 Rehydration is the most important aspect of management
•	 Consult MO if:

–
vomiting and unable to tolerate oral fluids - IV fluids may be required2

–
severe symptoms

–
pregnant, immunocompromised or elderly
•	 If minimal or no dehydration:

–
advise to continue eating + drinking as tolerated
•	 If mild to moderate dehydration:

–
give ORS, even if intermittent vomiting

–
note: if ORS is not available, use 1/2 level teaspoon of salt + 6 level teaspoons
of sugar in 1 L of clean drinking water

–
aim for 2–3 L over 24 hours. Frequent small volumes may be better tolerated
eg 50 mL every 15–30 minutes

–
avoid fatty or sugary foods and excessive intake of soft drinks, sports/energy
drinks, cordials and fruit juice. If used they should be diluted

–
once tolerating/responding to ORS, advise patient to return to normal diet1
•	 Offer antiemetic if needed. See Nausea and vomiting

•	 Control the spread advice:

–
if prepares/serves food - avoid handling food until no diarrhoea or vomiting
for 48 hours2

–
if healthcare worker - do not return to work until no symptoms for 48 hours3

–
otherwise, if single case - avoid others until no symptoms, feeling well + no
loose bowel motions for at least 24 hours2
5.	 Follow up1
•	 Advise patient to return + contact MO if:

–
symptoms persist > 48 hours, or worsen

–
develops severe vomiting, abdominal cramps, or blood in stools
•	 Review stool pathology results
6.	 Referral/consultation2
•	 Notify Public Health Unit if ≥ 2 cases of diarrhoea ± vomiting in the same location
or single case in a food handler 
