Dengue fever
Background
Dengue fever is a viral illness transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes, primarily Aedes
aegypti. It is endemic in tropical and subtropical areas. Dengue is characterized by
high fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain, muscle and joint pain, and rash.
Recommendations
•	 Prevention involves controlling mosquito populations and reducing mosquito
breeding sites.
•	 Symptomatic treatment is the primary approach, with monitoring for warning
signs of severe dengue, such as bleeding and shock.
1.
May present with
•	 High fever
•	 Severe headache
•	 Retro-orbital pain (pain behind the eyes)
•	 Muscle and joint pains ("breakbone fever")
•	 Rash (can appear 3-4 days after the fever)
•	 Nausea and vomiting
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Symptomatic treatment: Paracetamol for fever, hydration, and rest.
•	 Monitor for warning signs of severe dengue (e.g., bleeding, shock, organ failure).

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Check for signs of dehydration and bleeding (e.g., petechiae, bleeding gums).
•	 Measure blood pressure, heart rate, and temperature.
•	 Assess for warning signs of severe dengue such as sudden abdominal pain,
persistent vomiting, or bleeding.

4.	 Management
•	 Provide supportive care: Rest, fluids, and paracetamol for fever.
•	 Monitor for signs of severe dengue.
•	 If signs of severe dengue develop, initiate appropriate treatment for shock,
bleeding, or organ failure.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Follow-up within 24-48 hours to assess clinical improvement or worsening
symptoms.
•	 Immediate follow-up if warning signs of severe dengue appear.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to a medical officer immediately if signs of severe dengue such as shock,
bleeding, or organ dysfunction are observed.
