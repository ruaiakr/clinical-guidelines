# Oral facial fractures (Kiribati Original Protocol)

Oral facial fractures (Kiribati Original Protocol)
Purpose:
To assist Medical Assistants and Nurses in the primary care setting to recognize,
assess, provide initial management, and appropriately refer patients presenting with
oral facial fractures.
Background
Facial fractures are increasingly common presentations in primary healthcare.
Prompt recognition and appropriate management are critical to prevent permanent
functional, cosmetic, or vision-threatening complications.
General Principles
•	 Follow Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) protocols for all trauma cases.
•	 Conduct a full visual assessment in any facial trauma.
•	 Identify red flags requiring immediate referral.
•	 Provide early pain management and wound care as appropriate.
1.
May present with
•	 Nasal Fractures: Often resulting from interpersonal violence, nasal fractures
are the most common facial fractures, accounting for up to 58.6% of all facial
fractures. Symptoms include nasal deformity, epistaxis, and periorbital bruising.

•	 Mandibular Fractures: These may present with pain, swelling, malocclusion, and

may destabilize the airway. www.entnet.org/wp-content/uploads/files/Trauma-

•	 Zygomatic Fractures: Symptoms include periorbital swelling, infraorbital nerve
paraesthesia, and restricted eye movement.
•	 Orbital Fractures: Signs include diplopia, reduced visual acuity, and orbital rim
tenderness.
•	 Frontal Bone Fractures: Present with forehead deformity, possible CSF
rhinorrhoea, and periorbital bruising.

2.	 Immediate management
•	 Airway Management: Ensure airway patency, especially in cases of mandibular
fractures that may compromise the airway. https://link.springer.com/
chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-1346-6_48?utm_source=chatgpt.com
•	 Hemostasis: Control bleeding using direct pressure, nasal packing, or hemostatic
agents as appropriate.
•	 Analgesia: Administer appropriate pain relief, considering the severity of the
injury.
•	 Antibiotics: Prophylactic antibiotics may be indicated in cases of open fractures
or significant soft tissue involvement. https://tsaco.bmj.com/content/9/1/
e001304?utm_source=chatgpt.com
•	 Imaging: Obtain appropriate imaging studies, such as X-rays or CT scans, to
assess the extent of the fractures.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 History: Obtain details of the mechanism of injury, time of injury, and any
associated symptoms.
•	 Examination: Perform a thorough head and neck examination, assessing for
deformities, swelling, tenderness, and neurological deficits.
•	 Functional Assessment: Evaluate the patient's ability to open and close the
mouth, chew, and speak.
•	 Neurological Examination: Assess cranial nerve function, particularly the
trigeminal nerve, for sensory deficits.

4.	 Management
•	 Conservative Management: Indicated for non-displaced fractures without
significant functional impairment. Includes analgesia, soft diet, and follow-up.
https://www.rch.org.au/trauma-service/manual/maxillofacial-injury/?utm_
source=chatgpt.com
•	 Surgical Intervention: Required for displaced fractures, fractures with
airway compromise, or those causing significant functional impairment.
https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/maxillofacial-trauma-19339123/19339123
•	 Referral: Refer to oral and maxillofacial specialists for definitive management.
https://metronorth.health.qld.gov.au/specialist_service/refer-your-patient/oral-
maxillo-facial/facial-fractures-lacerations?utm_source=chatgpt.com
5.	 Follow up
•	 Monitoring: Regular follow-up to assess healing, manage complications, and
address any functional issues.
•	 Rehabilitation: May include physiotherapy for jaw mobility and speech therapy if
necessary.
•	 Psychological Support: Provide support for patients dealing with the
psychological impact of facial trauma.
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 Urgent Referral: For patients with airway compromise, significant bleeding, or
complex fractures.
•	 Routine Referral: For non-displaced fractures without functional impairment.
•	 Specialist Consultation: Consult with oral and maxillofacial surgeons for
