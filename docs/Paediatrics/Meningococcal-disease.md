Meningococcal disease – Child
Background
•	 Acute meningococcal disease may present as severe sepsis with a progressive
non-blanching petechial/purpuric rash, or meningitis with or without a rash
•	 Rarer presentations include septic arthritis, pneumonia, pharyngitis and occult
bacteremia
•	 There are 13 serogroups of Neisseria meningitis (the cause of meningococcal
disease)
   ISOLATION:
•	 Meningococcal disease is spread from person to person by respiratory droplets;
therefore, patients should be isolated with droplet precautions.
1.
May present with
•	 Rapid onset (&lt;12hrs) of headache, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, sore throat
and coryza
•	 Fever
•	 Infantas may have reduced feeds, irritability
•	 Leg pain and myalgia
•	 Late signs (>12hrs)
•	 Altered conscious state
•	 Neck stiffness headache, photophobia, bulging fontanelle
•	 Non-blanching rash: petechiae/purpura
NOTE: a blanching rash does not exclude meningococcal disease( can initially be
macular or maculopapular)

2.	 Immediate management
•	 Use the ABCD approach

–
A: Maintain airway

–
B: Provide oxygen if respiratory distress

–
C: Ensure vascular access and give fluids if needed (2/3 IV maintenance)

–
D: Manage seizures and check blood glucose
•	 Treat fever with paracetamol
•	 Arrange immediate referral to hospital
•	 Begin antibiotics without delay
•	 If in SHOCK – bolus 20mls/kg n/s or Hartmann’s, can repeat bolus 2x
•	 PATIENT SHOULD BE ISOLATED FOR 24 HOURS AFTER ADMINISTRATION OF
ANTIBIOITICS
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, capillary refill
•	 Assess general condition and consciousness
•	 Check for signs of raised intracranial pressure
•	 Examine fontanelle in infants
•	 Perform a brief screening physical exam
•	 Doctor will assess for focal neurological signs and plan investigations
