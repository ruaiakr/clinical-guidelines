<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Dermatitis (Eczema) – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start with treating any underlying or secondary cause (e.g. <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Scabies">scabies</a>, fungal infection, bacterial infection). If no improvement, commence dermatitis management with moisturisers and topical steroids. Refer if not responding or if diagnosis is uncertain.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Dermatitis, also known as eczema, refers to a group of itchy, inflammatory skin conditions caused by allergic, irritant, or internal (endogenous) factors. It can be classified by cause, clinical appearance, or body site.</li>
<li>Acute dermatitis lasts less than 6 weeks and shows redness, swelling, papules, vesicles, oozing, and even blistering.</li>
<li>Chronic dermatitis lasts more than 6 weeks and presents with dry skin, thickened skin with prominent creases, hyperkeratosis, scaling, fissuring, excoriation, and hyperpigmentation.</li>
<li>Subacute dermatitis has features of both acute and chronic forms.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="acute-dermatitis">Acute Dermatitis</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Redness</li>
<li>Swelling</li>
<li>Papulation</li>
<li>Vesiculation</li>
<li>Oozing</li>
<li>Weeping</li>
<li>Blistering</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="chronic-dermatitis">Chronic Dermatitis</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Dry skin</li>
<li>Skin thickening</li>
<li>Hyperkeratosis</li>
<li>Scaling</li>
<li>Fissuring</li>
<li>Excoriation</li>
<li>Hyperpigmentation</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Take a detailed history and perform a thorough examination</li>
<li>Identify any known irritants or allergens</li>
<li>Consider differential diagnoses:
<ul>
<li><a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Impetigo-Child-Adult">Impetigo</a>, fungal infection, <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Scabies">scabies</a></li>
<li>Lupus erythematosus, dermatomyositis</li>
<li>Rosacea, blistering diseases, skin tumour</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Assess the clinical form (acute, chronic, or subacute)</li>
<li>Perform available laboratory tests (if feasible)</li>
<li>If no test available, treat for <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Scabies">scabies</a> or fungal infection first. If no response, proceed with dermatitis treatment</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="general-measures">General measures</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Remove or avoid known irritants/allergens (e.g. soaps, gels, dust, organic solvents)</li>
<li>Use moisturisers regularly (e.g. coconut oil, sorbolene) 3–4 times daily</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="topical-therapy">Topical therapy</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use ointments for dry skin; creams for wet/weeping skin</li>
<li>Apply topical steroids as follows:</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="oral-therapy">Oral therapy</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Antihistamines (e.g. Phenergan) for itch – use sedating types at night</li>
<li>If eczema is secondarily infected:
<ul>
<li>Use antibiotics for bacterial infection</li>
<li>Use antivirals if herpes simplex is suspected</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>In severe or resistant cases (not first-line):
<ul>
<li>Oral Prednisolone 20–30 mg daily for 1 week, then taper if not fully improved</li>
<li>Not routinely recommended without specific indication</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review in 1–2 weeks</li>
<li>If fully cured, stop treatment</li>
<li>If improving, continue treatment for another 1–2 weeks as needed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>For nurses and MAs on outer islands, consult via Skin group Messenger chat</li>
<li>Refer to Emergency Department at Tungaru Central Hospital (TCH) if not improving or diagnosis is unclear</li>
</ul>
