<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) - Adult</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Common cold, influenza, bronchitis, sore throat</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Common cold (acute viral rhinosinusitis)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Blocked nose</li>
<li>Runny nose or posterior nasal drip (can trigger cough)</li>
<li>Facial pain/pressure</li>
<li>↓ sense of smell</li>
<li>If fever, usually mild</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Influenza</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As per common cold:
<ul>
<li>+ sudden onset of fever, headache, muscle + joint pain, severe malaise, sore throat<sup>2,3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Bronchitis - inflammation of bronchi that usually follows an URTI<sup>4,5</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Cough ±
<ul>
<li>purulent or coloured sputum</li>
<li>SOB, wheeze</li>
<li>chest discomfort/pain due to frequent coughing</li>
<li>nasal congestion</li>
<li>headache, fever</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If cough not prominent feature, consider likelihood of other URTI eg common cold, tonsillitis, pharyngitis. See <a href="/docs/Ear, Nose and Throat/Sore-throat">Sore throat</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use appropriate PPE</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>onset, duration, severity of symptoms</li>
<li>fever</li>
<li>SOB, cough, any sputum - colour</li>
<li>facial pain, chest discomfort/pain</li>
<li>asthma, COPD, immunocompromised<sup>1,6</sup></li>
<li>recent international travel<sup>7</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>vital signs</li>
<li>check/listen for:
<ul>
<li>WOB</li>
<li>↓ air entry, wheeze, crackles</li>
<li>ENT - red throat, enlarged tonsils, bulging red eardrums</li>
<li>enlarged head/neck lymph glands</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Most respiratory tract infections are viral + get better without antibiotics<sup>8</sup></li>
<li>If severe symptoms + patient unwell consult MO</li>
<li>Paracetamol or ibuprofen if needed. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
<li>Advise to treat symptoms eg:
<ul>
<li>gargle warm salty water</li>
<li>suck on an ice cube or lozenges</li>
<li>have a soothing drink eg honey and lemon</li>
<li>use saline nasal drops/spray</li>
<li>wash hands, cover mouth when sneezing/coughing to help stop others catching it</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Common cold likely</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Symptoms usually clear within 7 days<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Advise to return if develops features of bronchitis and/or Bacterial sinusitis</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Influenza likely</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider nose and throat swab PCR to confirm (or as per Public Health Unit advice)</li>
<li>Consult MO if at risk of complications (antiviral medicine(s) may be indicated), including:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>≥ 65 years</li>
<li>immunocompromised, chronic conditions</li>
<li>pregnant woman</li>
<li>obesity</li>
<li>homeless person</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Bronchitis likely<sup>4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise usually resolves without antibiotics within 2–3 weeks. Cough can persist longer, sometimes up to 8 weeks</li>
<li>Consider other causes of cough eg:
<ul>
<li><a href="/docs/Cardiovascular%20and%20respiratory%20disorders/Pneumonia">Pneumonia - adult</a></li>
<li><a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Pertussis">Pertussis</a></li>
<li>influenza</li>
<li>heart failure</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to be reviewed if not improving in a few days, symptoms worsen, new symptoms, or concerned. Consult MO</li>
<li>If cough continues &gt; 3 weeks, advise to see MO at next clinic - may need further investigations</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Laboratory confirmed influenza is notifiable ☎</li>
</ul>
