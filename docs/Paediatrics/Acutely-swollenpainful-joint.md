<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acutely swollen/painful joint - Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>The causes can be difficult to diagnose. Be suspicious of septic arthritis (orthopaedic emergency), acute osteomyelitis and <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-rheumatic-fever">ARF</a></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Also see Limp - emergency management in children<br /><a href="https://www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/qpec-statewide-guidelines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/qpec-statewide-guidelines/</a></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,4-6</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hot, swollen, tender joint(s) ± fever, malaise and fatigue</li>
<li>Painful hip</li>
<li>Joint pain on movement</li>
<li>↓ mobility, limp, problems with weight bearing</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Vital signs</li>
<li>Screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>2-6</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess for Red flags - may require urgent further assessment ± evacuation</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-red-flags">
<div class="pccm-red-flags-banner">Red flags<sup>1</sup></div>
<div class="pccm-red-flags-body">
<ul>
<li>Systemic symptoms eg T ≥ 38.5, malaise, weight loss, night sweats</li>
<li>&lt; 4 years of age</li>
<li>Inability to weight bear or severe, localised joint pain</li>
<li>Inflammation of 1 joint<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Bony pain</li>
<li>Possible unwitnessed trauma/non-accidental injury</li>
<li>Overweight adolescent</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>pain - acute or gradual onset</li>
<li>ARF/RHD diagnosis or family history of<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>recent injury/wound - can be minor<sup>1,3</sup></li>
<li>recent joint surgery</li>
<li>other symptoms eg skin infections, sore throat, diarrhoea, fatigue, rash<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>IV drug use<sup>6</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Urinalysis</li>
<li>Examine joints:
<ul>
<li>swelling, tenderness, warmth and mobility</li>
<li>in younger children watch how they move, weight bear, crawl, walk<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>bony point tenderness - may indicate acute osteomyelitis<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Consider <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-rheumatic-fever">ARF</a> - especially if pain seems out of proportion to the joint signs</li>
<li>Check:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>for swollen lymph nodes</li>
<li>skin for bruising, recent sores</li>
<li>throat for redness</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>2-7</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always consider septic arthritis, acute osteomyelitis and ARF, as cause</li>
<li>If any red flags contact MO urgently</li>
<li>Always contact MO for all acute swollen/painful joint presentations, who may advise:
<ul>
<li>blood cultures, IV antibiotics</li>
<li>evacuation/hospitalisation ± referral to orthopaedic specialist/paediatrician</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
</ul>
