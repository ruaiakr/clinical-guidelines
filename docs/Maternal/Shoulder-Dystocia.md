Shoulder Dystocia (Kiribati Protocol)
Background
•	 Shoulder dystocia occurs when, after delivery of the fetal head, one or both
shoulders become impacted against the maternal pelvis, preventing delivery.
This leads to compression of the umbilical cord and interruption of fetal oxygen
supply. It is an unpredictable emergency that can occur in any birth but is
more common in large babies and in mothers with certain risk factors. Rapid
recognition and coordinated manoeuvres are essential to reduce the risk of fetal
injury, including hypoxia, nerve injury, and death.
Recommended:
•	 Shoulder dystocia is an obstetric emergency where the shoulders fail to deliver
after the head has been born. This can rapidly lead to fetal hypoxia and injury.
Immediate, coordinated action is required to deliver the baby safely.
1.
May present with
•	 Head delivers but shoulders do not follow
•	 “Turtle sign” – retraction of the fetal head against the perineum
•	 Difficulty delivering the shoulders
•	 Prolonged second stage of labour
•	 Failure of normal traction to deliver shoulders
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Call for help immediately
•	 Call doctor/obstetrician urgently if available
•	 Explain situation to the woman
•	 DO NOT pull on the fetal head (risk of severe injury)
•	 Position woman immediately for McRoberts manoeuvre:

–
Move woman to edge of bed

–
Flex both legs tightly onto abdomen

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Confirm diagnosis (head delivered, shoulders stuck)
•	 Note time of head delivery
•	 Assess fetal condition
•	 Identify risk factors if known (macrosomia, diabetes, prolonged labour)
4.	 Management

•	 H – Help

–
Call for assistance immediately
•	 E – Episiotomy

–
Consider if additional space required
•	 L – Legs (McRoberts manoeuvre)

–
Hyperflex hips tightly to abdomen

–
Two assistants may be needed
•	 P – Suprapubic pressure

–
Third assistant applies pressure above pubic bone

–

•	 E – Enter manoeuvres (internal rotation)

–
If trained, insert hand into vagina

–
