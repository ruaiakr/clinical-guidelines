# Shingles – Adult/Child

Shingles – Adult/Child
Recommend
Start antiviral treatment early (within 72 hours) to reduce the risk of complications.
Manage pain and prevent spread to others. Refer urgently if face, eye, pregnancy,
child, or immunocompromised.
Background
Shingles (Herpes Zoster) is caused by reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus
(VZV) in a person who previously had chickenpox. It is more common in people
over 50 years. While usually self-limiting, it can lead to serious complications such
as persistent pain (post-herpetic neuralgia), blindness, pneumonia, hearing loss,
encephalitis, or death.
1.
May present with
•	 Painful rash developing into small blisters within 24–48 hours

–
Usually affects one side of the body along a nerve pathway

–
Crusts over within 5 days
•	 48–72 hours before the rash appears, patient may report:

–
Headache, photophobia, malaise

–
Itching, tingling, or severe pain in the affected area
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Not applicable
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Take a history, including:

–
Description of rash

–
Other signs and symptoms

–
History of chickenpox

–
Immunocompromised status
•	 Check vital signs
•	 Examine the skin – see Skin assessment – adult or child
•	 Take a swab of lesion for VZV PCR if available

4.	 Management
•	 Consult MO promptly if:

–
Patient is a child

–
Rash is on the face or eye (may require evacuation and ophthalmology review)
	›
MO may consider early acyclovir eye ointment

–
Patient is immunocompromised or pregnant
•	 For pain relief:

–
Offer paracetamol – see Acute Pain

–
Ice packs and protective dressings may help

–
Lidocaine (lignocaine) ointment or similar may be used for short-term relief

–
If severe pain and not relieved by paracetamol, consult MO – they may
prescribe oral prednisolone and/or amitriptyline

•	 Safety notes:

–
Drink at least 1.5L of fluids per day

–
May cause dizziness or confusion

–
If renal impairment, seek MO advice

–
Pregnancy: Acyclovir is preferred and may be used from 36 weeks gestation

–
Contraindication: Allergy to acyclovir or valaciclovir
•	 If secondary skin infection present, treat as for Impetigo, or Cellulitis
•	 Provide education on preventing spread – see Shingles Advice below
Shingles Advice
•	 Cover the rash if possible

–
If child cannot keep rash covered, exclude from school until no new blisters for
24 hours
•	 Avoid touching or scratching the rash
•	 Wash hands often
•	 Avoid contact with the following people until rash has crusted over:

–
Pregnant women who have not had chickenpox or the vaccine (shingles is not
usually harmful to unborn baby)

–
Premature or low birth weight babies

–
Children who have not had chickenpox or the vaccine

–
People with weakened immune systems (e.g. chemotherapy, transplant
recipients, HIV)
•	 Zoster vaccine is still recommended at the appropriate age
