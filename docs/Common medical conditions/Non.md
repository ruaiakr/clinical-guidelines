<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Non-Communicable Disease (NCD) Assessment and management</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess all adult patients for common NCDs: obesity, hypertension, and diabetes</li>
<li>Use risk-based approach for prevention and treatment</li>
<li>Counsel patients on lifestyle changes and adherence to medications</li>
<li>Refer to hospital or physician when urgent criteria are met</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are a major public health concern in Kiribati. The most prevalent NCDs include cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and associated metabolic conditions such as obesity and dyslipidemia. Effective management requires a comprehensive approach including lifestyle interventions, medication adherence, regular screening, and timely referral</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>General fatigue or weakness</li>
<li>Excess weight or central obesity</li>
<li>Headaches, dizziness</li>
<li>Blurred vision or visual changes</li>
<li>Excessive thirst, hunger, urination</li>
<li>Sores on feet or infections that heal slowly</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check vital signs: BP, HR, weight, height (calculate BMI)</li>
<li>Measure random or fasting blood glucose</li>
<li>Ask about lifestyle, symptoms, family history</li>
<li>If symptoms suggest urgent referral (e.g. BP &gt;200/120 or blood glucose &gt;30 mmol/L), refer immediately</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>BMI:
<ul>
<li>Overweight: 25.0–29.9</li>
<li>Obesity: ≥30</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">How to measure BMI and screen for obesity</h2>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">MEASURE BODY MASS INDEX (BMI):</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Using the adult height board, take the client’s height in centimetres</li>
<li>Using the beam balance (weighing scale), take the client’s weight in kilograms</li>
<li>Use the BMI chart or the formula below to compute for the BMI</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)<sup>2</sup></strong> &nbsp;&nbsp;or&nbsp;&nbsp; <strong>= weight (kg) ÷ height (cm) ÷ height (cm) × 10,000</strong></p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">CLASSIFY</h2>
<p>If the client’s BMI is equal to or above 30, the client is obese and is <strong>AT RISK</strong>.</p>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>BP classification:<br />If the client’s average blood pressure is below 120/80 mm Hg, classify as <strong>NOT AT RISK</strong>. Otherwise, refer below.
<ul>
<li>Normal: &lt;120/80</li>
<li>Prehypertension: 120–139/80–89</li>
<li>Stage 1 HTN: 140–159/90–99</li>
<li>Stage 2 HTN: ≥160/≥100</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Blood glucose:
<ul>
<li>FBS ≥6.1 mmol/L or RBS ≥11.1 mmol/L = raised</li>
<li>Confirm DM if FBS &gt;7.0 mmol/L or PPBS &gt;11.1 mmol/L twice</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Obesity</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Counsel on dietary changes, portion control, physical activity</li>
<li>Monitor BMI and waist circumference</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="management-of-hypertension" class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Management of Hypertension</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start treatment within 4 weeks of diagnosis</li>
<li>&lt;65 yrs: HCTZ 12.5–25 mg/day or Enalapril 5–20 mg/day</li>
<li>≥65 yrs: Amlodipine 2.5–10 mg/day or HCTZ</li>
<li>Use combinations as needed (e.g. Enalapril + HCTZ)</li>
<li>Avoid Atenolol in asthma; avoid HCTZ in gout</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="management-of-diabetes" class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Management of Diabetes</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Metformin 500 mg bd with meals (max 1 g tid)</li>
<li>Add Glibenclamide or Glipizide if uncontrolled</li>
<li>If still uncontrolled, add insulin (Isophane 10 U at night)</li>
<li>Monitor fasting BGL daily, titrate as needed</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Supportive Management</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Statins (Simvastatin/Atorvastatin) for Type 2 DM ≥40 yrs or TC ≥8 mmol/L</li>
<li>Aspirin 75–100 mg if CVD risk ≥20% (no contraindications)</li>
<li>Inspect feet every visit; perform microfilament test every 6 months</li>
<li>Eye exam yearly; refer for vision changes</li>
<li>Check urine protein yearly</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="hypoglycaemia" class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Management of hypoglycaemia (emergency)</h2>
<p><em>(pg. 48 of PEN guideline)</em></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hypoglycaemia is blood glucose level of 4 mmol/L or less</li>
<li>The signs and symptoms of hypoglycaemia are:
<ul>
<li>Cold sweat</li>
<li>Tremor</li>
<li>Tingling in the lips or mouth</li>
<li>Hunger</li>
<li>Palpitations</li>
<li>Pounding headache</li>
<li>Feeling faint or hungry</li>
<li>Light headedness</li>
<li>Irritability</li>
<li>Confusion</li>
<li>Decreased or loss of consciousness</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If the patient is unconscious do not administer anything orally. Seek medical attention immediately</li>
<li>If the patient experiences symptoms of hypoglycaemia or a blood glucose level of 4 mmol/L or less and is conscious:
<ul>
<li>Administer quick-acting sugar immediately. Examples of quick-acting sugar include:
<ul>
<li>2 teaspoons of sugar</li>
<li>2 teaspoons of honey or jam</li>
<li>1 glass of fruit juice</li>
<li>½ can of soft drink</li>
<li>6 jelly beans or soft lollies</li>
<li>1 ripe banana</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Recheck blood glucose level in 15 minutes, if available</li>
<li>If blood glucose level is ≤4 mmol/L or the patient is not feeling better, give quick-acting sugar again or give long acting carbohydrate or let the patient have meal if it is due. Example of long acting carbohydrates include:
<ul>
<li>1 piece of fruit</li>
<li>Sandwich</li>
<li>Biscuits</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Let the patient rest</li>
<li>Identify the cause of hypoglycaemia, which may include:
<ul>
<li>Missing a meal, delayed meal or no carbohydrate in previous meal</li>
<li>Physical activity or exercise</li>
<li>Too much oral hypoglycaemic or insulin</li>
<li>Too much intake of alcohol or intake of alcohol on an empty stomach</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise the patient to carry quick-acting sugar with them at all times</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="dka" class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Management of diabetic ketoacidosis (emergency)</h2>
<p><em>(pg. 47 of PEN guideline)</em></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is characterized by ketosis, hyperglycaemia and acidosis. DKA occurs predominantly in Type 1 diabetic patients but can occur in Type 2 diabetes</li>
<li>The common precipitating factors of DKA are omission of insulin, infection or sepsis, drugs such as corticosteroids and recent trauma or acute coronary event</li>
<li>Symptoms include:
<ul>
<li>Vomiting</li>
<li>Abdominal pain</li>
<li>Kussmaul’s breathing (initially rapid and shallow but as acidosis worsens, breathing becomes deep and laboured)</li>
<li>Mental confusion progressing to coma</li>
<li>Dehydration</li>
<li>Ketotic breath</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Patients also notice rapid weight loss which can be a marker for dehydration. It is necessary to test urine for moderate to large ketone bodies</li>
<li>Management should be undertaken urgently, and the most important intervention is <strong>FLUID REPLACEMENT</strong>
<ul>
<li>Administer intravenous normal saline – first litre for 30 minutes, second litre for one hour, third litre for 2 hours and fourth litre for 4 hours</li>
<li>Transfer patient to the hospital once condition is stabilized</li>
<li>In case that an IV access cannot be established, give a short-acting insulin e.g. Actrapid IM at 8 units/hour. Insulin therapy can be started subsequently</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="aspirin-statins" class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Recommended guidelines on anti-platelet drug and lipid-lowering drugs (statins)</h2>
<p><em>(pg. 42–43 of the PEN guideline)</em></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>All individuals with established CVD or CVD risk ≥20% should be given low-dose Aspirin (start at 75 mg – 100 mg) if there are no contraindications (e.g. hypersensitivity to aspirin, bleeding GI ulcers, haemorrhagic diathesis, haemorrhoids, thrombocytopenia, ulcerative colitis etc.)</li>
<li>All individuals with total cholesterol at or above 8 mmol/L (320 mg/dl) should be advised to follow a lipid-lowering diet and given a statin (Simvastatin/Atorvastatin, start at 10 mg – 40 mg, nocte) to lower the risk of cardiovascular disease</li>
<li>All other individuals need to be managed according to the cardiovascular risk (10-year risk of cardiovascular event &lt;5%, 5 to &lt;10%, 10 to &lt;20%, ≥20%)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">8. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<p><strong>Urgent referral to hospital if:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>BP &gt;200/120 mmHg after rest</li>
<li>Blood glucose &gt;30 mmol/L or &gt;20 mmol/L with symptoms</li>
<li>Diabetic foot ulcers, severe infection, or vision loss</li>
<li>New chest pain, stroke/TIA symptoms</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Refer to physician if:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Persistent BP &gt;140/90 in people &lt;40 yrs</li>
<li>Target organ damage or known CVD</li>
<li>Poor glucose control despite oral meds</li>
<li>Newly diagnosed DM with urine ketones 2+</li>
<li>Total cholesterol &gt;8 mmol/L</li>
</ul>
