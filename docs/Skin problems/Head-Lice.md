# Head Lice: (Pediculosis)

Head Lice: (Pediculosis)
The head louse is an obligate human parasite that lives exclusively on scalp hair.
Transmission occurs mostly via direct head-to-head contact. The head louse can
survive up to 4 days on fomites such as brushes, barrettes, and headgear; therefore,
indirect transmission is possible.
Commonly seen in school age children- particularly girls.
1.
Immediate management Not applicable
2.	 Clinical assessment
Direct observation of adult lice and egg capsules (nits) is diagnostic:
•	 Adult lice are small, wingless ectoparasites measuring 1-3 mm long with
elongated bodies and 3 pairs of claw-like legs.
•	 Nits (ovoid eggs about the size of a sesame seed) appear as 0.5- to 1-mm, gray-
white specks that are firmly attached to individual hair shafts, typically located
within a centimeter of the scalp.
Excoriations, erythema, scaling, and/or papular urticaria may be present within the
scalp, behind the ears, and along the neck as a result of a hypersensitivity reaction
to live lice and resulting intense pruritus. Cervical and occipital lymph nodes may
swell because of secondary bacterial infection.
Pyoderma in the scalp or on the posterior neck along with occipital and cervical
lymphadenopathy should raise the suspicion for possible pediculosis infestation.
3.	 Management
Successful treatment requires elimination of all adult lice and eggs. This can be
accomplished via manual removal of nits and lice, such as with a nit comb, or death
of the active lice and nits.
Pharmacological
•	 Permethrin 1% or 5%, apply on head for 8-12hrs then rinse with warm water.
•	 Infected- Give antibiotic- flucloxacillin (according to age and weight) or septrin if
allergies to penicillin.
