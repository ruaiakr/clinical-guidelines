<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Neonatal care – newborn at birth</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Most newborns transition without difficulty, but ~10% need assistance to initiate breathing and &lt;1% need extensive resuscitation.</li>
<li>Anticipate problems if the following are present:
<ul>
<li>Prematurity</li>
<li>Signs of fetal or maternal distress</li>
<li>Severe maternal conditions (e.g. pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, hypertension, diabetes)</li>
<li>Maternal infections</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do not delay resuscitation to determine the 1-minute Apgar – resuscitate immediately when required.</li>
<li>Use the A-B-C-D approach during resuscitation.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>The period immediately after birth is critical for the newborn's transition to extrauterine life. The baby must adapt quickly to breathing independently, maintaining warmth, and establishing circulation. The three major priorities are:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Heat:</strong> Newborns lose heat quickly. Prevent this by drying the baby thoroughly and using skin-to-skin contact or a radiant heater.</li>
<li><strong>Respiration:</strong> If not breathing or only gasping, clear airway and begin resuscitation.</li>
<li><strong>Pulse:</strong> Assess via stethoscope or palpation of the umbilical pulse.</li>
</ul>
<p>Prepare the newborn resuscitation area with suction, oxygen, bag and mask, warm linen, and emergency drugs like adrenaline.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Cyanosis (blue or pale)</li>
<li>Limp or floppy tone</li>
<li>No cry or gasping only</li>
<li>Slow or absent heart rate</li>
<li>Meconium-stained liquor</li>
<li>History of prolonged labour, maternal illness or infection</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Wrap and place baby skin-to-skin with mother if stable</li>
<li>Dry thoroughly with clean towel or lava-lava</li>
<li>If baby or mother is unwell, use radiant heater</li>
<li>Maintain warmth during resuscitation</li>
<li>Prepare resuscitation trolley with:
<ul>
<li>Ambu bag and mask</li>
<li>Sucker</li>
<li>Oxygen</li>
<li>Stethoscope</li>
<li>Cannula and syringes</li>
<li>Cord clamp and Acriflavine</li>
<li>Adrenaline</li>
<li>Dry, clean linen</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Interpretation</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>9–10: Normal – no intervention</li>
<li>5–8: Mild asphyxia – stimulate and give oxygen</li>
<li>&lt;5: Intrapartum asphyxia – bag and mask ventilation</li>
<li>&lt;2: Severe asphyxia – intubation, suction, chest compressions</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="airway">A – Airway</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Suction mouth then nose</li>
<li>Use wide catheter for meconium</li>
<li>Avoid deep suctioning</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="breathing">B – Breathing</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Give oxygen</li>
<li>If not breathing after 30 seconds, start PPV with bag and mask</li>
<li>Ensure snug fit and correct neck position</li>
<li>Reassess if ventilation ineffective</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="circulation">C – Circulation</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check pulse at umbilicus or with stethoscope</li>
<li>If heart rate &lt;60 bpm, begin chest compressions
<ul>
<li>Use two thumbs on sternum</li>
<li>Compress 1/3 chest depth</li>
<li>3 compressions : 1 breath (90:30 per minute)</li>
<li>Reassess every 2 minutes</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="drugs">D – Drugs</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If HR remains &lt;60 bpm:
<ul>
<li>Give Adrenaline 1:10,000 IV</li>
<li>Dose: 0.1–0.3 mL/kg every 2 minutes</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Stop resuscitation when:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Baby is breathing regularly</li>
<li>Heart rate is &gt;100 bpm</li>
<li>Baby can be returned to mother for skin-to-skin and observation</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor breathing, tone, heart rate, temperature</li>
<li>Document all resuscitation steps and Apgar scores</li>
<li>Begin exclusive breastfeeding as early as possible</li>
<li>Provide routine neonatal care:
<ul>
<li>Eye care</li>
<li>Cord care (keep clean and dry, apply Acriflavine if available)</li>
<li>Monitor temperature and look for danger signs</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult Medical Officer immediately if:
<ul>
<li>Baby required PPV, chest compressions or drugs</li>
<li>No improvement after 10 minutes</li>
<li>Ongoing respiratory distress, seizures or poor tone</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Refer to hospital if:
<ul>
<li>Premature or low birth weight</li>
<li>Persistently unstable</li>
<li>Risk factors for sepsis or hypoxic encephalopathy</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
