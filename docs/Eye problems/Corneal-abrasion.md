<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Corneal abrasion - Adult/Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sudden pain + foreign body sensation - even if none is present ±</li>
<li>tearing, photophobia, blurred vision, difficulty opening eye, history of eye trauma</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management Not applicable</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history and examine eyes as per <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Eye-assessment">Eye assessment</a>.</li>
<li>Do vital signs?</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO urgently if:
<ul>
<li>suspected Corneal ulcer.</li>
<li>significant pain, photophobia, ↓ vision or worsening symptoms</li>
<li>wears contact lenses</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>MO may advise urgent evacuation for ophthalmology review ± topical antibiotics</li>
<li>If abrasion &lt; 4 mm + normal vision and resolving symptoms:<sup>3</sup>
<ul>
<li>give chloramphenicol<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>advise most heal in 1–2 days<sup>1-3</sup></li>
<li>do not patch or pad the eye<sup>4</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Offer ibuprofen.<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a>.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> May cause stinging or burning. Discard one month after opening. Can be stored at room temperature once opened. Do not wear contact lenses during treatment</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Ruptured eyeball or penetrating injury</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to be reviewed daily until healed or sooner if symptoms worsen:
<ul>
<li>repeat VA, stain with fluorescein - consult MO if worsening symptoms/no improvement</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise to see MO at next clinic</li>
</ul>

</ul>
