<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acute retention of urine - Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Predominantly affects men. Most common cause is obstruction eg enlarged prostate. Can also be caused from infection, constipation, inflammation or injury<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Inability to urinate or empty bladder effectively ± suprapubic pain, bloating, urgency, distress</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>1-3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1-3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>See <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Gastrointestinal-emergencies-Acute-abdominal-pain">Abdominal pain</a></li>
<li>Also ask about:
<ul>
<li>worsening urinary symptoms eg nocturia, frequency, urgency, weak stream, dribbling</li>
<li>systemic symptoms eg T ≥ 38, chills, sweats</li>
<li>previous episodes, prostate or bladder problems</li>
<li>prolapse of the bladder, rectum or uterus - in females</li>
<li>recent pelvic trauma, STI, IDC</li>
<li>nausea, vomiting, constipation</li>
<li>medications eg anticholinergic, antidepressants</li>
<li>recent weight loss</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>abdomen for distension and palpable bladder</li>
<li>check for swollen lymph nodes</li>
<li>urinalysis if possible + MSU for MCS or collect from IDC if advised to insert</li>
<li>bladder scan if available</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO/Surgeon, who may advise:
<ul>
<li>insert IDC:
<ul>
<li>if likely difficult catheterisation, urgent retrieval for urological review/suprapubic catheter</li>
<li><strong>DO NOT</strong> use force to push IDC through the obstructed urethra</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If it is still can’t and patient is really in distress – consult surgeon: Need temporary urine aspiration or cystostomy ( need guidance from the surgeon)</li>
<li>Collect urine for urine test – dipstick urine test</li>
<li>Start antibiotic – Ampicillin and Gentamycin</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Monitor - urine output, for haematuria, transient hypotension, and post-obstructive diuresis</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not retrieved, always close monitor and update the surgeon concerned.</li>
<li>Advise to see next MO clinic</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
</ul>
