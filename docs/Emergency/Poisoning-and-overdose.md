<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Poisoning and overdose – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul>
<li>Manage all suspected poisoning or overdose urgently</li>
<li>Contact the Medical Officer (MO) early</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul>
<li>Poisoning and overdose can result from accidental or deliberate ingestion, inhalation, injection, or dermal exposure to a wide range of substances. Early recognition and appropriate supportive care are essential. Avoid gastrointestinal decontamination (e.g. activated charcoal) until toxicology assessment is completed</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1-4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Confusion, drowsiness, loss of consciousness, fitting</li>
<li>Respiratory failure</li>
<li>Hyperthermia or hypothermia</li>
<li>Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea</li>
<li>Hypotension, hypertension, bradycardia, tachycardia, arrhythmias</li>
<li>Conscious and fully orientated</li>
<li>History suggestive of deliberate or accidental poisoning or drug taking</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>DRSABCD. Resuscitate if required. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Basic-life-support">BLS</a></li>
<li>Contact MA urgently</li>
<li>Insert IVC × 2 if required</li>
<li>If contamination suspected: remove all clothes; shower with soap and water; use PPE for staff safety</li>
<li>If patient is confused, withdrawn, aggressive, or acutely disturbed, prioritise safety. See <a href="/docs/Mental%20health/Acute-severe-behavioral-disturbance">Mental health emergency</a></li>
<li>Do not undertake gastrointestinal decontamination until full toxicology assessment has been completed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1-4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Perform a full Toxicology assessment – see table below</li>
<li>Monitor and record vital signs: BP, HR, RR, T, SpO<sub>2</sub>, BGL, GCS or AVPU scale</li>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> hyperthermia can be life-threatening</li>
</ul>

</table>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MA urgently</li>
<li>Following stabilisation, provide supportive care and monitoring</li>
<li>Use poison-specific treatments where indicated (see tables below)</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-consult-intro">
<div class="pccm-consult-intro-banner">Toxin Specific Management Considerations</div>
<div class="pccm-consult-intro-body">
<ul>
<li>Extra management considerations after: Contact with Poisons Information Centre (PIC) 13 11 26; MO consultation; Toxicology assessment</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<tr><td><strong>Barbiturates</strong><br />Phenobarbitone, primidone, pentobarbitone, thiopental</td><td><ul><li>CNS depression is common in large ingestion</li><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li></ul></td></tr>

<tr><td><strong>Calcium channel blockers (verapamil or diltiazem)</strong></td><td><ul><li>Consider activated charcoal within 12 hours</li><li>Consider calcium gluconate or calcium chloride for persistent hypotension and bradycardia</li><li>For ingestions &gt; 10 tablets, PIC will discuss other decontamination options</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Calcium channel blockers (others)</strong><br />Amlodipine, felodipine, lercanidipine, nifedipine, nimodipine</td><td><ul><li>Consider activated charcoal within last 4 hours</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Carbon monoxide inhalation</strong></td><td><ul><li>15 L/min O<sub>2</sub> via non-rebreather mask for 6 hours</li><li>Keep at rest to minimise oxygen needs</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Caustic or corrosive substances</strong><br />Domestic and industrial cleaning agents, oven cleaners, dishwasher detergents, acids, drain cleaners</td><td><ul><li>Nil by mouth</li><li>Offer analgesia. <tr><td><strong>Chloroquine, hydroxychloroquine or quinine</strong></td><td><ul><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Fitting">Fitting</a></li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Clonidine</strong></td><td><ul><li>Most ingestions result in prolonged drowsiness</li><li>Continue to monitor and provide symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Colchicine</strong></td><td><ul><li>Give activated charcoal for all ingestions</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Cyanide</strong><br />Inhalation in domestic or industrial fires, ingestion of cyanide-containing products</td><td><ul><li>Strict PPE precautions</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Digoxin</strong></td><td><ul><li>Atropine to treat bradycardia with hypotension</li><li>Consider digoxin immune Fab to bind and remove digoxin and treat hyperkalaemia (may be brought with retrieval team)</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Essential oils</strong><br />Eucalyptus oil, tea tree oil, aromatherapy oils</td><td><ul><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Flecainide</strong></td><td><ul><li>Consider sodium bicarbonate to treat ventricular arrhythmias associated with QRS widening</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB)</strong></td><td><ul><li>Alternating coma and agitation is a common presentation</li><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Fitting">Fitting</a></li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Hallucinogens</strong><br />LSD</td><td><ul><li>Consider benzodiazepines for agitation and acute behavioural disturbances. See <a href="/docs/Mental%20health/Acute-severe-behavioral-disturbance">Mental health emergency</a></li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Herbicide (chlorophenoxy)</strong></td><td><ul><li>Rapid cooling techniques for hyperthermia</li><li>Consider sodium bicarbonate for urinary alkalinisation</li><li>Consider potassium chloride for hypokalaemia</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Herbicide (glyphosate)</strong></td><td><ul><li>Consider sodium bicarbonate for metabolic acidosis</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Herbicide (paraquat)</strong></td><td><ul><li>Strict PPE precautions</li><li>Do not routinely administer O<sub>2</sub></li><li>For decontamination consider: activated charcoal; soil (mix with water); Fuller’s earth (calcium montmorillonite)</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Hydrocarbons</strong><br />Petrol, fuels, oils, aerosols (e.g. chroming, sniffing)</td><td><ul><li>PPE precautions</li><li>Consider salbutamol MDI/NEB for bronchospasm. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Acute-asthma">Asthma</a></li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Hydrofluoric acid</strong></td><td><ul><li>Extremely toxic. Treatment guided by PIC</li><li>For dermal exposure, remove all clothes and wash with soap and water</li><li>Analgesia for pain</li><li>Consider calcium gluconate: topical for dermal; IV for metabolic, cardiovascular, or CNS effects</li><li>Consider midazolam for seizures. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Fitting">Fitting</a></li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Insecticides</strong></td><td><ul><li>See Pesticides</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Iron</strong><br />Ferrous fumarate, gluconate, sulfate</td><td><ul><li>Activated charcoal not indicated</li><li>Desferrioxamine indicated for severe systemic toxicity</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Lead</strong></td><td><ul><li>Evacuate for urgent assessment, decontamination and chelation therapy</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Lithium</strong></td><td><ul><li>Activated charcoal is not indicated</li><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Local anaesthetics</strong><br />Articaine, benzocaine, bupivacaine, cinchocaine, cocaine, levobupivacaine, lignocaine (lidocaine), mepivacaine, oxybuprocaine, prilocaine, propycaine, ropivacaine, tetracaine (amethocaine)</td><td><ul><li>QRS widening signals progression of toxicity</li><li>Treat seizures and ventricular arrhythmias with IV sodium bicarbonate</li><li>Hyperventilate by invasive or non-invasive ventilation</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Metformin</strong></td><td><ul><li>Causes lactic acidosis not hypoglycaemia</li><li>Consider IV sodium bicarbonate for metabolic acidosis</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Methotrexate</strong></td><td><ul><li>Consider calcium folinate as antidotal therapy</li><li>Brought with retrieval team</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Mirtazapine</strong></td><td><ul><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs)</strong><br />Phenelzine, tranylcypromine</td><td><ul><li>Treat hypertension with benzodiazepines as first-line and GTN if hypertension persists</li><li>Cooling techniques for hyperthermia</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Nicotine</strong></td><td><ul><li>Consider atropine for excessive secretions, bradycardia, bronchoconstriction</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>NSAIDs</strong><br />Celecoxib, diclofenac, etoricoxib, ibuprofen, indomethacin, ketoprofen, ketorolac, mefenamic acid, meloxicam, naproxen, piroxicam, sulindac, tiaprofenic acid</td><td><ul><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Opioids</strong><br />Alfentanil, buprenorphine, codeine, dextropropoxyphene, fentanyl, hydromorphone, methadone, morphine, oxycodone, pethidine, remifentanil, sufentanil, tramadol</td><td><ul><li>Potential for significant toxicity, especially in opioid-naive patients and exposures to long-acting preparations</li><li>Give naloxone as antidotal therapy</li><li>Check for and remove opioid transdermal patches</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Pesticides or insecticides (organophosphates)</strong><br />Chlorpyrifos, diazinon, dimethoate, fenthion, malathion</td><td><ul><li>Strict PPE precautions</li><li>For dermal exposures, remove all clothing and shower with soap and water</li><li>Stat doses or infusion of IV atropine to treat bradycardia</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Paracetamol</strong></td><td><ul><li>Give IV acetylcysteine. Brought with retrieval team</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Potassium</strong></td><td><ul><li>Activated charcoal is not indicated</li><li>Consider calcium gluconate for cardiac arrhythmias</li><li>To decrease serum potassium give: IV insulin + glucose; sodium bicarbonate; nebulised salbutamol</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>SSRI</strong><br />Citalopram, dapoxetine, escitalopram, fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, paroxetine, sertraline</td><td><ul><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>SNRI</strong><br />Atomoxetine, desvenlafaxine, duloxetine, reboxetine, venlafaxine</td><td><ul><li>Symptomatic treatment and supportive care</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li><li>Consider benzodiazepines for agitation</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Stimulant drugs</strong><br />Amphetamines, cocaine, dexamphetamine, MDMA (ecstasy), methylphenidate, piperazines, lisdexamfetamine</td><td><ul><li>Active rapid cooling for hyperthermia</li><li>Consider benzodiazepines for stimulant induced hypertension or tachycardia</li><li>Consider benzodiazepines for agitation</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li><li>Consider hypertonic saline for severe hyponatraemia</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Sulfonylurea</strong><br />Glibenclamide, gliclazide, glimepiride, glipizide</td><td><ul><li>Treat hypoglycaemia with glucose. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Hypoglycaemia">Hypoglycaemia</a></li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Superwarfarins (rat and mouse poison)</strong><br />Brodifacoum, bromadiolone, coumatetralyl, difenacoum, difethialone, diphacinone, flocoumafen, pindone</td><td><ul><li>Consider antidotal vitamin K (phytomenadione)</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Theophylline and caffeine</strong></td><td><ul><li>Consider metaraminol or noradrenaline for hypotension unresponsive to IV fluids</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li><li>Consider benzodiazepines for agitation</li><li>Consider potassium chloride to treat hypokalaemia</li><li>Give antiemetic for nausea and vomiting</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Toxic alcohol</strong><br />Methanol, ethylene glycol</td><td><ul><li>Treat with fomepizole or ethanol</li><li>Activated charcoal is not indicated</li><li>If ethanol 10% not available, administer white spirits or sweet alcoholic formulations orally or via nasogastric tube</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs)</strong><br />Amitriptyline, clomipramine, dothiepin, doxepin, imipramine, nortriptyline</td><td><ul><li>Potential for significant toxicity, symptoms develop early</li><li>IV sodium bicarbonate for QRS widening</li><li>Consider midazolam for persistent seizures</li></ul></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Warfarin</strong> (also see Superwarfarins)</td><td><ul><li>Consider antidotal vitamin K (phytomenadione)</li></ul></td></tr>
</table>
</div>

<div class="pccm-drug-header">
<span class="pccm-drug-name">ACTIVATED CHARCOAL</span>
<span class="pccm-drug-level">L3</span>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap" style="margin:0; overflow-x:auto;">
<table class="pccm-drug-table">
<tr><th>FORM</th><th>STRENGTH</th><th>ROUTE</th><th>DOSE</th><th>DURATION</th></tr>
<tr>
<td>Oral liquid</td>
<td>50 g/250 mL</td>
<td>Oral<br />Nasogastric<br />Orogastric</td>
<td><strong>Adult</strong> 50 g<br /><strong>Child</strong> 1 g/kg (max. 50 g)</td>
<td>Stat<br />Repeat doses on MO advice</td>
</tr>
</table>
</div>
<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> May cause colicky abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting or constipation</p>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> Never administer to unconscious, sleepy or dizzy patient unless intubated. Rarely indicated in children unless risk suggests poor outcome. Improve palatability by chilling, serve in a covered container with a straw, drink with eyes shut etc</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Children &lt; 6 years of age with accidental liquid paracetamol ingestion. Strong acids or alkalis, iron sulfate or iron salts, cyanides, sulfonylureas, malathion, lithium, ethanol, methanol, ethylene glycol, hydrocarbons. Bowel obstruction</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO</p>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As advised by MO/MA</li>
<li>Monitor for delayed symptoms or complications depending on the toxin involved</li>
<li>Reassess vital signs, mental status, and clinical progression regularly</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As advised by MO/MA</li>
<li>Refer to tertiary or regional hospital if: prolonged unconsciousness; seizures or cardiac arrhythmias; respiratory failure; requirement for antidotes not available locally</li>
</ul>
</div>
