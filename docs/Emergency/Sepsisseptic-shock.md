<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Sepsis/septic shock - Adult/Child</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sepsis is a medical emergency - early recognition + rapid treatment is imperative for survival. Consider in every patient with fever or acute illness 1,2</li>
<li>Initial presentation can be vague. Always have a high index of suspicion in neonates, young infants, the elderly and immunocompromised patients</li>
<li>If suspected sepsis – initiate treatment  + discuss with MA.</li>
<li>Sepsis = infection + organ dysfunction 1,2</li>
<li>Children can have sepsis with normal BP. Hypotension is a late sign of shock</li>
<li>Neonates + infants &lt; 1 year are at highest risk as immature immune systems are unable to ward off severe infections.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Septic shock = shock + infection. Sepsis = infection + organ dysfunction<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment 1-2</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about recent history of:<ul><li>llness, operations/hospitalisation, postpartum, skin infections</li><li>antimicrobial use within the last 3 months - which one(s), what for</li><li>travel - where to/when</li></ul></li>
<li>Past history -<ul><li>diabetes, immunosuppressive medications, chemotherapy</li></ul></li>
<li>Ask about/look for source of infection, Note: source might be unclear:<ul><li>cough, sputum, breathlessness - pneumonia is the most common cause of sepsis</li><li>listen to chest for:<ul><li>air entry</li><li>crackles or wheeze</li></ul></li><li>dysuria, frequency:<ul><li>do urinalysis + MSU for MCS + pregnancy test if possible</li></ul></li><li>abdominal pain, distension</li><li>listen for bowel sounds - paralytic ileus may be present</li><li>cellulitis, septic arthritis, infected wound, device related infection</li><li>neck stiffness, photophobia, non-blanching rash, new onset confusion, headache, vomiting,</li><li>nausea. See Meningitis</li></ul></li>
<li>Check all skin surfaces for:<ul><li>bruising/bleeding</li><li>rash at pressure points + under clothing. Note: petechiae + purpura do not fade on pressure</li></ul></li>
<li>Check vaccination status</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management 1,2</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ongoing management as per MO</li>
<li>Monitor closely until retrieval, including:<ul><li>continuous - SpO#, RR, HR</li><li>15 minutely - AVPU, capillary re%ll time, BP</li><li>60 minutely - T, strict fluid balance + urine output (consider inserting IDC)</li><li>4 hourly - lactate, VBG, BGL or as per MO</li></ul></li>
<li>If child - in addition to above, reassess for Persistent signs of sepsis after:<ul><li>any intervention</li><li>vital signs changes, and</li><li>every 60 minutes as a minimum</li></ul></li>
<li>Discuss with patient/family, as appropriate eg:<ul><li>explanation of sepsis</li></ul></li>
<li>Patient/parent/carer information sheet (see Sepsis pathway, link in Recommend)<ul><li>family questions</li><li>offer referral to supportive services if available eg social work</li></ul></li>
<li>Further doses of antibiotics may be required in 6–8 hours - contact MO if still waiting for retrieval</li>
</ul>
