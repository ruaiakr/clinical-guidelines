<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management. Primary survey and</h2>
<p>resuscitation DRSCABCD 1,6-9 Danger - eg body fluids, toxins, traffic, perpetrators of crime, &lt; 10 m from fallen powerlines</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If there is immediate threat to a person’s life and rapid movement is needed, make all efforts to limit spinal movement without delaying treatment.10 If you are by yourself and patient needs moving out of danger, you may need to drag them via ankle or arm/shoulder<sup>3</sup> Response Send for help - ideally 1 or more assistants are needed Catastrophic haemorrhage - control before checking airway 1,2,7,8</li>
<li>Do not remove any penetrating/impaled objects eg knife</li>
<li>Use direct firm pressure - with hands + combine pads and crepe bandages. If not working, check pad(s) is over bleeding point/using firmer pressure with 1 or 2 pads over smaller area</li>
<li>If unable to control:<ul><li>use haemostatic dressing – clean wound / compress dressing to apply: use clean dry gause to pack the wound only, cover the wound again with dry gause and use bandage to compress the dressing. Elevate the limp above the level of the heart and closely monitor for signs of ongoing bleeding.<ul><li>If the wound still bleeding, then need to apply a tourniquet</li></ul></li><li>arterial tourniquet (if limb): 6<ul><li>use a tourniquet rubber or a cloth</li><li>apply firmly 5 to 10 cm above the injury/amputation (not over a joint/ wound)</li><li>tighten until bleeding stops » should elevate the limb above the level of the heart » the tourniquet should only applied for only 1 hour to prevent risk of limb ischaemia » Remove the tournique after 1 hour but if need to re-apply re-tighten the tourniquet again after 20 – 30 minutes. » Limb should be always elevated when applying a tourniquet and when re-applying it.</li><li>Consult surgeon for urgent referral</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>Airway and cervical spine (c-spine)<sup>1,3</sup>,4,7,9</li>
<li>Look - signs of airway obstruction eg use of accessory muscles<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Listen - upper airway noises + breath sounds - absent, diminished, noisy snoring, gurgling, stridor)</li>
<li>Open airway - chin lift/jaw thrust ±<ul><li>airway adjuncts eg oro/nasopharyngeal (not if base of skull fracture), LMA</li><li>suction/remove loose foreign bodies -blood, vomit, oedema, loose teeth/ objects</li></ul></li>
<li>Maintain c-spine stabilisation:3,4,9<ul><li>support head in a neutral position</li><li>consider soft collar ± sand bags to support neck</li></ul></li>
<li>If needed, roll a ‘face-down’ non-responsive person onto their back to assess airway<sup>5</sup><ul><li>if excessive bleeding into the airway that suction cannot remove, roll onto side<sup>1</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Check for wounds/injury</li>
<li>If motorbike helmet<sup>5</sup> remove while assistant restricts movement of c-spine<sup>7</sup> Breathing 1,6-9</li>
<li>Fully expose chest. Assess:<ul><li>RR, effort, open wounds, bruising, deformity</li><li>circumferential or airway burn ie singed facial hair, soot in mouth/nose</li><li>use of accessory muscles, symmetrical/paradoxical chest movements</li><li>auscultate for breath sounds/air entry (listen in axilla area)<sup>1</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>If tension pneumothorax - do immediate Needle decompression, with 14 G needle 7,9 Signs of tension pneumothorax - life-threatening</li>
<li>Decreased/absent breath sounds</li>
<li>Hyper-resonance on percussion</li>
<li>↑ HR, ↓ BP, ↑ SOB</li>
<li>Distended neck veins</li>
<li>If open/sucking chest wound - apply occlusive 3 sided dressing /vented chest seal</li>
<li>If any abnormalities, see Chest injuries, for possible causes + immediate management</li>
<li>Give O₂ to maintain SpO<sup>2</sup> ≥ 94%</li>
<li>Start bag-valve mask ventilation if indicated</li>
<li>Circulation + haemorrhage control 7-9<ul><li>Control external haemorrhage by direct pressure/bandaging ± elevate + reassess tourniquet</li><li>application</li><li>HR, central capillary refill time</li><li>Insert IVC x 2 large bore OR Intraosseous</li></ul></li>
<li>If shock (cool, ↑ HR, capillary refill &gt; 2 seconds):7<ul><li>IV fluid bolus (warmed if possible) - sodium chloride 0.9% or Hartmann’s:<ul><li>adult 1 L (250 mL increments, aim to maintain sBP at 90). Child 10–20 mL/ kg</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>try to find source of haemorrhage eg chest, abdomen, pelvis, long bones Disability - basic neurological evaluation 1,6-9</li>
<li>Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive</li>
<li>BGL</li>
<li>Pupils - size, equal + reacting</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment. Secondary survey - EFGHIJ</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start only after lifesaving interventions/management have been initiated</li>
<li>If ongoing bleeding/life-threatening injury - consult MO urgently Exposure, Environment, Early transfer notification 1,7,9</li>
<li>Assess using Criteria for early notification of trauma for interfacility transfer, (inside front cover)</li>
<li>Remove all clothing</li>
<li>Keep warm/prevent hypothermia - cover with blanket/space blanket Full vital signs, eFAST, Family</li>
<li>Vital signs + neurological observations - GCS, motor response in limbs, fontanelle in infant</li>
<li>Consider eFAST if trained/available 1,6,7,9</li>
<li>Communicate with/support family/friends Give pain relief. Get resuscitation adjuncts<sup>5</sup></li>
<li>Lab/bloods - FBC, UE, lactate, troponin, BGL, LFT, group and hold, coagulation studies, blood gases</li>
<li>± blood alcohol/toxin screen 1,9</li>
<li>Monitor vital signs at least 15 minutely. Attach cardiac monitor + ECG</li>
<li>Naso/orogastric tube (consider) - not NG if suspect base of skull fracture 1,9</li>
<li>Oxygen/capnography as indicated</li>
<li>Pain - assess + analgesia eg morphine or fentanyl. 7,9 See Acute pain,<ul><li>if head injury suspected, do not give opioid until discussion with MO History from patient/witnesses 1,7,9</li></ul></li>
<li>Allergies + check for medic alert eg jewellery, key ring, USB stick, shoe tag, anklet, watch, tattoo</li>
<li>Medications + anticoagulation/antiplatelet therapy</li>
<li>Past medical history + bleeding disorder, Tetanus immunisation,</li>
<li>last menstrual period, possible pregnancy</li>
<li>Last meal</li>
<li>Events related to injury:5  Mechanism:<ul><li>blunt - speed of patient/objects, seatbelt use, air bag deployment, impact direction</li><li>damage to car/bike, distance ejected from vehicle, height of fall/body part landed on</li><li>penetrating - type of firearm/distance from, number of shots, type/length of blade</li><li>any loss of consciousness + duration</li><li>amnesia</li><li>alcohol/drug use</li><li>social issues eg domestic and family violence Head to toe assessment- search everywhere for injuries as per table below 1,5,7,9 CHECK FOR - PAIN, DEFORMITIES, BRUISING, SWELLING, LACERATIONS, ABRASIONS, BURNS, IMPALED OBJECT(S) EG KNIFE (DO NOT REMOVE IMPALED OBJECTS) General appearance</li></ul></li>
<li>Position found, posture, guarding/self-protection movements</li>
<li>Odours - alcohol, petrol, chemicals, vomit, urine, faeces Head and face<sup>1,7</sup></li>
<li>Inspect face and scalp for lacerations/bruising/penetrating injury + check:<ul><li>eyes - size, equal + reacting, foreign body/contact lenses, subconjunctival haemorrhage, hyphaema, irregular iris, penetrating injury</li><li>movements - ask to follow your moving finger in all directions</li><li>also see Eye injury</li><li>nose - deformity, CSF leak, bleeding, look in nose - any asymmetry of septum ± bluish/reddish swelling (may be septal haematoma). Also see epistaxis</li><li>mouth - lacerations to gums, lips, tongue, palate. Check if any missing/loose/ broken teeth.' Bite malocclusion - may be Fractured jaw</li><li>ears - bleeding, blood behind eardrum, CSF leak - do NOT pack to stop drainage</li></ul></li>
<li>Bruising behind ears (Tattle sign') or periorbital (raccoon's eyes) = base of skull fracture</li>
<li>Palpate - skull, orbit, nose and jaw, noting:<ul><li>depression(s) in skull, boggy swelling of scalp, bony tenderness, subcutaneous emphysema*, fontanelle in baby<sup>9</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>If ANY trauma to head, see Head injuries</li>
<li>Scalp lacerations can result in major blood lose<sup>7</sup> - close with staples or sutures prior to transfer if able (or tie hair to close) as per Acute wounds CHECK FOR - PAIN, DEFORMITIES, BRUISING, SWELLING, LACERATIONS, ABRASIONS, BURNS, IMPALED OBJECT(S) EG KNIFE (DO NOT REMOVE IMPALED OBJECTS) Neck<sup>1,6</sup>,7</li>
<li>Palpate neck while maintaining in-line immobilisation of the c-spine head and neck immobilised with 2 hands by assistant. Check if:<ul><li>pain, deformity</li><li>bony/laryngeal tenderness or crepitus/step, subcutaneous emphysema#</li><li>veins - distended/flattened</li></ul></li>
<li>Assume c-spine injury until excluded. See NEXUS tool in Spinal injuries Chest<sup>6-9</sup></li>
<li>Inspect + palpate entire chest wall - including clavicles, sternum, ribs, axilla (do NOT spring rib cage). Check:<ul><li>expansion, paradoxical movement, accessory muscle use</li><li>tenderness, crepitus,</li><li>subcutaneous emphysema#</li></ul></li>
<li>Auscultate + percuss:<ul><li>breath sounds/lack of, wheeze, crackles (listen in axilla area)</li><li>heart sounds - murmurs, friction rubs, muffled sounds</li><li>hyper-resonance/dullness</li></ul></li>
<li>If significant chest injury suspected - auscultate BP in both arms and note If difference &gt; 10–15 may indicate aortic injury</li>
<li>If any abnormalities, see Chest injuries</li>
<li>Haemothorax can result in major blood loss Abdomen and flanks 1,6-9</li>
<li>Inspect for lacerations, seat-belt bruising/red marks</li>
<li>Auscultate bowel sounds - present/absent (before palpating)</li>
<li>Palpate - tenderness, guarding, rigidity, rebound tenderness, masses:<ul><li>especially over liver, spleen, kidneys and bladder<sup>1</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Assess repeatedly - injury may not be obvious. See Abdominal injuries Pelvis<sup>1,7</sup></li>
<li>Gently palpate for tenderness. Do NOT spring pelvis</li>
<li>If fracture suspected apply pelvic binder, NOW - even if low suspicion - do not long roll. Once binder in place, only log roll if essential</li>
<li>Fractured pelvis, can result in rapid major blood loss<sup>7</sup> Perineum, genitalia<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Inspect for bruising/bleeding</li>
<li>If bleeding from urethral opening/scrotal haematoma - suspect pelvic fracture<sup>7</sup></li>
<li>Note priapism (persistent erection) - symptom of spinal injury CHECK FOR - PAIN, DEFORMITIES, BRUISING, SWELLING, LACERATIONS, ABRASIONS, BURNS, IMPALED OBJECT(S) EG KNIFE (DO NOT REMOVE IMPALED OBJECTS) Limbs<sup>1,7</sup></li>
<li>Inspect and palpate entire length of limbs:<ul><li>tenderness, deformity, swelling, open fractures</li><li>movement of joints and strength/range of motion (passive and active)</li><li>colour, distal pulses, warmth, movement, sensation, capillary refill</li></ul></li>
<li>Apply splint(s) as per Simple fracture limbs, and do neurovascular observations as needed</li>
<li>If discrepancy in leg length or rotational deformity in leg without obvious fracture, suspect Fractured pelvis</li>
<li>Fractured femur and any open long bone fractures can result in major blood loss<sup>7</sup> Inspect back<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Log roll, if assistance available (may be best achieved at time of transfer) do not roll onto side with injury</li>
<li>Ask patient to squeeze buttocks if awake</li>
<li>Inspect and palpate - back/chest, flanks, buttocks, perineum and posterior thighs:<ul><li>deformity, bruising, swelling, tenderness/deformity/step (along spine)</li><li>subcutaneous emphysema#</li></ul></li>
<li>Auscultate for air entry, abnormal sounds #Subcutaneous emphysema - air trapped under the skin (skin crackles when palpating) Jot it down</li>
<li>Ensure clear, accurate, concise documentation 1,7</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continually reassess primary survey + monitor for signs of airway compromise</li>
<li>Continue c-spine precautions + spinal immobilisation until evacuated:<ul><li>for guidance on moving trauma patient see Spinal injuries.</li></ul></li>
<li>Treat wounds - remove gross contamination/irrigate + cover<ul><li>Wound wash: give local anaesthesia around the wound</li><li>Flush normal saline solution mixed with betadine if available, into the wound</li><li>Remove all debris and foreign materials from the wound</li><li>Use soaked gauze with saline solution and pack in the wound</li><li>Dry gauze over wound dressing</li><li>Never close the wound : concidered dirty / contaminated wound</li></ul></li>
<li>If impaled object eg knife, VERY CAREFULLY pack around with gauze soaked in sodium chloride</li>
<li>0.9% and secure</li>
<li>Splint fractures<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>MO will advise ongoing management, which may include: 1,7,9<ul><li>IV fluids</li><li>tranexamic acid (within 3 hours) if haemorrhage</li><li>antibiotics eg if open fracture,7</li><li>penetrating trauma<sup>1</sup></li><li>monitor vital signs + neurological observations</li><li>keep warm/prevent heat loss</li><li>nil by mouth</li><li>IDC + urinalysis, monitor urine output hourly</li><li>x-ray(s)</li></ul></li>
<li>Give Tetanus immunisation, if indicated</li>
<li>Also consider Electrocution, Compartment syndrome, Burns.</li>
<li>If not evacuated but affected by drugs/alcohol:<ul><li>encourage to stay under observation until nonaffected, or if discharged, into the care of a</li></ul></li>
<li>responsible nonaffected adult</li>
<li>Consider non-accidental injury (if injury/presentation inconsistent with history) eg self inflicted</li>
<li>injury, elder abuse, Domestic and family violence, + consider Child protection. Pregnant woman with trauma 7</li>
<li>Can lose a significant amount of blood before ↑ HR, ↓ BP, signs of shock evident</li>
<li>If &gt; 20 weeks pregnant (uterus at level of umbilicus):<ul><li>lie on left side, or if spinal precautions/lying on back, tilt the right side 15– by placing a wedge under her hip/spinal board</li><li>note: baby can compress the vena cava causing hypotension if lying flat</li></ul></li>
<li>Have high index of suspicion for Abdominal injuries, even with minor trauma</li>
<li>Also see Qld Clinical Guideline Trauma in pregnancy www.health.qld.gov.au/qcg/publications#maternity Amputated body part 7</li>
<li>Stump – open wound need thorough wash with saline and betadine</li>
