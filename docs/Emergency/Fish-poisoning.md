# Fish poisoning (Ciguatera)

Fish poisoning (Ciguatera)
Recommend
•	 Educate patients about the risks of eating reef fish from areas known for
ciguatera contamination.
•	 Check with local authorities to determine which fish in your area present the
highest risk.
•	 Early management includes supportive care and symptomatic treatment for the
neurological effects of poisoning.
•	 Advise avoidance of any further consumption of contaminated fish.
Background
Ciguatera fish poisoning is caused by eating reef fish that have accumulated toxins
produced by marine dinoflagellates. These toxins are commonly found in large reef
fish such as barracuda, grouper, and snapper.
The illness may present with gastrointestinal and neurological symptoms within 1 to
24 hours of consumption.
Suspected case: One or more of nausea, vomiting, or diarrhoea, and neurological
signs within 24 hours of eating reef fish.
Confirmed case: Diagnosis is usually clinical but may be confirmed by detection of
ciguatoxin in the consumed fish using approved testing methods.
Incubation period: Less than 1 hour to 24 hours.
Notify and investigate: One suspected case with classic neurological symptoms
should trigger public health notification and investigation.
