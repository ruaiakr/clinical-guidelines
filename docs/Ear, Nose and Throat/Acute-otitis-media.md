<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acute otitis media (AOM) - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Acute middle ear infection ± perforation</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>A red eardrum alone is not diagnostic of AOM. It can also be caused by crying, fever, URTI</li>
<li>Always consider other causes of illness in a sick child with fever</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Perforation of the eardrum is common, resulting in ear discharge and relief of pain<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1-3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Acute onset of:
<ul>
<li>bulging eardrum (can look red or white/yellow)</li>
<li>fluid in the middle ear OR ear discharge for &lt; 2 weeks</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>± symptoms eg:
<ul>
<li>pain, fever, lethargy</li>
<li>URTI symptoms eg cough, runny nose</li>
<li>pulling/rubbing/holding ear(s)</li>
<li>unsettled/irritable (young child)</li>
<li>infant may present with feeding difficulties</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Advise Ear Hygiene.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>pain - location, onset</li>
<li>discharge from ear - duration<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>previous ear infections - when, how treated</li>
<li>grommets</li>
<li>other symptoms - eg fever, cough, runny nose, rash, nausea/vomiting, diarrhoea</li>
<li>feeding - normal or ↓ intake</li>
<li>times passed urine/number of wet nappies in last 24 hours</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>full examination if unwell child</li>
<li>ears as per <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Ear-assessment">Ear assessment</a>
<ul>
<li>eardrum - look for bulging OR perforation with discharge</li>
<li>it may be difficult to see eardrum if profuse discharge, especially in infants<sup>4</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>nose, throat, chest</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If ear discharge, take swab for MCS</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management<sup>1-4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start Antibiotic: Amoxycillin 25mg/kg (adult 1g) oral BD x 7days,
<ul>
<li>if completed but no changes (still bulging) then can increase to 50mg/kg (adult 2g) BD x 7days,</li>
<li>if no change after 4-7 days, then give Augmentin: child amoxycillin component 45mg/kg/dose up to 1.5g BD. Adult 1500mg + 375mg BD</li>
<li>If still bulging, then consult or refer.</li>
<li>Painkiller: (Panadol/ibuprofen). See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
<li>Ear hygiene.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If any of the following. (Admit, start IV antibiotic and consult).
<ul>
<li>looks sick, lethargic, pale, irritable, not alert or interactive when T reduced</li>
<li>rash, ↑ RR, or respiratory distress</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If antibiotics NOT indicated:<sup>4</sup>
<ul>
<li>reassure AOM will often resolve by itself</li>
<li>advise to be reviewed in 2–3 days or sooner if becomes more unwell</li>
<li>if after 2–3 days there is no improvement, offer antibiotics</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>In all ear cases, advise Ear Hygiene.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Follow up results of MCS. Check if sensitive to current antibiotics (discuss with MO if not)</li>
<li>Check taking antibiotics correctly at each review. Offer support as needed</li>
<li>If unwell advice daily review until improving, or sooner if concerned (consult MO if concerned):
<ul>
<li>then advise review after 7 days + weekly until resolved</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If acute perforation, advise review again at 6 weeks. If still perforated, refer to MO clinic</li>
<li>If eardrum intact, advise review again at 3 months:
<ul>
<li>check for persistent fluid behind the eardrum. If present, see <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Otitis-media-with-effusion">OME</a>.</li>
<li>note: it is common for fluid to be present for about a month after AOM but it is mostly gone by 3 months</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If AOM resolved, but recurrent ie ≥ 3 episodes in prior 6 months OR ≥ 4 episodes in prior year:
<ul>
<li>if ‘child at high risk of complications’ refer to next MO clinic:
<ul>
<li>may need 3–6 months of prophylactic antibiotics</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>review monthly for 3 months:
<ul>
<li>if further episodes of AOM in the 3 month period, arrange ENT referral + hearing assessment</li>
<li>if no further AOM advise parent/carer to return if any concerns</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Continue routine screening as per Ears and hearing checks in the Chronic conditions manual <a href="https://www.health.qld.gov.au/rrcsu/clinical-manuals/chronic-conditions-manual-ccm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.health.qld.gov.au/rrcsu/clinical-manuals/chronic-conditions-manual-ccm</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If there are concerns about child’s hearing, speech development, behaviour or school progress, refer for formal hearing assessment if not done recently.</li>
</ul>
</div>
