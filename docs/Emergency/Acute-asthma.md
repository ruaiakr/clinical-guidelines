<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acute asthma - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul>
<li>Beware of severe asthma in patient with no wheeze and unable to speak</li>
<li>Consider conditions which can present similar to asthma eg inhaled FB, <a href="/docs/Emergency/Diabetic-ketoacidosis">DKA</a>, pneumonia, croup<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Breathlessness, speaking in short sentences</li>
<li>Wheeze/cough</li>
<li>↑WOB, ↓SpO<sub>2</sub></li>
<li>Symptoms continue despite using puffer</li>
<li>Cyanosis, exhaustion, drowsy/collapsed, quiet chest, poor respiratory effort</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider and treat <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a> if asthma like symptoms AND:
<ul>
<li>features of anaphylaxis eg itchy rash, angio-oedema (swelling) OR</li>
<li>history of allergy OR</li>
<li>if suspected or cannot be excluded</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If &lt; 12 months of age - do not treat as asthma. Bronchiolitis, or small floppy airways likely cause of wheeze. Consult MO</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-consult-intro-banner">Step 1 - rapidly assess severity</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-consult-intro">
<div class="pccm-consult-intro-banner">Step 2 - give salbutamol immediately<br />(+ ipratropium if severe/life-threatening)</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<tr class="pccm-table-banner"><td colspan="3">GIVE SALBUTAMOL ± IPRATROPIUM</td></tr>
<tr>
<th>MILD/MODERATE</th>
<th>SEVERE</th>
<th>LIFE-THREATENING</th>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Salbutamol puffer with spacer</td>
<td>Salbutamol + ipratropium puffer with spacer<br />or NEB if unable use puffer</td>
<td>Continuous salbutamol NEB<br />+<br />ADD ipratropium to NEB</td>
</tr>
</table>
</div>

<div class="pccm-note-panel">
<ul>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> NEBs carry high risk of transmitting viral infections - use PPE</li>
<li>Aerosol droplets can spread for several metres and stay airborne for &gt; 30 minutes<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-consult-intro">
<div class="pccm-consult-intro-banner">Step 3</div>
<div class="pccm-consult-intro-body">
<ul>
<li>If unresponsive, cannot inhale bronchodilators OR respiratory arrest imminent:
<ul>
<li>Give IM adrenaline (epinephrine) - same dose as <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a></li>
<li>Repeat 3–5 minutely if needed</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help</li>
<li>Give O<sub>2</sub> if SpO<sub>2</sub> &lt; 92% in adult or &lt; 95% in child:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>target: adult 93–95%, if COPD 88–92%; child &gt; 95%</li>
<li>do not over-oxygenate</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If severe or life-threatening at any time:
<ul>
<li>Call for help</li>
<li>Urgently contact MO</li>
<li>Urgent retrieval</li>
<li>Be prepared for rapid deterioration ± cardiorespiratory arrest</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">Within minutes reassess severity<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>general appearance</li>
<li>LOC, speech</li>
<li>listen to chest, look for use of accessory muscles</li>
<li>vital signs</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<tr class="pccm-table-banner"><td colspan="3">Severity reassessment table</td></tr>
<tr>
<th>MILD/MODERATE</th>
<th>SEVERE</th>
<th>LIFE-THREATENING</th>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>
<strong>ALL of:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Alert, can walk</li>
<li>Can finish a sentence in 1 breath</li>

</ul>
</td>
<td>
<strong>ANY of:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Can only speak a few words in 1 breath</li>
<li>Lethargic</li>
<li>Unable to lie flat due to SOB</li>
<li>Sitting hunched forward</li>
<li>Use of accessory muscles: chest sucks in when breathes in, or neck or intercostal, or tracheal tug or abdominal breathing (subcostal recession)</li>

</ul>
</td>
<td>
<strong>ANY of:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Drowsy or unconscious</li>
<li>Can’t speak</li>
<li>Collapsed or exhausted</li>
<li>Severe respiratory distress or poor respiratory effort</li>
<li>Cyanosis</li>
<li>Soft/absent breath sounds</li>
<li>Silent chest or ↓air entry</li>
<li>Abnormally slow breathing (respiratory exhaustion)</li>
<li>Arrhythmia or bradycardia - may occur just before arrest</li>
<li>SpO<sub>2</sub> &lt; 90%</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
</table>
</div>

<ul>
<li>*Child vital signs as per CEWT</li>
<li>Reduced wheezing alone is an unreliable indicator of improvement. It may mean deterioration</li>
<li>Go to step 4</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get rapid history:
<ul>
<li>reliever puffer taken for this episode - dose, number of doses, time of last dose</li>
<li>current asthma medicine(s) - regular + as needed; type of devices used</li>
<li>if prescribed preventer puffer, are they using it correctly</li>
<li>what triggered this episode, if known eg allergy, hypersensitivity, medicine, URTI</li>
<li>heart or lung disease, COPD</li>
<li>smoker, exposure to second hand smoke</li>
<li>previous ICU/hospital admission(s) for asthma</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continue to manage severe and life-threatening asthma, or mild asthma that is not responding, in collaboration with MO</li>
<li>In all cases - repeatedly assess response to treatment + vital signs - in particular fatigue, LOC, RR, HR, SpO<sub>2</sub></li>
<li>MO may consider retrieval/hospitalisation if any of:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>hypoxia at presentation</li>
<li>abnormal spirometry (if done) 1–2 hours after presentation</li>
<li>respiratory distress, ↑WOB unresolved or unable to lie flat without SOB 1–2 hours after presentation</li>
<li>history of ICU admission for asthma; or (if child) hospitalised in prior 12 months</li>
<li>acute asthma in past 4 weeks, or frequent presentations for acute asthma</li>
<li>high recent use of salbutamol/other beta<sub>2</sub> agonists</li>
<li>patient cannot be monitored well at home or cannot return easily</li>
<li>other risk factors eg poor asthma control, chronic lung disease, cardiovascular disease; if child - confirmed food allergy, incorrect inhaler technique, poor adherence to preventer/asthma action plan, exposure to tobacco smoke/clinically relevant allergens</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">Within 1st hour start corticosteroids - all presentations, even mild</div>
<ul>
<li><strong>Adult and child ≥ 6 years:</strong> oral prednisolone OR oral dexamethasone (child); if oral route not possible, IV hydrocortisone OR IV/IM dexamethasone (child)</li>
<li><strong>Child 1–5 years</strong> - only on MO order + severe wheezing</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">1 hour after starting salbutamol - reassess response to treatment</div>
<ul>
<li>Use assessment table in Step 3</li>
<li>Do spirometry if available, patient able, and staff trained</li>
<li>If adult - check for SOB while lying flat</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">If still not improving</div>
<ul>
<li>If persisting respiratory distress OR ↑WOB OR not able to lie flat without SOB: urgently contact MO; continue salbutamol every 20 minutes; MO may consider add-on treatments eg IV magnesium sulfate; retrieval/hospitalisation</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">If mild/moderate episode, no fever, and SOB/WOB resolves<sup>1</sup></div>
<ul>
<li>Observe for at least 3 hours (or as per MO) after breathing difficulty resolves</li>
<li>if breathing difficulty persists OR returns, contact MO; if no further breathing difficulty patient may return home</li>
<li>Advise to continue usual asthma medicines, including salbutamol puffer 4 hourly as needed - supply remainder of MDI; preventer puffer (as prescribed)</li>
<li>Check patient knows how to recognise asthma symptoms and what to do</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to return the following day for review, or sooner if concerned/needing to use salbutamol more than 4 hourly. If returns earlier consult MO</li>
<li>On review the next day: if wheeze/other symptoms present consult MO; if no wheeze present advise to be reviewed at next MO clinic and again in 2–4 weeks</li>
<li>Patients, relatives and friends of people with asthma should have an Asthma Action Plan + know asthma first aid</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO as above</li>
</ul>
</div>
