Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) ΓÇô
Adult
Recommend
Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP) is an infection of preexisting ascitic fluid in
the absence of any secondary intra-abdominal source (e.g. perforated viscus). SBP
typically occurs in patients with end-stage liver disease. Without a high index of
suspicion and early antibiotic treatment, mortality is high.
Background
SBP is caused by translocation of intestinal bacteria into ascitic fluid, most
commonly Escherichia coli, Klebsiella spp., and streptococci. Diagnosis is clinical
and confirmed by ascitic fluid analysis with a PMN leukocyte count ΓëÑ250 cells/
microlitre. Patients may present with non-specific or no symptoms, so clinical
suspicion is essential.
