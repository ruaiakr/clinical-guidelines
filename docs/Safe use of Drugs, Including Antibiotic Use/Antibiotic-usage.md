<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Antibiotic usage</h1>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Standard guidelines are used in prescribing antibiotics. Antibiotics are chosen on the basis of known or likely organisms. For example, most respiratory and skin infections are caused by gram positive organisms. Penicillin is quite effective against most gram positive organisms. Genitourinary infections are usually caused by gram negative organisms. Amoxicillin and Cotrimoxazole are effective against many gram negative organisms. Some antibiotics are “broad-spectrum,” they are effective against both gram positive and gram negative organisms. Amoxicillin, erythromycin and tetracycline are broad spectrum antibiotics. Before treating with antibiotics, consider the cause of the infection. Viral infections should not be treated with antibiotics.</li>
<li><strong>OVERUSAGE OF ANTIBIOTICS</strong> is very dangerous for a number of reasons. Overusage is costly, can result in dangerous allergic reactions and also results in bacteria becoming resistant to many antibiotics. As bacteria become resistant, certain antibiotics are no longer effective, resulting in possible death of the patient, and eventual costly use of more expensive and more toxic antibiotics.</li>
<li>Antibiotics are also chosen on the basis of safety, effectiveness, few or minimal side effects, and low cost. Many antibiotics, because of possible fetal anomalies are not given in pregnancy; the safest antibiotic in pregnancy is penicillin or amoxycillin. The dose of antibiotic varies according to age, weight, renal and liver function and severity of infection. Serious infections can result in death if too low of an antibiotic dose is given. Antibiotic doses that are too low also can result in bacteria continuing to grow and becoming resistant to the antibiotic. Certain antibiotics have strict dose ranges however, such as Gentamycin, because of toxic side effects.</li>
<li>The route of administration often depends on the severity of the infection. Serious, life-threatening infections require IV antibiotics. When a patient is seriously ill, the body is very busy fighting the infection, blood supply to the gut and muscles may not be sufficient for drug absorption.</li>
<li>Duration of therapy depends on the nature of the infection and the response to therapy. Always explain to patients the importance of taking the full course of antibiotic. Prolonged courses, however, are costly, wasteful and may lead to side effects. Certain infections do require long courses of antibiotics—such as tuberculosis and osteomyelitis.</li>
</ul>
