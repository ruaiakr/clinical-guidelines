Scarlet Fever
Background
Scarlet fever is a bacterial infection caused by Streptococcus pyogenes, which is
the same bacterium that causes strep throat. It primarily affects children and is
characterized by a red, sandpaper-like rash and high fever. While it can be treated
with antibiotics, it requires prompt intervention to prevent complications.
Recommendations
•	 Early diagnosis and antibiotic treatment are key to preventing complications like
rheumatic fever.
•	 Ensure the patient stays hydrated and receives adequate rest.
•	 Isolate the patient to reduce transmission to others, particularly in close-contact
settings like schools.
1.
May present with
•	 High fever (above 38.3°C)
•	 Red, sore throat with white patches
•	 A red rash that feels like sandpaper (often starting on the neck, chest, and back)
•	 "Strawberry tongue" (bright red, swollen tongue with prominent papillae)
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Initiate oral antibiotics (e.g., penicillin) as soon as the diagnosis is confirmed.
•	 Offer antipyretics (e.g., paracetamol) to manage fever.
•	 Ensure proper hydration and nutrition.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Examine the throat for redness and pus and the rash for characteristic features.
•	 Assess for any signs of complications such as septic arthritis or
glomerulonephritis.
•	 Review the patient's immunization and family history to check for other possible
sources of infection.
4.	 Management
•	 Initiate antibiotic therapy promptly and monitor for improvement.
•	 Provide symptomatic care including hydration and pain management.
•	 Educate the patient and caregivers about preventing the spread of infection.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Follow-up after 2-3 days to ensure the patient is responding to antibiotics.
•	 Reassess if symptoms persist or worsen, as this may indicate complications.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to a specialist if complications such as rheumatic fever, glomerulonephritis,
or septic arthritis are suspected.
