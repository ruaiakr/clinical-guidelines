<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Hypertensive emergency - Adult</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>BP ≥ 180/110 ±<ul><li>headache, dizziness, visual disturbances</li><li>SOB, lung crackles, nausea, vomiting</li><li>chest pain</li><li>altered LOC, Stroke, (↓sensation, movement)</li><li>↓ urine output</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>1-3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do pregnancy test if female of reproductive age. If +ve, see Hypertension in pregnancy</li>
<li>If trauma related, urgently contact MO</li>
<li>Do vital signs +<ul><li>check BP on both arms (+ a lower limb if Aortic dissection, suspected)<sup>1</sup></li><li>if automated BP machine, check manually</li></ul></li>
<li>Get rapid history, including:<sup>1</sup><ul><li>allergies</li><li>any chest pain, SOB, headache, visual change, neurological symptoms</li><li>recent drug use eg cocaine, ice/amphetamines</li><li>medicines - for BP, aspirin, anticoagulants, other (are they taken correctly)</li><li>past medical history - prior ↑ BP, heart problems, renal disease, aneurysm</li></ul></li>
<li>Rapidly assess severity and manage as per table (next page) RAPIDLY ASSESS SEVERITY<sup>1,2</sup> SEVERELY ELEVATED BP HYPERTENSIVE URGENCY HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCY</li>
<li> BP ≥ 180/</li>
<li> No symptoms</li>
<li> No signs of acute organ damage<sup>1</sup></li>
<li> BP ≥ 180/</li>
<li> Persistently elevated BP</li>
<li> Minor symptoms eg headache, dizziness</li>
<li> High BP - often ≥ 200/120 but may be lower</li>
<li> PLUS symptoms of acute organ damage eg:<ul><li>SOB/signs of APO</li><li>altered LOC/stroke</li><li>seizures, lethargy, vision changes</li><li>↓ urine output</li><li>chest pain</li><li>aortic dissection</li></ul></li>
<li> Do not usually need immediate treatment</li>
<li> If patient already on BP medicine and has not taken, give usual dose Consult MO who may advise<sup>1</sup></li>
<li> Observe and re-check BP several times over 1–2 hours to see if it settles</li>
<li> Initiating or ↑oral antihypertensive medication</li>
<li> Home with follow up over next 2–3 days to see if BP reduces + review at next MO clinic</li>
<li> Or hospitalisation if risk factors/concerns</li>
<li> If patient already on BP medicine and has not taken, give usual dose Urgently consult MO who may advise<sup>1</sup></li>
<li> If not on BP medicine give oral anti hypertensive eg nifedipine (slow release)<sup>1</sup> or amlodipine<sup>2</sup></li>
<li> Aim for a slow controlled reduction of BP<sup>1</sup></li>
<li> Monitor BP and symptoms for at least 2 hours<sup>1</sup></li>
<li> If sBP reduced (ideally &lt; 160) AND symptoms gone, may consider home with review next day</li>
<li> Or hospitalisation/retrieval if symptoms ongoing, BP difficult to control, or risk factors/ concerns Life-threatening Urgently consult MO who may advise<sup>1</sup></li>
<li> Urgent retrieval</li>
<li> BP 5 minutely</li>
<li> ± medicine as per cause</li>
<li> It may be harmful to ↓BP too rapidly or too much</li>
<li> MO may seek specialist advice for target BP</li>
<li> Insert IVC x 2</li>
<li> Continue BP 5 minutely until stabilises HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCY - ON MO ORDER ONLY<sup>1</sup> BP REDUCTION RATE/ TARGET<sup>1</sup> Acute pulmonary oedema</li>
<li> Subling/IV GTN or patch if IV not available in rural or remote area.<sup>4</sup> See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Acute-pulmonary-oedema">APO</a></li>
<li> Immediate reduction of BP</li>
<li> Aim for sBP &lt; Acute coronary event Stroke</li>
<li> MO may seek specialist advice</li>
<li> Avoid aggressive BP reduction - can worsen ischaemic stroke<sup>1</sup> Aortic dissection</li>
<li> Beta blocker eg esmolol</li>
<li> Subling/IV GTN</li>
<li> Analgesia<sup>1</sup></li>
<li> Immediate reduction of BP and HR</li>
<li> Aim for sBP &lt; 120 and HR &lt; Hypertensive encephalopathy</li>
<li> IV labetalol if available</li>
<li> MO may seek specialist advice</li>
<li> Aim to ↓mean arterial pressure by 20–25%</li>
<li> Immediate reduction of BP Malignant hypertension ie advanced retinopathy</li>
<li>Reduce BP over several hours</li>
<li>Adrenergic crisis eg from stimulant misuse (rare). MO to seek specialist advice<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MO may advise (depending on severity):</li>
<li>ECG + continuous cardiac monitoring</li>
<li>listen to chest for air entry, crackles or wheeze</li>
<li>IVC + bloods,  VBG, troponin, FBC / UECr</li>
<li>urinalysis (protein, blood) + urine microscopy</li>
<li>chest x-ray</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continue to manage according to clinical condition involved, as guided by MO</li>
<li>If acute pulmonary oedema, also see <a href="/docs/Emergency/Acute-pulmonary-oedema">APO</a></li>
<li>If discharged, support patient to take oral antihypertensive medicine(s) correctly</li>
</ul>
