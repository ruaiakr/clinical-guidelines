<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Meningitis - Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Typically presents with acute history of fever, neck stiffness + altered conscious state ±
<ul>
<li>headache, photophobia + nausea or vomiting - often prominent<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>petechial rash - very late sign + indicative of meningococcal sepsis</li>
<li>focal neurological deficit, seizures, shock</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Older children may present with any combination of the above<sup>2</sup> ±
<ul>
<li>upper or lower respiratory tract symptoms, myalgia + abdominal pain</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>&lt; 3 months - bulging fontanelle, high pitched cry, poor feeding, apnoea, seizures, vomiting<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start by positioning the patient to the recovery position to protect and maintain the airway.</li>
<li>If fitting see <a href="/docs/Emergency/Fitting">Fitting</a> for midazolam/diazepam iv/rectal dosing</li>
<li>Consult MO/MA urgently</li>
<li>Get rapid history + manage concurrently</li>
<li>Do vital signs +
<ul>
<li>central capillary refill time</li>
<li>GCS</li>
<li>BGL</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Give O<sub>2</sub> to maintain SpO<sub>2</sub> ≥ 94%</li>
<li>Insert IVC x 2 or Intraosseous, if 2 failed attempts</li>
<li>Correct BGL if needed:
<ul>
<li>give glucose 10% 2 mL/kg - as per MO/MA</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check allergies</li>
<li>MO/MA will order antibiotics - give within 30 minutes - DO NOT DELAY</li>
<li>If child &gt; 3 months + adult, give dexamethasone before or with 1st dose of antibiotic</li>
<li>If not allergic, MO/MA may order:<sup>3,4</sup>
<ul>
<li>Benzylpenicillin + Chloramphenicol (refer to Kiribati Antibiotic Guideline)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>IM route can be used if timely IV/intraosseous access not possible</li>
<li>Start fluid resuscitation as clinically indicated ≤ 30 minutes:<sup>2-4</sup>
<ul>
<li>give sodium chloride 0.9% 20 mL/kg fluid bolus</li>
<li>repeat if needed as per MO/MA</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Arrange urgent evacuation</li>
<li>Consider <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a> as a differential diagnosis</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Note: apparent explanation for fever (eg pharyngitis, UTI or otitis media) does not rule out diagnosis<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>headache, irritability, fever, rash, neck stiffness, lethargy, confusion<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>immunisations - Hib, meningococcal, pneumococcal + check vaccination status</li>
<li>prior use of oral antibiotics (may modify clinical features)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check for risk factors:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>recent contact with a case of bacterial meningitis (especially in family)</li>
<li>recent contact with HSV ‘cold sores’ or confirmed enterovirus infection</li>
<li>recent overseas travel</li>
<li>maternal Group B Strep (GBS) colonisation if &lt; 3 months</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
<li>recent neurosurgical procedure, penetrating head injury, ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt, cochlear implant</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Weight - bare weight if &lt; 2 years</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>listen to chest for air entry - any crackles, wheeze</li>
<li>skin for rashes including at pressure points + under nappies/clothing:
<ul>
<li>petechiae or purpura (do not fade on pressure)</li>
<li>note: if child, rash may not appear until child is rehydrated<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Hydration assessment - adult, or child (see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Gastroenteritisdehydration">Gastroenteritis/dehydration</a>)</li>
<li>ENT</li>
<li>fontanelle in infants - any fullness, bulging</li>
<li>neck stiffness:
<ul>
<li>with patient lying down, put hand behind head + gently raise</li>
<li>note: may not complain of neck stiffness, but with passive or active flexion - cannot touch chin to chest</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>+ve Brudzinski’s sign - reflex flexion of the hip + knee when the neck is passively flexed OR</li>
<li>+ve Kernig’s sign - pain along spinal cord ± resistance to knee extension when hip and knee are flexed to 90°
<ul>
<li>may indicate bacterial meningitis<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Child - careful management of fluid + electrolyte balance is important - discuss with paediatrician as soon as possible</li>
<li>Adult -
<ul>
<li>Monitor closely until evacuation:
<ul>
<li>vital signs + BGL</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>give analgesia + antiemetic as needed</li>
<li>fluid balance</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check timing of further antibiotic doses if evacuation is delayed/prolonged - contact with MO/MA</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="infection-prevention-control">5. Infection and prevention control</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ideally, respiratory isolation is required for meningitis caused by <em>H. influenzae</em>, <em>N. meningitidis</em> for the first 24 hours of therapy. <em>S. pneumoniae</em> does not require respiratory isolation</li>
<li>Chemoprophylaxis for contacts. – notify infectious disease</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">6. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chemoprophylaxis will be required for staff/close contacts of a patient with either meningococcal or Hib meningitis. Unvaccinated contacts of Hib meningitis &lt; 5 years should be immunized as soon as possible. Public Health Unit will advise</li>
<li>Refer for audiology assessment 6–8 weeks after discharge if bacterial meningitis<sup>13</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">7. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>For all suspected or confirmed cases of meningitis or meningococcal disease notify the Public Health Unit within 6 hours</li>
</ul>

<p>A second <strong>Meningitis – Child</strong> block appears later in the Green Book. Content below is unique or differently framed relative to the primary protocol above and is retained for reference.</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Meningitis is a life-threatening infection of the tissues surrounding the brain and spinal cord. Bacterial meningitis is a medical emergency. Rapid diagnosis and treatment improve outcomes, but mortality remains high in low-resource settings.</li>
<li>The most common bacterial causes are <em>Streptococcus pneumoniae</em>, <em>Haemophilus influenzae</em> type B, and <em>Neisseria meningitidis</em>. These are vaccine-preventable. In malnourished or HIV-positive children, gram-negative bacilli (especially <em>Salmonella</em>) and <em>Mycobacterium tuberculosis</em> are more common. Children with sickle cell anaemia are more likely to have <em>Salmonella</em> and <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em> infections.</li>
<li>TB meningitis usually occurs in children under 5 years as a secondary infection after TB elsewhere in the body. HIV infection increases the risk. Consider TB meningitis if the child has weight loss, chronic cough, recurrent fevers, night sweats, or failure to gain weight.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommendations</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Suspected meningitis must be treated urgently. Start antibiotics immediately. Provide supportive care and refer to hospital. In neonates and young infants, signs may be subtle. All suspected cases must be discussed with a doctor.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="supplement-may-present">May present with (supplement)</h3>
<p><strong>In infants and young children:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever, vomiting, poor feeding or refusal to suck</li>
<li>Irritability, lethargy, pallor</li>
<li>Apnoea or breathing difficulty</li>
<li>Temperature instability (fever or hypothermia)</li>
<li>Hypoglycaemia without clear cause</li>
<li>Bulging fontanelle</li>
<li>Seizures</li>
<li>Floppy or poor tone</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>In older children:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever, nausea, vomiting, photophobia</li>
<li>Irritability or confusion</li>
<li>Seizures (often generalised)</li>
<li>Headache, neck stiffness (may be absent)</li>
<li>Focal neurological signs (e.g. facial weakness, visual changes)</li>
<li>Petechiae or purpura (suggestive of meningococcal sepsis)</li>
<li>Signs of raised intracranial pressure (e.g. bulging fontanelle, altered consciousness)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="supplement-immediate">Immediate management (supplement)</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use the ABCD approach
<ul>
<li>A: Maintain airway</li>
<li>B: Provide oxygen if respiratory distress</li>
<li>C: Ensure vascular access and give fluids if needed (2/3 IV maintenance)</li>
<li>D: Manage seizures and check blood glucose</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Treat fever with paracetamol</li>
<li>Arrange immediate referral to hospital</li>
<li>Begin antibiotics without delay</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="supplement-assessment">Clinical assessment (supplement)</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, capillary refill</li>
<li>Assess general condition and consciousness</li>
<li>Check for signs of raised intracranial pressure</li>
<li>Examine fontanelle in infants</li>
<li>Perform a brief screening physical exam</li>
<li>Doctor will assess for focal neurological signs and plan investigations</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="supplement-follow-up">Follow up (supplement)</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor for improvement after starting treatment</li>
<li>If recurrent or chronic: consider HIV or TB and refer for further testing</li>
<li>Ensure urgent referral to hospital for all confirmed or suspected cases</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="supplement-referral">Referral/Consultation (supplement)</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer immediately to hospital</li>
<li>Consult doctor for lumbar puncture and investigations (FBC, blood culture, LP)</li>
<li>Discuss TB testing if weight loss, chronic cough, or failure to thrive</li>
</ul>
