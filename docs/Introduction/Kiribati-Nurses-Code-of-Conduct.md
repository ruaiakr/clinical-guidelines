# Kiribati Nurses Code of Conduct

Kiribati Nurses Code of Conduct
The Nursing Council, under the I-Kiribati Medical Services Act (1996), is the
statutory authority that oversees and governs the practices of nursing in
Kiribati. The fundamental purpose of the ‘Act’ is to protect the health and
safety of the general public.
The implementation of the inaugural ‘Anuan Te Neeti’ (The Code) is a
vital feature of the Act’s commitment to the protection of the public. The
code provides a set of professional standards that nurses must adhere
to in demonstrating their being ‘fit for practice’ and maintaining their
registration.
The Code evolved during the development of the I-Kiribati Nursing
Cultural Model of Care which was informed through extensive consultation
with the public and the profession. This included a rigorous review of
Kiribati nursing consultation documents and an international literature
review of other codes.
Incorporated within the Anuan te Neeti are I-Kiribati cultural values defined
by the Nursing Council as acceptable professional conduct. To ensure public
confidence and trust, nurses are required to uphold an exceptional standard
of behaviour both in their professional and personal lives.
Anuan te Neeti code outlines the professional behaviour expected of
nurses in all practice settings as set by the Nursing Council under sections
47 & 48 of the Kiribati Medical Services Act (1996). The code is written
with the complete understanding that nursing practice is not limited to the
provision of direct clinical care delivery. Nursing practice settings extend
to all areas of nursing. This includes working in a non-clinical capacity
involving patients or members of the public, or any other role where the
integrity of the profession is upheld.
Anuan te Neeti can be utilized by:
•	 Patients or service users, families or public, employers, nurses,
professional organizations, and the statutory body (Nursing Council) as
a guide to evaluate the conduct of nurses.

Failure to uphold these professional standards of behavior, may result in a
disciplinary inquiry. The term ‘patient’ has been incorporated throughout
this document to align accordingly with the Medical Services Act (1996).
Contextually, the term ‘patient’ refers to the individuals, families, groups
and communities who are the recipient of nursing care (1996 Medical
Services Act, I,p.44). While mandatory language such as ‘must’, ‘shall’ and
‘will’ is not sought throughout this code, it is an expectation that nurses
understand the foundational principles of the code and how it impacts on
their professional standing.
Please note: The terms conduct and behaviour are one and the same,
hence have been used inter-changeably throughout this document.

10 principles of nursing including standards are provided in the code.
These characteristics are fundamental to I-Kiribati cultural values and are
in harmony with each other.
The term ‘trust’ has been used deliberately as an entry-point to defining
the 10 principles. This is because the first step to achieving successful
partnerships of any kind, is in the patient trusting that the nurse has their
best interests at heart. This is the Kiribati way:
Principle 1: Kateimatoaon kamanoan kakawakii aoraki
Translation: Maintain patient trust by protecting their dignity.
Principle 2: Kateimatoaen te reitaki ae tamaroa rinanon te makuri;
Translation: Maintain patient trust by working in partnership
Principle 3: Kateimatoaan ao uarokoan makuri ni kuakua rinanon te katei
ao te aroaro.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by providing culturally responsive care
Principle 4: Kateimatoaan ao kamanoan rongorongoia ma tararuakiia
aoraki.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by ensuring privacy and confidentiality
Principle 5: Kateimatoaen ao kanakoroaon te kuakua ae oioi iaoiia ke
mairouii aoraki.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by the provision of patient-centred care
Principle 6: Kateimatoaan te tararua ae manoroai, nakoroai ao n rokoroi.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by providing safe and competent care
Principle 7: Kateimatoaan onimakinan te makuri ni kuakua ao n tararua
iroiiia Neeti.
Translation: Maintain patient trust in the nursing profession
Principle 8: Kateimatoaan onimakinan karinean inaomatan te aoraki.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by providing informed consent
Principle 9: Kateimatoaan karinean te reitaki ae tamaroa imarenaia Neeti
ao raoia ni beku.
Translation: Maintain patient trust through respectful relationships with
colleagues
Principle 10: Kateimatoaen te onimakinaki rinanon kakaraon te koaua, te
eti ao te riai.
Translation: Maintain patient trust by working with integrity and honesty
