<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Omphalitis (Cord Sepsis) (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Omphalitis is a neonatal emergency. Any signs of infection of the umbilicus or surrounding tissue require immediate treatment.</li>
<li>All cases should receive topical antiseptic treatment.</li>
<li>Systemic antibiotics are needed for moderate or severe infection, or if systemic signs are present.</li>
<li>Urgent referral is required if necrotising fasciitis is suspected.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Omphalitis is an infection of the umbilicus and surrounding tissues. It can range from mild local infection to severe systemic illness and necrotising fasciitis. It is a polymicrobial infection involving gram-positive, gram-negative and anaerobic organisms.</li>
<li>Common pathogens include <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em>, <em>Escherichia coli</em>, and anaerobes.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="risk-factors">Risk factors</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Prolonged rupture of membranes &gt; 18 hours</li>
<li>Prolonged labour</li>
<li>Maternal infection</li>
<li>Low birth weight</li>
<li>Birth outside health facility</li>
<li>Unhygienic or cultural cord care (e.g. use of cow dung, butter, talc, palm oil)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<p><strong>Local signs:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Purulent or foul-smelling discharge from the umbilicus</li>
<li>Redness, swelling or tenderness around the stump (peri-umbilical erythema/oedema)</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Systemic signs:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Lethargy or poor tone</li>
<li>Respiratory distress</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Necrotising fasciitis should be suspected if:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Skin discolouration, bruising, tissue necrosis or crepitus</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Differential diagnosis:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Umbilical granuloma</li>
<li>Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC)</li>
<li>Neonatal sepsis — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Neonatal-sepsis">Neonatal sepsis</a></li>
<li>Patent vitello-intestinal duct (faecal drainage)</li>
<li>Patent urachus (urine drainage)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Initiate emergency care if systemic signs present (see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Approach-to-a-sick-neonate">Approach to a sick neonate</a>)</li>
<li>Begin local antiseptic cord treatment immediately</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Local antiseptic treatment (for all cases):</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chlorhexidine digluconate 7.1% dermal gel (delivers 4% chlorhexidine): apply to cord 3 times daily</li>
<li>If unavailable, use 0.9% sodium chloride</li>
<li>Do not use povidone iodine (10%) more than 3 times due to iodine toxicity</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess severity: localised vs systemic infection</li>
<li>Examine umbilicus and surrounding tissue</li>
<li>Check for fever, poor feeding, abnormal tone or respiratory signs</li>
<li>Weigh baby and assess general condition</li>
<li>Record cord care history (what has been applied to the cord)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>

<li>Immediate IV antibiotics and surgical consultation</li>
<li>Antibiotics for 10–14 days minimum</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Reassess daily</li>
<li>Monitor umbilical area for resolution of swelling and discharge</li>
<li>Check for any signs of sepsis or necrosis</li>
<li>Continue antiseptic treatment until stump falls off and area is dry</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer urgently if:
<ul>
<li>Signs of necrotising fasciitis</li>
<li>Systemic signs of sepsis not improving after 24 hours</li>
<li>Recurrent infection</li>
<li>No access to IV antibiotics</li>
<li>Requires surgical intervention</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="prevention">Prevention</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Promote hygienic birth and cord care practices</li>
<li>Educate family to:
<ul>
<li>Keep cord clean and dry</li>
<li>Avoid applying substances such as milk, soil, butter, cow dung</li>
<li>Report redness, discharge, or swelling early</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
