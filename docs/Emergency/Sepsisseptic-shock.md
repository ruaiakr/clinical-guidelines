Sepsis/septic shock - Adult/Child
Recommend
•	 Sepsis is a medical emergency - early recognition + rapid treatment is imperative
for survival. Consider in every patient with fever or acute illness 1,2
•	 Initial presentation can be vague. Always have a high index of suspicion in
neonates, young infants, the elderly and immunocompromised patients
•	 If suspected sepsis – initiate treatment  + discuss with MA.
•	 Sepsis = infection + organ dysfunction 1,2
•	 Children can have sepsis with normal BP. Hypotension is a late sign of shock
•	 Neonates + infants &lt; 1 year are at highest risk as immature immune systems are
unable to ward off severe infections.
Background
•	 Septic shock = shock + infection. Sepsis = infection + organ dysfunction2

Start resuscitation + treatment now - child + adult1,2
•	 Call for help
•	 Consult MO urgently + arrange early retrieval - senior MO to diagnose sepsis
where possible
•	 Maintain SpO2 " 94% (88–92% if COPD). Give O2 if needed
•	 Insert IVC x 2 or Intraosseous, if 2 failed attempts
Give antibiotics within 1 hour - do not delay1,2
•	 Give within 30 minutes if febrile neutropenia or suspected meningococcus or
meningitis
•	 MO will order Empirical antibiotics or target to source of infection if known +

–
check allergies, MRSA risk, history of multidrug-resistant (MDR) organisms

–
if allergic to penicillins, see Sepsis pathway for alternative antibiotics

–
may contact ID physician if shock, *MDR infection, encephalitis, pregnant,
antibiotic contraindications, extremes of weight, immunocompromised,
dialysis, IV drug use, significant underlying conditions + known to be colonised
with antibiotic-resistant organisms
•	 Give antibiotics with shorter infusion times first. If IV/intraosseous failed, give IM
unless contraindicated
