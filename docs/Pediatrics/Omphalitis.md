Omphalitis (Cord Sepsis)
(Kiribati Original Protocol)
Recommend
•	 Omphalitis is a neonatal emergency. Any signs of infection of the umbilicus or
surrounding tissue require immediate treatment.
•	 All cases should receive topical antiseptic treatment.
•	 Systemic antibiotics are needed for moderate or severe infection, or if systemic
signs are present.
•	 Urgent referral is required if necrotising fasciitis is suspected.
Background
•	 Omphalitis is an infection of the umbilicus and surrounding tissues. It can range
from mild local infection to severe systemic illness and necrotising fasciitis. It is
a polymicrobial infection involving gram-positive, gram-negative and anaerobic
organisms.
•	 Common pathogens include Staphylococcus aureus, Escherichia coli, and
anaerobes.
Risk factors
•	 Prolonged rupture of membranes > 18 hours
•	 Prolonged labour
•	 Maternal infection
•	 Low birth weight
•	 Birth outside health facility
•	 Unhygienic or cultural cord care (e.g. use of cow dung, butter, talc, palm oil)
1.
May present with
Local signs:
•	 Purulent or foul-smelling discharge from the umbilicus
•	 Redness, swelling or tenderness around the stump (peri-umbilical erythema/
oedema)
Systemic signs:
•	 Fever
•	 Lethargy or poor tone
•	 Respiratory distress
Necrotising fasciitis should be suspected if:
•	 Skin discolouration, bruising, tissue necrosis or crepitus

Differential diagnosis:
•	 Umbilical granuloma
•	 Necrotising enterocolitis (NEC)
•	 Neonatal sepsis
•	 Patent vitello-intestinal duct (faecal drainage)
•	 Patent urachus (urine drainage)
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Initiate emergency care if systemic signs present (see ‘Approach to Sick
Neonate’)
•	 Begin local antiseptic cord treatment immediately
Local antiseptic treatment (for all cases):
•	 Chlorhexidine digluconate 7.1% dermal gel (delivers 4% chlorhexidine): apply to
cord 3 times daily
•	 If unavailable, use 0.9% sodium chloride
•	 Do not use povidone iodine (10%) more than 3 times due to iodine toxicity
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess severity: localised vs systemic infection
•	 Examine umbilicus and surrounding tissue
•	 Check for fever, poor feeding, abnormal tone or respiratory signs
•	 Weigh baby and assess general condition
•	 Record cord care history (what has been applied to the cord)
4.	 Management
A.	Moderate to severe omphalitis (systemic signs or worsening):
•	 Cloxacillin IV (infused over 60 minutes)

1–7 days

&lt;2 kg: 50 mg/kg every 12 hrs
	›
≥2 kg: 50 mg/kg every 8 hrs

–
8 days to &lt;1 month
	›
&lt;2 kg: 50 mg/kg every 8 hrs
	›
≥2 kg: 50 mg/kg every 6 hrs
•	 Duration: 7 to 10 days depending on clinical response

4.	 Follow up
•	 Reassess daily
•	 Monitor umbilical area for resolution of swelling and discharge
•	 Check for any signs of sepsis or necrosis
•	 Continue antiseptic treatment until stump falls off and area is dry

5.	 Referral / Consultation
•	 Refer urgently if:

–
Signs of necrotising fasciitis

–
Systemic signs of sepsis not improving after 24 hours

–
Recurrent infection

–
No access to IV antibiotics

–
Requires surgical intervention
Prevention
•	 Promote hygienic birth and cord care practices
•	 Educate family to:

–
Keep cord clean and dry

–
Avoid applying substances such as milk, soil, butter, cow dung

–
Report redness, discharge, or swelling early
