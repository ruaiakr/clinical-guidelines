<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Transient ischaemic attack (TIA) and stroke - Adult</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>TIA + stroke are medical emergencies. Urgent evacuation + timing of treatment is critical<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Advise community about FAST stroke recognition message:2<ul><li>Facial weakness, Arm ± leg weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to act fast</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>A stroke occurs when the arteries to the brain become blocked (ischaemic stroke/cerebral infarction) or rupture (haemorrhagic stroke), disrupting blood supply leading to brain damage<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>TIA - transient episode of neurological dysfunction, most are brief eg &lt; 1 hour + resolve in 24hours with no brain infarction 1,3</li>
<li>See Transient ischaemic attack (TIA)/stroke clinical pathway https:// clinicalexcellence.qld.gov.au/resources/clinical-pathways/tia-stroke-clinical- pathways</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Suspect stroke if abrupt onset of ≥ 1 of:<ul><li>asymmetric:<ul><li>face weakness</li><li>arm weakness</li><li>leg weakness</li></ul></li><li>speech disturbance eg slurred speech</li><li>visual field defect/vision loss</li></ul></li>
<li>Note: any abrupt change in neurological function could represent stroke</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>DRSABCD</li>
<li>Do BGL - if &lt; 3.5 contact MO urgently + treat as per MO 1,3</li>
<li>If persistent symptoms for &lt; 9 hours - patient may be thrombolysis candidate - avoid delays<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Get rapid history, including:3<ul><li>symptoms + time of onset. If unknown, the last time the patient was seen to be well</li><li>are symptoms resolving or persisting</li><li>current medicines eg anticoagulant/antiplatelet medications</li><li>any seizures, loss of consciousness or syncope</li></ul></li>
<li>Suspect TIA if symptoms have resolved. Ask about duration:1</li>
<li>&lt; 10 minutes, 10 minutes–1 hour or &gt; 1 hour</li>
<li>Contact MO/MA urgently in all cases of suspected stroke/TIA + urgent evacuation</li>
<li>Do vital signs + GCS</li>
<li>Only give O<sup>2</sup> if SpO<sup>2</sup> &lt; 92% 1,4</li>
<li>ECG only if does not cause delay. If new atrial fibrillation, send copy of ECG with patient</li>
<li>Continuous cardiac monitoring</li>
<li>Insert IVC x 2</li>
<li>Take bloods - FBC, UE, LFT, coagulation studies, group and hold for x-match</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get past history + any TIA/stroke or similar symptoms<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Do physical examination + weight (if possible)<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ongoing management as per MO</li>
<li>Brain imaging eg CT scan equired to determine cause<sup>1-3</sup></li>
<li>Check ability to swallow before giving oral medications:<ul><li>if difficulty swallowing - keep nil by mouth</li></ul></li>
<li>Give paracetamol if fever</li>
<li>If ischaemic stroke:1<ul><li>cautious ↓ BP if markedly raised eg &gt; 220/110. Note: most instances resolve spontaneously</li><li>do not give aspirin until brain imaging excludes intracranial haemorrhage</li><li>monitor BGL - avoid aggressive management/IV fluids with glucose</li></ul></li>
<li>If haemorrhagic stroke:1<ul><li>urgent reduction of BP may be necessary to ↓ haematoma expansion</li></ul></li>
<li>If TIA - management is similar to ischaemic stroke<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Monitor closely until evacuation:<ul><li>vital signs + GCS</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As per MO</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">

</ul>
