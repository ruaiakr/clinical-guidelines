<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Bowel obstruction (large and small bowel)</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Intestinal obstruction is an important cause of acute abdomen and accounts for approximately 10% of emergency surgical admissions. It occurs when the normal flow of intestinal contents is interrupted, either partially or completely. Obstruction can occur in the small or large bowel and may be classified in several ways:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Permanent or intermittent</li>
<li>Complete or partial</li>
<li>Acute or chronic</li>
<li>Intrinsic or extrinsic</li>
<li>Mechanical or functional</li>
</ul>
<p>Mortality varies depending on the cause and associated complications. Mortality is 100% in untreated strangulated obstructions. Morbidity and mortality are significantly increased when diagnosis is delayed or management is inappropriate. Common causes include:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Volvulus</li>
<li>Strangulated hernias</li>
<li>Faecal impaction (e.g. Hirschsprung’s disease)</li>
<li>Neoplastic causes (e.g. bowel cancer)</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>All patients with suspected bowel obstruction must be admitted for close monitoring and stabilisation</li>
<li>Initiate IV fluids, insert a nasogastric tube for decompression, commence broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, and arrange urgent referral to the main hospital for definitive investigation and management</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Abdominal pain</li>
<li>Abdominal distension or bloating</li>
<li>Nausea and vomiting</li>
<li>Constipation or obstipation (no passage of faeces or flatus)</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Admit patient</li>
<li>Monitor vital signs regularly</li>
<li>Keep nil by mouth (NBM)</li>
<li>Insert nasogastric tube (NGT) for decompression and free drainage</li>
<li>Begin IV fluid resuscitation as per weight</li>
<li>Start IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (e.g. Ampicillin + Metronidazole)</li>
<li>Organise urgent referral to main hospital for surgical review and further management</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<p><strong>General appearance</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Patient may or may not show signs of distress</li>
<li>Blood pressure and pulse may be elevated</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Abdominal examination</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Abdominal distension</li>
<li>Visible or obvious bowel peristalsis</li>
<li>Tense and tender abdomen</li>
<li>Bowel sounds:<ul><li>High-pitched or “tinkling” in early obstruction</li><li>Reduced or absent in late obstruction or paralytic ileus</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ampicillin IV — adult 1 g every 6 hours until referral (adjust dose in renal impairment)</li>
<li>Metronidazole (Flagyl) IV — adult 500 mg every 8 hours until referral (avoid alcohol; caution in liver dysfunction)</li>
<li>Sodium chloride / RL IV — based on body weight and hydration until stable; monitor urine output, BP, and fluid balance</li>
<li>Paracetamol oral — adult 500–1000 mg every 6 hours as needed (only if not vomiting; max 4 g/day)</li>
<li>Do not give any oral medications (except minimal analgesia) until obstruction is resolved or ruled out</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor NG tube drainage and reassess regularly</li>
<li>Continue IV fluids and electrolyte replacement as needed</li>
<li>Observe for signs of deterioration (fever, tachycardia, worsening tenderness, guarding)</li>
<li>Ensure patient is stable for transfer</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral / consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Urgent referral to the main hospital for further imaging and surgical assessment</li>
<li>If signs of strangulation or worsening clinical status, escalate immediately to surgical team</li>
<li>Document and hand over vital signs, resuscitation measures, and medication given prior to transfer</li>
</ul>
</div>
