<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Adult presentation</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">History and physical examination - adult</p>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul>
<li>If child, see History and physical examination - child</li>
<li>Always think could it be <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a>, if signs of infection or history/evidence of fever or hypothermia</li>
<li>For specific presentations, see:
<ul>
<li><a href="/docs/Emergency/Traumatic-injuries">Traumatic injuries</a></li>
<li><a href="/docs/Emergency/Chest-pain-assessment">Chest pain assessment</a></li>
<li>Mental health emergency</li>
<li>STI/BBV assessment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Ensure a culturally safe environment. As appropriate, be guided by local health workers</li>
<li>Document your findings clearly, concisely and in logical sequence - use this section to assist</li>
<li>Offer opportunistic health promotion, screening + brief intervention as appropriate.<sup>1,2</sup> See Chronic Conditions Manual</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul>
<li>The history is the most powerful tool for identifying the likely diagnosis in most cases<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Types of history:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li><strong>emergency</strong> - urgent, rapid collection of crucial information compiled concurrently with life-threatening measures. Take a comprehensive history once patient is stabilised</li>
<li><strong>focused/problem centred</strong> - shorter and specific to the patient's current presenting concern</li>
<li><strong>complete</strong> - comprehensive history of the patient's past and present health status. Usually done at initial visit in a non-emergency situation</li>
<li><strong>follow up</strong> - evaluation of problem from preceding visit</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

</div><div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">Step 1: Obtain history of the presenting concern/problem<sup>1,6</sup></div>
<ul>
<li>Taking the history is the first step in making a diagnosis</li>
<li>Use the history to guide the physical examination/further investigations</li>
<li>An accurately acquired history will usually suggest the diagnosis. The physical examination and investigations should be used to confirm your diagnostic impression</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<tr class="pccm-table-banner"><td colspan="2">HISTORY OF THE PRESENTING CONCERN/PROBLEM</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Presenting concern/ problem</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Ask what the problem is</li>
<li>Use open ended questioning</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">For each symptom ask about (as relevant)</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Ask about length of illness and details of each symptom(s). Use SOCRATES mnemonic</li>
<li><strong>Site</strong> - where is the symptom - localised or diffuse</li>
<li><strong>Onset</strong>:
<ul>
<li>gradual, rapid or sudden</li>
<li>continuous or intermittent</li>
<li>what were they doing when it started</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Character</strong> eg if pain:
<ul>
<li>sharp, dull, burning, stabbing, cramp like, crushing, tingling</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Radiation</strong> - (if localised) does it travel elsewhere</li>
<li><strong>Alleviating factors</strong> - does anything make it better eg sitting up, medicine(s), analgesia</li>
<li><strong>Timing</strong>:
<ul>
<li>when did it first begin, duration</li>
<li>have they had it before</li>
<li>any increase in severity</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Exacerbating factors</strong> - does anything make it worse eg movement, exercise</li>
<li><strong>Severity</strong>:
<ul>
<li>does it interfere with sleep or normal activities</li>
<li>if pain - mild, moderate or severe OR severity on scale of 1–10</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Associated/other symptoms</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Ask specifically about <strong>fever/rigors, pain, shortness of breath (SOB), diarrhoea, weight loss</strong></li>
<li>Eg nausea, vomiting, photophobia, headache, appetite, urine, bowels, energy</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Treatment ± medicine(s) taken during this illness</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>What, how much, when, how often, effectiveness</li>
</ul></td></tr>
</table>
</div>
<div class="pccm-note-panel">
<ul>
<li>Ask if there are any other concerns</li>
<li>Consider possible differential diagnosis</li>
<li>Subsequently use closed ended questions to confirm or refute your differential diagnoses</li>
</ul>
</div><div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">Step 2: Ask about past history<sup>1,6</sup></div>
<ul>
<li>Review and update past history in medical record each visit.</li>
<li>Consider past history that may assist with differential diagnosis this visit</li>
<li>Always ask about allergies and medicines</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<tr class="pccm-table-banner"><td colspan="2">PAST HISTORY</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Past medical and surgical history</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Significant illnesses in the past</li>
<li>Ask about - diabetes, hypertension, angina and myocardial infarcts, epilepsy, asthma, mood or mental health problems</li>
<li>Previous hospital admissions, operations or injuries - where, when, why</li>
<li>Is patient immunocompromised eg:
<ul>
<li>diabetes, chronic kidney disease or hepatitis, alcoholism, no spleen, lupus, multiple sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, HIV, Down syndrome, corticosteroids, chemotherapy, immunosuppressants<sup>7</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Family history</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Health problems in siblings and parents eg diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, stroke, epilepsy, asthma, cancer, mental health</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Social history</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Usual GP + contact details if available</li>
<li>Job, living conditions, who else lives at home and what responsibilities do they have in the family. Means of communication for contact/follow up eg phone</li>
<li>Who their partner is or if they are in a relationship</li>
<li>Gender identification + pronouns eg her/him</li>
<li>Smoking/ever smoked - how many a day, ever tried giving up; vapes</li>
<li>Alcohol - what, how much/often. Express in standard drinks/day or week</li>
<li>Recreational drugs</li>
<li>Recent overseas travel (if infectious disease possible) - where/when</li>
<li>Diet, exercise</li>
<li>Risk factors + indicators for Domestic and family violence</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Medicines Also see Best possible medication history</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Regular and prn medicines - prescribed/over-the-counter, complementary, alternative, bush medicines, vitamins, oral/other contraceptive (females):</li>
<li>generic name, dose, route, frequency</li>
<li>explore adherence and any barriers to taking medicines as prescribed</li>
<li>recently changed/course completed</li>
<li>If patient has medicines with them, check against medical record</li>
</ul></td></tr>
</table>

<li>Allergies/reactions + type of reaction (anaphylaxis, skin reaction, other) to:</li>
<li>medicines</li>
<li>other eg honey bee stings, sticking plaster, food</li>

<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Opportunistic health checks (offer or refer as appropriate)</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>

</div><div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">Step 3: Do physical examination<sup>1,6</sup></div>
<ul>
<li>Most information will be gained from history taking. Use this information to guide examination</li>
<li><strong>In an adult who is not sick</strong>:
<ul>
<li>examine the relevant system first</li>
<li>proceed to further examination if required. Be guided by your findings</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>In a sick adult</strong>:
<ul>
<li>examine the relevant system first followed by ALL other systems</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Use a systematic approach to physical examination</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<tr class="pccm-table-banner"><td colspan="2">PHYSICAL EXAMINATION - ADULT</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Vital signs</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>RR, HR, BP, T, SpO2</li>
<li>Conscious state - GCS/AVPU</li>
<li>If indicated:
<ul>
<li>BGL</li>
<li>capillary refill time (≤ 2 seconds)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Beware of abdominal vital signs, closely monitor and consult for help from MA, MO.</strong></li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">General appearance</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Do they look sick/not sick</li>
<li>Observe:
<ul>
<li>posture, mobility</li>
<li>any ↑work of breathing (WOB), breathlessness</li>
<li>conjunctiva and nail beds - are they pale</li>
<li>lips, tongue and fingers - are they blue</li>
<li>general skin colour - pale/jaundiced</li>
<li>agitation, distressed</li>
<li>sweating</li>
<li>are they well nourished</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Weight ± height, BMI and waist measurement</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Hydration</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Eyes - normal or sunken</li>
<li>Mouth and tongue - wet or dry</li>
<li>Skin turgor - normal or reduced. Pinch skin - normal skin returns immediately on release (normal can be reduced in elderly)</li>
<li>Dry axillae</li>
<li>Recent weight loss/gain</li>
<li>Also see Hydration assessment - adult</li>
</ul></td></tr>
</table>

<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<tr class="pccm-table-banner"><td colspan="2">PHYSICAL EXAMINATION - ADULT</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Skin</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Check the whole body in a sick patient:
<ul>
<li>consider removing clothing to underwear</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Look for:
<ul>
<li>rashes - vesicular, macular, papular, petechiae, purpura</li>
<li>signs of infection - redness, swelling, tenderness, warmth</li>
<li>bruising, unexplained or unusual marks</li>
<li>general pigmentation - areas where skin is lighter or darker</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Any skin lesions or sores:
<ul>
<li>colour, shape, size, location, distribution on body</li>
<li>non-blanching, erythema/redness</li>
<li>vesicles present</li>
<li>exudate eg clear, pus, bloody, malodorous</li>
<li>any family members/close contacts with similar lesions</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Palpate/feel skin:
<ul>
<li>temperature, dryness/moisture, clamminess</li>
<li>any tender/enlarged lymph nodes in the neck, axillae or groin</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>See Rash as needed</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Cardiovascular system</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Also see Chest pain assessment</li>
<li>Any:
<ul>
<li>pain/pressure in neck, chest, arms, or radiating pain</li>
<li>SOB on exertion</li>
<li>evidence of oedema, particularly feet, hands, face or sacrum</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check skin:
<ul>
<li>colour - pink, white, grey, mottling. Compare trunk with limbs</li>
<li>temperature - hot, warm, cool or cold. Compare trunk with limbs</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Central perfusion - blanch skin over the sternum with your thumb for 5 seconds. Time how long it takes the colour to return in seconds</li>
<li>Peripheral perfusion - blanch the skin on a finger or toe for 5 seconds. Time how long it takes for the colour to return in seconds</li>
<li>Look for distended neck veins</li>
<li>If skilled listen to heart sounds - any murmur</li>
</ul></td></tr>
</table>
</div><div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap">
<table class="pccm-assessment-table">
<tr class="pccm-table-banner"><td colspan="2">PHYSICAL EXAMINATION - ADULT</td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Respiratory system</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Most information is gained from simple observation</li>
<li>Inspect anterior/posterior chest - equal chest expansion, abnormal chest movement, ↑WOB, use of accessory muscles, tracheal tug</li>
<li>Can they talk in full sentences, single words or unable to talk at all</li>
<li>Measure RR over 1 minute - note rhythm, depth and effort of breathing</li>
<li>Listen for extra noises - cough (loose, dry, muffled ± sputum), wheeze, stridor, hoarseness</li>
<li>Auscultate for air entry into both lung fields:
<ul>
<li>equal, adequate</li>
<li>any wheeze or crackles - on inspiration or expiration</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Percuss lung fields - dull, resonant, hyper-resonant</li>
<li>Can they lie flat without breathlessness</li>
</ul></td></tr>
<tr><td class="pccm-row-label">Gastrointestinal/ reproductive system Consider cultural safety/sensitivity</td><td class="pccm-row-content"><ul>
<li>Inspect abdomen for scars, distension, hernias, bruising, masses</li>
<li>Auscultate bowel sounds in all 4 quadrants - present or absent</li>
<li>Palpate abdomen:
<ul>
<li>soft or firm</li>
<li>obvious masses</li>
<li>tender to touch. Identify abdominal quadrant and exact area</li>
<li>guarding or rigidity even when the patient is relaxed</li>
<li>rebound tenderness - press down and take your hand away very quickly, is pain greater when you do this</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Change of bowel habits</li>
<li>Ask women:
<ul>
<li>date of last normal menstrual period</li>
<li>abnormal vaginal bleeding or discharge, itch</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do pregnancy test if reproductive age with abdominal pain</li>
<li>In men:
<ul>
<li>if relevant check the testes - any redness, swelling, tenderness, itch, lesions</li>
<li>ask about penile discharge</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>See Abdominal examination for detailed assessment</li>
</ul></td></tr>
</table>

<li>eye movements</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>See Eye assessment for detailed assessment</li>
</ul></td></tr>
</table>
