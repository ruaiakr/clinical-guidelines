<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Gastrointestinal emergencies<br />Acute abdominal pain - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always consider:
<ul>
<li>Ectopic pregnancy, in all females of reproductive age with abdominal pain ± bleeding<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>PID, in sexually active females, particularly if &lt; 30 years, with new onset of pelvic pain<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Testicular torsion, and check testes in males with abdominal pain + male infants with inconsolable crying<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If child, also see QPEC guidelines Acute abdominal pain - emergency management in children</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Abdominal pain</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>If shock - pale, sweaty, ↑HR, capillary refill &gt; 2 seconds ± ↓LOC:
<ul>
<li>contact MO urgently</li>
<li>start treatment for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a></li>
<li>Screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
<li>If severe pain:
<ul>
<li>insert IVC x 2, do rapid assessment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Offer analgesia ± antiemetic. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a>. <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Nausea-and-vomiting">Nausea and vomiting</a>
<ul>
<li>note: analgesia does not mask physical signs/hinder diagnosis<sup>3-5</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>It is not necessary to make a definitive diagnosis. It is more important to recognise cases which are significant</li>
<li>If child, also see Child with abdominal pain</li>
<li>Ask about the pain:<sup>4</sup>
<ul>
<li>Site - where is it, ask patient to pinpoint location</li>
<li>Onset:
<ul>
<li>gradual, rapid or sudden</li>
<li>continuous or intermittent</li>
<li>what were they doing when it started</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Character - sharp, dull, burning, stabbing, cramp like, crushing, tingling</li>
<li>Radiate - anywhere eg shoulder tip, back</li>
<li>Alleviating factors eg sitting up, medicine, relief by moving about eg renal colic, or from lying very still eg peritonitis</li>
<li>Timing:
<ul>
<li>when did it first begin, duration</li>
<li>have they had it before, when, what happened</li>
<li>any increase in severity</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Exacerbating factors - does anything make it worse eg movement</li>
<li>Severity - mild, moderate or severe (scale 1–10):
<ul>
<li>does it interfere with sleep or normal activities</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Ask about:<sup>4</sup>
<ul>
<li>associated symptoms eg fever, SOB, headache, nausea, vomiting - any blood, bile or green vomit - does it look/smell like faeces</li>
<li>recent weight loss, changes in appetite, food refusal/poor feeding<sup>6</sup></li>
<li>bowels - any diarrhoea, constipation, blood, black stools/melaena, time of last motion, passing wind<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>urine - dysuria, frequency, urgency, haematuria<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>vaginal - bleeding, discharge. Note: abnormal discharge may indicate PID</li>
<li>possible causes of pain, including:
<ul>
<li>foreign body ingestion + consider <a href="/docs/Emergency/Button-battery-ingestioninsertion">Button battery</a><sup>6</sup></li>
<li>poisoning eg venoms, paracetamol, lead, iron<sup>5</sup></li>
<li>recent trauma eg abdominal, scrotal<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Get past history, including:<sup>7,8</sup>
<ul>
<li>medical + surgical</li>
<li>smoking, alcohol + other drugs</li>
<li>medications + family</li>
<li>menstrual - last period, was it normal, are periods regular, any contraception<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Do physical examination, including:</strong></p>
<p><strong>Red flags from abdominal examination - if any contact MO urgently</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Pain radiating to the back + palpable pulsatile mass - could be AAA<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>Localised tenderness + distension</li>
<li>Guarding -↑muscle tightness in response to palpation or board like rigidity</li>
<li>Rebound tenderness - pain when pressure is applied + then released suddenly</li>
<li>Pain disproportionate to presentation/working diagnosis</li>
<li>Abdominal examination:<sup>3-5,8</sup>
<ul>
<li>look for:
<ul>
<li>symmetry, shape eg distended or sunken</li>
<li>surgical scars, bruises, distended veins, hernias</li>
<li>signs of non-accidental injury</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>listen for bowel sounds - absent, decreased or hyperactive:
<ul>
<li>normal are low pitched + gurgling 2–5/minute. Hyperactive/high pitched suggests Bowel obstruction</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>palpate - start lightly, away from site of pain + move through the 9 areas. Follow with deep palpation:
<ul>
<li>palpate kidneys - with 1 hand underneath + 1 hand above (below rib line), gently push down</li>
<li>check testes/groin in males<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>percuss (tap) all 4 quadrants to check for dullness</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Listen to chest - any wheeze, crackles. Note: pneumonia + ACS, can present with abdominal pain</li>
<li>ECG</li>
<li>Urinalysis + pregnancy test if reproductive age - if +ve go to Ectopic pregnancy</li>
<li>BGL - if &gt; 10 check for ketones + consider <a href="/docs/Emergency/Diabetic-ketoacidosis">DKA</a></li>
<li>Bloods/i-STAT including lactate, bladder scan if available</li>
<li>Any jaundice, enlarged lymph nodes</li>
<li>Hydration assessment - adult, or child</li>
<li>Rectal examination - may be indicated if suspected <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Upper-gastrointestinal-bleeding">Upper GI bleeding</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>8</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO in all cases if:
<ul>
<li>infant/child, pregnant woman or &gt; 60 years</li>
<li>moderate to severe pain</li>
<li>cause of pain not known</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>MO may order:
<ul>
<li>chest + abdominal x-ray</li>
<li>urgent retrieval/hospitalisation</li>
<li>antibiotics, IDC, NGT, nil by mouth</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up<sup>4,5</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not retrieved, advise patient/carer to be reviewed next day, or sooner if:
<ul>
<li>pain persists &gt; 24 hours, worsens or changes from generalised to localised, or</li>
<li>worsening symptoms or new symptoms develop eg vomiting, fever</li>
<li>if any of the above, contact MO</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
</ul>
