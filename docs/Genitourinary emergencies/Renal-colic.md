<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Renal colic - Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Pain can be caused by kidney stone passage or an obstruction in the urinary tract</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>2,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Severe 1 sided flank pain radiating to the groin:
<ul>
<li>usually episodic (colicky), typically lasting 20–60 minutes + may not completely resolve before next wave<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>very distressed and unable to find a comfortable position</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Nausea + vomiting, sweating, looks unwell</li>
<li>± haematuria</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>5</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Give oral ibuprofen + paracetamol or ± antiemetic. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a>, <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Nausea-and-vomiting">Nausea and vomiting</a></li>
<li>If severe pain, may need IV morphine or diclofenac injection with buscopan until above works</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>5</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>See <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Gastrointestinal-emergencies-Acute-abdominal-pain">Abdominal pain</a></li>
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>nausea + vomiting, fever</li>
<li>urinary symptoms eg dysuria, frequency, urgency</li>
<li>past history of kidney stones, previous episodes, renal impairment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>Hydration assessment - adult</li>
<li>urinalysis to confirm haematuria + MSU for MCS if nitrites or leucocytes. <strong>Note:</strong> no haematuria does not exclude renal colic</li>
<li>pregnancy test if reproductive age</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>5</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Contact MO urgently if:</strong>
<ul>
<li>a pulsatile abdominal mass and ↓BP - a ruptured AAA can mimic renal colic</li>
<li>fever, pyuria (eg cloudy urine) or significant tenderness - suggests infected obstructed kidney</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Main aim of treatment is analgesia + IV fluids if significantly dehydrated and to start IV antibiotic (check antibiotic guideline for <a href="/docs/Genitourinary%20emergencies/Urinary-Tract-Infection">urinary tract infection</a>). <strong>Note:</strong> most stones pass spontaneously &lt; 1 month<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Consult MO, who may advise:
<ul>
<li>retrieval</li>
<li>IV fluids, bloods/i-STAT - check kidney function/not going into renal failure</li>
<li><strong>or</strong> consider sending home if:
<ul>
<li>afebrile, passing urine, able to tolerate oral fluids</li>
<li>adequate pain relief with oral analgesia</li>
<li>and with follow up at next MO clinic</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If pain persist and patient is showing signs of Obstructed uropathy – need urgent referral
<ul>
<li>and with follow up at next MA clinic</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not retrieved, advise to:
<ul>
<li>be reviewed if develops fever, vomiting or pain returns - contact MO</li>
<li>see MO at next clinic ± urology referral</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
</ul>
