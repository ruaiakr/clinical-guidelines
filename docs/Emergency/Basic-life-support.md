# Basic life support (BLS) - Adult/Child DRSABCD resuscitation, the collapsed patient

Basic life support (BLS) - Adult/Child
DRSABCD resuscitation, the collapsed patient
Recommend 1-4
•	 Good quality CPR and reducing time to defibrillation are the highest priorities in
resuscitation from sudden cardiac arrest
•	 Airway takes precedent over any injury, including possibility of spinal injury -
handle gently with no twisting or bending of spinal column or neck1
•	 If pregnant, once CPR in progress put padding such as a towel under the
woman’s right hip to tilt the hips about 15–30⁰, but leave her shoulders flat
•	 Child/infant - central pulse palpation may be used to asess circulation. If a pulse
cannot be definitely identified within 10 seconds (or HR &lt; 60 with signs of poor
circulation) start CPR
Background
•	 Agonal breaths (occasional irregular gasps) are common in early stages of
cardiac arrest
1.
May present with 2
•	 Sudden collapse
•	 Unresponsive and absent or abnormal breathing ie gasping
2.	 Immediate management 1-6
•	 DRSABCD as per Basic life support flowchart
•	 Danger - check the environment/patient surroundings are safe before
proceeding. PPE as needed
•	 Response - verbal and tactile stimuli (talk and touch)
•	 Send for help - shout for help. If help not arrived in 1 minute go get help 5+
contact MO/RSQ
•	 If trauma related control catastrophic haemorrhage
•	 Airway (leave person in position found):1-5

–
open using head tilt–chin lift or jaw thrust (if neck injury, only use jaw thrust) +
	›
child - head in ‘sniffing position’
	›
infant &lt; 1 year - head in neutral position

–
if fluid/matter obstructs airway at any time:
	›
use suction if available OR if unavailable, roll on to side to clear airway 1
	›
if visible material, remove if possible eg using rescuer’s fingers 1

•	 Breathing:

–
Look (movement of chest/abdomen), Listen (for breath sounds), Feel (air on
rescuer’s cheek)

–
if unresponsive but breathing normally, see Unconscious patient
•	 If unresponsive + breathing absent or NOT normal, place on firm surface and
start CPR:

–
30 compressions: 2 ventilations (adult + child) OR

–
if paediatric BLS trained - child: 2  ventilations (first): 15 compressions 5

–
100–120 compressions per minute
•	 Defibrillation - attach automated external defibrillator (AED) as soon as available
and follow prompts:

–
adult and child > 8 use adult pads. Child 1–8 use paediatric pads (or adult size
if not available)

–
continue CPR + minimise interruptions to chest compressions

–
alternate person doing compressions when AED advises to stop
(eg to analyse rhythm/shock) or if fatigued
•	 Get ALS trained clinician(s) if available
•	 Continue CPR until responsive, normal breathing returns, or MO/SNO /MA
instructs otherwise
3.	 Clinical assessment - See Immediate management
4.	 Management
•	 Control bleeding, protect from weather + other first aid measures depending on
circumstances
5.	 Follow up
•	 According to patient’s condition/presentation
•	 Sensitive, professional debriefing of people involved in resuscitation is valuable
•	 Provide support for family members
•	 If required, see Patient death in absence of MA,
6.	 Referral/consultation
Always contact Medical Officer
