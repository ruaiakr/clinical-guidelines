<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Constipation - Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>The normal frequency of stools decreases with age from infancy until around 3 years when the average is 1 stool/day</li>
<li>Also see Constipation - emergency management in children</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hard or painful stools eg pellets, large</li>
<li>Stools that are less regular than usual</li>
<li>Passing small amounts of liquid stool in underwear</li>
<li>Avoiding or holding off passing a stool</li>
<li>Chronic abdominal pain may be only symptom</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management Not applicable</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>what the child does when the family/carer thinks the child needs to poo</li>
<li>frequency and consistency of stools</li>
<li>disrupted routine eg toilet training, illness, travel, new sibling, starting day care/school</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do:
<ul>
<li>vital signs</li>
<li>weight, bare weight if &lt; 2 years. Assess against recent weights</li>
<li>palpate abdomen - check left lower quadrant for palpable masses - can indicate fecal impaction</li>
<li>check anus - position, fissures, bleeding, other abnormalities</li>
<li>note: digital rectal examination is not required</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If stools are infrequent but remain soft:
<ul>
<li>reassure parents/carer this is not constipation</li>
<li>some breastfed babies have bowel motions every 7–14 days. As long the stool is soft this is normal<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If child has infrequent eg &lt; 3 hard stools per week, for at least 2 weeks:
<ul>
<li>child is likely constipated</li>
<li>contact MO who may consider:
<ul>
<li>laxative treatment</li>
<li>referral to paediatrician</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if toilet training, hold off until stools are soft and regular</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If child anxious or fearful of passing stool, consider discussing with parents/carer:
<ul>
<li>positive reinforcement, avoid blaming the child</li>
<li>encourage child to sit on toilet after meals, reward even if no stool is passed</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If given laxatives advise to be reviewed:
<ul>
<li>in 3–7 days to check on progress, or</li>
<li>sooner if concerned or child is unwell</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider referral to the next child health nurse/MO clinic</li>
</ul>
</div>
