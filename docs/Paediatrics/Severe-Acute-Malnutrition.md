# Severe Acute Malnutrition – Child(Kiribati Original Protocol)

Severe Acute Malnutrition – Child
(Kiribati Original Protocol)
Recommendations
•	 All children with severe acute malnutrition (SAM) should be assessed urgently.
•	 If hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, infection or dehydration are suspected, initiate
treatment immediately.
•	 Children who fail the appetite test or present with danger signs require inpatient
care and referral.
•	 Begin F-75 therapeutic milk and administer antibiotics and rehydration as
appropriate.
•	 Monitor closely for hypoglycaemia, infection, overhydration and complications.
Background
Severe acute malnutrition is defined as:
•	 Weight-for-height/length < –3 SD (wasted)
•	 Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) &lt; 115 mm
•	 Bilateral pitting oedema (regardless of weight-for-height)
Children may be stunted (weight-for-age < –3 SD) but not wasted. These children
do not require hospitalisation unless another serious illness is present. Treatment is
similar for severe wasting and oedematous malnutrition (kwashiorkor).
1.
May present with
•	 Noted weight loss or failure to gain weight
•	 Poor appetite
•	 Diarrhoea or vomiting
•	 Sleepiness or lethargy
•	 Head lag
•	 Recurrent fevers or illness
•	 Pallor
•	 Poor feeding
•	 Hypoglycaemia or hypothermia
•	 Oedema (bilateral pitting feet/ankles)
•	 Skin changes, oral thrush, ulcers, or eye signs (vitamin A deficiency)
2.	 Immediate Management
Hypoglycaemia (RBS &lt; 3 mmol/L)
•	 If RBS unavailable, assume hypoglycaemia and treat.
•	 Give 50 mL of 10% glucose/sugar solution orally or via NGT.
•	 Begin F-75 as soon as possible and feed every 2 hours.
•	 If unconscious, give IV 10% glucose (5 mL/kg) or NGT sugar solution. If not
available, give 1 tsp sugar sublingually every 20 minutes.

Hypothermia (Temp &lt;35.5°C rectal / &lt;35°C axillary)
•	 Assume hypothermia if temp is low or not registering.
•	 Feed immediately and 2-hourly.
•	 Rewarm: wrap child, use heater/lamp (indirect), or skin-to-skin with mother.
•	 Keep child covered, especially head.
Dehydration
•	 Suspect in children with diarrhoea or reduced urine.
•	 Do not use IV fluids unless in shock.
•	 Rehydrate slowly with ReSoMal orally or via NGT:

–
5 mL/kg every 30 minutes for 2 hours, then

–
5–10 mL/kg/hr for next 4–10 hours alternating with F-75.
•	 If rehydration needed >10 hrs, replace ReSoMal with same volume of F-75.
•	 In shock and unable to tolerate oral: use IV Ringer’s lactate + 5% dextrose or
0.45% saline + 5% dextrose.
Infection
•	 Assume infection in all SAM children.
•	 Start broad-spectrum antibiotics:

–
Uncomplicated: Amoxicillin 30 mg/kg BID x 5 days

–
Complicated (lethargy, danger signs):
	›
Benzylpenicillin 50,000 U/kg IV/IM q6h or
	›
Ampicillin 50 mg/kg IV/IM q6h for 2 days
	›
Gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV/IM daily x 7 days

–
Optional: Metronidazole 7.5 mg/kg q8h x 7 days

–
Give measles vaccine if ≥6 months and unvaccinated (delay if in shock)
Electrolyte imbalance
•	 Give potassium 3–4 mmol/kg/day
•	 Do not use diuretics
•	 Avoid sodium-containing fluids
Initial feeding
•	 F-75 therapeutic milk: 135 mL/kg/day ÷ 8 feeds (first 4 days)
•	 If dehydrated or in shock, delay feeds until stabilised

3.	 Clinical Assessment
•	 Check general appearance and vital signs
•	 Appetite test: if poor, manage as inpatient
•	 Look for signs of dehydration, oedema, hypothermia, lethargy
•	 Perform full physical exam, including:

–
Shock (cold hands, weak pulse, cap refill >3s)

–
Severe pallor

–
Skin signs of kwashiorkor

–
Oral thrush, ulcers

–
Eye signs (dryness, corneal ulceration, Bitot’s spots)

–
Local infections (ear, skin, throat, chest)

–
Fever or hypothermia
4.	 Management
•	 Treat underlying complications urgently (hypoglycaemia, dehydration,
hypothermia, infection)
•	 Monitor RBS if available; treat again if &lt;3 mmol/L
•	 Continue 2–3 hourly feeding with F-75
•	 Provide warmth and oxygen as needed
•	 Monitor fluid intake, signs of overhydration (e.g. rapid weight gain, oedema)
•	 Avoid high sodium fluids and diuretics
•	 Support mother to feed, observe, and keep child warm
•	 Identify and treat other infections (e.g. pneumonia, skin infection)
5.	 Follow Up
•	 Feed every 2–3 hours for first days
•	 Monitor weight, oedema, consciousness, temp
•	 Monitor for overhydration
•	 Reassess appetite daily
•	 Provide micronutrients as per SAM guideline (not covered in this section)
6.	 Referral / Consultation
•	 Discuss all SAM children with Medical Assistant or Medical Officer
•	 Children with:

–
Failed appetite test

–
Severe oedema (+++)

–
Danger signs (shock, unconscious, persistent vomiting)

–
Suspected underlying disease (e.g. TB, HIV)

–
Require inpatient referral
•	 If unable to refer immediately, continue with feeding and supportive care
