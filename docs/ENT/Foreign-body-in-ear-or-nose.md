How to do Ear Flushing:
Method
ΓÇó	 The patient must sit with the ear
facing the light and you must be
able to see the entrance to the
ear canal clearly.
ΓÇó	 Fill the syringe with warm water
and squirt it hard a few times
back into the water container to
check that it works well and the
tip does not come off.
ΓÇó	 Gently pull the pinna backward
away from the head.
ΓÇó	 Place the tip of the syringe just inside the canal and squirt the water into the
ear canal.
ΓÇó	 Water should be directed around the foreign body. The water will then be behind
the foreign body and should push it out.
ΓÇó	 Catch the water that comes out in a bowl. Check to see what has come out of
the ear canal.
ΓÇó	 Examine the ear canal with an otoscope after every five syringes.
ΓÇó	 Once the foreign body has come out of the ear canal you should be able to see
the ear drum.
ΓÇó	 Check both ears.
NOTE
ΓÇó	 Remember: the water used to syringe the ear MUST be the same as body
temperature ΓÇö 37┬░C, warm but comfortable on the hand.

ΓÇô
o	 If you use cold or hot water for ear flushing, the patient may feel dizzy,
nausea or vomiting.

ΓÇô
o	 If the patient complains of dizziness, stop syringing and make the patient
comfortable with support.
ΓÇó	 DO NOT direct the water straight onto the foreign body.

ΓÇô
o	 This will push the foreign body further into the ear canal.
ΓÇó	 DO NOT SYRINGE if the ear is dry and if you know or suspect there is a
perforation in the ear drum. Refer these patients.

ΓÇô
o	 Syringing a dry perforated ear may lead to suppurative otitis media.
ΓÇó	 If you see any trauma to the ear canal after removal of the foreign body:

ΓÇô
o	 Fill the ear canal with eardrops.

ΓÇô
o	 Give the patient eardrops to take home and use four times each day.

ΓÇô
o	 Check the ear again after two days.
Attention
ΓÇó	 Do not syringe ear if there is pain in the ear or recent trauma.
ΓÇó	 Always look in the ear before syringing. If any pain ΓÇö stop and look again.
ΓÇó	 If CSOM ΓÇö syringe with diluted povidone-iodine 10%.
