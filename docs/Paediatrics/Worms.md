<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Worms - Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Poor growth, anemia</li>
<li>Abdominal distension, pain, nausea, diarrhoea</li>
<li>Positive pathology results</li>
<li>Visible worms in stool eg roundworm, threadworm</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="threadworm">Threadworm (pinworm) - most common<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Itchy bottom ± rash from scratching</li>
<li>Tiny white worms seen around anus or in stool</li>
<li>Redness and itching around vaginal area in girls</li>
<li>Irritability</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management Not applicable</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>immunocompromised<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>recent travel</li>
<li>last treated for worms</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do:
<ul>
<li>vital signs</li>
<li>weight - bare weight if &lt; 2 years. Assess against recent weights</li>
<li>offer to look for threadworms around anus</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If not already done:
<ul>
<li>capillary Hb - check normal values in <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Anaemia">Anaemia</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>3,4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO:
<ul>
<li>&lt; 6 months of age</li>
<li>tapeworm - may order praziquantel</li>
<li>strongyloides - may order ivermectin</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If threadworm:
<ul>
<li>give albendazole OR mebendazole OR pyrantel single dose</li>
<li>treat household contacts at same time</li>
<li>if still symptoms or visible worms in stools after initial treatment, advise to repeat treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If patient has worms on pathology treat as per drug box</li>
<li>Reinfection of threadworm and whipworm is common. Advise on hygiene measures eg:<sup>3</sup>
<ul>
<li>shower/bath daily, frequent hand washing</li>
<li>keep finger nails short, avoid scratching bottom</li>
<li>wash bedding, towels and clothes in hot water where possible</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-box" id="albendazole">
<div class="pccm-drug-header">
<span class="pccm-drug-name">ALBENDAZOLE</span>
<span class="pccm-drug-level">L2B</span>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap" style="margin:0; overflow-x:auto;">
<table class="pccm-drug-table">
<tr><th>FORM</th><th>STRENGTH</th><th>ROUTE</th><th>DOSE</th><th>DURATION</th></tr>
<tr>
<td>Tablet</td>
<td>200 mg<br />400 mg</td>
<td>Oral</td>
<td>Adult and child &gt; 10 kg — 400 mg<br />Child &gt; 6 months and &lt; 10 kg — 200 mg</td>
<td>once<br />or<br />If whipworm confirmed on pathology — daily for 3 days</td>
</tr>
</table>
</div>
<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> Take on an empty stomach. Tablets may be crushed, chewed or swallowed whole. May cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, headache, dizziness, fever or abdominal pain</p>
<p><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> Avoid during 1st trimester. Use contraception during and for 1 month after treatment. Seek advice for use beyond 1st trimester</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Ocular cysticercosis (tapeworm infection of the eye)</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a><sup>3,5</sup></p>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If treatment repeated and symptoms continue, or visible worms in stools, contact MO</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO as above</li>
</ul>
