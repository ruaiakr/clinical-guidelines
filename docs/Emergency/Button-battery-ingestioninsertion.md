<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Button battery ingestion/insertion - Adult/Child</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend1,2</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>A suspected or proven battery ingestion is a time critical emergency - urgent x-ray needed</li>
<li>Suspect button battery ingestion in all children &lt; 5 years of age with non-specific symptoms</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background1,2</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Button (disc) batteries cause burns resulting in life-threatening injuries</li>
<li>Failure to remove from the oesophagus within 2 hours can lead to perforation of the</li>
<li>oesophagus, fistula (eg oesophageal-aortal/tracheal) and catastrophic haemorrhage</li>
<li>Oral honey may slow the development of a chemical burn for a swallowed button battery<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with 1,2,4</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Battery missing</li>
<li>Seen to have been playing with battery</li>
<li>Self reported by child (older)</li>
<li>Gagging, gulp, cough or choking episode:<ul><li>may be overheard when child swallows battery rather than directly observed</li></ul></li>
<li>Child may deny ingestion</li>
<li>Very non-specific symptoms:<ul><li>unexplained partial food refusal - may still take soft food/fluids</li><li>drooling or regurgitation</li><li>croup like cough</li><li>chest pain</li><li>upper GI bleeding (melaena/black stools; haematemesis - may mimic epistaxis):</li><li>may be small bleed initially, then catastrophic bleed</li><li>fever/vomiting/signs of infection without a clear focus</li><li>pain/grunting</li><li>Bloody discharge from ear, nose, vagina, rectum - may have inserted battery</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If inhaled, see Choking</li>
<li>If haematemesis or melaena:<ul><li>insert IVC x 2</li><li>get rapid history</li><li>urgently consult MO</li></ul></li>
<li>Do vital signs If ingestion (swallowed) suspected</li>
<li>Urgently contact MO:<ul><li>if no x-ray facilities, we will need urgent evacuation for x-ray</li></ul></li>
<li>If the x-ray shows battery is located in the hypopharynx (bottom of throat) or oesophagus:5<ul><li>URGENT evacuation for removal via endoscopy required</li></ul></li>
<li>Give oral honey if ingestion suspected &lt; 12 hours and:3,6<ul><li>child ≥ 12 months old</li><li>able to swallow</li><li>give 10 mL (2 teaspoons) every 10 minutes, up to 6 doses (or as guided by MO)</li></ul></li>
<li>keep nil by mouth otherwise</li>
<li>If the battery is in the stomach or beyond:2<ul><li>management will depend on factors such as age of child, symptoms, size of battery, time of suspected ingestion</li><li>MO will advise</li></ul></li>
<li>Monitor vital signs</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about - if known:<ul><li>timing of ingestion</li><li>size of battery (if ≥ 2 cm, more likely to lodge in oesophagus)</li><li>was ingestion witnessed</li><li>what treatment (if any) has been tried at home</li><li>colour of stools, any blood in vomit, or any other signs/symptoms</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continue management of suspected swallowed battery in collaboration with MO</li>
<li>If insertion suspected eg into vagina, ear, rectum, nose:<ul><li>consult MO regarding urgent removal</li><li>x-ray may be required if unexplained nasal/ear/vaginal discharge and unable to visualise cause</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As advised by MO. Note: even after removal of a button battery, ongoing damage can occur<sup>7</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always consult MO</li>
</ul>
</div>
