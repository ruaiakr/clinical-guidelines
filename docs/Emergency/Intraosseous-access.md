<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Intraosseous access - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul>
<li>Use if urgent need for fluids/medications + difficult/failed IVC access<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Pain from intraosseous infusion under pressure is more severe than insertion<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Check for contraindications:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>fracture in or adjacent to the target bone</li>
<li>excessive tissue ± absence of adequate anatomical landmarks</li>
<li>infection at site</li>
<li>previous significant orthopaedic procedure at the site, prosthetic joint</li>
<li>Intraosseous access/attempted access in targeted bone within last 48 hours</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Prepare equipment + site:
<ul>
<li>select site + needle set, as per Insertion guide (below)<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li>clean site using antiseptic + aseptic technique</li>
<li><strong>note:</strong> lidocaine (lignocaine) is not usually needed for insertion of intraosseous needle</li>
<li>if responsive to pain, before infusion prime extension set with lidocaine (lignocaine) 1% approx. 1 mL (10 mg)<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Insert intraosseous needle (instructions below)</li>
<li>Once inserted:<sup>1-3</sup>
<ul>
<li>place stabilising dressing over the hub</li>
<li>aspirate for blood/bone marrow. The inability to withdraw/aspirate blood does not mean unsuccessful insertion, consider re-trying after the flush</li>
<li>attach primed extension set + flush with lidocaine (lignocaine) 1% OR sodium chloride 0.9% if unresponsive</li>
<li>connect fluids. Infusion may need to be pressurised to get desired rate ie pressure bag/pump</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">Insertion guide<sup>2</sup></h2>

<figure class="pccm-flowchart-figure">
<img src="/img/emergency/ez-io-insertion-guide.png" alt="EZ-IO insertion guide - needle sets and insertion sites for proximal humerus, proximal tibia, distal tibia and distal femur" />
<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Arrow EZ-IO insertion guide</p>
</figure>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Needle set</strong>
<ul>
<li>Select needle set based on patient weight, anatomy + clinical judgment</li>
<li>45 mm needle set (yellow hub) proximal humerus &gt; 40 kg + patients with excessive tissue over any insertion site</li>
<li>25 mm needle set (blue hub) &gt; 3 kg</li>
<li>15 mm needle set (pink hub) approx. 3–39 kg</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Proximal humerus</strong>
<ul>
<li>Place patient’s hand over the abdomen, place your palm on the shoulder anteriorly. The ‘ball’ under your palm is the general target area. Aim needle downward at approx. 45° angle</li>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> abduction of arm after placement will dislodge needle</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Proximal tibia</strong>
<ul>
<li>Extend the leg, feel for flat aspect of tibia</li>
<li>Approx. 3 cm (1–2 cm in infant/child) below the patella + approx. 2 cm medial (1 cm in infant/child) to the tibial tuberosity along the flat aspect of the tibia (depending on anatomy). Aim needle at a 90° angle to the bone</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Distal tibia</strong>
<ul>
<li>Approx. 3 cm (infant 1 cm/1 finger) above most prominent aspect of medial malleolus. Feel for anterior and posterior borders of tibia to assure that insertion site is in the flat centre of the bone. Aim needle at a 90° angle to the bone</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Distal femur - child only</strong>
<ul>
<li>Secure site with leg outstretched to ensure knee does not bend. Feel for patella, insertion site is just proximal to superior border (max. 1–2 cm) and approx. 1–2 cm medial</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Arrow EZ-IO Vascular Access System images courtesy of Teleflex</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">How to insert Arrow EZ-IO® device<sup>2</sup></h2>

<figure class="pccm-flowchart-figure">
<img src="/img/emergency/ez-io-how-to-insert.png" alt="How to insert Arrow EZ-IO device - attach needle, drill to medullary space, remove stylet" />
<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">How to insert Arrow EZ-IO® device</p>
</figure>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Attach needle set to driver + remove safety cap</li>
<li>Push needle tip through the skin until tip touches the bone:
<ul>
<li>before pressing the trigger, check at least 1 black line is visible above the skin to confirm adequate needle set length</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Gently drill. Immediately stop advancing + release the trigger when you feel a ‘pop’ or ‘give’ as the needle set enters the medullary space:
<ul>
<li>do NOT pull/jerk back on the driver when releasing the trigger</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Hold the hub + pull the driver straight off needle set. Continue to hold hub in place while twisting the stylet off with anticlockwise rotations</li>
<li>See instructions + video at <a href="https://www.teleflex.com/anz/en/product-areas/emergency-medicine/intraosseous-access/arrow-ez-io-system/clinical-resources/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.teleflex.com/anz/en/product-areas/emergency-medicine/intraosseous-access/arrow-ez-io-system/clinical-resources/index.html</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">Manual insertion</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hold the needle set with the catheter hub and stylet as 1 piece. Ensure stylet + hub stay screwed together</li>
<li>Rotate clockwise/anticlockwise while applying gentle, moderate steady downward pressure without rocking the needle set. Allow rotation + pressure to penetrate the bone cortex, not excessive force</li>
<li>Child - stop when change of pressure or resistance felt as a ‘pop’ or ‘give’ indicating entry to medullary space</li>
<li>Adult - advance needle set approx. 1–2 cm after entry into medullary space, which is felt as a change in resistance; in the proximal humerus for most adults the needle set should be advanced 2 cm or until hub is flush or against the skin</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">After insertion</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor closely + contact MO if:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>intraosseous lidocaine (lignocaine) is not sufficient + the patient is still in pain:
<ul>
<li>MO may consider systemic analgesia</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>change in patient’s response to medications</li>
<li>↓flow rate/alarms on infusion pump</li>
<li>fever or signs of:
<ul>
<li>extravasation/infiltration at site</li>
<li><a href="/docs/Emergency/Compartment-syndrome">Compartment syndrome</a>, in limb</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
