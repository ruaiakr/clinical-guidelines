# Hepatitis C – Adult/Child

Hepatitis C – Adult/Child
Recommend1
•	 Acute hepatitis C is a notifiable condition
•	 For all patients with anti-HCV antibodies: perform HCV PCR (viral load) to
confirm active infection.
•	 Screen all at-risk patients (STI-positive, IV drug users, sex workers) for hepatitis
B, C, HIV and syphilis
•	 Include universal antenatal screening for hepatitis B, HIV, and syphilis using
WHO pre-qualified point-of-care tests in all health facilities
Background2
•	 Hepatitis C virus (HCV) is uncommon in Kiribati
•	 Acute HCV = first 6 months after infection
•	 Most cases are asymptomatic; spontaneous clearance in 20–25% of cases
•	 Causes chronic infection and should be treated with antivirals
•	 WHO-recommends treatment initiation for chronic HCV.
•	 Chronic HCV is curable
•	 ≥2 cases = outbreak3
1.
May present with3
•	 No symptoms (most cases)
•	 If present:

–
Jaundice, fatigue, anorexia, nausea

–
Vomiting, upper abdominal pain
•	 Laboratory findings:

–
Positive anti-HCV

–
Positive HCV RNA

–
Elevated ALT
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Not applicable

3.	 Clinical assessment4
•	 History:

–
IV drug use, tattooing, blood exposure, shared razors

–
Contact with HCV-positive individuals

–
Alcohol intake
•	 Observations:

–
Full vitals (Q-ADDS/CEWT)

–
Urinalysis for bilirubin/urobilinogen
•	 Examination:

–
Jaundice, hepatomegaly, RUQ tenderness
•	 Blood tests:

–
LFTs, INR, UEC – calculate APRI-score

–
HCV RNA (viral load)

–
HIV, HBV serology
•	 In pregnant women:

–
Perform routine hepatitis C screening

–
Coordinate with Hepatitis Clinic/ANC for follow-up

–
While PMTCT risk for HCV is lower than HBV, screening is essential for
maternal counselling and postnatal infant follow-up
4.	 Management2,3
•	 Consult MO for all suspected cases
•	 Urgent referral if INR ≥ 1.5 or if systemically unwell
•	 Supportive treatment only for acute phase
•	 Bed rest advised if jaundiced
•	 Educate patient and contacts:

–
Avoid sharing injecting equipment, razors, toothbrushes

–
Do not donate blood

–
Avoid paracetamol and address alcohol intake
•	 Vaccinate against hepatitis A and B – if unvaccinated
•	 Contact tracing (up to 180 days exposure) in consultation with MO or Public
Health
•	 Chronic HCV infection: requires treatment with antivirals (Sofosbuvir &
Daclatasvir) – consult MO/Hepatitis Program
•	 Pregnant women:

–
Provide education and linkage to early infant screening

–
Ensure no delay in treatment planning if chronic HCV confirmed postpartum

5.	 Follow up3
•	 Review in 24 hours, reinforce education (i.e. alcohol use, adherence)
•	 Repeat HCV RNA after 6 months to confirm clearance or chronicity
•	 Monitor LFTs and INR
•	 Annual HCV RNA testing if risk factors persist
6.	 Referral / consultation
•	 Consult MO as required
•	 Notify Public Health Unit / Hepatitis Program
•	 Refer to hepatitis specialist/clinic if chronic HCV or if treatment is indicated
•	 Coordinate care with Hepatitis services for pregnant patients and infant testing
