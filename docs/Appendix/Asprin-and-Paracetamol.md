Asprin and Paracetamol
Aspirin and paracetamol are commonly used to treat fevers and to reduce pain.
Health care workers should not automatically give drugs to reduce temperature
to all children with fever. First the cause of the fever should be determined, then
appropriate treatment given. If a child’s fever lasts beyond 2 days or reappears
within 14 days and the cause cannot be determined, the nurse or medical assistant
should consult the doctor about the fever.
USE OF PARACETAMOL
Paracetamol is given to reduce the discomfort of children with high fevers only (>
38.5°C)."Treat the child, not the thermometer."
USE OF PARACETAMOL


 Paracetamol is the drug of choice for fever in children. The dose is 15 mg/kg
body weight—see drug reference tables


 Paracetamol is TOXIC TO THE LIVER if given in too high doses or if the liver is
inefficient or unable to metabolize it because of inflammation or disease (such as
hepatitis or cirrhosis)


 Paracetamol can also cause kidney damage if given in too high doses


 Use paracetamol only when needed. Like all drugs it has side effects and
potential TOXIC EFFECTS
WHY WE AVOID USING ASPIRIN IN CHILDREN


 Aspirin use in children has many bad effects, compared to those of paracetamol


 Aspirin irritates the stomach and increases the RISK OF ULCER, HAEMORRHAGE
and PERFORATION


 Aspirin also increases the RISK OF BLEEDING


 Aspirin has also been linked to REYE’S SYNDROME. Reye’s syndrome, a disease
associated with liver, brain and neurological problems, has been linked to the use
of aspirin in children with viral infections

5 For these reasons, we use paracetamol, not aspirin in children and adolescents
