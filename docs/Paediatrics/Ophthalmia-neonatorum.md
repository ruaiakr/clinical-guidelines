<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Ophthalmia neonatorum – neonatal Conjunctivitis (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Related topics — <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Conjunctivitis">Conjunctivitis - Adult/Child/Newborn</a></p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>All neonates with conjunctivitis in the first month of life require prompt treatment.</li>
<li>Suspect gonococcal or chlamydial conjunctivitis if the mother has untreated or suspected sexually transmitted infection (STI).</li>
<li>Provide systemic antibiotics for all moderate to severe cases or when STI pathogens are suspected.</li>
<li>Clean eyes regularly with sterile isotonic solution.</li>
<li>Ensure mother and partner are assessed and treated where relevant.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ophthalmia neonatorum is an acute conjunctivitis occurring within the first 28 days of life. It may be caused by <em>Neisseria gonorrhoeae</em>, <em>Chlamydia trachomatis</em>, other skin or gastrointestinal bacteria, or herpes simplex virus. Infection may be associated with systemic illness or sepsis. Early recognition and treatment are critical to prevent complications such as corneal ulceration and vision loss.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="gonococcal-conjunctivitis">Gonococcal conjunctivitis</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Onset: 2–7 days after birth</li>
<li>Bilateral purulent discharge</li>
<li>Profuse exudate</li>
<li>Eyelid swelling (oedema)</li>
<li>Risk of corneal ulceration and scarring</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="chlamydial-conjunctivitis">Chlamydial conjunctivitis</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Onset: 5–14 days after birth</li>
<li>Watery discharge becomes mucopurulent</li>
<li>Unilateral or bilateral involvement</li>
<li>Red, thickened conjunctivae (chemosis)</li>
<li>Pseudomembrane formation may occur</li>
<li>May be associated with pneumonia — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Pneumonia">Pneumonia</a></li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="bacterial-conjunctivitis">Bacterial conjunctivitis</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Onset: 2–5 days after birth</li>
<li>Purulent discharge</li>
<li>Similar to gonococcal infection</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="herpes-simplex-keratoconjunctivitis">Herpes simplex keratoconjunctivitis</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Onset: second week of life</li>
<li>Watery eyes, red conjunctiva, eye pain</li>
<li>May have skin vesicles</li>
<li>Can progress to cataracts or chorioretinitis</li>
<li>Often part of systemic herpes infection (see Section 6.12.5)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Clean eyes with 0.9% sodium chloride or Ringer lactate 4 times daily or more until discharge resolves</li>
<li>If systemic signs (e.g. fever, lethargy, seizures), follow emergency management of the sick neonate — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Approach-to-a-sick-neonate">Approach to a sick neonate</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess eye discharge (timing, character, laterality)</li>
<li>Examine for eyelid swelling, corneal involvement</li>
<li>Look for systemic illness (fever, rash, poor feeding, lethargy)</li>
<li>Review maternal STI history or signs</li>
<li>Record any vesicular lesions (consider herpes)</li>
<li>Check respiratory status (for associated chlamydial pneumonia)</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="chlamydial-management">Chlamydial conjunctivitis</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Azithromycin PO: 20 mg/kg once daily for 3 days</li>
<li>Alternative: Erythromycin PO: 12.5 mg/kg 4 times daily for 14 days</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>Note:</strong> Both azithromycin and erythromycin carry a risk of infantile hypertrophic pyloric stenosis, especially in the first 2 weeks of life. Monitor for vomiting or feeding intolerance. See <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Pyloric-stenosis">Pyloric stenosis</a>.
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Topical antibiotics are not needed if systemic treatment is given.</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="herpes-management">Herpes simplex keratoconjunctivitis</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Clean eyes with 0.9% sodium chloride or Ringer lactate</li>
<li>Refer to Section 6.12.5 for systemic treatment</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Reassess after 48 hours of starting treatment</li>
<li>Continue eye cleaning until discharge stops</li>
<li>Monitor for complications (e.g. corneal clouding, eyelid swelling, systemic illness)</li>
<li>Observe for medication side effects (especially vomiting with macrolides)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer if:
<ul>
<li>No improvement after 48 hours of appropriate antibiotic treatment</li>
<li>Signs of systemic infection</li>
<li>Suspected or confirmed herpes simplex virus</li>
<li>Corneal involvement (cloudiness, ulceration)</li>
<li>Generalised illness (pneumonia, sepsis)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="prevention">Prevention</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>All neonates should receive prophylactic 1% tetracycline eye ointment at birth (see Section 1.3.4 / <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Neonatal-care">Neonatal care</a>)</li>
<li>Educate families on early signs of infection</li>
<li>Ensure screening and treatment of mother and partner for STIs</li>
</ul>
