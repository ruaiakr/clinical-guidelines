<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Meningitis - Adult/Child</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Meningitis is a medical emergency. Early recognition + treatment is imperative<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li>If suspected, but lumbar puncture cannot be done ≤ 30 minutes, give IV antibiotics<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li>Do NOT use hyponatraemic solutions eg glucose 4% with sodium chloride 0.18% or sodium</li>
<li>chloride 0.45% - ↑ risk of cerebral oedema</li>
<li>Suspect meningitis if sick child with no obvious source of infection</li>
<li>Parents/carers may notice early, subtle changes in child’s LOC - do not ignore their concerns</li>
<li>Meningococcal infection is a notifiable disease ) notify Public Health Unit within 6 hours</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Children often present with non-specific symptoms and not the classic triad of fever, headache + neck stiffness<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Meningitis is an inflammation or infection of the membranes that surround the brain + spinal cord. It can lead to serious long-term neurological complications + death</li>
<li>Viral meningitis is more common than bacterial meningitis, but is usually less severe</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Typically presents with acute history of fever, neck stiffness + altered conscious state ±<ul><li>headache, photophobia + nausea or vomiting - often prominent<sup>1</sup></li><li>petechial rash - very late sign + indicative of meningococcal sepsis</li><li>focal neurological deficit, seizures, shock</li></ul></li>
<li>Older children may present with any combination of the above2± – upper or lower respiratory tract symptoms, myalgia + abdominal pain</li>
<li>&lt; 3 months - bulging fontanelle, high pitched cry, poor feeding, apnoea, seizures, vomiting<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If fitting see Fitting for midazolam dosing</li>
<li>Consult MO urgently</li>
<li>Get rapid history + manage concurrently</li>
<li>Do vital signs +<ul><li>central capillary refill time</li><li>GCS</li><li>BGL</li></ul></li>
<li>Give O<sup>2</sup> to maintain SpO<sup>2</sup> ≥ 94%</li>
<li>Insert IVC x 2 or Intraosseous, if 2 failed attempts</li>
<li>Correct BGL if needed:<ul><li>give glucose 10% 2 mL/kg - as per MO<sup>2</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Check allergies</li>
<li>MO will order antibiotics - give within 30 minutes - DO NOT DELAY</li>
<li>If child &gt; 3 months + adult, give dexamethasone before or with 1st dose of antibiotic<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>If not allergic, MA may order<ul><li>Children &amp; Adult: Cloxacillin 2 g IV, 4-hourly PLUS</li><li>Gentamicin IV: adult 4 to 5 mg/kg (7 mg/kg for critically ill adults).</li><li>Neonate:<ul><li>Ampicillin 50 mg/kg IV: week 1 of life: 12-hourly weeks 2 to 4 of life: 6 to 8-hourly PLUS</li><li>Gentamicin IV once daily: week 1 of life: 5 mg/kg weeks 2 to 4 of life: 7.5 mg/kg.</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>IM route can be used if timely IV/intraosseous access not possible</li>
<li>Start fluid resuscitation as clinically indicated ≤ 30 minutes:2-4<ul><li>give sodium chloride 0.9% 20 mL/kg fluid bolus</li><li>repeat if needed as per MO</li></ul></li>
<li>Arrange urgent evacuation</li>
<li>Consider Sepsis, as a differential diagnosis</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Note: apparent explanation for fever (eg pharyngitis, UTI or otitis media) does not rule out diagnosis<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Ask about:<ul><li>headache, irritability, fever, rash, neck stiffness, lethargy, confusion<sup>1</sup></li><li>immunisations - Hib, meningococcal, pneumococcal + check vaccination status</li><li>prior use of oral antibiotics (may modify clinical features)</li></ul></li>
<li>Check for risk factors:2<ul><li>recent contact with a case of bacterial meningitis (especially in family)</li><li>recent contact with HSV ‘cold sores’ or confirmed enterovirus infection</li><li>recent overseas travel</li><li>maternal Group B Strep (GBS) colonisation if &lt; 3 months</li><li>immunocompromised</li><li>recent neurosurgical procedure, penetrating head injury, ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt,cochlear implant</li></ul></li>
<li>Weight - bare weight if &lt; 2 years</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:<ul><li>listen to chest for air entry - any crackles, wheeze</li><li>skin for rashes including at pressure points + under nappies/clothing:<ul><li>petechiae or purpura (do not fade on pressure)</li><li>note: if child, rash may not appear until child is rehydrated<sup>2</sup></li></ul></li><li>Hydration assessment - adult, or child</li><li>ENT</li><li>fontanelle in infants - any fullness, bulging</li><li>neck stiffness:<ul><li>with patient lying down, put hand behind head + gently raise</li><li>note: may not complain of neck stiffness, but with passive or active flexion</li></ul></li><li>cannot touch chin to chest</li><li>+ve Brudzinski’s sign - reflex flexion of the hip + knee when the neck is passively flexed OR</li><li>+ve Kernig’s sign - pain along spinal cord ± resistance to knee extension when hip and knee are flexed to 90°<ul><li>may indicate bacterial meningitis<sup>2</sup></li></ul></li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult the Kiribati Antibiotic Guideline 2022.</li>
<li>Careful management of fluid + electrolyte balance is important - discuss with paediatrician as soon as possible</li>
<li>Monitor closely until evacuation:<ul><li>vital signs + BGL</li><li>give analgesia + antiemetic as needed</li><li>fluid balance</li></ul></li>
<li>Check timing of further antibiotic doses if evacuation is delayed/prolonged - contact MO</li>
<li>If child &gt; 3 months + adult, give dexamethasone before or with 1st dose of antibiotic (do not delay antibiotics if dexamethasone not available)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chemoprophylaxis will be required for staff/close contacts of a patient with either meningococcal or Hib meningitis. Unvaccinated contacts of Hib meningitis &lt; 5 years should be immunised as soon as possible. Public Health Unit will advise</li>
<li>Refer for audiology assessment 6–8 weeks after discharge if bacterial meningitis</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>For all suspected or confirmed cases of meningitis or meningococcal disease notify the Public Health Unit within 6 hours</li>
</ul>
