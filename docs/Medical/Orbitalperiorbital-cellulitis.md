Orbital/periorbital cellulitis - Adult/Child
Background
•	 Orbital cellulitis is an infection of the eye socket + surrounding tissue. It is an
emergency + can cause blindness + intracranial infection1
•	 Periorbital cellulitis is an infection of the eyelid1
•	 Overlapping clinical features makes it difficult to differentiate2
•	 Children &lt; 4 years are at ↑risk of both due to a undeveloped orbital septum1
1.
May present with
•	 Single eye with:2

–
swelling + redness of eyelid

–
eye pain or tenderness

–
Low grade fever

–
Ptosis (Drooping eyelid)

–
Usually follows trauma around the eye or
	›
Facial Infection
	›
Hordeolum
	›
Following upper-respiratory tract infection
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Do vital signs
•	 Screen for Sepsis
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Get rapid history:1,2

–
pain - eye, ear, facial

–
fever, malaise

–
headache - severe or persistent
	–
↓ vision, double vision

–
recent stye, trauma or insect bite to eye/eyelid

–
Bacterial sinusitis, tooth or ear infection3

–
Hib immunisation history
•	 Check head + neck, look for obvious signs of infection eg:

–
insect bite to the eye, wound, stye, infected tooth, enlarged lymph nodes,
tender sinuses3
•	 Examine both eyes. Check for:1-3
	–
↓ VA, pupil reaction to light not equal

–
degree of swelling, reduced eye opening - does this limit your ability to
examine the eye

–
painful/limited eye movements - unable to look in different directions

–
protrusion of the eyeball

4.	 Management
•	 If both eyes are swollen, or non-tender (painless) swelling in a well looking
patient, more likely to
•	 be an allergic reaction.2 Consult MO
•	 Contact MO urgently in all other cases, who may advise:
•	 if periorbital cellulitis likely AND patient otherwise well eg no fever, malaise:

–
oral antibiotics
•	 if orbital cellulitis OR severe periorbital cellulitis suspected:

–
insert IVC

–
blood cultures, FBC 1,2

–
Pus swab (For culture)

–
IV antibiotics

–
urgent evacuation ± CT scan1

–
urgent ENT + ophthalmology advice
•	 Offer analgesia. See Acute pain
Antibiotics1 MO may order:

–
oral trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole (PLUS IV cefotaxime if severe)
•	 If non remote community and NO prior history of MRSA infection:

–
oral flucloxacillin (cefalexin if mild allergy, clindamycin if immediate sensitivity)
OR

–
oral amoxicillin + clavulanic acid (if features of sinusitis, cefalexin if mild
allergy)
