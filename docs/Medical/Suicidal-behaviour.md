Suicidal behaviour – Adult/Child
Recommend
•	 Identify and respond to suicidal thoughts or behaviours early
•	 Assess risk and implement appropriate safety and management plans
•	 Consult MO for assessment, planning and ongoing care
•	 Refer all cases of suicide attempt to MO for review
Background
•	 Suicidal behaviour includes thoughts, plans, or actions intended to cause self-
harm or death
•	 It is often associated with recent stressors, mental illness, or limited psychosocial
supports
•	 Both adults and children may present with suicidal ideation or actions
•	 Timely assessment and appropriate safety planning are essential to prevent
further harm
1.
May present with¹ ²
•	 Verbalises suicidal ideas or suicidal intent
•	 Previous presentations for suicidal thoughts or deliberate self-harm
•	 A recent psychosocial stressor or loss e.g. grief, relationship breakdown, loss of
job, pregnancy or new birth
•	 Feelings of helplessness or hopelessness
2.	 Immediate management¹
•	 DRSABCD
•	 Do not leave the patient alone
•	 If the person has survived an attempted suicide or self-harm event, manage
accordingly
•	 Consider injuries consistent with self-harm attempt, e.g. for hanging, see
Traumatic injuries, Spinal injuries
•	 If the patient is highly agitated, expressing intent to suicide, aggressive or violent,
see Mental health emergency
•	 It is important to ask the person directly about feelings or thoughts of suicide
•	 Asking about self-harm does not provoke acts of self-harm
•	 Discuss the balance of confidentiality versus notifying others to ensure safety

3.	 Clinical assessment¹
•	 Ensure the patient has an opportunity to discuss their suicidality alone
•	 With consent, seek information from family, friends, support persons or others
Communication skills¹
•	 Listen. Allow the patient to speak without interruption
•	 Develop and maintain rapport
•	 Be mindful of your own values and beliefs in relation to suicide
•	 Convey warmth, non-judgemental acceptance, and interest in the patient’s
distress
•	 Be respectful and empathic
•	 Avoid minimising the seriousness of suicide risk
Ask Questions about suicidal thoughts:² ⁴
•	 Ask the person directly about suicide
•	 Asking about self-harm does not provoke self-harm⁴
•	 Discuss confidentiality versus the need to notify others for safety

Questions about suicidal thoughts¹-⁴
Suicide risk
•	 When people feel like you have been feeling, they sometimes think life is not
worth living. Have you been thinking like that?
•	 Have you been thinking of harming yourself?
•	 Are you thinking of suicide?
•	 If yes, how often?

–
Have you thought about how you would act? Is there a plan? Is it feasible and
likely to be lethal?

–
Have you thought about when you might act?

–
Are there any reasons that stop you from acting on these thoughts?
•	 Have you tried to harm yourself in the past?

–
If yes, how many times?

–
When was the most recent time?
•	 Do you know anyone who has recently attempted suicide?
•	 Do you feel safe at the moment?
If a suicide attempt has been made
•	 What did you hope would happen as a result?
•	 Did you want to die, or to end your pain?
•	 Do you regret not succeeding?
•	 Do you still have access to the method used?
•	 Did you use alcohol or drugs before the attempt? What did you use?
•	 Do you have easy access to a weapon?
Risk of harm to others
•	 Have you thought of hurting anyone else?

–
If yes, have you acted on these thoughts?
•	 Have you been in any recent fights?

–
If yes, were drugs or alcohol involved?

Determining suicidal intent
 Patientstrengths
•	 Therapeutic relationship between
clinician and patient
•	 Family warmth, support and
acceptance
•	 Willingness to access and engage
with professional help
•	 Activities/hobbies that an
individual finds meaningful
•	 Community support and cultural
identity
•	 Having children and child rearing
responsibilities •
•	 Sense of belonging and connection
•	 Skills in coping and problem
solving, conflict resolution and non-
violent ways of handling disputes
 Risk factors
•	 Mental health issues eg depression
•	 Gender (male)
•	 Relationship or family problems,
violence, abuse or custody issues,
pregnancy
•	 Substance misuse
•	 Social or geographical isolation
•	 Financial stress, unemployment,
impending court case
•	 Recent bereavement
•	 Prior suicide attempt
•	 Bullying, sexual assault, torture, or
refugee status
•	 Losing a friend or family member to
suicide
 Warning signs
•	 Hopelessness
•	 Feeling trapped
•	 Escalating substance misuse
•	 Giving away possessions
•	 Seeking ways to kill oneself ie
searching online or buying a means
•	 Withdrawing from friends, family or
society
•	 Voicing no reason for living, no
sense of purpose in life, being a
burden to others •
•	 Uncharacteristic or impaired
judgement or behaviour
•	 Verbalising or writing about
wanting to die
 Tipping points
•	 Relationship separation
•	 Loss of status or respect
•	 Recent death or suicide of relative
or friend
•	 Recent argumentat home
•	 Current abuse or bullying
•	 Debilitating physical illness,
accident or pain
Imminent risk
•	 Expressed intent to die
•	 Has plan in mind
•	 Impulsive, aggressive or anti-social
behaviour
•	 Has access to let hal means
