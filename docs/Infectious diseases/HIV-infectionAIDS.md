<h1 class="pccm-page-title">HIV infection/AIDS</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Early testing and diagnosis are essential for effective treatment and management.</li>
<li>Antiretroviral therapy (ART) is the cornerstone of treatment to suppress viral replication and prevent progression to AIDS.</li>
<li>Educate patients on prevention methods, including safe sex practices and harm reduction strategies.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) attacks the immune system, specifically the CD4+ T cells, and can lead to Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS) if untreated. HIV is transmitted through blood, sexual contact, and from mother to child during childbirth or breastfeeding.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Acute HIV infection (fever, rash, swollen lymph nodes)</li>
<li>Weight loss and fatigue</li>
<li>Recurrent infections</li>
<li>Opportunistic infections like <a href="/docs/Infectious%20diseases/Tuberculosis">tuberculosis (TB)</a> or <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Candidiasis">candidiasis</a> in advanced disease</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Begin ART as soon as HIV is diagnosed, unless contraindicated.</li>
<li>Provide supportive care for opportunistic infections.</li>
<li>Offer counseling and support services for patients and their families.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess the patient's CD4 count and viral load to determine disease progression.</li>
<li>Monitor for symptoms of opportunistic infections.</li>
<li>Screen for comorbid conditions like tuberculosis and <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Hepatitis-B">hepatitis B</a>/<a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Hepatitis-C">C</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Initiate ART, including a combination of drugs like tenofovir, emtricitabine, and efavirenz.</li>
<li>Offer prophylaxis for opportunistic infections.</li>
<li>Provide mental health support and education on adherence to treatment.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow-up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Regular follow-up visits every 3-6 months to monitor viral load, CD4 count, and adherence to ART.</li>
<li>Monitor for side effects of medications.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to a specialist for advanced HIV care or treatment of opportunistic infections.</li>
<li>Consider referral for psychosocial support or counseling services.</li>
</ul>
