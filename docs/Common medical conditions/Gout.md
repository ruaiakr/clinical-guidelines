<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Gout - Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Gout occurs from deposits of urate crystals in the body. Causes joint pain + swelling<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Painful, red + swollen joint(s) eg big toe, knee</li>
<li>Attack of gout</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>previous diagnosis, past episodes of gout + treatment eg urate lowering medicines</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Vital signs</li>
<li>Examine joint(s) for swelling, tenderness, warmth and mobility:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>hard nodules over elbows, knees + feet may indicate chronic gout<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always consider another cause of the pain. See <a href="/docs/Common medical conditions/Acutely-swollenpainful-joint">Swollen/painful joint</a></li>
<li>Consult MO if:
<ul>
<li>systemic symptoms eg ↑ HR, fever, malaise, night sweats, weight loss</li>
<li>first attack of gout (can mimic septic arthritis, an orthopaedic emergency<sup>1</sup>)</li>
<li>re-presentation for this attack</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If acute attack of previously diagnosed gout, offer short-term pain relief:<sup>1,3</sup>
<ul>
<li>NSAID eg ibuprofen or indomethacin for 3–5 days. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
<li><strong>OR</strong> colchicine as a single 1 day course (start as soon as possible)<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> May take up to 48 hours for inflammation to subside. May cause diarrhoea, nausea, abdominal discomfort, vomiting, sore throat, rash or GI bleeding. If already taking colchicine wait at least 12 hours before next dose</p>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> If renal or hepatic impairment, seek MO advice</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Blood dyscrasias, severe GI disease, corneal wounds or ulcers</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a><sup>1,3</sup></p>
</div>
