<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Chronic suppurative otitis media (CSOM) – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chronic discharging ear(s) can cause hearing impairment and disability. Occasionally serious complications can occur eg intracranial infection or <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Mastoiditis">mastoiditis</a><sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ear discharge present for ≥ 2 weeks + eardrum perforation (hole)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history as per <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Ear-assessment">Ear assessment</a>, including:
<ul>
<li>amount + duration of ear discharge</li>
<li>any concerns about hearing, speech and learning/behavioural problems, development</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Examine ears:
<ul>
<li>take swab for MCS first</li>
<li>use Tissue spears, to clean</li>
<li>document size and location of hole in eardrum eg draw picture in notes</li>
<li>if grommet - note position (still in eardrum); any granulation tissue around base</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If pus thick and you are unable to view eardrum, consider initial irrigation of ear. Use diluted Betadine® (1 part Betadine® to 20 parts warm water) followed by Tissue spears, or suctioning.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If discharge &lt; 2 weeks, treat as <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Acute-otitis-media">AOM</a>, with perforation. Unless child has history of dry perforation with intermittent discharge, in which case continue as below</li>
<li>If discharge ≥ 2 weeks<sup>3</sup>
<ul>
<li>Give ciprofloxacin ear drops 4-5 drops BD for 7 days.</li>
<li>If hole in eardrum is not visible or very small (pin hole size), advise Ear Hygiene</li>
<li>amoxicillin or Augmentin as per <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Acute-otitis-media">AOM</a>.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise:
<ul>
<li>Dry mopping (Using a tissue wick/toilet paper or Cotton with a Wooden stick applicator). BEFORE ear drops.</li>
<li>importance of continuing tissue spears + ear drops until ears dry (may be &gt; 2 weeks/long-term)</li>
<li>water precautions (when swimming/bathing) - use cotton wool in ear with Vaseline® over top,</li>
<li>hearing loss likely while discharging</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if discharge &gt; than 2 weeks with:
<ul>
<li>T &gt; 38.5 or systemically unwell</li>
<li>redness/swelling behind the ear</li>
<li>perforation in Attic area.</li>
<li>crusting or granulation tissue - in attic area or around base of grommet</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Then admit, start IV Antibiotic, eardrop (ciprofloxacin/chloramphenicol) Ear hygiene and consult or refer to ENT.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="tissue-spears">Tissue spears</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Wash hands thoroughly before and after the procedure</li>
<li>Use a clean tissue. Tear strips 1–1.5 cm wide along the length of the tissue and fold each strip in half</li>
<li>Gently pull the ear to straighten the ear canal:
<ul>
<li>Pull ‘back’ for young children</li>
<li>Pull ‘back and up’ for older children</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Gently insert the tissue spear into the ear canal. Slowly twist it in until resistance is felt, or the child cries, coughs, or blinks</li>
<li>Leave in place for approximately 30 seconds to absorb discharge</li>
<li>Remove and discard. Repeat with a fresh spear until the ear is dry</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> Clean ear(s) with Tissue spears first. The drops will only work if pushed through the hole in the eardrum using ‘tragal pumping’ (press several times on the flap of skin in front of the ear canal). Show parent/carer how to do</p>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> 1 bottle should last for 9–10 days</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a>.</p>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>review weekly until resolved + to get more ear drops</li>
<li>at each visit check:
<ul>
<li>any discharge on otoscopy (only profuse discharge is visible outside the ear)</li>
<li>teaches using ear drops correctly</li>
<li>adequate Tissue spearing</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Review 4–6 weeks after resolved for inspection of TM ± residual perforation</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer for hearing test at time of diagnosis</li>
<li>If language, learning or behavioural problems, refer to speech pathology.</li>
</ul>
</div>
