Suspected underlying disease (e.g. TB, HIV)

Require inpatient referral

Febrile Convulsions – Child
Background
•	 Febrile seizures are the most common cause of convulsions in children aged
3 months to 6 years and are associated with fever, usually from viral infections.
They are not caused by intracranial infections such as meningitis. Most febrile
seizures are short (less than 10 minutes), generalised, and self-limiting. A family
history of febrile convulsions is common. Fewer than 10% of affected children
have seizures beyond the age of 6 years or develop epilepsy.
Recommendations
•	 Always rule out meningitis or other serious infections.
•	 Treat the seizure first, then identify and manage the cause of the fever.
•	 Prevent recurrence in high-risk children with phenobarbitone prophylaxis.
1.
May present with
•	 History of fever (often due to viral respiratory infection or otitis media)
•	 Generalised seizure activity, usually lasting &lt;10 minutes
•	 No signs of neck stiffness, bulging fontanelle, or meningitis
•	 Family history of febrile seizures
2.	 Immediate management
•	 If seizing on arrival:

–
Ensure airway is clear (place child on side)

–
Do not insert spoon or spatula into mouth

–
If seizure continues >15 minutes:
	›
Give Diazepam (Valium) 0.2 mg/kg slow IV (1 ml/min). Dilute 1:1 with sterile
water, D5W, or NS.
	›
If IV access not possible, give rectally (insert syringe or shortened
nasogastric tube 4–5 cm into rectum and flush with 5 ml water).
	›
Repeat once after 10 minutes if still seizing.
	›
Do not give intramuscular Valium.
•	 If seizure persists after second Diazepam dose:

–
Give Phenobarbitone IM or Paraldehyde IM (as per dosage guidelines)
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess airway, breathing, circulation, and vital signs (including temperature)
•	 Perform full physical examination, looking for cause of fever (e.g. ear, throat,
chest)
•	 Perform neurological assessment:

–
Check for neck stiffness or bulging fontanelle

–
Attempt chin-to-chest flexion to rule out meningitis
•	 Laboratory tests are not required unless meningitis or septicaemia is suspected

4.	 Management
•	 Fever management:

–
Give Paracetamol 10–15 mg/kg oral every 4–6 hours
	›
Syrup (120 mg/5 ml) or tablets (100 mg or 500 mg) depending on weight/
age

–
Lower temperature with tepid sponging or wet towel
•	 Prevent recurrence:

–
For high-risk children (seizure >30 mins or recurrent febrile seizures), start
Phenobarbitone 3–5 mg/kg orally at night as a single dose
	›
Continue for at least 1 month after last seizure
	›
Taper gradually before stopping—do not stop suddenly
	›
Always consult a doctor before stopping
•	 Treat the underlying infection (e.g. URTI, otitis media)
5.	 Follow up
•	 Review response to treatment and monitor for recurrence
•	 Educate caregiver on fever management and seizure first aid
•	 Monitor adherence to prophylactic treatment if started
•	 Discuss medication tapering plan with doctor if seizure-free for 1 month
6.	 Referral / Consultation
•	 Always consult a doctor before:

–
Stopping prophylactic medication

–
If seizure lasts >10 minutes or recurs

–
If signs of meningitis or unclear diagnosis
