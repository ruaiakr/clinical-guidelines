<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Vaginal itchiness (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Vaginal itchiness is a common complaint and may be caused by infectious, irritant, or allergic conditions. Accurate diagnosis is important to ensure appropriate treatment and avoid unnecessary antibiotics.</li>
<li>If suspicious cervical or vulval lesions are seen, refer for visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA), colposcopy, or treatment such as LLETZ where appropriate.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Vaginal itchiness may be caused by infections such as candidiasis, bacterial vaginosis (BV), or trichomoniasis, as well as irritant contact dermatitis, atrophic vaginitis (in post-menopausal women), or rarely cervical or vulval intraepithelial neoplasia. Diagnosis is largely clinical in low-resource settings, supported by pH testing and microscopy where available.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Vaginal or vulval itching, burning, irritation</li>
<li>Vaginal discharge (may be thin, thick, white, grey, green, or yellow)</li>
<li>Odour (especially fishy, suggestive of BV)</li>
<li>Dysuria or superficial dyspareunia</li>
<li>Redness, swelling, or visible lesions on vulva or cervix</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess and reassure</li>
<li>Provide a private space for examination</li>
<li>Always consider and exclude pregnancy if relevant</li>
<li>Examine vulva and vagina with clean gloves and light source</li>
<li>Look for:
<ul>
<li>Discharge characteristics (colour, consistency, odour)</li>
<li>Vulval or vaginal redness or swelling</li>
<li>Ulcers, sores, or white plaques</li>
<li>Cervical appearance (contact bleeding, lesions, ectopy)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>Onset, duration, and severity of itching</li>
<li>Changes in discharge or odour</li>
<li>Sexual history and contraception</li>
<li>Recent antibiotic use or new products (e.g. soaps, sprays)</li>
<li>Menstrual status or menopause</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Consider vaginal pH testing if available:
<ul>
<li>pH &gt;4.5 suggests BV or trichomoniasis</li>
<li>pH ≤4.5 suggests candidiasis</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<p>Empiric treatment based on clinical suspicion if diagnostic testing not available.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="candidiasis">Suspected Candidiasis (no odour, thick white discharge, intense itching)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Clotrimazole pessary 500 mg single dose at night<br />OR</li>
<li>Miconazole 1200 mg single dose vaginal suppository<br />OR</li>
<li>Fluconazole 150 mg oral single dose (if available)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="bacterial-vaginosis">Suspected Bacterial Vaginosis (fishy odour, greyish discharge, mild irritation)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Metronidazole 400 mg orally twice daily for 7 days<br />OR</li>
<li>Metronidazole 2 g orally as a single dose (less preferred if recurrent)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="trichomoniasis">Suspected Trichomoniasis (frothy discharge, STI risk)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Metronidazole 2 g orally single dose</li>
<li>Treat sexual partner(s) as well</li>
<li>Advise to abstain from sex until both partners treated</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="irritant-dermatitis">Irritant dermatitis</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Avoid irritants (soap, douches, sprays)</li>
<li>Apply hydrocortisone cream 1% thinly to affected area twice daily for up to 7 days</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="atrophic-vaginitis">Atrophic vaginitis (postmenopausal women)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider topical oestrogen pessary (e.g. estriol 0.5 mg vaginal pessary at night for 2–4 weeks then twice weekly) if recurrent or persistent symptoms</li>
<li>Discuss with MO before prescribing</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="unclear-diagnosis">If unclear diagnosis</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider combined treatment:
<ul>
<li>Metronidazole 400 mg bd x 7 days</li>
<li>Clotrimazole 500 mg pessary single dose</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Review response in 7 days</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review in 7 days if symptoms persist</li>
<li>Reinforce STI prevention and safe sex practices</li>
<li>Advise not to douche or use irritants in the genital area</li>
<li>Counsel on recurrence prevention for candidiasis (avoid tight clothing, stay dry)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Case discussed for management with medical assistant first</li>
<li>Medical assistant needs to discuss further with the consultant oncall if unable to manage the case then to refer if not manageable on the outer islands for all gynae patients</li>
<li>Refer if:
<ul>
<li>Ulcers, lesions, or cervical abnormalities are seen</li>
<li>Persistent symptoms despite treatment</li>
<li>Suspicion of cervical or vulval neoplasia</li>
<li>Postmenopausal women with bleeding or persistent irritation</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="via">Visual inspection with acetic acid (VIA)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>VIA involves applying 3–5% acetic acid to the cervix and observing for acetowhite changes that may indicate cervical intraepithelial neoplasia</li>
<li>If suspicious lesions seen, refer for colposcopy or LLETZ treatment if available</li>
<li>See also <a href="/docs/Obstetric%20and%20Gynecological/Cervical-cancer-screening-adult-female">Cervical cancer screening adult female</a>.</li>
</ul>
