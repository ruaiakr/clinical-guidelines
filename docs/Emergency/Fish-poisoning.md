<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Fish poisoning (Ciguatera)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul>
<li>Educate patients about the risks of eating reef fish from areas known for ciguatera contamination</li>
<li>Check with local authorities to determine which fish in your area present the highest risk</li>
<li>Early management includes supportive care and symptomatic treatment for the neurological effects of poisoning</li>
<li>Advise avoidance of any further consumption of contaminated fish</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul>
<li>Ciguatera fish poisoning is caused by eating reef fish that have accumulated toxins produced by marine dinoflagellates. These toxins are commonly found in large reef fish such as barracuda, grouper, and snapper</li>
<li>The illness may present with gastrointestinal and neurological symptoms within 1 to 24 hours of consumption</li>
<li><strong>Suspected case:</strong> One or more of nausea, vomiting, or diarrhoea, and neurological signs within 24 hours of eating reef fish</li>
<li><strong>Confirmed case:</strong> Diagnosis is usually clinical but may be confirmed by detection of ciguatoxin in the consumed fish using approved testing methods</li>
<li><strong>Incubation period:</strong> Less than 1 hour to 24 hours</li>
<li><strong>Notify and investigate:</strong> One suspected case with classic neurological symptoms should trigger public health notification and investigation</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Provide symptomatic treatment:
<ul>
<li>Give antiemetics for nausea</li>
<li>Give fluids for dehydration and shock</li>
<li>Give paracetamol for pain relief</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>No specific antidote available</li>
<li>Treat supportively based on severity of symptoms</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check for dehydration and neurological symptoms (e.g. tingling, temperature reversal)</li>
<li>Measure vital signs including BP, HR, and respiratory rate</li>
<li>Assess for signs of severe poisoning such as breathing difficulty or paralysis</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Encourage frequent small sips of ORS, hot sweetened tea, fresh toddy or moimoto to maintain hydration</li>
<li>If unable to tolerate oral fluids, give IV fluids (refer to diarrhoea/vomiting and dehydration protocol)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review the patient within 24–48 hours</li>
<li>Monitor for prolonged neurological symptoms – may persist for weeks or months</li>
<li>Advise to remain well hydrated</li>
<li>Avoid physical exertion</li>
<li>Avoid eating all fish (including freshwater), alcohol, caffeine, pork, and chicken during recovery</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to a medical officer urgently if:
<ul>
<li>Difficulty breathing</li>
<li>Severe or progressive neurological symptoms</li>
<li>Signs of shock or paralysis</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
