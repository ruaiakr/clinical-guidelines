<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Trachoma - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Repeated infection by the bacterium Chlamydia trachomatis causing Conjunctivitis and possibly leading to trichiasis</li>
<li>Different strains from the STI chlamydia.</li>
<li>can lead to eyelid contraction and in-turned eyelashes that rub on the eyeball, causing painful</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Child - repeated conjunctivitis</li>
<li>Adult - in-turned eyelashes, cloudy cornea
<ul>
<li>Painful eyes</li>
<li>White or watery discharge</li>
<li>Photophobia</li>
<li>Irritated Red eye</li>
<li>Nearly always bilateral</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management Not applicable</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>previous episodes for patient and family</li>
<li>time spent in known area of outbreak</li>
<li>contact with person(s) with similar symptoms</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Examine eyes as per <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Eye-assessment">Eye assessment</a>, including:
<ul>
<li>evert the upper eyelids, look for:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>≥ 5 pale round spots (follicles)</li>
<li>intense redness, swelling - can you see normal blood vessels</li>
<li>note: follicles ± intense redness are indications of active trachoma - take eyelid swab for chlamydia PCR</li>
<li>visible scarring, in-turned eyelashes, signs of pulling out eyelashes</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>check cornea - clear or cloudy</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Surgery: For those who have entropion or Trichiasis
<ul>
<li>Epilation of in-turned lashes if surgery is not available</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Antibiotics:
<ul>
<li>Treats the Infected but also helps prevent the spread</li>
<li>Azithromycin (Zithromax)
<ul>
<li>Now the main treatment for active Trachoma</li>
<li>Single dose</li>
<li>PO once per year</li>
<li>Available in tablets and liquid (Paediatric Oral Suspension) for 6 months old - 7 years old plus children who are unable to swallow tablets.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>note: aim to treat all members of relevant household(s) within 1 week of starting treatment<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Tetracycline eye ointment
<ul>
<li>Old Treatment</li>
<li>Apply BID for 6 weeks for &lt;6 months old.</li>
<li>Treats eye infection only</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Facial Cleanliness:
<ul>
<li>Facial washing is an important component</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Environmental Improvement:
<ul>
<li>Remove the environmental risk factors for trachoma</li>
<li>Includes bringing better water supply and toilets to a community</li>
<li>May have to convince people to change their habits:
<ul>
<li>People must use the clean water made available to clean their hands and faces</li>
<li>People must not leave human and animal waste uncovered as it attracts flies</li>
<li>It is often very difficult for people to change their behaviours.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check swab result:
<ul>
<li>if Chlamydia trachomatis is identified notify Public Health Unit<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise to return if worsening or new symptoms, contact MO who may:
<ul>
<li>order swabs for MCS and Chlamydia trachomatis PCR</li>
<li>arrange ophthalmic referral if repeated infections, in-turned eyelashes or corneal changes</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
<li>Chlamydia trachomatis is a notifiable disease by pathological diagnosis</li>
</ul>
