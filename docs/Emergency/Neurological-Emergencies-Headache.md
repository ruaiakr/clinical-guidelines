<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Neurological Emergencies</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Aim to identify red flags of serious causes of headaches</li>
<li>Also see Headache - emergency management in children https://www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/qpec-statewide-guidelines/ Related Topics – Meningitis and Fitting</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Headache</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO/MA urgently if any of:<ul><li>sudden onset severe headache - ‘thunderclap headache’ or ‘worst headache of life’:</li><li>suspect subarachnoid haemorrhage</li><li>confusion, drowsiness or neurological changes</li><li>severe ↑ BP. Also see Hypertensive emergency</li></ul></li>
<li>MO/MA may advise:<ul><li>urgent evacuation</li><li>insert IVC</li><li>analgesia</li></ul></li>
<li>closely monitor:<ul><li>vital signs</li><li>conscious level. See GCS/AVPU</li><li>neurological observations</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. Clinical assessment 1-3</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history of headache:<ul><li>pain - nature, intensity, impact on normal activities</li><li>is this a ‘typical’ headache</li><li>onset - when, sudden/slow</li><li>provoking factors eg exercise, cough, sneeze, sexual activity</li><li>exacerbating factors eg erect position, exertion/valsalva, worse in mornings, food eg caffeine</li><li>other symptoms:<ul><li>fever, vomiting</li><li>aura, visual symptoms, photophobia</li><li>neck pain, facial pain/tenderness</li></ul></li><li>recent:<ul><li>head trauma</li><li>travel</li><li>infection in neck or head area</li><li>sun/heat exposure</li></ul></li><li>pregnant or recently postnatal</li><li>drug/alcohol use</li></ul></li>
<li>Past medical history:<ul><li>recent medication change</li><li>immunocompromised, cancer</li><li>hypertension</li></ul></li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:<ul><li>vital signs</li><li>GCS</li><li>check for Neck stiffness</li><li>Hydration assessment - adult, or child</li><li>pregnancy test if female of reproductive age</li></ul></li>
<li>Assess for ANY red flags</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Management 1-3</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO/MA promptly if:<ul><li>any red flags</li><li>headache is atypical or not ‘usual’ for patient</li><li>persistent headache, despite treatment with oral analgesia</li></ul></li>
<li>If pregnant assess for Preeclampsia Migraine<sup>2,4</sup></li>
<li>If child consult MO/MA</li>
<li>If adult says headache is typical of a migraine (that they have previously had) offer:<ul><li>ibuprofen with paracetamol (avoid opioids). See Acute pain</li><li>AND metoclopramide - even if not nauseous as improves absorption of analgesia. See Nausea and vomiting</li></ul></li>
<li>Consider:<ul><li>cold packs over forehead and back of skull</li><li>hot packs to neck and shoulders</li><li>neck stretches</li><li>rest in a dark room</li><li>Lifestyle habits Tension type headache 2,5</li></ul></li>
<li>Usually bilateral, feels like pressure or tightness in head, mild to moderate intensity (not severe enough to prevent walking or climbing stairs), ± photophobia, sensitivity to noise, not usually nauseous:<ul><li>offer ibuprofen ± paracetamol. See Acute pain</li><li>consider advice on Lifestyle habits Lifestyle habits - to help prevent migraines and tension type headaches 4,5</li></ul></li>
<li>Regular sleep schedule</li>
<li>Minimise variations in BGL eg eat regular meals, avoid excess sugary food/drinks</li>
<li>Stay hydrated eg drink 1.5–2 L of water/day</li>
<li>Limit caffeinated drinks to 1–2 cups/day</li>
<li>Regular exercise</li>
<li>Use relaxation techniques to manage stress eg mindfulness, yoga, breathing techniques</li>
<li>Avoid known triggers for patient eg alcohol, citrus fruit, chocolate, preserved meats, perfume</li>
<li>If posture problems or neck muscle tightness, refer to a physiotherapist</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated, advise to return if headache does not improve or if concerned</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO as above</li>
<li>Might need Medical Clinic referral</li>
</ul>
