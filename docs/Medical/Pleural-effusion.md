# Pleural effusion - Adult

Pleural effusion - Adult
Recommend
Pleural effusion is a potentially serious condition that may indicate infection,
heart failure, or malignancy. Evaluation of the underlying cause and referral for
definitive management is essential. If an underlying lung infection is suspected, treat
empirically for pneumonia. Provide oxygen support if SpO₂ is less than 94%.
Background
Pleural effusion is the collection of fluid in the pleural space surrounding the lungs.
It commonly occurs due to tuberculosis, congestive heart failure, malignancy, or
bacterial infections. Symptoms can be subtle but may include cough, shortness of
breath, and chest discomfort. Diagnosis requires clinical suspicion and confirmation
via chest examination or imaging.
1.
May present with
•	 Shortness of breath, especially on exertion
•	 Chest heaviness or discomfort
•	 Cough, which may be dry or productive
•	 Fever or night sweats, especially if due to infection or TB
•	 General fatigue
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Assess airway and breathing
•	 Support the patient in a comfortable sitting position
•	 Administer oxygen if SpO₂ is less than 94%
•	 Take history:
	›
Duration of symptoms
	›
History of TB, heart failure, or cancer
	›
Night sweats, weight loss, recent illness
•	 Physical examination:
	›
Reduced chest movement on affected side
	›
Dullness to percussion over fluid
	›
Decreased or absent breath sounds
	›
Reduced vocal fremitus
	›
Possible tracheal deviation in large effusions
•	 Check for associated signs:
	›
Lymphadenopathy
	›
Peripheral oedema (suggestive of heart failure)
	›
Signs of systemic disease
