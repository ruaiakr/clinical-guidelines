<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Cord Presentation and Cord Prolapse (Kiribati Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Umbilical cord complications occur when the cord lies below or alongside the presenting part. This can lead to compression of the cord, reducing or stopping blood flow to the fetus. Cord prolapse, in particular, is a life-threatening emergency and requires immediate intervention to prevent fetal hypoxia and death. These events most commonly occur after rupture of membranes and are more likely when the presenting part is not well engaged.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommended</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Umbilical cord complications are obstetric emergencies due to risk of fetal hypoxia and death from cord compression. Immediate action is required to relieve pressure on the cord and arrange urgent delivery.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="definitions">Definition (important clinical distinctions)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Overt cord presentation</strong> – the umbilical cord lies below the presenting part but the membranes are still intact</li>
<li><strong>Occult cord presentation (occult prolapse)</strong> – the cord lies alongside the presenting part and is not visible or felt, but may cause fetal distress</li>
<li><strong>Cord prolapse</strong> – the cord passes through the cervix below the presenting part after rupture of membranes and may be felt or seen in the vagina or outside the introitus
<ul>
<li>Cord prolapse is an immediate emergency requiring urgent delivery</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Cord felt during vaginal examination</li>
<li>Cord visible at introitus or protruding from vagina</li>
<li>Sudden fetal distress (abnormal or bradycardic fetal heart rate)</li>
<li>Change after rupture of membranes</li>
<li>In occult cases: fetal distress without visible cord</li>
<li>Reduced or absent fetal movements (late sign)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help immediately</li>
<li>Always call the hospital and discuss the case with the obstetrician immediately</li>
<li>Explain the situation clearly to the woman and obtain verbal consent where possible</li>
<li>Position the woman to relieve cord compression:
<ul>
<li>Knee-chest position (preferred if feasible)</li>
<li>OR left lateral (exaggerated Sims position) with pillows under hips</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<figure class="pccm-flowchart-figure">
<img src="/img/obstetric-gynecological/cord-prolapse-positioning.png" alt="Maternal positioning for cord prolapse: knee-chest position and left lateral (Sims) position with hips elevated" />
<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Figure 2: Knee-chest position and Sims (left lateral) position</p>
</figure>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Insert a gloved hand into the vagina and:
<ul>
<li>Gently lift the presenting part off the cord</li>
<li>Maintain this position continuously until delivery or handover</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do NOT handle, pull, or compress the cord</li>
<li>If cord is outside the vagina:
<ul>
<li>Cover with sterile gauze soaked in warm saline</li>
<li>Prevent drying and vasospasm</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Administer oxygen if available</li>
<li>Stop oxytocin infusion immediately if running</li>
<li>Prepare for urgent delivery or transfer</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check fetal heart rate (continuous if possible)</li>
<li>Assess if cord is pulsating (indicates fetal viability)</li>
<li>Confirm rupture of membranes</li>
<li>Assess cervical dilation if skilled to do so</li>
<li>Estimate time to delivery vs transfer</li>
<li>Assess maternal condition</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Maintain continuous manual elevation of presenting part</li>
<li>Keep woman in position that reduces cord compression</li>
<li>Minimise handling and vaginal examinations</li>
<li>If delivery is imminent:
<ul>
<li>Prepare for assisted vaginal delivery</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If delivery is NOT imminent:
<ul>
<li>Arrange urgent transfer to hospital</li>
<li>Ensure continuous decompression of cord during transfer</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If delay expected:
<ul>
<li>Insert urinary catheter</li>
<li>Fill bladder with 500–700 mL normal saline to elevate presenting part</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Continue oxygen and monitoring</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continuous monitoring of fetal condition</li>
<li>Monitor maternal vital signs</li>
<li>Document:
<ul>
<li>Time of diagnosis</li>
<li>Fetal heart rate</li>
<li>Interventions performed</li>
<li>Time of referral</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral and consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>This is an obstetric emergency – immediate referral required</li>
<li>Always contact hospital and obstetrician immediately</li>
<li>Arrange urgent transport</li>
<li>Maintain all interventions during transfer</li>
<li>Ensure skilled escort if available</li>
</ul>
</div>
