# Pneumonia - Adult

Pneumonia - Adult
Recommend
•	 Pneumonia is a common and potentially serious lung infection requiring timely
recognition and appropriate management.
•	 Antibiotic treatment should be initiated early in suspected cases.
•	 Patients with moderate or severe pneumonia may require referral or
hospitalisation.
Background
Pneumonia is an acute infection of the lung parenchyma, often presenting with
cough, fever, pleuritic chest pain, and shortness of breath. It is caused by a range of
pathogens, including bacteria, viruses, and fungi, and its severity can vary widely.
Risk factors include chronic illness, advanced age, recent travel, hazardous alcohol
use, and exposure to environmental sources (e.g. muddy soil/water).
1.
May present with
•	 Pleuritic chest pain
•	 Fever, rigors
•	 Cough with sputum production, shortness of breath
•	 Fatigue, muscle aches or generalised body pain
2.	 Immediate management Clinical assessment
•	 •
Take detailed history, including:

–
Chest pain, fever, rigors, night sweats

–
Cough and sputum characteristics (purulent or coloured)

–
Shortness of breath, increased respiratory rate

–
Existing chronic illnesses (e.g. diabetes, CKD, CLD), immunocompromised
status

–
Hazardous alcohol intake

–
Recent travel or exposure to muddy soil or water
•	 Physical examination:

–
Observe work of breathing

–
Listen for decreased air entry, wheeze, crackles (do they clear with cough?)

–
Assess for dullness on percussion

–
Inspect skin for ulcers, abscesses, non-healing sores
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Contact MO in all cases for further advice

–
May recommend antibiotics, sputum for MCS ± PCR, IV fluids, or evacuation
