# Gastrointestinal emergencies Acute abdominal pain - Adult/Child

Gastrointestinal

Gastrointestinal emergencies
Acute abdominal pain - Adult/Child
Recommend
•	 Always consider:

–
Ectopic pregnancy, in all females of reproductive age with abdominal pain ±
bleeding1

–
PID, in sexually active females, particularly if &lt; 30 years, with new onset of
pelvic pain1

–
Testicular torsion, and check testes in males with abdominal pain + male
infants with inconsolable crying2
Background

–
If child, also see QPEC guidelines Acute abdominal pain - emergency
management in children

start treatment for Shock

	›

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 It is not necessary to make a definitive diagnosis. It is more important to
recognise cases which are significant
•	 If child, also see Child with abdominal pain
•	 Ask about the pain:4

–
Site - where is it, ask patient to pinpoint location

–
Onset:
	›
gradual, rapid or sudden
	›
continuous or intermittent
	›
what were they doing when it started

–
Character - sharp, dull, burning, stabbing, cramp like, crushing, tingling

–
Radiate - anywhere eg shoulder tip, back

–
Alleviating factors eg sitting up, medicine, relief by moving about eg renal
colic, or from lying very still eg peritonitis

–
Timing:
	›
when did it first begin, duration
	›
have they had it before, when, what happened
	›
any increase in severity

–
Exacerbating factors - does anything make it worse eg movement

–
Severity - mild, moderate or severe (scale 1–10):
	›
does it interfere with sleep or normal activities
•	 Ask about:4

–
associated symptoms eg fever, SOB, headache, nausea, vomiting - any blood,
bile or green vomit - does it look/smell like faeces

–
recent weight loss, changes in appetite, food refusal/poor feeding6

–
bowels - any diarrhoea, constipation, blood, black stools/melaena, time of last
motion, passing wind1

–
urine - dysuria, frequency, urgency, haematuria1

–
vaginal - bleeding, discharge. Note: abnormal discharge may indicate PID

–
possible causes of pain, including:
	›
foreign body ingestion + consider Button battery6
	›
poisoning eg venoms, paracetamol, lead, iron5
	›
recent trauma eg abdominal, scrotal2
•	 Get past history, including:7,8

–
medical + surgical

–
smoking, alcohol + other drugs

–
medications + family

–
menstrual - last period, was it normal, are periods regular, any contraception1

Do physical examination, including:
Red flags from abdominal examination - if any contact MO urgently
•	 Pain radiating to the back + palpable pulsatile mass - could be AAA4
•	 Localised tenderness + distension
•	 Guarding -↑muscle tightness in response to palpation or board like rigidity
•	 Rebound tenderness - pain when pressure is applied + then released suddenly
•	 Pain disproportionate to presentation/working diagnosis
•	 Abdominal examination:3-5,8

–
look for:

–
symmetry, shape eg distended or sunken
	›
surgical scars, bruises, distended veins, hernias
	›
signs of non-accidental injury

–
listen for bowel sounds - absent, decreased or hyperactive:
	›
normal are low pitched + gurgling 2–5/minute. Hyperactive/high pitched
suggests Bowel obstruction

–
palpate - start lightly, away from site of pain + move through the 9 areas.
Follow with deep palpation:
	›
palpate kidneys - with 1 hand underneath + 1 hand above (below rib line),
gently push down
	›
check testes/groin in males2

–
percuss (tap) all 4 quadrants to check for dullness
•	 Listen to chest - any wheeze, crackles. Note: pneumonia + ACS, can present with
abdominal pain
•	 ECG
•	 Urinalysis + pregnancy test if reproductive age - if +ve go to Ectopic pregnancy
•	 BGL - if > 10 check for ketones + consider DKA
•	 Bloods/i-STAT including lactate, bladder scan if available
•	 Any jaundice, enlarged lymph nodes
•	 Hydration assessment - adult, or child
•	 Rectal examination - may be indicated if suspected Upper GI bleeding

Abdominal quadrants
4.	 Management8
•	 Consult MO in all cases if:

–
infant/child, pregnant woman or > 60 years

–
moderate to severe pain

–
cause of pain not known
•	 MO may order:

–
chest + abdominal x-ray

–
urgent retrieval/hospitalisation

–
antibiotics, IDC, NGT, nil by mouth
5.	 Follow up4,5
•	 If not retrieved, advise patient/carer to be reviewed next day, or sooner if:

–
pain persists > 24 hours, worsens or changes from generalised to localised, or

–
worsening symptoms or new symptoms develop eg vomiting, fever

–
if any of the above, contact MO
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 As above
