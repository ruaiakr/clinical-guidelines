<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Common Conditions Requiring IV Fluids</h1>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>KEEP VEIN OPEN</strong><br />Dextrose Water is often used to "keep vein open." It is NOT used for ongoing fluid requirements as it doesn't contain sodium and other electrolytes.</li>
<li><strong>DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS</strong><br />Normal Saline is used as the first IV fluid for diabetic ketoacidosis. As the blood sugar is lowered, the fluid is later changed to Dextrose Water. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Diabetic-ketoacidosis">Diabetic ketoacidosis</a>.</li>
<li><strong>HEMORRHAGIC OR HYPOVOLEMIC SHOCK</strong><br />Use either Normal Saline or Hartmann’s solution, in combination with Haemaccel or blood. Dextrose water does not maintain the blood volume and is not used in shock unless there are no other IV fluids. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a>.</li>
<li><strong>DEHYDRATION</strong><br />Use either Hartmann’s or Normal Saline. Potassium may need to be added to IV fluids after the initial rapid rehydration. See <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Gastroenteritisdehydration">Gastroenteritis/dehydration</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="regulating-iv-flow-rates">Regulating IV Flow Rates</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Each IV infusion set has a DRIP CHAMBER. Drip chambers have different amounts of DROPS/ML (drops/ml = drip factor). IV sets in Kiribati have either 15 drops/ml (adult set) or 60 microdrops/ml (paediatric sets). Check the set to ensure you know the DRIP FACTOR or DROPS/ML.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="flow-rate-calculation">Flow Rate Calculation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>TOTAL INFUSION VOLUME (ml) ÷ TOTAL INFUSION TIME = ml/hour</li>
<li>TOTAL INFUSION VOLUME (ml) × DRIP FACTOR ÷ TOTAL TIME OF INFUSION (mins) = Rate in Drops/Minute</li>
</ul>
<p><em>Example</em></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>1200 ml of normal saline to be infused in 6 hours. IV set has drip factor of 15 drops/ml.</li>
<li>ml/hour = 1200 ÷ 6 = 200 ml/hour</li>
<li>Drops/minute = 1200 ml × 15 ÷ 360 mins = 50 drops/minute</li>
<li>OR calculate by the hour: 200 ml × 15 ÷ 60 minutes = 50 drops/minute</li>
<li>Paediatric Sets with DRIP FACTORS OF 60. The number of drops/minute is the same as ml/hour.</li>
<li>If 400 ml of Dextrose Water is to run for 8 hours (paediatric set with a drip factor of 60): 400 ml × 60 ÷ 480 mins = 50 drops/minute (and IV infusion rate = 50 ml/hour)</li>
</ul>
