Hepatitis D – Adult/Child (Kiribati Original Protocol)
Recommend
•	 All patients with advanced liver disease should be assessed for HDV
•	 All patients who are HBV-positive should be screened for HDV (Anti-HDV test)
and linked to care
•	 Patients with an acute flare of hepatitis should be considered for screening for
anti-HDV
Background
•	 Hepatitis D (delta) virus (HDV) is endemic in Kiribati
•	 HDV is an enveloped RNA virus
•	 It is an incomplete (satellite) virus that depends on Hepatitis B virus (HBV) for its
survival – causing HBV/HDV co-infection
•	 Elimination of HDV is closely associated tied with HBV elimination – via birth
dose vaccination and preventative practices
•	 Treatment for HDV exists but is currently unavailable in Kiribati
•	 All HBV/HDV co-infected patients should be offered antiviral therapy for HBV
1.
May present with
•	 Acute HDV infection of an HBV-positive patient can be asymptomatic
•	 If present:

–
Jaundice, fatigue, anorexia, nausea

–
Vomiting, upper abdominal pain
•	 Laboratory findings:

–
Positive HBsAg

–
Positive anti-HDV

–
Positive HDV RNA

–
Elevated ALT
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Not applicable

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 History:

–
HbsAg-positive, tattooing, blood exposure, shared razors

–
Contact with HBV-positive individuals / mother
•	 Observations:

–
Full vitals (Q-ADDS/CEWT)

–
Urinalysis for bilirubin/urobilinogen
•	 Examination:

–
Jaundice, hepatomegaly, RUQ tenderness
•	 Blood tests:

–
LFTs, INR, UEC – calculate APRI-score

–
HDV RNA (viral load)

–
HIV, HBV serology
•	 In pregnant women:

–
Perform routine hepatitis B screening

–
Coordinate with Hepatitis Clinic / ANC for follow-up

–
Due to MTCT risk for HDV is believed to be lower in children, screening is
essential for maternal counselling and postnatal infant follow-up
4.	 Management
•	 Consult MO for all suspected cases
•	 Urgent referral if INR ≥ 1.5 or if systemically unwell
•	 Supportive treatment only for acute phase
•	 Bed rest advised if jaundiced
•	 Educate patient and contacts:

–
Avoid sharing injecting equipment, razors, toothbrushes

–
Do not donate blood

–
Avoid paracetamol
•	 Vaccinate against hepatitis A – if unvaccinated
•	 Contact tracing with HbsAg-screening and linkage to care
•	 HDV infection requires treatment with immunoglobulins (pegylated interferon)
and antivirals (Bulevirtide) – consult MO/Hepatitis Program
•	 Pregnant women:

–
Ensure HBV-positive pregnant mothers are treated with prophylaxis antivirals
(Tenofovir) from 24 weeks gestation onwards – see Hepatitis B

–
Provide education and linkage to early infant screening

–
Ensure all HBV-positive mothers are linked to care with Hepatitis Program
