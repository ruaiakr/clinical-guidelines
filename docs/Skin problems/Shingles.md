<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Shingles – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start antiviral treatment early (within 72 hours) to reduce the risk of complications. Manage pain and prevent spread to others. Refer urgently if face, eye, pregnancy, child, or immunocompromised.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Shingles (Herpes Zoster) is caused by reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus (VZV) in a person who previously had chickenpox. It is more common in people over 50 years. While usually self-limiting, it can lead to serious complications such as persistent pain (post-herpetic neuralgia), blindness, pneumonia, hearing loss, encephalitis, or death.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Painful rash developing into small blisters within 24–48 hours
<ul>
<li>Usually affects one side of the body along a nerve pathway</li>
<li>Crusts over within 5 days</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>48–72 hours before the rash appears, patient may report:
<ul>
<li>Headache, photophobia, malaise</li>
<li>Itching, tingling, or severe pain in the affected area</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Take a history, including:
<ul>
<li>Description of rash</li>
<li>Other signs and symptoms</li>
<li>History of chickenpox</li>
<li>Immunocompromised status</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check vital signs</li>
<li>Examine the skin – see Skin assessment – adult or child</li>
<li>Take a swab of lesion for VZV PCR if available</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO promptly if:
<ul>
<li>Patient is a child</li>
<li>Rash is on the face or eye (may require evacuation and ophthalmology review)
<ul>
<li>MO may consider early acyclovir eye ointment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Patient is immunocompromised or pregnant</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>For pain relief:
<ul>
<li>Offer paracetamol – see <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute Pain</a></li>
<li>Ice packs and protective dressings may help</li>
<li>Lidocaine (lignocaine) ointment or similar may be used for short-term relief</li>
<li>If severe pain and not relieved by paracetamol, consult MO – they may prescribe oral prednisolone and/or amitriptyline</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If rash began less than 72 hours ago, give:</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-box" id="acyclovir">

<span class="pccm-drug-name">ACYCLOVIR</span>

</div>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Authority:</strong> RN must consult MO. RIPRN may proceed.</p>
<p><strong>Safety notes:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Drink at least 1.5L of fluids per day</li>
<li>May cause dizziness or confusion</li>
<li>If renal impairment, seek MO advice</li>
<li>Pregnancy: Acyclovir is preferred and may be used from 36 weeks gestation</li>
<li>Contraindication: Allergy to acyclovir or valaciclovir</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a></p>
</div>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If secondary skin infection present, treat as for <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Impetigo-Child-Adult">Impetigo</a>, or Cellulitis</li>
<li>Provide education on preventing spread – see Shingles Advice below</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="shingles-advice">Shingles Advice</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Cover the rash if possible
<ul>
<li>If child cannot keep rash covered, exclude from school until no new blisters for 24 hours</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Avoid touching or scratching the rash</li>
<li>Wash hands often</li>
<li>Avoid contact with the following people until rash has crusted over:
<ul>
<li>Pregnant women who have not had chickenpox or the vaccine (shingles is not usually harmful to unborn baby)</li>
<li>Premature or low birth weight babies</li>
<li>Children who have not had chickenpox or the vaccine</li>
<li>People with weakened immune systems (e.g. chemotherapy, transplant recipients, HIV)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Zoster vaccine is still recommended at the appropriate age</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review at next MO clinic</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If patient is not improving, consult Skin Clinical Nurses or Skin Doctor at TCH via phone or Messenger group chat</li>
<li>Seek urgent approval for referral if severe or worsening</li>
</ul>
