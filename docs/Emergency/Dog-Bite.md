<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Dog Bite – all ages (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">rabies (if rabies is endemic).</p>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Dog bites can cause serious injury and infection, including tetanus, sepsis, and</li>
<li>Early assessment and wound care are essential to reduce complications.</li>
<li>Tetanus toxoid (TT) should be given based on immunisation history and wound risk.</li>
<li>In rabies-endemic areas, rabies post-exposure prophylaxis may be needed.</li>
<li>Bitten by a dog (domestic or wild), may or may not break the skin</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Skin puncture, laceration or crush injury from dog bite</li>
<li>Bleeding, swelling, redness, or signs of infection (pain, pus, fever)</li>
<li>History of animal attack or close contact with saliva through broken skin</li>
<li>Anxiety or distress, especially in children</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Apply basic first aid: control bleeding and clean the wound with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes</li>
<li>Remove any foreign material if visible</li>
<li>Cover with a clean dressing</li>
<li>Reassure the patient and caregiver</li>
<li>Urgently assess TT status (see table on following pages)</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment<sup>1,2,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Obtain history:<ul><li>Time and location of the bite</li><li>Whether dog is domestic or stray, vaccinated, or known to the patient</li><li>Provoked or unprovoked attack</li><li>TT and rabies immunisation history<ul><li>Assess wound:</li></ul></li><li>Site, depth, contamination, and evidence of infection</li><li>High-risk sites: face, hands, feet, genitals<ul><li>Do full vital signs</li><li>Consider mental health impact (particularly in children) Consider the Dunbar Scale to assess the levl of bite severity and whether the patient needs review by the surgical team: An assessment of the severity of biting problems based on an objective evaluation of wound pathology</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>Level 1. Obnoxious or aggressive behavior but no skin-contact by teeth.</li>
<li>Level 2. Skin-contact by teeth but no skin-puncture. However, may be skin nicks (less than one tenth of an inch deep) and slight bleeding caused by forward or lateral movement of teeth against skin, but no vertical punctures.</li>
<li>Level 3. One to four punctures from a single bite with no puncture deeper than half the length of the dog’s canine teeth. Maybe lacerations in a single direction, caused by victim pulling hand away, owner pulling dog away, or gravity (little dog jumps, bites and drops to floor).</li>
<li>Level 4. One to four punctures from a single bite with at least one puncture deeper than half the length of the dog’s canine teeth. May also have deep bruising around the wound (dog held on for N seconds and bore down) or lacerations in both directions (dog held on and shook its head from side to side).</li>
<li>Level 5. Multiple-bite incident with at least two Level 4 bites or multiple-attack incident with at least one Level 4 bite in each.</li>
<li>Level 6. Victim dead.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<p><strong>Antibiotics</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>All moderate or severe bites, or if patient is at high risk of infection:</li>
<li>Amoxicillin + Clavulanate<ul><li>Child: 20–40 mg/kg/dose (based on amoxicillin component) PO bd</li><li>Adult: 875 mg/125 mg PO bd</li><li>Duration: 5 days, extend to 7 days if signs of infection</li></ul></li>
<li>If penicillin-allergic: doxycycline + metronidazole (consult MO) Wound care</li>
<li>Clean thoroughly with saline after initial soap/water wash</li>
<li>Do not suture unless wound is large and not grossly contaminated (consult MO)</li>
<li>Update tetanus immunisation (see table)</li>

<p><strong>Follow up</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review in 2 days to check wound healing and signs of infection</li>
<li>Advise patient to return earlier if:<ul><li>Fever, increasing redness/swelling</li><li>Pus or worsening pain</li><li>Reduced mobility of affected limb<ul><li>Continue dressing changes as needed</li><li>Document TT administration and wound progression</li></ul></li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Urgent referral to MO if:<ul><li>Deep wounds or high-risk site (face, hand, joint)</li><li>Clinical signs of sepsis or systemic illness</li><li>Suspected rabies exposure</li><li>Consider surgical consult if wound is not healing or needs closure</li></ul></li>
</ul>
