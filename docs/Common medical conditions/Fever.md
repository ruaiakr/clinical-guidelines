<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Fever – Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever in adults may have a wide range of causes. A careful clinical history and examination can help narrow the differential diagnosis</li>
<li>If fever persists for &gt;3 weeks without diagnosis, and evaluation has failed to identify a cause, the condition may be classified as “fever of unknown origin” (FUO)</li>
<li>Refer to medical officer if patient is clinically unwell, deteriorating, or the cause of fever remains unclear</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever is a common symptom in adults and may reflect infections, malignancies, autoimmune conditions, or other systemic disorders. Persistent or unexplained fever, particularly when accompanied by weight loss, night sweats, or localising symptoms, warrants systematic evaluation. Clinical features may help distinguish between infectious and non-infectious causes, such as malignancy or autoimmune disease</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with <em>(see table below)</em></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever (subjective or documented ≥38°C)</li>
<li>Chills, night sweats, weight loss, anorexia</li>
<li>Localised pain (headache, abdominal pain, back pain, sore throat)</li>
<li>Fatigue, generalised weakness</li>
<li>Cough, hemoptysis</li>
<li>Rash, myalgias, joint pain</li>
<li>Urinary symptoms</li>
<li>Neurological symptoms (confusion, headache, visual changes)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess and stabilise airway, breathing, circulation (ABC) if acutely unwell</li>
<li>Monitor vital signs: temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation</li>
<li>Commence IV fluids if hypotensive or dehydrated</li>
<li>Administer antipyretics (e.g. paracetamol 1 g orally every 6 hours if needed)</li>
<li>Isolate patient if suspicion of a contagious infection (e.g. tuberculosis, typhoid, viral hemorrhagic fever)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Obtain detailed history:
<ul>
<li>Onset and duration of fever</li>
<li>Associated symptoms (see above)</li>
<li>Recent travel, exposures (e.g. TB contacts, animals, unsafe food or water)</li>
<li>Immunocompromised status (HIV, diabetes, steroid use)</li>
<li>Vaccination history</li>
<li>Medication history</li>
<li>Menstrual and sexual history in women</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Perform physical examination:
<ul>
<li>Look for lymphadenopathy, jaundice, rashes, hepatosplenomegaly</li>
<li>Cardiac exam: new murmurs (suggestive of endocarditis)</li>
<li>Respiratory exam: crackles, reduced air entry (TB or pneumonia)</li>
<li>Neurological exam: meningism, confusion</li>
<li>Abdominal exam: tenderness, guarding, rebound, ascites</li>
<li>Genital and rectal exam as appropriate</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Treat empirically only if a likely diagnosis is made or patient is severely ill</li>
<li>Common syndromes and actions:
<ul>
<li>Suspected malaria: Refer and commence antimalarials if rapid test or thick smear is positive</li>
<li>Suspected pneumonia: Consider antibiotics (e.g. amoxicillin or doxycycline)</li>
<li>Suspected TB: Refer for sputum testing and evaluation</li>
<li>Suspected UTI: Treat with appropriate antibiotics (e.g. nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim)</li>
<li>Suspected typhoid: Ceftriaxone IV or azithromycin orally</li>
<li>Suspected endocarditis: Refer urgently for medical review and blood cultures</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Provide supportive care: fluids, nutrition, rest</li>
<li>Monitor progress daily</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review in 2–3 days or sooner if symptoms worsen</li>
<li>If no improvement after 5–7 days of empirical treatment, consult medical officer</li>
<li>Investigate further if fever continues beyond 2 weeks</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<p>Refer to a doctor or hospital if:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever &gt;7 days with no clear cause</li>
<li>Severe or rapidly worsening symptoms</li>
<li>Suspected endocarditis, meningitis, or TB</li>
<li>Known malignancy or immunocompromised patient</li>
<li>Need for further laboratory investigations or imaging</li>
</ul>
