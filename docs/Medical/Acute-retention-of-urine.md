Acute retention of urine - Adult
Background
•	 Predominantly affects men. Most common cause is obstruction eg enlarged
prostate. Can also be caused from infection, constipation, inflammation or injury1
1.
May present with1,2
•	 Inability to urinate or empty bladder effectively ± suprapubic pain, bloating,
urgency, distress
2.	 Immediate management1-3
•	 Do vital signs
•	 Screen for Sepsis
•	 Offer analgesia. See Acute pain
3.	 Clinical assessment1-3
•	 See Abdominal pain
•	 Also ask about:

–
worsening urinary symptoms eg nocturia, frequency, urgency, weak stream,
dribbling

–
systemic symptoms eg T ≥ 38, chills, sweats

–
previous episodes, prostate or bladder problems

–
prolapse of the bladder, rectum or uterus - in females

–
recent pelvic trauma, STI, IDC

–
nausea, vomiting, constipation

–
medications eg anticholinergic, antidepressants

–
recent weight loss
•	 Do physical examination, including:

–
abdomen for distension and palpable bladder

–
check for swollen lymph nodes

–
urinalysis if possible + MSU for MCS or collect from IDC if advised to insert

–
bladder scan if available

4.	 Management1,3
•	 Contact MO/Surgeon, who may advise:

–
insert IDC:
	›
if likely difficult catheterisation, urgent retrieval for urological review/
suprapubic catheter
	›
DO NOT use force to push IDC through the obstructed urethra

–
If it is still can’t and patient is really in distress – consult surgeon: Need
temporary urine aspiration or cystostomy ( need guidance from the surgeon)

–
Collect urine for urine test – dipstick urine test

–
Start antibiotic – Ampicillin and Gentamycin
•	 Monitor - urine output, for haematuria, transient hypotension, and post-
obstructive diuresis
5.	 Follow up
•	 If not retrieved, always close monitor and update the surgeon concerned.
•	 Advise to see next MO clinic
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 As above
