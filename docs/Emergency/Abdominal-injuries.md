Blunt abdominal trauma is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality
among all age groups. Identification of serious intra-abdominal pathology
is often challenging; many injuries may not manifest during the initial
assessment and treatment period.

Blunt Abdominal Injury:
•	 History taking and identifying the mechanism of abdominal injury is crucial in
establishing the severity of the abdominal injury.
•	 Two major life-threatening conditions result from blunt abdominal injury

I.
Internal bleeding from ruptured major vessels or organs

II.
Leakage from visceral content from perforated viscous – peritoneal
contamination.
Signs and symptoms
The initial clinical assessment of patients with blunt abdominal trauma is often
difficult and notably inaccurate. The most reliable signs and symptoms in alert
patients are as follows:
•	 Pain
•	 Tenderness
•	 Gastrointestinal hemorrhage
•	 Hypovolemia
•	 Evidence of peritoneal irritation (board like abdomen/rigid and positive rebound
tenderness)
If the patient had the above symptoms – warrant admission for close monitoring
and to reassess regularly
Management:
•	 Admit
•	 Do primary survey DRSCABCD as per ATLS
•	 If hypotensive and tachycardia – insert 2 large IV cannulas
•	 Resuscitate with IV fluid crytalloid fuids (give Boluses of fluid if hypotensive)
•	 Insert IDC to monitor and assess the patients response to IVF resuscitation
