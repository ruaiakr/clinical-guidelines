<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Tinea versicolor (Pityriasis versicolor) – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Treat tinea versicolor with antifungal shampoo. Reassure that treatment is for cosmetic purposes. Refer if no improvement after 2 weeks.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Tinea versicolor is a superficial fungal (yeast) infection that causes changes in skin pigmentation. It is more common in adolescents and young adults, particularly in tropical climates and among people who sweat heavily. It is not dangerous and treatment is primarily for cosmetic appearance.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Flat patches of hyperpigmented or hypopigmented skin – may appear pink, tan, brown, or red
<ul>
<li>In dark-skinned individuals, often causes light patches</li>
<li>May have fine scales on the surface</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Common on chest, back, neck, and upper arms (not face)</li>
<li>Common in adolescents and young adults living in tropical or humid climates</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Take a history</li>
<li>Perform vital signs</li>
<li>Examine the skin – see Skin assessment – adult, or Skin assessment – child</li>
<li>Consider other diagnoses:
<ul>
<li><a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Tinea">Tinea/ringworm</a></li>
<li>Secondary syphilis</li>
<li><a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Leprosy">Leprosy</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Treat with antifungal shampoo:</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-box" id="ketoconazole">
<div class="pccm-drug-header">
<span class="pccm-drug-name">KETOCONAZOLE</span>
<span class="pccm-drug-level">L3</span>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap" style="margin:0; overflow-x:auto;">
<table class="pccm-drug-table">
<tr><th>FORM</th><th>STRENGTH</th><th>ROUTE</th><th>DOSE</th><th>DURATION</th></tr>
<tr>
<td>Shampoo</td>
<td>—</td>
<td>Topical</td>
<td>Apply to skin patch, leave for 10 minutes, then rinse</td>
<td>2–4 times per week for 4 weeks</td>
</tr>
</table>
</div>
<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Authority:</strong> RN may proceed. RN may administer</p>
<p><strong>Safety advice:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>May cause burning, stinging, itching, or redness</li>
<li>A single application may be effective in some cases</li>
<li>Avoid during pregnancy; safe during breastfeeding</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Additional advice:
<ul>
<li>Wash the whole body with the antifungal shampoo, including hair</li>
<li>Caused by Malassezia yeast, normally present on the skin</li>
<li>Treatment is only for cosmetic reasons</li>
<li>It may take weeks or months for normal skin colour to return</li>
<li>Recurrence is common – repeated treatment may be needed</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="management-of-associated-emergency">Management of associated emergency</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Review in 2 weeks</li>
<li>If no improvement, refer to the Skin Clinic</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Discuss the case first with Skin Clinical Nurses via phone or messenger</li>
<li>They will inform the Skin Doctor, or you may contact the Doctor directly if available</li>
</ul>
