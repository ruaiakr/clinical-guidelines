<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Chromoblastomycosis</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>is a subcutaneous fungal infection caused by one of several dematiaceous (pigmented) fungi. Chromoblastomycosis is officially recognized by the World Health Organization (WHO) as a neglected tropical disease (NTD).</li>
<li>The fungus is found in soil, plants, and decaying wood. Splinters and traumatic penetration are common sources of inoculation, occurring about 1-2 months before the development of skin lesions</li>
<li>It is typically found on the lower extremities of middle-aged men in rural areas of tropical and subtropical climates.</li>
<li>It is a locally progressive disease, usually evolving very slowly over many years without many associated symptoms. Early on, there may be an erythematous papule or a warty plaque. This gradually enlarges if untreated and may become annular, vegetating, or nodular. Lymphatic and cutaneous dissemination have been described</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">1. Management</h2>
<p>Therapy options include oral antifungal agents, depending on sensitivities, alone or in combination with cryotherapy:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Itraconazole 200-400 mg by mouth daily for 6-12 months (with or without flucytosine 30 mg/kg 4 times daily), or</li>
<li>Terbinafine 500-1000 mg by mouth daily for 6-12 months (efficacy of lower doses [250 mg/day] has also been reported), or</li>
<li>Posaconazole 800 mg/day (good efficacy and tolerance, but high cost).</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="alternative-possible-treatments">2. Alternative possible treatments:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer for Cryotherapy with liquid nitrogen has a 40% cure rate when used as monotherapy (Not applicable in Outer islands)</li>
<li>Heat therapy with pocket warmers 24 hours/day for a couple of months.</li>
<li>Surgery should only be considered for limited or refractory lesions due to concern for dissemination with manipulation of the disease.</li>
<li>Topical application of imiquimod has shown marked improvement in lesions, either alone or combined with oral antifungal therapy.</li>
</ul>
