<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Critical emergencies</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Adult/Child</p>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Unconsciousness is a medical emergency - early stability + diagnosis are vital<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Assume a serious cause until proven otherwise<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>2</sup></h2>
<p><strong>Unconscious/altered level of consciousness (LOC)</strong></p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>DRSABCD as per BLS</li>
<li>Assist patient onto the ground/bed + position on side</li>
<li>Do NOT leave sitting in a chair or put head between their knees</li>
<li>Open airway - airway takes precedence over any injury:<ul><li>handle gently, avoid twisting or forward movement of head + spine</li><li>use head tilt/chin lift for adult + child &gt; 1 years</li><li>infant - keep head neutral</li></ul></li>
<li>Call for help + contact MO urgently</li>
<li>Stop any bleeding</li>
<li>Do:<ul><li>vital signs:<ul><li>note pattern + regularity of breathing:1 » deep, laboured (Kussmaul respiration) - often associated with DKA » shallow with extremely ↓ RR - seen in opioid overdose » hyperventilation</li></ul></li><li>give O<sup>2</sup> if required to maintain SpO<sup>2</sup> ≥ 94%</li><li>BGL - if &lt; 4 treat immediately. See Hypoglycaemia</li><li>insert IVC x 2</li><li>neurological observations ie:3<ul><li>GCS - if ≤ 8 requires secured airway, consider LMA, until patient can be intubated<sup>1</sup></li><li>pupil size + reaction to light</li><li>motor response in limbs</li><li>fontanelle in infant</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>If hypotensive, give rapid IV sodium chloride 0.9% 10–20 mL/kg. See Shock</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do not leave patient alone if possible</li>
<li>Constantly re-check for any change, including:2,4</li>
<li>airway for any signs of obstruction eg:<ul><li>laboured/noisy breathing, or no sound of breathing</li><li>↑ WOB eg intercostal recession, nasal flaring in infants</li><li>abdomen moves in/out, but loss of natural rise of chest</li><li>GCS - if drops ≥ 2 points urgently contact MO</li></ul></li>
<li>Get rapid history from friends/relatives or bystanders:1<ul><li>was the loss of consciousness witnessed</li><li>when/what happened prior/what were they doing</li><li>prior headache/chest pain/other symptom</li><li>ingestions, IV drug use</li><li>trauma</li><li>seizures/abnormal movements</li></ul></li>
<li>Look for clues/causes eg:1<ul><li>trauma</li><li>signs of stroke</li><li>overdose eg suicide note, empty medicine packet(s), needle + syringe<ul><li>if suspected opioid give naloxone</li><li>see Toxicology assessment</li></ul></li><li>alcohol or substance/drug use<ul><li>note: if alcohol intoxication suspected, continue to look for other causes<sup>1</sup></li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>infection, especially the elderly</li>
<li>bruising/minor injuries</li>
<li>indications of known allergies eg jewellery/accessory eg key ring, USB stick, shoe tag, anklet, watch, tattoo POSSIBLE CAUSES OF ALTERED LOC<sup>1</sup> NEUROLOGICAL METABOLIC</li>
<li>Head injury/trauma, consider non- accidental injury in child</li>
<li>Stroke</li>
<li>Seizure/post seizure</li>
<li>Meningitis, encephalitis</li>
<li>Epilepsy</li>
<li>Tumour</li>
<li>Hypo/hyperglycaemia</li>
<li>Intoxication - alcohol, inhalants</li>
<li>Poisoning or overdose</li>
<li>Sepsis</li>
<li>Electrolyte derangement</li>
<li>Encephalopathy</li>
<li>Liver failure, kidney failure LOW OXYGEN/HYPOXIA HEART + CIRCULATION</li>
<li>Airway obstruction, respiratory failure</li>
<li>Choking/foreign body</li>
<li>Allergy/anaphylaxis</li>
<li>Croup/epiglottitis</li>
<li>Asthma/COPD</li>
<li>Burns</li>
<li>Toxic gas/smoke/steam inhalation</li>
<li>Drowning/near drowning</li>
<li>Pneumonia</li>
<li>PE, pulmonary oedema</li>
<li>Hypotension</li>
<li>Trauma/haemorrhage</li>
<li>GI bleed</li>
<li>Ectopic pregnancy</li>
<li>Ruptured aortic aneurysm</li>
<li>Cardiac arrest/arrhythmia</li>
<li>Intracranial haemorrhage</li>
<li>Hypo/hyperthermia</li>
<li>Get past history as able (from relatives/friends/medical records):<ul><li>known underlying illness eg epilepsy, diabetes, cancer</li><li>allergies</li><li>medications eg anticoagulants</li><li>recent surgery/hospitalisation</li></ul></li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:1<ul><li>urinalysis + pregnancy test if female of reproductive age</li><li>listen to chest - any wheeze, crackles</li><li>head to toe examination - check for:<ul><li>any odour eg fruity sweet smelling breath. Note: acetone - similar to paint thinner or nail polish remover - may indicate DKA,</li><li>skin eg rash, drug injection sites, puncture wounds</li><li>remove all clothing as you move down, maintain privacy + keep patient warm</li></ul></li></ul></li>
</ul>

</ul>

</ul>
