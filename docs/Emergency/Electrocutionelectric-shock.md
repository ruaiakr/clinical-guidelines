<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Electrocution/electric shock - Adult/Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Electrical exposure</li>
<li>Lightning strike</li>
<li>Trauma, burns</li>
<li>Cardiac arrest</li>
<li>Conducted energy weapons eg Taser® - negligible risk of injury in healthy person</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call 194 immediately and provide Situation Report if patient unconscious or still in contact with electrical source High voltage from powerlines, train lines, industrial generators - HIGH DANGER</li>
<li>Voltage from several 1,000 V to 200,000 V possible</li>
<li>High danger for rescuers to get electrocuted - without perceiving they are close to the source</li>
<li>Stay away at a safe distance - at least 1 m per 10,000 V or as directed by emergency services:<ul><li>high voltage can cause instant death if a flash/arcing occurs</li><li>electricity can travel through wet ground or air for many metres</li></ul></li>
<li>Call the electricity provider/railway company. If unknown call ooo to get support</li>
<li>Do not get closer until disconnection of power is confirmed and the line is grounded on both sides (high voltage electrician, fire and rescue or SES can provide this, not healthcare providers) Do NOT approach patient until SAFE Household electricity</li>
<li>Electrical source must be turned off and protected against it being switched on again:<ul><li>ensure person or lock is at the fuse box where electricity has been switched off OR power plug pulled and loose end taken with the rescuer</li></ul></li>
<li>If not possible, ask for support to remove electrical cables from the patient using dry timber or rubber (non conducting). This task is risky and should ideally be performed by an electrician, fire and rescue person or SES - not healthcare providers</li>
<li>DRSCABCD as per Traumatic injuries</li>
<li>If in cardiac arrest see BLS, Do prolonged CPR - high likelihood of recovery</li>
<li>Do:<ul><li>vital signs + continuous cardiac monitoring</li><li>ECG</li><li>insert IVC</li></ul></li>
<li>If hypotensive - suspect internal injury:<ul><li>give fluid bolus of sodium chloride 0.9% or Hartmann’s 10–20 mL/kg</li><li>repeat as advised by MO</li></ul></li>
<li>Urgently consult MO for all electrocutions</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask patient/witness:<ul><li>type of electrical exposure + voltage if known</li><li>other circumstances of injury eg fall, in water</li><li>loss of consciousness or seizures</li></ul></li>
<li>Head to toe assessment as per EFGHIJ in Traumatic injuries</li>
<li>Look for entry and exit wound</li>
<li>Urinalysis - check for blood</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MO will guide management</li>
<li>Evacuation if:<ul><li>high voltage exposure</li><li>loss of consciousness, seizures or any other symptoms</li><li>entry and exit wound present - suspect serious deep tissue injury even if looks minor</li><li>severe pain/tenderness over limb - monitor closely for Compartment syndrome</li><li>numbness or palsy - indicates critical injury</li><li>visual disturbance - indicates eye injury</li><li>trauma/injury/burns</li></ul></li>
<li>Treat injuries or Burns, as needed</li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See Acute pain</li>
<li>If patient has pacemaker or implantable defibrillator, will need function checking:<ul><li>continuous cardiac monitoring required - as soon as available</li></ul></li>
<li>MO may allow home after observation if:<ul><li>Low voltage exposure</li><li>asymptomatic</li><li>no loss of consciousness, seizures or cardiac arrhythmia</li><li>ECG, urinalysis normal</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated advise to see MO at next clinic</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO on all occasions</li>
<li>Ophthalmology referral as risk of delayed ocular complications<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
