<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Conjunctivitis - Adult/Child/Newborn</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Related topics - <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Eye-differential-diagnosis">Eye differential diagnosis</a></p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Newborn:
<ul>
<li>Starts within a few days or weeks after birth</li>
<li>Usually Bilateral</li>
<li>Red eyes</li>
<li>Pus discharge (mucopurulent)</li>
<li>Swollen eyelids</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Itchy/irritated ± red eye(s)</li>
<li>Discharge - watery, pus, mucous</li>
<li>Crusting of eyelids/eye lashes</li>
<li>Pain</li>
<li>swollen</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management Not applicable</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>pain, photophobia or changes in vision</li>
<li>onset/duration of symptoms</li>
<li>one eye or both</li>
<li>contact with person with red eyes/conjunctivitis</li>
<li>recent URTI, sinusitis, flu-like symptoms</li>
<li>hay fever, known allergies</li>
<li>contact lenses</li>
<li>vesicular rash, cold sores, shingles</li>
<li>exposure to irritants eg smoke or chlorine - usually self-limiting. If chemical exposure, also see <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Chemical-burn-to-eye">Chemical burn to eye</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Check for swollen glands in neck - may indicate viral conjunctivitis</li>
<li>Examine both eyes as per <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Eye-assessment">Eye assessment</a>, including:
<ul>
<li>VA + stain with fluorescein</li>
<li>evert eyelids - small round bumps under eyelid may indicate allergic conjunctivitis</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Use flow chart for further assessment</li>
</ul>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Rule of Thumb:</strong>
<ul>
<li>If it started in first 5 days after birth probably Gonorrhoeal</li>
<li>If it started after 5 days probably Chlamydia</li>
<li>If it started after 3 weeks it is not urgent</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO urgently if:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>pain, photophobia or reduced vision, or worsening symptoms</li>
<li>fluorescein uptake shows <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Corneal-abrasion">Corneal abrasion</a></li>
<li>wears contact lenses, or has herpes/shingles infection</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="newborn-and-young-infant">Newborn and young infant</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>2–12 months:
<ul>
<li>if sticky/watery discharge is the ONLY symptom (no redness):</li>
<li>likely blocked tear duct rather than conjunctivitis<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>usually gets better without treatment</li>
<li>seek advice from midwife/child health nurse/MO as needed</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if other signs of conjunctivitis eg red eye(s):
<ul>
<li>consult MO</li>
<li>if suspected bacterial conjunctivitis give chloramphenicol<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="neonate">Neonate:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>consult MO</li>
<li>do swabs for MCS + gonorrhoea and chlamydia PCR:
<ul>
<li>if gonorrhoea - ophthalmic emergency<sup>2</sup> consult MO urgently</li>
<li>if chlamydia - MO may order azithromycin<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>treat mother + contact tracing.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if suspected bacterial conjunctivitis, MO will order chloramphenicol</li>
<li>note: chlamydia/gonorrhoea in neonate may reflect mother-to-child transmission, accidental transmission or sexual abuse.<sup>2</sup> See <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-protection">Child protection</a> if concerns</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="child-and-adult">Child and adult</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Viral conjunctivitis:
<ul>
<li>usually in one eye, but often spreads to both</li>
<li>reassure patient usually self-limiting but may take weeks to resolve</li>
<li>do not give chloramphenicol<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>lubricating eye drops may provide symptomatic relief</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Allergic conjunctivitis:
<ul>
<li>if patient has <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Allergic-rhinitis">Allergic rhinitis</a>, oral antihistamines may help<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>antihistamine eye drops eg ketotifen, may help reduce symptoms and lubricating eye drops may help to remove allergen<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Bacterial conjunctivitis:
<ul>
<li>most cases will resolve in 7 days without treatment<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>if marked symptoms eg purulent discharge, give chloramphenicol<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Chlamydial conjunctivitis:
<ul>
<li>consider if sexually active with one red eye + mucopurulent discharge, or chronic conjunctivitis:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>do STI/BBV tests, + swab eye for chlamydia PCR (write ‘eye’ on pathology form)</li>
<li>give oral azithromycin</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>In all cases:
<ul>
<li>symptomatic treatment may help eg:
<ul>
<li>cold compresses several times/day or ice packs<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>clean eye(s) as needed with clean water to remove crusting and discharge. Gently wipe from inside to outside to avoid spreading infection to other eye<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>frequent hand washing</li>
<li>avoid sharing towels, pillows</li>
<li>keep children away from school/child care until no discharge<sup>4</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to return if worsening or new symptoms. Contact MO who may:
<ul>
<li>order bacterial + viral swabs</li>
<li>arrange ophthalmic referral to exclude alternative diagnoses</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If bacterial and not resolved in 5 days, patient should have immediate ophthalmic review<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
