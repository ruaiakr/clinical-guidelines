<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Spontaneous Bacterial Peritonitis (SBP) – Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<p>Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP) is an infection of preexisting ascitic fluid in the absence of any secondary intra-abdominal source (e.g. perforated viscus). SBP typically occurs in patients with end-stage liver disease. Without a high index of suspicion and early antibiotic treatment, mortality is high.</p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>SBP is caused by translocation of intestinal bacteria into ascitic fluid, most commonly Escherichia coli, Klebsiella spp., and streptococci. Diagnosis is clinical and confirmed by ascitic fluid analysis with a PMN leukocyte count ≥250 cells/microlitre. Patients may present with non-specific or no symptoms, so clinical suspicion is essential.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Asymptomatic</li>
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Hypotension</li>
<li>Abdominal pain</li>
<li>Abdominal tenderness</li>
<li>Altered mental status</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help – inform MO</li>
<li>Triage patient and assess vital signs</li>
<li>Start empiric antibiotic therapy to cover intestinal bacteria:
<ul>
<li>Ceftriaxone 2g IV OD, if available</li>
<li>OR Ampicillin 1g IV q6h + Gentamicin 240mg IV OD</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Continue antibiotics for 5–10 days depending on clinical response</li>
<li>Treat underlying liver disease – if HBsAg-positive, initiate Tenofovir once the patient is clinically improving</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess for fever, abdominal tenderness, or changes in mental status in any patient with known cirrhosis and ascites</li>
<li>Perform abdominal examination for signs of peritonitis</li>
<li>If trained and resources permit, perform diagnostic paracentesis
<ul>
<li>Diagnosis confirmed if PMN count ≥250 cells/microlitre</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Exclude secondary causes of infection (e.g. perforation)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<p><strong>Empiric antibiotic therapy:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ceftriaxone 2g IV OD</li>
<li>OR Ampicillin 1g IV q6h + Gentamicin 240mg IV OD</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supportive care:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor blood pressure, temperature, fluid status, and mental status</li>
<li>Continue routine cirrhosis management, including dietary sodium restriction and diuretics unless contraindicated</li>
<li>If HBV positive, initiate Tenofovir once stable and link to Hepatitis Program</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Prophylaxis (in discussion with MO):</strong></p>
<p>Patients at increased risk of SBP (e.g. previous SBP, variceal bleeding, or ascitic fluid protein &lt;1 g/dL) may benefit from antibiotic prophylaxis with:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (Septrin) 960mg PO OD</li>
<li>OR Ciprofloxacin 500mg PO OD</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor temperature, vitals, abdominal tenderness, and mental status daily while on treatment</li>
<li>Review antibiotic response after 48–72 hours</li>
<li>Educate patient and family on signs of recurrence (fever, confusion, worsening abdominal pain)</li>
<li>Ensure linkage to Hepatitis Program if HBsAg-positive</li>
<li>Assess need for long-term prophylaxis with MO</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / consultation</h2>
<p>Refer to medical officer or hospital if:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Paracentesis cannot be safely performed</li>
<li>Patient is septic, hypotensive, or deteriorating despite treatment</li>
<li>No clinical improvement within 72 hours</li>
<li>HBV-positive patient needing antiviral therapy or specialist liver care</li>
</ul>
