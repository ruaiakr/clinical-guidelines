# IV Therapy / IV Flow Rates

IV Therapy / IV Flow Rates
BODY FLUIDS AND ELECTROLYTES
Our survival depends on the balance of fluids and electrolytes (salts or minerals
with an electrical charge—sodium, potassium, chloride etc) in our bodies. Our total
body weight is made up of between 50% to 70% water (dependent on age, sex and
amount of body fat). Intracellular fluid (ICF) is located inside the cells and accounts
for 2/3 of the body fluid. The other 1/3 is extracellular fluid (ECF)—found in the
blood vessels (plasma) and in the spaces between cells in the body.
Illness, trauma, surgery all change the balance of fluids and electrolytes in the body.
We treat these imbalances with IV fluids. IV therapy however, can be dangerous to
the patient—especially if the patient gets too much or too little fluid.
IV fluids vary in their concentration of dissolved particles—their TONICITY. That is
why we give different fluids for different conditions.
ISOTONIC
Isotonic fluids have the same concentration of dissolved particles (tonicity) as the
fluid inside the cells of our body. When we give these fluids, water doesn’t move
across cell membranes. 0.9% Normal Saline (Na 154, Cl 154) is an ISOTONIC fluid.
HYPERTONIC
Hypertonic fluids have a concentration greater than that in cells. When we give it,
water comes out of the cells and cells shrink. These fluids usually cause diuresis
(increased urine output). 10% and 50% Dextrose and Mannitol are HYPERTONIC
fluids. Note that 10% Dextrose is often used in babies.
HYPOTONIC
Hypotonic fluid has a concentration less than that of the fluid in cells. When it
is given to a patient, cells swell. Sterile or distilled water is an example of very
HYPOTONIC FLUID. That is why we only use it for diluting medicines (& then dilute
it further with IV fluid in the chamber/burette), we never administer it as IV fluid.
ELECTROLYTE SOLUTIONS
Electrolyte solutions are used to hydrate, prevent dehydration, or to correct
fluid and electrolyte imbalance. Electrolyte solutions are 0.9% Sodium Chloride,
Hartmann’s (Na 129, K 5, Ca 2, Cl 109, Bicarb(HCO3) 29) and Barts (0.18% Saline in
4% Dextrose) solutions.
Hartmann’s solution (Lactated Ringers) solution contains multiple electrolytes
and bicarbonate (as lactate). It doesn’t contain glucose, calories and can’t replace
potassium losses. It should NOT be given to patients with liver disease or lactic
acidosis because of the lactate.
