# Ear, Nose and Throat (ENT)

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Distinguish between likely cardiac and respiratory causes:

–
Cardiac: history of heart failure, dry cough, white frothy sputum, orthopnoea,
peripheral oedema, crepitations, improved with diuretics

–
Respiratory: history of asthma or chronic lung disease, purulent sputum,
wheeze, improved with bronchodilators

–
PE: sudden onset, tachypnoea, tachycardia, immobility history
•	 Identify red flags:

–
Confusion, cyanosis, very low SpO₂, inability to speak full sentences
4.	 Management
•	 Start initial treatment based on suspected cause (e.g. salbutamol for asthma,
frusemide for heart failure)
•	 Provide oxygen if desaturation is present
•	 Consult MO for further management or referral
•	 Prepare for evacuation if severe symptoms or uncertain diagnosis
5.	 Follow up
•	 Monitor vital signs regularly
•	 Reassess respiratory status every 2–4 hours
•	 Review in clinic once stable to confirm underlying diagnosis and ongoing
treatment
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to medical officer for all moderate to severe cases or if diagnosis unclear
•	 Evacuate if respiratory distress does not improve with initial treatment or if
hypoxia persists
