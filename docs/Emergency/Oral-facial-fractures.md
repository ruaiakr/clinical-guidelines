Foreword
On behalf of the Ministry of Health and Medical Services, it is my great
pleasure to introduce the Kiribati Primary Clinical Care Manual. This manual
is a milestone in our commitment to improving the quality and accessibility
of healthcare across Kiribati, ensuring that all I-Kiribati have access to safe,
effective, and culturally appropriate care.
The development of this manual has been a collaborative effort, drawing
on local expertise and the valuable knowledge and resources shared
through partnerships with international organizations. It will serve as an
essential tool for healthcare workers across our nation, empowering them
to deliver care that meets the highest standards of clinical practice.

guidance on managing medical conditions, responding to emergencies,
promoting health and well-being, and enhancing the patient experience.
It also emphasizes the importance of community-based care, with a focus
on health promotion and prevention.
As we continue to address the health challenges faced by our
communities, this manual will play a key role in strengthening our
healthcare system, improving health outcomes, and enhancing the lives
of all those we serve. The Ministry remains committed to supporting the
health workforce and ensuring that our services meet the diverse needs of
the I-Kiribati population.

Introduction

Introduction
This manual has been designed to serve as a comprehensive guide for
healthcare providers, including nurses, medical assistants, and allied
health professionals, working within Kiribati’s Primary Health Care (PHC)
system. It offers essential clinical information, guidelines, protocols, and
best practices for managing common health conditions, diseases, and
emergency situations, ensuring that healthcare providers can deliver
quality care at the community level. The manual will support healthcare
workers in conducting high-level patient assessments, managing
medications, and following evidence-based protocols that align with
international and local healthcare standards.
The primary purpose of this manual is to empower healthcare providers
with the necessary knowledge and resources to deliver safe, effective,
and patient-centered care. It is intended to be a practical tool that can
be referred to on a daily basis, supporting clinical decision-making and
facilitating consistent, high-quality healthcare delivery across all health
centers and outreach services in Kiribati.
Structure of the Manual
The manual is organized into clear chapters based on the most common
health issues encountered in primary care settings in Kiribati. Each chapter
includes:
•	 Overview of the health condition or issue – A concise explanation of the
disease or condition, its prevalence, and its impact on public health in
Kiribati.
•	 Diagnosis and clinical assessment – Key diagnostic criteria, assessment
techniques, and patient history gathering to make an accurate
diagnosis.
•	 Management and treatment protocols – Evidence-based
recommendations for managing the condition, including
pharmacological and non-pharmacological treatment options.
•	 Prevention and health promotion – Guidance on how to prevent the
condition, including community-based prevention strategies and
education.
•	 Referral guidelines – When and how to refer patients for further
diagnostic testing, specialist care, or hospital-based treatment.

Each chapter also highlights important considerations such as patient
safety, cultural factors, and common challenges in the Kiribati context.
Throughout the manual, clinical flow charts, decision-making algorithms,
and checklists are included to guide healthcare providers through the
diagnostic and treatment process, ensuring that no steps are overlooked.
Integration of the Manual Within the Model of Care
This manual is not an isolated resource; it is an integral part of the broader
healthcare model in Kiribati. The primary goal of the manual is to ensure
that all healthcare providers can confidently and competently deliver
essential services within the PHC framework. This manual complements
the Model of Care by guiding healthcare workers in providing services that
align with national health priorities and international standards.
The Model of Care in Kiribati is based on a comprehensive and patient-
centered approach to healthcare, which includes prevention, diagnosis,
treatment, and follow-up. The manual supports this approach by offering
evidence-based guidelines for managing health conditions at the
community level and ensuring that healthcare providers are well-equipped
to address the most common health issues. It ensures that patients receive
consistent and high-quality care, whether they are in urban or rural areas,
and helps maintain continuity of care by guiding healthcare providers on
follow-up visits and patient education.
Furthermore, the manual's integration with the regulatory and governance
frameworks, such as the Pharmaceuticals Legislation and Professional
Governance, ensures that healthcare providers adhere to the legal and
ethical standards governing medical practice in Kiribati. It also reinforces
the importance of maintaining scope of practice for nurses, medical
assistants, and doctors, ensuring that each provider works within their
professional boundaries, making appropriate referrals, and collaborating
effectively within the healthcare system.
Use of Antibiotics: All antimicrobial prescribing must align with the Kiribati
Antibiotic Guidelines (Second edition, 2022, Ministry of Health and Medical
Services, Government of Kiribati) or the most recent version. Clinicians
should use these Guidelines alongside this Manual to ensure compliance
with national policy and antimicrobial stewardship. Where there is
uncertainty or a deviation from the Guidelines is being considered, seek
advice from MHMS.

The Manual’s Role in Education and Training
The manual is also a critical tool for education and training, especially for
nurses, medical assistants, and other healthcare providers who may be
less familiar with advanced medical procedures. It serves as a practical
resource during formal and informal training, allowing trainers to use it
as a reference for case studies, skills-building exercises, and continuing
education. Nurses and medical assistants can learn best practices, clinical
guidelines, and patient management techniques that will improve their
clinical competencies, helping them to undertake high-level patient
assessments and interventions with greater confidence.
For those involved in capacity building and training, the manual should
be used as a reference point in teaching clinical skills and protocols,
ensuring that PHC workers have a standard and consistent approach to
care. This also allows for continuous improvement as health conditions
and treatment guidelines evolve, ensuring that healthcare workers stay
updated with the latest evidence and standards of care.
Feedback and Continuous Improvement
To ensure that the manual remains a living document that evolves with the
needs of the healthcare system, healthcare providers are encouraged to
provide feedback on the manual’s usability, content, and clinical guidelines.
This feedback will help identify areas for improvement and updates in
future editions, ensuring that the manual remains relevant, practical,
and effective in meeting the healthcare needs of the Kiribati population.

Kiribati Nurses Code of Conduct
The Nursing Council, under the I-Kiribati Medical Services Act (1996), is the
statutory authority that oversees and governs the practices of nursing in
Kiribati. The fundamental purpose of the ‘Act’ is to protect the health and
safety of the general public.
The implementation of the inaugural ‘Anuan Te Neeti’ (The Code) is a
vital feature of the Act’s commitment to the protection of the public. The
code provides a set of professional standards that nurses must adhere
to in demonstrating their being ‘fit for practice’ and maintaining their
registration.
The Code evolved during the development of the I-Kiribati Nursing
Cultural Model of Care which was informed through extensive consultation
with the public and the profession. This included a rigorous review of
Kiribati nursing consultation documents and an international literature
review of other codes.
Incorporated within the Anuan te Neeti are I-Kiribati cultural values defined
by the Nursing Council as acceptable professional conduct. To ensure public
confidence and trust, nurses are required to uphold an exceptional standard
of behaviour both in their professional and personal lives.
Anuan te Neeti code outlines the professional behaviour expected of
nurses in all practice settings as set by the Nursing Council under sections
47 & 48 of the Kiribati Medical Services Act (1996). The code is written
with the complete understanding that nursing practice is not limited to the
provision of direct clinical care delivery. Nursing practice settings extend
to all areas of nursing. This includes working in a non-clinical capacity
involving patients or members of the public, or any other role where the
integrity of the profession is upheld.
Anuan te Neeti can be utilized by:
•	 Patients or service users, families or public, employers, nurses,
professional organizations, and the statutory body (Nursing Council) as
a guide to evaluate the conduct of nurses.

Failure to uphold these professional standards of behavior, may result in a
disciplinary inquiry. The term ‘patient’ has been incorporated throughout
this document to align accordingly with the Medical Services Act (1996).
Contextually, the term ‘patient’ refers to the individuals, families, groups
and communities who are the recipient of nursing care (1996 Medical
Services Act, I,p.44). While mandatory language such as ‘must’, ‘shall’ and
‘will’ is not sought throughout this code, it is an expectation that nurses
understand the foundational principles of the code and how it impacts on
their professional standing.
Please note: The terms conduct and behaviour are one and the same,
hence have been used inter-changeably throughout this document.

Maneaba: I-Kiribati Nursing Model of Care
Maneaba means accommodating and welcoming: A safe place to rest, to
meet or get together or take refuge.
Historically, the maneaba was considered a meeting place for villages/
island to congregate and make important decisions. Customarily, subject
to context e.g. church gatherings/tribal affiliations or individual status,
special seating allocations within the maneaba are adhered to accordingly.
Traditional beliefs suggests that the Maneaba had been ‘purposefully’ built
with a lowly-positioned roof to serve as a constant reminder to I-Kiribati
that on entering the dwelling, they must always bow or kneel to show
respect for their Unimwane & Unaine / Elders. This was also symbolic of
acknowledging their ancestral gods, prior to the arrival of Christianity on
their shores.
In present-day, although many members of the Kiribati community have
adapted to Western ideology, traditional values and beliefs are still
maintained. Hence, the symbolic gesture in the use of the Maneaba as a
cultural framework for nursing is befitting.
This is likened to other Polynesian conceptual frameworks where
traditional thatched or flax-woven shelters have been utilised
metaphorically to depict the significance of culture in their care delivery.

Kiribati Decision-Making Framework
(Abridged Version)
The Kiribati Decision-Making Framework (KDMF) provides a clear and
consistent process for nurses and medical assistants to determine whether
a clinical activity should be undertaken. This summary outlines the key
principles only. All clinicians must refer to the full KDMF for detailed
requirements, examples, and procedural steps.
Purpose
Clinical decision-making is a core professional responsibility. In Kiribati,
where many clinicians work with limited resources or in remote outer
island settings, decisions about treatment, medication administration,
delegation, and escalation must be safe, lawful, and accountable. The
KDMF supports clinicians by providing a structured approach to these
decisions, ensuring consistent and patient-centred care across all facilities.
The Framework is grounded in the ICN Definition of ‘Nursing’ and of ‘a
Nurse’ (2026).
Why the Framework Is Needed
Kiribati’s health system requires a structured decision support process
because of:
•	 Geographical isolation and limited access to higher-level services
•	 Resource constraints that affect what can be done safely in each setting
•	 Multiple cadres working together across facilities and islands
•	 Legal requirements governing the use of medicines
•	 Ongoing system development, including new PCCM interventions
The KDMF ensures that decisions are consistent, safe, and legally
defensible in all contexts.
Kiribati Primary Clinical Care Manual | 11

Foundations of Safe Decision-Making
The KDMF is based on three domains that must all be met before any
activity is undertaken:
1.	 Regulation and Legislation: activities must comply with the Legislation
(ie: Medicines Act 2018), MHMS policy, and professional regulation and
standards.
2.	 Professional Competence: clinicians must be ecducated / trained,
competent, and have recency of practice.
3.	 Context of Practice: decisions must reflect local resources, supervision,
staffing, and referral pathways.
Only when all three domains are satisfied is an activity considered within
safe scope of practice.
Guiding Principles
The Framework is underpinned by the following principles:
•	 Patient safety comes first in all decisions.
•	 Practice must be lawful and within authorised scope.
•	 Accountability is individual—each clinician is responsible for their own
decisions.
•	 Competence and recency are essential for safe practice.
•	 Context determines safety, particularly in remote or resource-limited
settings.
•	 Collaboration with colleagues strengthens decision-making.
•	 Alignment with professional identity, consistent with the ICN Definitions.

Supervision and Delegation
Supervision and delegation are integral to scope of practice in Kiribati.
Registered nurses remain accountable for decisions to delegate and must
ensure the delegatee is authorised, competent, and supported. Supervision
may be direct, indirect, or remote, depending on the complexity of the
activity and the competence of the clinician involved. These processes
support patient safety and help build workforce capacity.
Expanding Scope of Practice
Scope of practice is dynamic. Expansion must occur through a structured
and governed process, including legal review, MHMS endorsement,
education and training, supervised practice, competence assessment, and
ongoing monitoring. Any new activity added to scope must remain lawful,
supported, and in the best interests of patients.
This summary is for quick reference only. All nurses and medical assistants
must refer to the full Kiribati Decision-Making Framework for complete
guidance, detailed explanations, and examples.
Statutory Authority to Use Medicines
Medicines Act 2018, Part 4
This manual must be read together with the Kiribati Medicines Act 2018
(Part 4, ss.17–20). The manual does not create new authorities; it guides
practice within the authorities below and within each practitioner’s scope,
competence and local governance.

Who may prescribe:

•	 Pharmacists may obtain, compound, prepare, store, distribute, supply or

•	 Medical practitioners may administer any medicine on the Register for
persons under their care.
•	 Nurses may dispense and administer Level 1 and Level 2 medicines for
persons under their care.
•	 Medical assistants, and nurses supervised by a medical assistant, may
dispense and administer Level 1, Level 2 and Level 2B medicines for
persons under their care.

•	 Protocols list recommended medicines and doses. You may only carry out
the parts of a protocol that fall within your statutory authority and scope.
•	 Where a protocol includes a medicine above your authorised level (e.g.,

or any medicine requiring a prescription), a medical practitioner (or
dentist for Level D) must prescribe and, where applicable, administer.

and undertaken with required supervision, competence and local
approvals.

each medicine; the manual will reflect these once confirmed. Until then,
check the Register of Medicines or consult MHMS Pharmacy before

Intravenous (IV) cannulation
General considerations
Insertion of a cannula into a vein to administer fluids or medications.
•	 Common in emergency and elective settings.
•	 Requires good knowledge of anatomy, technique, and asepsis
•	 It is recommended to choose a straight portion of a vein to minimize the chance
of hitting valves.
•	 Use the patient’s non­ dominant arm (if possible)
•	 For prolonged courses of therapy, it is recommended to start distally and move
proximally as distal catheters are replaced.
•	 The superficial veins of the upper extremities are preferred to those of the
lower extremities for peripheral venous access as they interfere less with patient
mobility and pose a lower risk for phlebitis.
•	 DO NOT INSERT A CANNULA IN AN ARTERY – risk of limb ischemia.
•	 In a diabetic patient, avoid lower limb cannulation
Venous access allows:
•	 sampling of blood
•	 administration of IV fluids and medications
Contraindications
•	 No absolute contraindications to intravenous cannulation exist
•	 Avoid peripheral venous access in an injured, infected, or burned extremity if
possible

•	 Non-sterile gloves,
•	 Tourniquet,

Diving accidents and decompression sickness
Recommend
•	 Emphasise diver safety.
•	 Advise all divers to attend certified safe diving training classes.
•	 Visitors should only dive if qualified.
•	 There is no hyperbaric chamber in the islands.
Background
Diving-related problems are increasingly common due to air travel and deeper
dives. As depth increases, pressure rises from air and water, leading to increased risk
of barotrauma and gas-related injury. Most underwater deaths are related to diver
inexperience, carelessness, or equipment failure. Key hazards include air embolism,
decompression sickness, and sinus-related injuries due to blocked passages in the
nose, ears, and sinuses.

•	 Seizures
•	 Loss of consciousness
•	 Neurological deficits (motor or sensory)
•	 Pneumothorax
•	 Shock

•	 Skin bubbles, mottling, rashes, itching, crawling sensation

•	 Hearing difficulty

ciguatoxin in the consumed fish using approved testing methods.

Notify and investigate: One suspected case with classic neurological symptoms
should trigger public health notification and investigation.
