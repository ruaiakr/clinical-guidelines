# Ear assessment - Adult/Child

Ear, Nose and Throat
(ENT)

Ear assessment - Adult/Child
Recommendations
•	 Check every child’s ears at each clinic visit
•	 Encourage families to bring their child to the clinic if they notice any ear
symptoms or have concerns about hearing or speech development
•	 If hearing loss is suspected, liaise with the school or daycare to support the
child with classroom strategies such as sound field amplification or seating
arrangements
Background
•	 Otitis media (OM) is inflammation or infection of the middle ear. In children,
OM may develop within the first months of life and often becomes recurrent or
chronic, potentially causing hearing loss
•	 Hearing loss can significantly affect speech and language development and
educational outcomes

Preventing Ear Infections
•	 Practise regular handwashing, especially after nose blowing or coughing
•	 Keep hands and face free of nasal discharge
•	 Ensure children are up to date with pneumococcal and influenza vaccinations
•	 Teach and model ‘Blow, Breathe, Cough’ technique
•	 Breastfeed if possible; if bottle feeding, use an upright feeding position (avoid
feeding while lying flat)
•	 Avoid the use of dummies for children over 6 months of age
•	 Keep sick children away from babies
•	 Avoid exposing children to tobacco smoke

Eardrum Red Flags
If any of the following signs are observed, further assessment is required:
•	 Hole or retraction in the upper (attic) part of the eardrum
•	 Crusting, granulation tissue, or discharge in the attic region
•	 Eardrum appears severely retracted (sucked in)
•	 Presence of a dull white mass behind the eardrum
•	 Perforation located near the edge of the eardrum
 Possible cholesteatoma
Consult a MA or Medical Assistance urgently for ENT referral.

•	 Hearing Test:
“The Audiometer” is the machine measures the sound levels that people can hear
in units called decibels in hearing level (dB HL). For example, people with normal
hearing may be able to hear a sound at a level of 0 decibel (0 dB HL). A person
with hearing loss may only be able to hear a sound at a level of 50 decibels
which is in the range for moderate hearing impairment. This test available now at
the central hospital (TCH-Nawerewere).
•	 Weber’s and Rinne’s tests:
These tests assess air conduction (AC) and bone conduction (BC) and are used
to help delineate whether hearing loss is sensorineural (SNHL)or conductive
(CHL) in origin
