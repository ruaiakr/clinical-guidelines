Dermatitis (Eczema) – Adult/Child
Recommend
Start with treating any underlying or secondary cause (e.g. scabies, fungal infection,
bacterial infection). If no improvement, commence dermatitis management with
moisturisers and topical steroids. Refer if not responding or if diagnosis is uncertain.
Background
Dermatitis, also known as eczema, refers to a group of itchy, inflammatory skin
conditions caused by allergic, irritant, or internal (endogenous) factors. It can be
classified by cause, clinical appearance, or body site.
•	 Acute dermatitis lasts less than 6 weeks and shows redness, swelling, papules,
vesicles, oozing, and even blistering.
•	 Chronic dermatitis lasts more than 6 weeks and presents with dry skin, thickened
skin with prominent creases, hyperkeratosis, scaling, fissuring, excoriation, and
hyperpigmentation.
•	 Subacute dermatitis has features of both acute and chronic forms.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Take a detailed history and perform a thorough examination
•	 Identify any known irritants or allergens
•	 Consider differential diagnoses:

–
Impetigo, fungal infection, scabies

–
Lupus erythematosus, dermatomyositis

–
Rosacea, blistering diseases, skin tumour
•	 Assess the clinical form (acute, chronic, or subacute)
•	 Perform available laboratory tests (if feasible)
•	 If no test available, treat for scabies or fungal infection first. If no response,
proceed with dermatitis treatment
4.	 Management
General measures
•	 Remove or avoid known irritants/allergens (e.g. soaps, gels, dust, organic
solvents)
•	 Use moisturisers regularly (e.g. coconut oil, sorbolene) 3–4 times daily
Topical therapy
•	 Use ointments for dry skin; creams for wet/weeping skin
•	 Apply topical steroids as follows:
