# Renal colic - Adult

Genitourinary emergencies

Renal colic - Adult
Background1
•	 Pain can be caused by kidney stone passage or an obstruction in the urinary tract
1.
May present with2,3
•	 Severe 1 sided flank pain radiating to the groin:

–
usually episodic (colicky), typically lasting 20–60 minutes + may not
completely resolve before next wave4

–
very distressed and unable to find a comfortable position
•	 Nausea + vomiting, sweating, looks unwell
•	 ± haematuria
2.	 Immediate management5
•	 Do vital signs
•	 Give oral ibuprofen + paracetamol or ± antiemetic. See Acute pain, Nausea and
vomiting
•	 If severe pain, may need IV morphine or diclofenac injection with buscopan until
above works
3.	 Clinical assessment5
•	 See Abdominal pain
•	 Get history, including:

–
nausea + vomiting, fever

–
urinary symptoms eg dysuria, frequency, urgency

–
past history of kidney stones, previous episodes, renal impairment
•	 Do physical examination, including:

–
Hydration assessment - adult

–
urinalysis to confirm haematuria + MSU for MCS if nitrites or leucocytes.
Note: no haematuria does not exclude renal colic

–
pregnancy test if reproductive age
4.	 Management5
•	 Contact MO urgently if:

–
a pulsatile abdominal mass and "BP - a ruptured AAA can mimic renal colic

–
fever, pyuria (eg cloudy urine) or significant tenderness - suggests infected
obstructed kidney
•	 Main aim of treatment is analgesia + IV fluids if significantly dehydrated and to
start IV antibiotic (check antibiotic guideline for urinary tract infection).
Note: most stones pass spontaneously &lt; 1 month3

	›

	›

–

5.	 Follow up
•	 If not retrieved, advise to:

–
be reviewed if develops fever, vomiting or pain returns - contact MO

–
see MO at next clinic ± urology referral
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 As above
