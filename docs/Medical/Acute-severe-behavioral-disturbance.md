# Acute severe behavioral disturbance (ASBD)

Mental health

Acute severe behavioral disturbance (ASBD)
1.
May present with
•	 Violent behaviour or extreme agitation
•	 Self-destructive, physically or verbally aggressive or threatening behaviour
•	 Possession of a weapon with intent to use
•	 Bizarre, disoriented behaviour:
•	 Hallucinations:

–
Delusions:

–
Withdrawn e.g refusing to talk

–
Current suicide attempt, or:

–
Situational crisis

–
Family member seeking help for any of the above.
2.	 Immediate management
•	 DRSABCD
•	 If person has survived an attempted suicide or a self-harm event, manage
accordingly:

–
E.g, hanging see Traumatic injuries, spinal injuries.
•	 Consider injuries consistent with self-harm attempt
Stay safe:
•	 Never approach a patient who has a weapon.
•	 If needed take protective measures e.g lock yourself in your clinic, leave your
facility
•	 Consider risks in the immediate environment e.g access to knives, scissors, IV
poles
•	 Note exits for escape
•	 Remove other people and bystanders
•	 Assess in an open space
•	 Minimise distractions and give full attention to the patient
•	 Use a calm, confident manner, avoid sudden or threatening gestures
•	 Avoid prolonged eye contact, and do not confront, corner or stand over the
patient
•	 Be familiar with duress alarms
 Then
•	 Call police if concerned about safety
•	 Call Mental Health Doctor / DPNO
•	 Ensure at least 2 staff are always on hand
•	 Always remain with patient unless your safety is at risk

If required attempt de-escalation
•	 Listen. Allow patient to speak without interruption
•	 Be empathic, non-judgemental and respectful
•	 Monitor changes in mood or composure that may lead to aggression
•	 Continually show respect and empathy for patient
•	 Identify any patient needs that have not been met
•	 Use a slow, clear and steady voice, and do not raise your voice
•	 If the patient raises their voice, pause and listen to the patient vent their
frustrations
•	 For children, consider removing people who may be the source of violent or
aggressive behaviour
•	 If restraints is to be used, it should be undertaken in accordance with the KSPEC
Training guidelines and the Mental Health Act 2022.
Never undertake physical restraint
•	 In Outer Islands clinics and health centres lack the resources to undertake this
procedure safety
•	 As a matter of urgency staff are to:

–
Call the police

–
Protect themselves and others – retreat from the situation

If patient presents otherwise (e.g with family):
•	 If a new case, the family will to take the patient to the Emergency Department
for Assessment and Examination and if the ED Dr finds that patient has a mental
disorder, patient will be referred to the Mental Health Doctor for admission and
treatment at the Meeria ward.
•	 If a relapse case, the family will take the patient to the KTM clinic or Meeria ward
for assessment, treatment and admission.
•	 In Outer-islands, family bring the patient (both new case and relapse case)
to Health Center to the Medical Assistant for Assessment. Treatment will be
provided and patient aggressive and violent, patient will be detain at the Police
cell for safety and will be transferred to Meeria ward for further assessment and
treatment.
3.	 Clinical assessment
Get history, including
•	 Current mental health management plan and related presentations
•	 Establishing patient’s behaviour plus premorbid personality. Via family and
friends if needed.
•	 Do vital signs

–
BSL + SPO2 to exclude glycaemic plus hypoxic causes of behaviour change.
•	 Do physical examination – consider injuries consistent with self-harm attempt
•	 Do a Mental State Examination (MSE). See following box for observations plus
example questions.
•	 For children, assess for sudden or significant, unexplained changes of behaviour
or emotional state such as

–
Unsual fearfulness or severe distress e.g. inconsolable crying

–
Self-harm or social withdrawal

–
Aggression or running away from home

–
Indiscriminate attention seeking with adults

–
Development of new behaviours e.g. soiling or wetting, thumb sucking

–
Consider alternate cause where child presentation is inconsistent with history.
See child protection
