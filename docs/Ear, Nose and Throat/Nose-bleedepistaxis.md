<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Nose bleed/epistaxis - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>95% of all nose bleeds are anterior and are usually managed by simple compression of the nose.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Nose bleed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>ALERT</strong> suspect Button battery insertion or ingestion in child with blood around nostrils. If lodged in the oesophagus, it can burn a hole through to the aorta causing catastrophic haemorrhage. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Button-battery-ingestioninsertion">Button battery ingestion/insertion</a>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><a href="/docs/Emergency/Basic-life-support">DRSABCD</a></li>
<li>If active bleeding + SHOCKED<sup>2</sup> (major obvious epistaxis in process) (cool, ↑HR, CRT &gt; 2 seconds):
<ul>
<li>Admit</li>
<li>urgently consult MA/NP + urgent evacuation</li>
<li>insert Anterior nasal pack (use cotton or ribbon gauze) soaked with adrenaline 0.5mg (5mls of 1:10,000).</li>
<li>Insert 2 large IV cannula</li>
<li>start IVF resuscitation with sodium chloride 0.9% 10–20 mL/kg. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Shock">Shock</a></li>
<li>If not controlled,
<ul>
<li>Then apply Posterior nasal pack, (take out anterior pack first)</li>
<li>Patient positioning sits upright, nose suction.</li>
<li>Insert a lubricated Foley catheter (10-14 Fr) gently passing along the floor of the nose until it appears at the back of the oropharynx.</li>
<li>Inflate with a small amount of saline (7-10mls)</li>
<li>Gently pulled forward until the inflated balloon seats firmly against the posterior nasal opening then insert anterior packing (Cotton/ribbon gauze) to fill up the front part of the nasal cavity.</li>
<li>Then clamp the catheter in front of the nostril entrance to maintain tension and prevent dislodgement.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Antibiotic: Augmentin or Septrin (when applying posterior nasal packing).</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If active bleeding + NOT shocked:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>apply continuous pressure on the lower third of nose with thumb and forefinger for 5–10 minutes</li>
<li>sit upright, lean forward</li>
<li>breathe through mouth + spit blood out rather than swallowing</li>
<li>if not stopped after 10–15 minutes:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>reapply pressure and reassess (incorrect pressure most common cause of continued bleeding) if still not stop</li>
<li>insert the anterior nasal pack with adrenaline and continue applying pressure</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if still bleeding:
<ul>
<li>Then apply posterior nasal pack</li>
<li>continue to manage as per ‘if active bleeding + shocked’ above</li>
<li>urgently consult MA/NP + urgent evacuation</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>onset, duration and severity of bleeding</li>
<li>prior nose bleeds - when, how often, severity, treatment</li>
<li>red flags for tumour e.g. nasal blockage, facial pain/swelling, headaches, deep toothache, teenage male<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>risk factors for nose bleed:
<ul>
<li>nose or facial trauma, nose picking, CPAP use</li>
<li>prior nasal surgery</li>
<li>intranasal medicine or drug use</li>
<li>bleeding disorders (or family history of), easy bruising</li>
<li>kidney/liver disease, hypertension</li>
<li>taking anticoagulants/antiplatelets e.g. NSAIDs</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If bleeding stops with compression alone, patient can go home with first aid advice:
<ul>
<li>return if bleeding re-starts and unable to control at home with compression</li>
<li>if risk factors/red flags present or recurrent bleed(s), advise to see MA/NP at next clinic</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If anterior nasal pack inserted, MA/NP may advise:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>evacuation/hospitalisation OR home with pack in situ + give Nasal pack discharge advice (remove in 24–72 hours)</li>
<li>± antibiotics</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="nasal-pack-discharge-advice">Nasal pack discharge advice<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>May have symptoms of a cold while in place eg blocked nose, ↓ smell, facial pressure, headaches, nasal drainage, tearing from eyes</li>
<li>Try not to blow nose. Sneeze with open mouth</li>
<li>To avoid ↑ blood flow to the nose and risk of further bleeding - avoid straining, lifting &gt; 5kg, strenuous exercise, bending over + sleep with head slightly elevated</li>
<li>Avoid aspirin and ibuprofen (may ↑ bleeding). Take paracetamol if needed for pain</li>
<li>Keep pack moist by spraying with sodium chloride 0.9%</li>
<li>Return immediately if bleeding starts (nose or mouth), fever, ↑ pain, vision changes, SOB, loss of colour of skin around nose, swelling of face, skin rash</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If nasal pack in situ and not evacuated, review next day or sooner if any concerns:
<ul>
<li>remove pack as advised by MA/NP</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MA/NP may consider ENT referral as appropriate</li>
</ul>
</div>
