3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess for any signs of secondary bacterial infection (e.g., impetigo or cellulitis).
•	 Monitor for complications such as pneumonia or encephalitis.
•	 Measure temperature and assess hydration status.
4.	 Management
•	 Symptomatic treatment to relieve fever and itching.
•	 Advise the patient to avoid scratching to prevent secondary infections.
•	 Antiviral treatment (e.g., acyclovir) in high-risk individuals.
•	 Antihistamines may be considered for itching relief.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Follow up in 3-5 days to assess for any signs of secondary infections or
complications.
•	 Further follow-up if symptoms worsen or new symptoms develop.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Referral to a medical officer if secondary bacterial infections, pneumonia, or
encephalitis are suspected.
•	 Consider referral for immunocompromised individuals or adults with severe

Cholera
Background
Cholera is an acute diarrheal disease caused by the bacterium Vibrio cholerae. It is
typically spread through contaminated water or food, particularly in areas with poor
sanitation. Cholera can lead to severe dehydration and death if untreated.
Recommendations
•	 Rehydration is the cornerstone of treatment. Oral rehydration solutions (ORS)
should be the first line of therapy for most cases.
•	 Antibiotics (e.g., doxycycline) may be indicated in severe cases to shorten the
duration of diarrhoea.
•	 Prevention involves improved sanitation, access to clean water, and vaccination in
endemic areas.
1.
May present with
•	 Acute watery diarrhoea
•	 Vomiting
•	 Dehydration (dry mouth, sunken eyes, skin tenting)
•	 Muscle cramps
•	 Shock in severe cases
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Immediate rehydration with oral rehydration salts (ORS).
•	 If severe dehydration or shock is suspected, IV fluids are required.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess for dehydration (mild, moderate, or severe).
•	 Measure vital signs, especially blood pressure and pulse.
•	 Monitor for signs of shock (rapid pulse, low BP, cold extremities).
4.	 Management
•	 Rehydrate with oral rehydration solution (ORS) in mild cases.
•	 For severe dehydration or shock, initiate IV fluids and closely monitor the
patient's status.
•	 Administer doxycycline or another appropriate antibiotic for cholera treatment.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Follow-up within 24-48 hours to monitor rehydration and recovery.
•	 Check for signs of complications like electrolyte imbalances.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to a medical officer if the patient does not respond to initial management
or if there are signs of severe dehydration or shock.
