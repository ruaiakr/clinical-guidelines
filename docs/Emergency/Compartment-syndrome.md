# Compartment syndrome - Adult/Child

Compartment syndrome - Adult/Child
GENERAL CONSIDERATION:
•	 Limb threatening emergency - urgent evacuation for surgical management 1,2
•	 Be alert in all limb injuries eg closed leg + forearm fractures, crush injuries or
treatment causing
•	 compression/constriction eg casts/splints, circumferential bandaging2
•	 Hypoxia of muscle + nerves caused by bleeding + swelling in closed muscle
cavity, leading to necrosis + amputation1
1.
May present with
•	 Limb injury + pain out of proportion to what would be expected ± not
responding to analgesia1
•	 If child - anxiety, agitation, unable to rest comfortably3
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Remove any bandaging and split the casts/splints to relieve pressure:3

–
leave a portion of cast/splint to stabilise fracture as needed

–
Elevate the limb with the splint above the level of the heart

–
Give analgesic medication – panadol / NSAIDs
•	 Rapidly assess:2

–
pain - severity, is it responding to analgesia

–
passively extend fingers/toes distal to injury, does this cause pain

–
any  sensation, numbness, pins + needles + absent pulses - compare to
opposite limb 1-4
•	 Contact MO urgently, even if low suspicion of compartment syndrome2
•	 Offer analgesia. See Acute pain
•	 Rest, apply ice packs + elevate the limb to heart level with pillows 1,4
•	 Insert IVC
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Ask about:

–
mechanism of injury, when, treatment so far

–
underlying bleeding disorders, anticoagulants2
•	 Do physical examination, including:

–
vital signs

–
check entire limb for swelling3

–
palpate muscles - firm, tight, tenderness3
