<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Neonatal sepsis (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Neonatal sepsis is a life-threatening emergency and must be suspected in any sick neonate.</li>
<li>Start antibiotics early—do not wait for test results.</li>
<li>Refer urgently to Tungaru Central Hospital (TCH) as soon as neonatal sepsis is suspected.</li>
<li>While awaiting retrieval or transfer, commence IV antibiotics and fluids.</li>
<li>Ensure the baby is hydrated—insert IV fluids if unable to feed.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Sepsis is a life-threatening condition caused by an uncontrolled response to infection. In neonates, it may present with subtle or non-specific signs. A low threshold for starting antibiotics can prevent rapid deterioration.</li>
<li>Common pathogens include <em>Klebsiella</em> spp., <em>E. coli</em>, and <em>Staphylococcus aureus</em>. Group B Streptococcus and coagulase-negative Staphylococcus are more common in high-resource settings; <em>Listeria monocytogenes</em> is rare.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Lethargy or movement only on stimulation</li>
<li>Poor feeding</li>
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Hypothermia (&lt; 35.5°C axillary)</li>
<li>Apnoea or increased apnoeic episodes</li>
<li>Tachycardia or bradycardia</li>
<li>Severe chest indrawing</li>
<li>Delayed capillary refill time (CRT &gt; 2 seconds)</li>
<li>Jaundice, vomiting, diarrhoea, or abdominal distension</li>
<li>Seizures or hypotonia</li>
<li>Irritability</li>
<li>Severe Chest Indrawing</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help and refer to TCH immediately</li>
<li>While awaiting transfer:
<ul>
<li>Keep airway patent (neutral head and neck position)</li>
<li>Start oxygen via nasal prongs (aim for SpO₂ 90–95%)</li>
<li>Use bag and mask ventilation if RR &lt; 20/min or apnoeic</li>
<li>Establish IV access</li>
<li>If signs of shock or poor perfusion:
<ul>
<li>Give IV 0.9% sodium chloride or Ringer lactate 10 mL/kg over 20 minutes</li>
<li>Repeat every 20–30 mins if needed (up to 3 boluses)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check blood glucose level (BGL):
<ul>
<li>If &lt; 2.5 mmol/L, give slow IV 2 mL/kg of 10% glucose</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If unable to feed, insert OGT and start IV fluids</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p>See also <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Approach-to-a-sick-neonate">Approach to a sick neonate</a>.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Perform full physical examination and check for localising signs of infection</li>
<li>Check temperature, perfusion, hydration, and neurologic status</li>
<li>Look for signs of:
<ul>
<li>Meningitis or encephalitis — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Meningitis">Meningitis</a></li>
<li>Pneumonia — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Pneumonia">Pneumonia</a></li>
<li>Urinary tract infection — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Urinary-tract-infection">Urinary tract infection</a></li>
<li>Omphalitis or soft tissue infection — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Omphalitis">Omphalitis</a></li>
<li>NEC or GI infection</li>
<li>Congenital or neonatal malaria</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Consider respiratory distress, birth trauma, or perinatal asphyxia as differentials</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<p><strong>First-line antibiotics</strong> (start immediately while awaiting referral):</p>
<p>Ampicillin IV slow push (3 minutes) for 7–10 days + Gentamicin IV slow push (3 minutes) or IM for 5 days</p>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="if-iv-access-not-possible">If IV access not possible</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Use ceftriaxone IM with caution (especially in preterm or jaundiced neonates)</li>
<li>Alternatively, use IM benzylpenicillin + gentamicin</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="duration-of-antibiotics">Duration of antibiotics</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>7–10 days in most cases</li>
<li>Stop after 5 days if symptoms resolve within 24 hrs</li>
<li>Adjust duration if blood culture results are known (de-escalate if possible)</li>
<li>Refer to <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Meningitis">Meningitis</a> section for extended treatment durations</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continue to monitor while awaiting transfer</li>
<li>Ensure hydration and temperature control</li>
<li>Reassess response to antibiotics daily</li>
<li>Update retrieval team or receiving hospital on clinical status</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer immediately to TCH if neonatal sepsis is suspected</li>
<li>Begin antibiotics and fluids before transfer</li>
<li>Continue stabilisation while waiting for retrieval or transport</li>
</ul>
