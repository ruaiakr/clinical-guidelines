# Foreign body in eye - Adult/Child

Foreign body in eye - Adult/Child
1.
May present with1-3
•	 Report of something in the eye - (Foreign Body sensation)

–
Pain, photophobia- more if corneal FB

–
Redness, epiphora

–
may be worse when blinking or with eye movements

–
Poor vision
•	 ± red, watery eye(s), swollen eyelid(s), photophobia
2.	 Immediate management
•	 See Eye injury. if:4

–
obvious penetrating injury, or caused from high speed and force (high
velocity)

–
protruding foreign body (FB) - do not remove
•	 Advise to not rub the eye
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Ask about:

–
time + how FB happened eg:4
	›
hammering metal, angle grinding, operation of high speed machinery - high
velocity
	›
sand, dust, eyelash 1,3 - low velocity
	›
if unsure, ask about all activities in preceding 24 hours, especially activities
that can cause

–
high velocity projectiles

–
use of protective eyewear 1,3

–
pain, changes in vision, photophobia 1,3

–
contact lenses - get patient to remove 1

–
previous eye conditions
•	 Do vital signs?
•	 Examine both eyes as per Eye assessment.

–
instil (or Plain Lignocaine 1%)if needed for pain1

–
visual acuity (VA) - test unaffected eye first (Always Right Eye first)

–
evert eyelids: 1,2
	›
if FB seen under eyelid, use moistened cotton tip with sodium chloride
0.9% to sweep away 1,2

–
stain with fluorescein - may help to see FB + show any Corneal abrasion.

4.	 Management
•	 If no FB visible, but fluorescein shows abrasion on cornea, see Corneal abrasion.
•	 Consult MO if:

–
high velocity projectile - x-ray may be needed

–
event happened a few days prior + there is ↑ pain, worsening vision + diffuse
redness2
	–
↓VA, pupils irregular or non-reactive

–
blood in anterior chamber (hyphaema) - suggests perforation of eyeball

–
FB ‘lodged’ in the cornea
•	 Only try to remove the FB if it:

–
looks superficial/not ‘lodged’ in the eye

–
is at least 4 mm from the pupil
Removal of foreign body2,3
•	 Lie patient down, with affected eye closest to you
•	 Ask patient to look up, approach from side to ↓ blinking. Hold eyelid open if
needed2
•	 First try to irrigate with sodium chloride 0.9% (use a syringe without needle) FB
may wash off2
•	 Or, try to dislodge with a moistened cotton tip applicator using ‘dab' or 'nudging'
motion2
•	 If still not dislodged, MO may consider removal from cornea with 25 G needle
(best done with slit lamp)4
•	 Consider instilling oxybuprocaine in both eyes to stop blinking to aid removal
•	 If unable to remove the FB, or there is a 'rust ring' after removal - consult MO
•	 If FB removed from cornea give chloramphenicol as per Corneal abrasion.
•	 Advise to wear protective eyewear during risky activities1
