<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) - Adult</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>A DVT is a blood clot in the deep vein of the leg or pelvis.  If not detected and treated can lead to a life-threatening pulmonary embolism</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Calf swelling</li>
<li>Pain/tenderness in one leg</li>
<li>Distension of surface veins over foot and leg</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If DVT suspected contact MO/MA urgently +<ul><li>in collaboration with MO/MA assess using Wells score if DVT suspected below WELLS SCORE IF DVT SUSPECTED<sup>1</sup> Active cancer - treated within ≤ 6 months, or palliative 1 Calf swollen ≥ 3 cm more than the other leg 1 Paralysis, muscle weakness or recent plaster to immobilise lower leg 1 Pitting oedema of the leg 1 Recently bed ridden for ≥ 3 days, or major surgery within 3 months 1 Superficial veins in same leg standing out (not varicose veins) 1 Entire leg swollen 1 Previous DVT 1 Tenderness along distribution of deep venous system 1 Could patient have an alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT</li><li>2 Score Score 0≤1 DVT unlikely D-dimer negative MO may consider other diagnoses Take blood for D-dimer<sup>1</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>If D-dimer not readily available, MO will advise (eg may seek specialist advice ± consider retrieval for USS)</li>
<li>If D-dimer available, but result will take &gt; 4 hours, MO may order anti coagulant Score 0≥2 DVT likely D-dimer positive</li>
<li> MO may order:<ul><li>anti coagulant</li><li>retrieval for USS within 24 hours Can patient have USS in &lt; 4 hours to confirm DVT Urgent retrieval for USS + D-dimer blood test</li></ul></li>
<li> MO may order:<ul><li>D-dimer blood test</li><li>anti coagulant</li><li>retrieval for USS within 24 hours Yes No</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about leg symptoms + past history:1<ul><li>onset of pain, severity, associated symptoms eg fever</li><li>pregnant or postpartum, oestrogen therapy</li></ul></li>
<li>Do physical examination including:<ul><li>vital signs</li><li>pregnancy test if female of reproductive age<sup>2</sup></li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MO/MA may advise:<ul><li>fractionated IV Heparin</li><li>take blood first - FBC, coagulation studies, UECr</li><li>urgent evacuation as needed</li></ul></li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See Acute pain Follow up</li>
<li>As advised by MO/MA</li>
</ul>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
</ul>
