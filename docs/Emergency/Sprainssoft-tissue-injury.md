<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Sprains/soft tissue injury - Adult/Child</h1>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Be alert to associated injuries eg fractures + dislocations<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>In children consider fibula fracture, often misdiagnosed as a sprain</li>
<li>Sprain is over stretching to incomplete or complete tear of the ligament<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Injury + swelling, bruising + pain on movement<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>No fracture seen on x-ray</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact MO if signs of neurovascular compromise distal to the site eg:<ul><li>weakness or numbness</li><li>↓ or absent pulses, delayed capillary refill</li><li>changes in colour + T</li></ul></li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See Acute pain</li>
<li>Support limb using pillows as needed + apply ice packs</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If child, infections of joints often present with a history of injury. See Swollen/ painful joint</li>
<li>Ask about mechanism of injury +<ul><li>timing + events surrounding the injury</li><li>any witnesses, any first aid</li><li>if ankle - could they weight bear immediately after injury - if not may indicate fracture<sup>2</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Get past history:<ul><li>similar injury, when, treatment - ankle sprains are often recurrent<sup>2</sup></li></ul></li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:1</li>
<li>vital signs</li>
<li>check for signs of severe sprain:<ul><li>severe pain, extensive bruising + swelling</li><li>complete inability to weight bear</li><li>loss of motion + stability + abnormal joint movement</li></ul></li>
<li>palpate joints above + below injury - any tenderness, swelling</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If severe contact MO, who may advise:<ul><li>x-ray ± cast for 10–14 days + non-weight bearing activity/crutches</li></ul></li>
<li>If mild or moderate sprain:<ul><li>Rest + limit activity until pain free use is possible:2<ul><li>may need crutches for 2–3 days until standing is no longer painful<sup>1,3</sup></li></ul></li><li>Ice for 48 hours - apply for 20 minutes on/20 minutes off as needed for pain/ swelling<sup>2</sup></li><li>Compression for 1st few days - bandage is better for ↓ swelling than rigid splint<sup>2</sup></li><li>Elevate above level of heart to ↓ pain + swelling<sup>2</sup></li><li>Refer to physiotherapist if available</li></ul></li>
<li>Reassure patient pain should ↓ gradually + most sprains heal well 1,2</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise patient to be reviewed if: 2,3<ul><li>changes to limb/joint - T, pallor, numbness, pins + needles or swelling</li><li>pain continues, increases or is not relieved by analgesia</li><li>contact MO + consider fracture, compartment syndrome</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
</ul>
