# Chronic suppurative otitis media (CSOM) – Adult/Child

Chronic suppurative otitis media (CSOM) –
Adult/Child
Recommend1,2
•	 Chronic discharging ear(s) can cause hearing impairment and disability.
Occasionally serious complications can occur eg intracranial infection or
mastoiditis1
1.
May present with1,3
•	 Ear discharge present for ≥ 2 weeks + eardrum perforation (hole)
2.	 Immediate management Not applicable
3.	 Clinical assessment3
•	 Get history as per Ear assessment, including:

–
amount + duration of ear discharge

–
any concerns about hearing, speech and learning/behavioural problems,
development
•	 Examine ears:

–
take swab for MCS first

–
use Tissue spears, to clean

–
document size and location of hole in eardrum eg draw picture in notes

–
if grommet - note position (still in eardrum); any granulation tissue around
base
•	 If pus thick and you are unable to view eardrum, consider initial irrigation of ear.
Use diluted Betadine® (1 part Betadine® to 20 parts warm water) followed by
Tissue spears,: or suctioning.
4.	 Management1,3
•	 If discharge &lt; 2 weeks, treat as AOM, with perforation. Unless child has history of
dry perforation with intermittent discharge, in which case continue as below
•	 If discharge ≥ 2 weeks3

–
Give ciprofloxacin ear drops 4-5 drops BD for 7 days.

–
If hole in eardrum is not visible or very small (pin hole size), advice Ear
Hygiene

–
amoxicillin or Augmentin as per AOM.

–
Dry mopping (Using a tissue wick/toilet paper or Cotton  with a Wooden stick

–
importance of continuing tissue spears + ear drops until ears dry (may be > 2

–
hearing loss likely while discharging
•	 if discharge > than 2 weeks with:

–
T > 38.5 or systemically unwell

–
redness/swelling behind the ear

–
perforation in Attic area.

–
crusting or granulation tissue - in attic area or around base of grommet
Then admit, start IV Antibiotic, eardrop (ciprofloxacin/chloramphenicol) Ear
hygiene and consult or refer to ENT.
Tissue spears
•	 Wash hands thoroughly before and after the procedure
•	 Use a clean tissue. Tear strips 1–1.5 cm wide along the length of the tissue and
fold each strip in half
•	 Gently pull the ear to straighten the ear canal:

–
Pull ‘back’ for young children

–
Pull ‘back and up’ for older children
•	 Gently insert the tissue spear into the ear canal. Slowly twist it in until resistance
is felt, or the child cries, coughs, or blinks
•	 Leave in place for approximately 30 seconds to absorb discharge
•	 Remove and discard. Repeat with a fresh spear until the ear is dry
