<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Chickenpox</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Vaccination is the most effective way to prevent chickenpox. The varicella vaccine is recommended for children, and unvaccinated adults should be encouraged to get vaccinated.</li>
<li>Early diagnosis and prompt management can help minimize complications.</li>
<li>Isolation is essential to prevent transmission, especially in community settings like schools.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chickenpox is a highly contagious viral infection caused by the varicella-zoster virus (VZV). It primarily affects children but can occur in adults who have not been vaccinated or previously infected. Primary infection is manifest by varicella (chickenpox), while reactivation of latent virus causes herpes zoster (<a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Shingles">shingles</a>), often in adults. While generally mild in children, it can cause severe complications in adults and immunocompromised individuals.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Malaise</li>
<li>Rash (starts as macules, progresses to papules, then vesicles, and eventually crusts)</li>
<li>Itching</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Symptomatic treatment: Paracetamol for fever, hydration, and topical anti-itch creams (e.g., calamine lotion).</li>
<li>Antiviral treatment if necessary (e.g., acyclovir) in immunocompromised individuals or adults.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess for any signs of secondary bacterial infection (e.g., <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Impetigo-Child-Adult">impetigo</a> or cellulitis).</li>
<li>Monitor for complications such as <a href="/docs/Cardiovascular%20and%20respiratory%20disorders/Pneumonia">pneumonia</a> or encephalitis.</li>
<li>Measure temperature and assess hydration status.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Symptomatic treatment to relieve fever and itching.</li>
<li>Advise the patient to avoid scratching to prevent secondary infections.</li>
<li>Antiviral treatment (e.g., acyclovir) in high-risk individuals.</li>
<li>Antihistamines may be considered for itching relief.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow-up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Follow up in 3-5 days to assess for any signs of secondary infections or complications.</li>
<li>Further follow-up if symptoms worsen or new symptoms develop.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Referral to a medical officer if secondary bacterial infections, pneumonia, or encephalitis are suspected.</li>
<li>Consider referral for immunocompromised individuals or adults with severe cases.</li>
</ul>
