# Tinea versicolor (Pityriasis versicolor) – Adult/Child

Tinea versicolor (Pityriasis versicolor) –
Adult/Child
Recommend
Treat tinea versicolor with antifungal shampoo. Reassure that treatment is for
cosmetic purposes. Refer if no improvement after 2 weeks.
Background
Tinea versicolor is a superficial fungal (yeast) infection that causes changes in skin
pigmentation. It is more common in adolescents and young adults, particularly in
tropical climates and among people who sweat heavily. It is not dangerous and
treatment is primarily for cosmetic appearance.
1.
May present with
•	 Flat patches of hyperpigmented or hypopigmented skin – may appear pink, tan,
brown, or red

–
In dark-skinned individuals, often causes light patches

–
May have fine scales on the surface
•	 Common on chest, back, neck, and upper arms (not face)
•	 Common in adolescents and young adults living in tropical or humid climates
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Not applicable
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Take a history
•	 Perform vital signs
•	 Examine the skin – see Skin assessment – adult, or Skin assessment – child
•	 Consider other diagnoses:

–
Tinea/ringworm

–
Secondary syphilis

–
Leprosy
