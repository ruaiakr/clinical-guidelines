<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Delirium - Adult</h1>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider delirium where a patient has one or more of the following risks:</li>
<li>age ≥ 65 years</li>
<li>history of cognitive impairment or dementia</li>
<li>severe medical illness</li>
<li>current hip fracture</li>
<li>cognitive concerns raised by others</li>
<li>Delirium may be confused with dementia</li>
<li>Medicine toxicity is a major cause of delirium</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>2,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Acute change in mental status</li>
<li>Difficulty focusing or keeping track, easily distracted</li>
<li>Behaviour fluctuates in severity over 24 hour period:<ul><li>hyperactive:<ul><li>disorganised or incoherent thinking, rambling or illogical flow of conversation</li><li>hyperalert, startles easily</li><li>restlessness, picking at clothes, tapping fingers or making frequent sudden changes of position</li></ul></li><li>hypoactive:<ul><li>lethargic, drowsy</li><li>difficult or unable to rouse</li><li>disoriented, unsure of where they are or what time it is</li><li>inability to recall events or instructions</li><li>sluggishness, staring into space, staying in one position for a long time or moving very slowly</li><li>disturbance of sleep-wake cycle, excessive daytime sleepiness and insomnia at night</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>Hallucinations, illusions or misinterpretations</li>
<li>Withdrawal states eg tremors, sweating, visual hallucinations</li>
<li>Fever</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If patient is highly agitated, threatening, aggressive or violent, see Mental health emergency</li>
<li>Screen for Sepsis</li>
<li>Do not leave patient alone</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment 2,4</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess and manage patient in quiet environment</li>
<li>Ask family or carer about recent behavioural change eg:<ul><li>confusion</li><li>concentration</li><li>agitation, restlessness</li><li>sleepiness, levels of consciousness</li><li>communication or response levels</li><li>difficulty meeting requests</li><li>alterations in mood</li></ul></li>
<li>Do full head to toe physical examination</li>
<li>Identify underlying acute medical causes:2,4<ul><li>infections eg meningitis, encephalitis, neurosyphilis, UTI, pneumonia</li><li>CNS conditions eg brain lesions or tumours, subdural haematoma, TIA, stroke</li><li>sodium, potassium and calcium abnormalities. Consider renal or liver disease</li><li>Hypoglycaemia, or hyperglycaemia</li><li>respiratory disorders leading to hypoxia or pulmonary embolism</li><li>myocardial infarction, arrhythmias, heart failure</li><li>seizures, epilepsy and postictal states</li><li>pain or discomfort eg urinary retention, constipation, hip fracture, dental</li><li>withdrawal eg alcohol, benzodiazepines</li></ul></li>
<li>Urgently identify possible medicines toxicity:2,4<ul><li>consider polypharmacy, particularly drugs with anticholinergic effects, given at high or. prolonged doses:<ul><li>anticholinergics » sedating antihistamines eg promethazine, doxylamine » drugs for bladder overactivity eg oxybutynin</li><li>psychotropics » antipsychotics eg clozapine, olanzapine » lithium » hypnotics eg benzodiazepines » tricyclic antidepressants eg amitriptyline</li><li>drugs for parkinsonism » anticholinergics eg benzatropine » dopamine agonists eg levodopa</li><li>cardiac medications » betablockers eg sotalol and propranolol » digoxin</li><li>analgesics » opioids » tramadol » nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)</li><li>antiemetics » prochlorperazine » hyoscine hydrobromide</li><li>corticosteroids (high dose)</li><li>alcohol and illicit drugs eg cannabis, methamphetamine</li></ul></li></ul></li>
<li>Do vital signs +<ul><li>SpO<sup>2</sup> and BGL to exclude hypoxia or hypoglycaemia as a cause</li></ul></li>
<li>Do:</li>
<li>bloods/i-STAT - BGL, FBC, UECr, LFT</li>
<li>urinalysis and MSU for MCS</li>
<li>ECG</li>
<li>drug screen:<ul><li>therapeutic drug monitoring if taking digoxin, lithium, quinidine</li><li>illicit drugs</li></ul></li>
<li>chest x-ray</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management 2,4</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Manage and treat underlying medical cause as above in consultation with MO</li>
<li>For suspected medicines or other substance use, see Poisoning and overdose</li>
<li>Contact pharmacist for medication review</li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See Acute pain</li>
<li>Address hydration, nutrition, ventilation, temperature control, skin care, bladder and bowel care</li>
<li>Provide familiar environmental cues ie clocks, calendars, with involvement from family</li>
<li>Commence regular observations. Observe at all times</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As per MO or specialist instructions</li>
<li>According to identified cause of delirium</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO and specialist</li>
<li>If no biochemical causes or sepsis identified, patient needs Psychiatric referral.</li>
</ul>
</div>
