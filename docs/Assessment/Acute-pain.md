# Acute pain - Adult/Child

Pain, nausea and vomiting

Acute pain - Adult/Child
1.
May present with
•	 Acute pain
2.	 Immediate management
•	 If chest pain, go to Chest pain assessment
•	 If severe pain:

–
get rapid history + check for allergies

–
contact MO urgently

–
adult - insert IVC + give IV morphine or fentanyl
•	 If IVC delay, consider alternative routes e.g. IM/subcut
•	 If severe pain + looks sick - screen for Sepsis
•	 If sudden onset severe headache/‘worst headache of life’ - go to Headache
•	 If abdominal pain still give analgesia. Note: will not mask physical signs/hinder
diagnosis
3.	 Clinical assessment1,2
•	 The most useful diagnostic approach to acute pain is to take a detailed and
systematic history
•	 Ask about:

–
Site - where is it

–
Onset - when did it start:
	›
sudden or gradual
	›
result of trauma/activity/cold/stress

–
Characteristics eg sharp, throbbing, aching, burning, stabbing

–
Radiation - does it spread anywhere else

–
Associated symptoms eg nausea, vomiting, sweating, fever

–
Timing - duration, constant or intermittent:
	›
has anything changed the pain
	›
ever had this pain before, how often does it occur

–
Exacerbating or relieving factors:

–
eg rest, medicines, eating, position changes, ice/splinting

–
Severity - at rest, on movement:
	›
mild, moderate, severe
	›
scale from 0–10 (0 = no pain, 10 = worst pain imaginable)
	›
if child consider FLACC or FACES

FACES 4+ years1,3
“These faces show how much something can hurt. This face [point to left-most
face] shows no pain. The faces show more and more pain [point to each from left
to right] up to this one [point to right-most face]. It shows very much pain. Point to
the face that shows how much you hurt [right now]”
Clinician to say ‘hurt’ or ‘pain’ (language child understands) - not words like ‘happy’
or ‘sad’
4.	 Management
•	 Consult MO if:

–
severe pain, even if settled after initial analgesia

–
fever, persistent tachycardia or tachypnoea, or hypotension

–
analgesia is not effective

–
unable to find cause of pain

–
recurrence of pre-existing condition

–
suspected opioid seeking
Analgesia1,4
•	 Consider medicine(s) already given
•	 Use Step wise approach + consider Non-pharmacological options
