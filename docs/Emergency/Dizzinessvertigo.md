Dizziness/vertigo - Adult
Recommend
•	 Dizziness and vertigo are common symptoms, with many causes. Aim to identify
serious causes
Background1
•	 Dizziness common causes - postural hypotension, arrhythmias, syncope,
anaemia, hypoglycaemia, hypercalcaemia, “vitamin B12, medicine side e ect,
anxiety,2 hyperventilation3
•	 Vertigo - can be central (brain stem or cerebellum) or peripheral (inner ear)
related
1.
May present with1
•	 Dizziness, faintness or feeling like body or surroundings are moving eg spinning
2.	 Immediate management1,2,4,5
•	 Check for Red flags:

–
if any, get rapid history and contact MO urgently. Suspect Stroke

–
MO may consider retrieval for CT/MRI ± referral to neurologist
Red flags
•	 Facial weakness/numbness
•	 Arm ± leg weakness, ataxia
•	 Speech di#culty/slurred
•	 Visual eld loss, double vision
•	 Difficulty swallowing
•	 Sensory loss eg in 1 side of body
•	 Confusion
•	 Headache
•	 New sudden unilateral hearing loss with 1st onset of continuous dizziness/vertigo
3.	 Clinical assessment1,2,4,5
•	 Get history of dizziness/vertigo, including:2

–
timing - episodic or continuous; gradual or sudden onset

–
when occurred, are episodes
 in severity or frequency4

–
duration of episodes - seconds/minutes/hours/days

–
character:
	›
vertigo - spinning, rocking or tilting2
	›
dizziness - o balance, giddiness (light headed), faint (presyncope)

–
spontaneous or triggered by eg head movement, sitting, standing, rolling over,
tipping head back, lying down, loud noises, anxiety/stress

Head
impulse test
•	 Sit opposite patient and ask them to stare at your nose
•	 Hold the patient's head with both of your hands and turn head
slowly side to side 10–20O then back to midpoint. Repeat
but rapidly rotate the head to 1 side and pause. Patient's eyes
should stay on the examiners nose with the rapid head turn
•	 Repeat again but this time rapidly rotate to the opposite side
•	 If eyes do a series of 'catch-up' movements to re-fixate on
nose, test is +ve
•	 Document the direction the head was rotated to when the
'catch-up' movement occurred - eg if catch-up movement was
seen when head was quickly rotated to the patient's le', then
document ‘+ve left head impulse test’
Nystagmus
•	 Ask patient to follow your finger as you slowly move it right to
left
•	 Observe direction of the fast phase of nystagmus - horizontal,
vertical, rotates, unidirectional or direction changing
•	 Does nystagmus stop if patient stares at object
Test of skew
•	 Ask patient to look straight ahead. Cover 1 of their eyes with
your hand. Then, quickly move your hand to cover the other
eye
•	 During this process, observe uncovered eye for any vertical/
diagonal corrective movement (if present, test is +ve)
Plus acute
hearing loss
•	 Rub fingers next to both ears and ask if new unilateral hearing
loss. If new and 1st onset of acute continuous vertigo, this is a
+ve for a central sign9
