<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Simple fracture of limbs - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Signs and symptoms</p>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Close fracture</li>
<li>Deformed or fracture limb with no open wound</li>
<li>History of trauma or injury<ul><li>Important to get a mechanism of injury</li><li>Timing of trauma</li></ul></li>
<li>Develop :<ul><li>Pain</li><li>swelling</li><li>bruising</li><li>deformity</li><li>↓range of motion (ROM) due to pain</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. Important clinical assessment need to document in</h2>
<p>the folder</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Examine the blood supply distal to the possible fracture.<ul><li>Is the extremity warm or cold?</li><li>Is the extremity pale?</li><li>Is there a puise distal to the fracture'?</li><li>Check capillary filling (press the nails)-docs the pink colour return within 1-2 seconds aller releasing pressure?)</li></ul></li>
<li>Examine the nerve supply to the distal limb<ul><li>Is there any feeling or sensation in the distal limb or hand (use a pin prick or cotton wool)?</li><li>Test both sides of the extremity for feeling.</li><li>Have the patient perform specific movements.</li></ul></li>
<li>Assessing nerve function if forearm fracture<ul><li>Are all finger movements distal to the fracture normal?</li><li>Can all the fingers move normally as if the patient is playing a piano (can the patient spread the fingers)?</li><li>Can the patient make a fist and flex and extend the hand at the wrist?</li><li>Can the patient hyperextend the thumb?</li><li>Can the patient move his thumb to touch his little finger'</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do primary + secondary survey as per Traumatic injuries</li>
<li>Assess against Criteria for early notification of trauma for interfacility transfer (inside front cover)</li>
<li>Immobilise/apply splint on a fractured limb in neutral position. Severely deformed limb from injury with highly suspicious of fracture or dislocation</li>
<li>NEED Manupulation under sedation (Adult):<ul><li>IV midazolam 2 - 3mg / IM Morphine 10mg  (dose 0.05mg – 0.1mg / kg / dose)</li><li>Reduction : Traction and counter-traction manouvre to achieve a straight and neutral position</li><li>Apply a splint or a back-slap to hold the limb in neutral position</li><li>Elevate</li><li>Consult surgeon/MA for urgent referral</li></ul></li>
<li>Regular pain relief – Panadol and NSAIDs</li>
<li>No need to give antibiotic ALWAYS DOCUMENT NEUROVASCULAR FINDINGS BEFORE AND AFTER MANUPULATION AND REDUCTION PROCEDURE PLASTER BACKSLAB<sup>2,4</sup> Positioning of slab</li>
<li>Discuss with MO + see Backslabs for guidance What you need</li>
<li>Plaster bandages eg 7.5/10 cm - short arms/wrists, 15/20 cm - long arm</li>
<li>Undercast padding</li>
<li>Bowl of cold water + trauma scissors</li>
<li>Crepe bandages + sling How to do</li>
<li>Remove all rings, bracelets, watches, restrictive clothing, shoes<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Apply 2-3 layers of padding + 50% overlap beyond margins of plaster</li>
<li>Measure a length of plaster 1 cm shorter than the padding/ stockinette at each end. Fold the roll in about ten layers to the same length</li>
<li>Submerge in water until bubbling stops, remove + squeeze out excess</li>
<li>Lightly mould to the contours of the limb in a neutral position</li>
<li>Plaster must not fully encircle the limb</li>
<li>Turn back padding over ends of plaster + apply crepe bandage firmly over slab</li>
<li>Elevate limb on pillows or sling for arm</li>
<li>Advise patient it may take 4-6 weeks to heal + Plaster care ± crutches if not evacuated PLASTER BACKSLAB<sup>2,4</sup> Plaster care advice<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>Elevate limb above level of heart when possible using a sling/ pillows, will ↓ pain + swelling</li>
<li>Exercise fingers/toes + other joints that aren't covered by the plaster by bending/stretching/wiggling them</li>
<li>Avoid applying pressure + heat to the cast</li>
<li>Use plastic bag to protect plaster while in shower + do not scratch under the cast with a sharp object, if damaged return to clinic</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated, admit for close monitoring and always check the injured limb for signs of compartment syndrome.</li>
<li>Always document and report all concern matters to MA/Orthopedic surgeon</li>
<li>if ↑ pain or swelling, numbness, tingling or skin colour changes to blue or white<sup>6</sup><ul><li>urgently contact MO for urgent referral</li><li>treat as compartment syndrome until proven otherwise.</li></ul></li>

</ul>
