<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Also see <a href="/docs/Emergency/Hyperosmolar-hyperglycaemic-state">Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state (HHS)</a></p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul>
<li>Can be rapidly fatal. Avoid delays to treatment - IV fluids, potassium replacement + IV insulin<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li>Be aware - excessive thirst + frequent urination can be a sign of undiagnosed diabetes</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul>
<li>DKA - BGL &gt; 11 + acidosis, sudden onset<sup>1,3</sup></li>
<li>HHS - BGL &gt; 33.3 + profound dehydration without ketosis - gradual onset, commonly caused by acute illness<sup>1</sup> — see <a href="/docs/Emergency/Hyperosmolar-hyperglycaemic-state">HHS</a></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>4</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>↑BGL</li>
<li>Rapid breathing (Kussmaul breathing)</li>
<li>Excessive thirst, dehydration</li>
<li>Abdominal pain ± vomiting</li>
<li>Fruity sweet smelling breath (acetone - similar to paint thinner or nail polish remover)</li>
<li>Confusion, ↓LOC or unresponsive</li>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> DKA occurs mainly in patients with type 1 diabetes. It is also seen in patients with type 2 diabetes, particularly if taking sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, when DKA may occur without hyperglycaemia<sup>5</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>2,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>DRSABCD</li>
<li>Rapidly assess + manage concurrently</li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Insert IVC x 2 into large veins</li>
<li>Start IV fluids - sodium chloride 0.9%
<ul>
<li>adult 1000 mL/hour, repeat if systolic BP &lt; 100</li>
<li>child only give on MO order</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Finger prick/blood ketones if available<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Contact MO urgently</li>
<li>Look for signs of acute illness<sup>1,3</sup> eg <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a>, ACS, Stroke</li>
<li>Start fluid balance</li>
<li>Continuous cardiac monitoring</li>
<li>Do neurological observations - excessive fluids can cause cerebral oedema eg headache, ↓LOC, agitation/aggression</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>polyuria + excessive thirst, note: may be absent in young child<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>if toilet trained child - any new bed wetting or ‘accidents’<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>recent weight loss, ↑appetite<sup>2,3</sup></li>
<li>vomiting, abdominal pain, non-specific symptoms + malaise<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If known diabetic, ask about:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>antihyperglycaemics, insulin - total daily dose, adherence to regimen<sup>5</sup></li>
<li>past DKA</li>
<li>initiation of new medicines including over-the-counter</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:<sup>2,3</sup>
<ul>
<li>look for signs of infection eg appendicitis, ileus + pancreatitis</li>
<li>weight</li>
<li>Hydration assessment - adult or child</li>
<li>MSU for MCS + pregnancy test if female of reproductive age</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MO will arrange urgent evacuation + may order:<sup>2,3</sup>
<ul>
<li>potassium replacement + IV short acting insulin infusion eg Actrapid®, see Management guide</li>
<li>give patient’s usual subcut long acting insulin (if already on it)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If ↑BGL + ketones but not acutely unwell, closely monitor + aggressively manage to prevent DKA<sup>2</sup></li>
</ul>
