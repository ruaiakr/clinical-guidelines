Mumps
Background
Mumps is a viral infection caused by an RNA paramyxovirus spread via respiratory
droplets that primarily affects the parotid glands, causing painful swelling. For
unvaccinated children, it often occurs in school-aged children It can lead to
complications such as orchitis, meningitis, and pancreatitis, particularly in post-
pubertal males.
Recommendations
•	 Vaccination with the MMR vaccine is the best way to prevent mumps.
•	 Isolate individuals with suspected mumps to prevent transmission.
•	 Supportive care is essential to manage symptoms.
1.
May present with
•	 Painful swelling of the parotid glands (near the jaw)
•	 Fever and headache
•	 Malaise and fatigue
•	 Pain during chewing or swallowing
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Isolate the patient to reduce the risk of transmission.
•	 Provide analgesics for pain relief (e.g., paracetamol).
•	 Encourage fluids and soft foods to ease swallowing.

4.	 Management
•	 Provide symptomatic relief with pain management and hydration.
•	 Monitor for complications such as orchitis, meningitis, and pancreatitis.
•	 Educate the patient on the importance of isolation to prevent spread.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Follow up within 2-3 weeks to monitor for complications.
•	 Check for any residual symptoms, particularly related to orchitis or hearing loss.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to a specialist if complications like orchitis or meningitis are suspected.
•	 Consider referral to a hearing specialist if there is suspected hearing loss.
