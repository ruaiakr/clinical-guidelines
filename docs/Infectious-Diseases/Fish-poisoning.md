Fish poisoning (Ciguatera)
Background
Ciguatera fish poisoning is caused by eating fish that have accumulated toxins
produced by marine dinoflagellates. The toxins are often found in large reef fish like
barracuda, grouper, and snapper, and can cause neurological symptoms.
Recommendations
•	 Educate patients about the risks of eating reef fish from known areas of ciguatera
contamination.
•	 Early management includes supportive care and symptomatic treatment for the
neurological effects of poisoning.
•	 Avoidance of further consumption of contaminated fish.
1.
May present with
•	 Nausea and vomiting
•	 Diarrhea
•	 Tingling or numbness, especially in the fingers and toes
•	 Muscle weakness
•	 Temperature reversal (cold feels hot and hot feels cold)
•	 Abdominal pain
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Symptomatic treatment: antiemetics for nausea, fluids for dehydration, and
analgesics for pain relief.
•	 No specific antidote; treatment is supportive.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess for signs of dehydration and neurological symptoms (e.g., tingling,
temperature reversal).
•	 Measure vital signs, including blood pressure and heart rate.
•	 Monitor for signs of severe poisoning, such as difficulty breathing or paralysis.

4.	 Management
•	 Administer fluids and electrolytes to prevent dehydration.
•	 Provide symptomatic relief for nausea, vomiting, and pain.
•	 Monitor closely for any worsening neurological symptoms.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Follow up within 24-48 hours to assess recovery.
•	 Monitor for lingering neurological symptoms, which may persist for weeks or
months.
