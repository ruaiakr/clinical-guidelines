<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acute pain - Adult/Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Acute pain</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If chest pain, go to <a href="/docs/Emergency/Chest-pain-assessment">Chest pain assessment</a></li>
<li>If severe pain:
<ul>
<li>get rapid history + check for allergies</li>
<li>contact MO urgently</li>
<li><strong>adult</strong> - insert IVC + give IV morphine or fentanyl</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If IVC delay, consider alternative routes e.g. IM/subcut</li>
<li>If severe pain + looks sick - screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
<li>If sudden onset severe headache/‘worst headache of life’ - go to Headache</li>
<li>If abdominal pain still give analgesia. <strong>Note:</strong> will not mask physical signs/hinder diagnosis</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>The most useful diagnostic approach to acute pain is to take a detailed and systematic history</li>
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li><strong>Site</strong> - where is it</li>
<li><strong>Onset</strong> - when did it start:
<ul>
<li>sudden or gradual</li>
<li>result of trauma/activity/cold/stress</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Characteristics</strong> eg sharp, throbbing, aching, burning, stabbing</li>
<li><strong>Radiation</strong> - does it spread anywhere else</li>
<li><strong>Associated symptoms</strong> eg nausea, vomiting, sweating, fever</li>
<li><strong>Timing</strong> - duration, constant or intermittent:
<ul>
<li>has anything changed the pain</li>
<li>ever had this pain before, how often does it occur</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Exacerbating or relieving factors:</strong>
<ul>
<li>eg rest, medicines, eating, position changes, ice/splinting</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Severity</strong> - at rest, on movement:
<ul>
<li>mild, moderate, severe</li>
<li>scale from 0–10 (0 = no pain, 10 = worst pain imaginable)</li>
<li>if child consider <strong>FLACC</strong> or <strong>FACES</strong></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>any pain relief already given/taken prior to presentation eg by carer, self, staff:
<ul>
<li>when, what, dose, how effective</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>pain relief used in past - what worked/did not work, side effects</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Get past history, including:
<ul>
<li>current medicines, over-the-counter medicines</li>
<li>opioid use (if any)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Take vital signs</li>
<li>If child - take weight</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-note-panel">
<ul>
<li><strong>Calculate score:</strong> 0 = relaxed + comfortable, 1–3 = mild discomfort, 4–6 = moderate pain, 7–10 = severe discomfort/pain</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">FACES 4+ years<sup>1,3</sup></div>
<p>“These faces show how much something can hurt. This face [point to left-most face] shows no pain. The faces show more and more pain [point to each from left to right] up to this one [point to right-most face]. It shows very much pain. Point to the face that shows how much you hurt [right now]”</p>

<ul>
<li>Clinician to say ‘hurt’ or ‘pain’ (language child understands) - not words like ‘happy’ or ‘sad’</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO if:
<ul>
<li>severe pain, even if settled after initial analgesia</li>
<li>fever, persistent tachycardia or tachypnoea, or hypotension</li>
<li>analgesia is not effective</li>
<li>unable to find cause of pain</li>
<li>recurrence of pre-existing condition</li>
<li>suspected opioid seeking</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-consult-intro">
<div class="pccm-consult-intro-banner">Analgesia<sup>1,4</sup></div>
<div class="pccm-consult-intro-body">
<ul>
<li>Consider medicine(s) already given</li>
<li>Use Step wise approach + consider Non-pharmacological options</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-step-panel">
<div class="pccm-step-heading">Non-pharmacological options<sup>1</sup></div>
<ul>
<li>Ice, massage, heat pack, repositioning, distraction, imagery, warm/quiet room/privacy, breathing techniques</li>
<li>Elevation + splinting of injuries/suspected fractures - immobilisation can ↓pain significantly</li>
<li>Reassurance - explain cause of pain + expected outcome to relieve anxiety</li>
<li>If infant/young child - breastfeeding, low lighting, bubbles, cuddling carer/parent, dummy use, swaddling</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">Monitor effect of analgesia</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Repeat pain scale:<sup>1,3</sup>
<ul>
<li>mild/moderate pain - 30–60 minutely as clinically indicated</li>
<li>severe pain - 10 minutely for 1st 30 minutes, then as required</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do vital signs as appropriate + give antiemetic if <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Nausea-and-vomiting">Nausea and vomiting</a></li>
<li><strong>Note:</strong> if given subcut, monitor for at least 2 hours due to delayed absorption/adverse effect</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer advice:</strong> Do not take if dehydrated eg due to vomiting or diarrhoea. Take with a glass of water. If upsets stomach take with food. May cause nausea, indigestion, GI bleeding, diarrhoea, headache, dizziness, fluid retention or hypertension</p>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> If renal impairment, taking diuretics, ACEIs, or ARBs consult MO. Use with caution if asthma, cardiovascular disease or ↑cardiovascular risk, taking lithium or anticoagulants</p>
<p><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> Avoid use. May ↑rate of miscarriage. Seek specialist advice for use in the 2nd half of pregnancy; do not use during the last few days before expected birth</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Dehydration, active peptic ulcer disease or GI bleeding, severe renal, heart or liver failure, coagulation disorders</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a><sup>7</sup></p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">

<p><strong>Note:</strong> If hepatic impairment consult MO. *Reduce dose if risk factors for toxicity ie <strong>Adult:</strong> (dehydration, alcohol use, under-nutrition/low body weight, anticonvulsants, elderly/frail) - if ≥ 50 kg, ↓max. dose to 3 g/24 hours; if &lt; 50 kg, give 15 mg/kg (max. 4 doses/24 hours). <strong>Child:</strong> (fever, dehydration, under-nutrition, or if &lt; 5 kg) - ↓max. dose to 45 mg/kg/24 hours (do not exceed 3 g/24 hours). Also see Guideline for safe paracetamol use <a href="https://www.health.qld.gov.au/clinical-practice/guidelines-procedures/medicines/safety" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://www.health.qld.gov.au/clinical-practice/guidelines-procedures/medicines/safety</a></p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. Recognise + treat suspected paracetamol toxicity without delay. Contact MA or MO at hospital</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If morphine/fentanyl needed, patient will likely need retrieval for further management</li>
<li>If not retrieved, follow up as per MO + likely cause of pain</li>
<li>If supplied with paracetamol or ibuprofen + further pain relief is needed after 48 hours, consult MO</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO if further analgesia is needed + max. dose has been given</li>
</ul>
</div>
