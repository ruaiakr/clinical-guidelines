<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Shoulder Dystocia (Kiribati Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Shoulder dystocia occurs when, after delivery of the fetal head, one or both shoulders become impacted against the maternal pelvis, preventing delivery. This leads to compression of the umbilical cord and interruption of fetal oxygen supply. It is an unpredictable emergency that can occur in any birth but is more common in large babies and in mothers with certain risk factors. Rapid recognition and coordinated manoeuvres are essential to reduce the risk of fetal injury, including hypoxia, nerve injury, and death.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommended</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Shoulder dystocia is an obstetric emergency where the shoulders fail to deliver after the head has been born. This can rapidly lead to fetal hypoxia and injury. Immediate, coordinated action is required to deliver the baby safely.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Head delivers but shoulders do not follow</li>
<li>“Turtle sign” – retraction of the fetal head against the perineum</li>
<li>Difficulty delivering the shoulders</li>
<li>Prolonged second stage of labour</li>
<li>Failure of normal traction to deliver shoulders</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help immediately</li>
<li>Call doctor/obstetrician urgently if available</li>
<li>Explain situation to the woman</li>
<li>DO NOT pull on the fetal head (risk of severe injury)</li>
<li>Position woman immediately for McRoberts manoeuvre:
<ul>
<li>Move woman to edge of bed</li>
<li>Flex both legs tightly onto abdomen</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Apply suprapubic pressure:
<ul>
<li>Apply firm downward pressure just above the pubic bone</li>
<li>Aim to rotate the baby’s shoulder under the symphysis pubis</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Confirm diagnosis (head delivered, shoulders stuck)</li>
<li>Note time of head delivery</li>
<li>Assess fetal condition</li>
<li>Identify risk factors if known (macrosomia, diabetes, prolonged labour)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<p>Follow HELPER mnemonic in sequence:</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="helperr-h">H – Help</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for assistance immediately</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="helperr-e-episiotomy">E – Episiotomy</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider if additional space required</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="helperr-l">L – Legs (McRoberts manoeuvre)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hyperflex hips tightly to abdomen</li>
<li>Two assistants may be needed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="helperr-p">P – Suprapubic pressure</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Third assistant applies pressure above pubic bone</li>
<li>Do NOT apply fundal pressure</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="helperr-e-enter">E – Enter manoeuvres (internal rotation)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If trained, insert hand into vagina</li>
<li>Rotate shoulders into oblique diameter</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="helperr-r-remove">R – Remove posterior arm</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Enter vagina posteriorly</li>
<li>Grasp posterior arm at axilla</li>
<li>Sweep arm across chest and deliver</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="helperr-r-roll">R – Roll the woman</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Turn onto all fours (if possible)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="additional-manoeuvres">Additional manoeuvres (if needed)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Apply gentle lateral traction only
<ul>
<li>Do NOT jerk or overstretch the neck</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If bilateral shoulder dystocia:
<ul>
<li>Push shoulder toward fetal chest</li>
<li>Rotate into oblique diameter</li>
<li>Deliver as for unilateral dystocia</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Alternative:
<ul>
<li>Rotate baby 180 degrees to bring posterior shoulder forward</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess newborn immediately:
<ul>
<li>Breathing, tone, injuries</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Monitor mother closely:
<ul>
<li>Postpartum haemorrhage (common)</li>
<li>Perineal trauma</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Insert IV line if required</li>
<li>Monitor uterus:
<ul>
<li>Every 15 min for first 2 hours</li>
<li>Then hourly for 4 hours</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check temperature 6-hourly for 2–3 days</li>
<li>Document:
<ul>
<li>Time of head delivery</li>
<li>Time of full delivery</li>
<li>Manoeuvres used</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Debrief the woman after delivery
<ul>
<li>Explain what happened</li>
<li>Discuss potential complications</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral and consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call doctor/obstetrician immediately where available</li>
<li>Prepare for neonatal resuscitation</li>
<li>Refer mother and baby if complications occur</li>
<li>Document and report incident</li>
</ul>
</div>
