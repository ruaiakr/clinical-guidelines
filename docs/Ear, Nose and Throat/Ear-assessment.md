<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Ear assessment - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommendations</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check every child’s ears at each clinic visit</li>
<li>Encourage families to bring their child to the clinic if they notice any ear symptoms or have concerns about hearing or speech development</li>
<li>If hearing loss is suspected, liaise with the school or daycare to support the child with classroom strategies such as sound field amplification or seating arrangements</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Otitis media (OM) is inflammation or infection of the middle ear. In children, OM may develop within the first months of life and often becomes recurrent or chronic, potentially causing hearing loss</li>
<li>Hearing loss can significantly affect speech and language development and educational outcomes</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Preventing Ear Infections</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Practise regular handwashing, especially after nose blowing or coughing</li>
<li>Keep hands and face free of nasal discharge</li>
<li>Ensure children are up to date with pneumococcal and influenza vaccinations</li>
<li>Teach and model ‘Blow, Breathe, Cough’ technique</li>
<li>Breastfeed if possible; if bottle feeding, use an upright feeding position (avoid feeding while lying flat)</li>
<li>Avoid the use of dummies for children over 6 months of age</li>
<li>Keep sick children away from babies</li>
<li>Avoid exposing children to tobacco smoke</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>No symptoms - routine check of ears</li>
<li>Sore, itchy, or discharging ear(s) or hearing loss</li>
<li>Young child - pulling/rubbing/holding ear(s), unsettled/irritable, ↓feeding</li>
<li>Concerns about ears, hearing or language/talking</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If unwell child with fever, admit and start antibiotic</li>
<li>Monitor vital sign</li>
<li>Consultation or refer.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If ear symptoms, ask about:
<ul>
<li>onset, severity, duration</li>
<li>pain/itchy</li>
<li>discharge - colour, amount</li>
<li>deafness, fullness</li>
<li>dizziness/tinnitus (ringing in the ear)</li>
<li>recent swimming/water sports</li>
<li>trauma/blow to ear eg slap, diving, object poked in<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Any other symptoms eg:
<ul>
<li>runny nose, sore throat, cough</li>
<li>hearing loss</li>
<li>concerns about language/talking, learning or behavioural problems (could mean ↓ hearing)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Get past history:
<ul>
<li>prior ear infection(s), perforation(s), operation(s) to ear, ENT review - what, when, treatment</li>
<li>prior serious illnesses eg meningitis</li>
<li>last hearing test/audiology (date), results</li>
<li>family history of ear/hearing concerns</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Examine ears<sup>1,4,6</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Examine both ears. Start with normal ear</li>
<li>Check pinna (outer ear). Any:
<ul>
<li>discharge, flaky or scaly skin</li>
<li>pain on moving pinna or tragus (flap of skin in front of ear canal)</li>
<li>is ear pushed out/forwards</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check behind ear for redness or swelling</li>
<li>Palpate for swollen/tender lymph glands - around ears and neck</li>
<li>Look inside ear with otoscope - if pain levels allow:
<ul>
<li>pull pinna to straighten canal: adult - up, out and back; child - down and back</li>
<li>if pus/discharge - gently clean with Tissue spears first</li>
<li>if impacted wax prevents examination consider syringing with warm water (if skilled):<sup>7</sup></li>
<li>note: do NOT irrigate if perforation likely, diabetes, immunocompromised or on anticoagulants<sup>7</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Ear canal:
<ul>
<li>swelling, redness, lump(s), flakes/scales</li>
<li>discharge, foreign body, debris, dislodged grommet (ventilation tube)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Tympanic membrane (TM) (eardrum):<sup>1,6</sup>
<ul>
<li>colour - pearly grey, yellow/amber, pink/red, white (pus), white chalky patches (scarring)</li>
<li>translucency - shiny/translucent (normal), opaque, dull/cloudy, fluid/bubbles</li>
<li>position - neutral, bulging or retracted (sucked in):</li>
<li>handle of malleus is prominent if retracted, or not seen well if bulging<sup>6</sup></li>
<li>perforation (hole) - position, duration, size (a readily visible hole is ‘moderate’ to ‘large’ size):</li>
<li>a severely retracted eardrum, retraction pocket or healed hole might be mistaken for a perforation</li>
<li>grommet</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>Eardrum Red Flags</strong>
<p>If <strong>any</strong> of the following signs are observed, further assessment is required:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hole or retraction in the upper (attic) part of the eardrum</li>
<li>Crusting, granulation tissue, or discharge in the attic region</li>
<li>Eardrum appears severely retracted (sucked in)</li>
<li>Presence of a dull white mass behind the eardrum</li>
<li>Perforation located near the edge of the eardrum</li>
</ul>
<p>➔ <strong>Possible cholesteatoma</strong></p>
<p>Consult a MA or Medical Assistance <strong>urgently</strong> for ENT referral.</p>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consider looking for movement of intact eardrum(s) if not too painful:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>if eardrum moves in and out easily you can exclude a perforation + fluid in the middle ear (ie exclude otitis media and acute otitis media)</li>
<li>check for movement by: gentle valsalva (hold nose and blow), pneumatic otoscopy or tympanometry (Type A is normal)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Hearing Test:<br />
“The Audiometer” is the machine measures the sound levels that people can hear in units called decibels in hearing level (dB HL). For example, people with normal hearing may be able to hear a sound at a level of 0 decibel (0 dB HL). A person with hearing loss may only be able to hear a sound at a level of 50 decibels which is in the range for moderate hearing impairment. This test available now at the central hospital (TCH-Nawerewere).</li>
<li>Weber’s and Rinne’s tests:<br />
These tests assess air conduction (AC) and bone conduction (BC) and are used to help delineate whether hearing loss is sensorineural (SNHL) or conductive (CHL) in origin</li>
<li>Check nose, throat, chest + other systems as needed.</li>
</ul>
</div>
