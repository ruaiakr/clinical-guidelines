Gastritis - Adult
Background1
•	 Inflammation of the gastric mucosa commonly caused from Helicobacter pylori
infection, use of NSAID, or alcohol
1.
May present with1,2
•	 Epigastric pain or discomfort ± nausea, vomiting, heartburn, reflux
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Do vital signs
•	 Insert IVC x 2
•	 If shock - pale, sweaty, HR, capillary refill > 2 seconds ± LOC:

–
contact MO urgently + see Shock
•	 Assess as per Chest pain assessment, + see ACS
•	 Offer analgesia ± antiemetic. See Acute pain, Nausea and vomiting
3.	 Clinical assessment1,2
•	 Do not jump to conclusions as to the cause of the epigastric pain in a person
who drinks alcohol
•	 Do NOT use medication cocktails eg pink lady, to assist in ruling out a cardiac
cause3
•	 Ask about:

–
pain - onset + duration

–
is it accompanied by nausea, vomiting or heartburn

–
any blood in vomit - fresh or coffee grounds, ± melaena, go to Upper GI
bleeding

–
recent heavy alcohol intake

–
are they taking NSAID, aspirin, antacid

–
previous episodes - when, any treatment

–
recent weight loss, difficulty or painful swallowing

–
other symptoms eg SOB, dizziness, syncope/collapse

–
recent food intake
•	 Get past history - heart disease, GORD, peptic ulcer disease, abdominal surgery
•	 Do physical examination, including Abdominal examination +

–
ECG, BGL + bloods/i-STAT - lactate

4.	 Management1,2
•	 Contact MO urgently if intense boring pain that radiates to the back
•	 Consult MO, who may advise:

–
IV pantoprazole ± thiamine if alcohol related

–
bloods eg lipase, LFTs

–
retrieval/hospitalisation ± referral for endoscopy
5.	 Follow up
•	 Advise to return if symptoms  or new symptoms eg blood in vomit - contact
MO urgently. Otherwise advise to see MO at next clinic
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 As above
