<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Suicidal behaviour – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Identify and respond to suicidal thoughts or behaviours early</li>
<li>Assess risk and implement appropriate safety and management plans</li>
<li>Consult MO for assessment, planning and ongoing care</li>
<li>Refer all cases of suicide attempt to MO for review</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Suicidal behaviour includes thoughts, plans, or actions intended to cause self-harm or death</li>
<li>It is often associated with recent stressors, mental illness, or limited psychosocial supports</li>
<li>Both adults and children may present with suicidal ideation or actions</li>
<li>Timely assessment and appropriate safety planning are essential to prevent further harm</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Verbalises suicidal ideas or suicidal intent</li>
<li>Previous presentations for suicidal thoughts or deliberate self-harm</li>
<li>A recent psychosocial stressor or loss e.g. grief, relationship breakdown, loss of job, pregnancy or new birth</li>
<li>Feelings of helplessness or hopelessness</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><a href="/docs/Emergency/Basic-life-support">DRSABCD</a></li>
<li>Do not leave the patient alone</li>
<li>If the person has survived an attempted suicide or self-harm event, manage accordingly</li>
<li>Consider injuries consistent with self-harm attempt, e.g. for hanging, see <a href="/docs/Emergency/Traumatic-injuries">Traumatic injuries</a>, <a href="/docs/Emergency/Spinal-injuries">Spinal injuries</a></li>
<li>If the patient is highly agitated, expressing intent to suicide, aggressive or violent, see <a href="/docs/Mental%20health/Acute-severe-behavioral-disturbance">Mental health emergency</a></li>
<li>It is important to ask the person directly about feelings or thoughts of suicide</li>
<li>Asking about self-harm does not provoke acts of self-harm</li>
<li>Discuss the balance of confidentiality versus notifying others to ensure safety</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ensure the patient has an opportunity to discuss their suicidality alone</li>
<li>With consent, seek information from family, friends, support persons or others</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="communication-skills">Communication skills<sup>1</sup></h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Listen. Allow the patient to speak without interruption</li>
<li>Develop and maintain rapport</li>
<li>Be mindful of your own values and beliefs in relation to suicide</li>
<li>Convey warmth, non-judgemental acceptance, and interest in the patient’s distress</li>
<li>Be respectful and empathic</li>
<li>Avoid minimising the seriousness of suicide risk</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Ask Questions about suicidal thoughts:</strong><sup>2,4</sup></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask the person directly about suicide</li>
<li>Asking about self-harm does not provoke self-harm<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>Discuss confidentiality versus the need to notify others for safety</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Questions about suicidal thoughts<sup>1–4</sup></h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Suicide risk</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>When people feel like you have been feeling, they sometimes think life is not worth living. Have you been thinking like that?</li>
<li>Have you been thinking of harming yourself?</li>
<li>Are you thinking of suicide?</li>
<li>If yes, how often?
<ul>
<li>Have you thought about how you would act? Is there a plan? Is it feasible and likely to be lethal?</li>
<li>Have you thought about when you might act?</li>
<li>Are there any reasons that stop you from acting on these thoughts?</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Have you tried to harm yourself in the past?
<ul>
<li>If yes, how many times?</li>
<li>When was the most recent time?</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do you know anyone who has recently attempted suicide?</li>
<li>Do you feel safe at the moment?</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">If a suicide attempt has been made</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>What did you hope would happen as a result?</li>
<li>Did you want to die, or to end your pain?</li>
<li>Do you regret not succeeding?</li>
<li>Do you still have access to the method used?</li>
<li>Did you use alcohol or drugs before the attempt? What did you use?</li>
<li>Do you have easy access to a weapon?</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange">Risk of harm to others</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Have you thought of hurting anyone else?
<ul>
<li>If yes, have you acted on these thoughts?</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Have you been in any recent fights?
<ul>
<li>If yes, were drugs or alcohol involved?</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO who will plan:
<ul>
<li>Immediate and ongoing management in the community, or</li>
<li>Transfer/evacuation for comprehensive care</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If the patient is the primary carer for children or vulnerable people, consider alternative care. See <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-protection">Child protection</a><sup>1,4</sup></li>
<li>Discuss the patient’s behaviour with family or friends to reduce their anxiety or anger</li>
<li>In consultation with MO, assess whether the patient is safe to be managed at home<sup>4,5</sup>
<ul>
<li>Acute problems are being addressed</li>
<li>Patient knows how to seek help if suicidal thoughts return</li>
<li>Patient is not intoxicated, sedated, delirious or psychotic</li>
<li>‘Lethal means’ counselling is completed:
<ul>
<li>Identify who will manage access to means of suicide (e.g. guns, knives, ropes, drugs, poisons, high places)</li>
<li>Discuss if occupation allows access to lethal means (e.g. pesticides, firearms, medications)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Identify who manages medications in the home</li>
<li>Reduce quantities of prescription medications</li>
<li>If the patient has access to a firearm, notify police at: <a href="https://www.police.qld.gov.au/weapon-licensing/mental-and-physical-health" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.police.qld.gov.au/weapon-licensing/mental-and-physical-health</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Ensure the following are in place:
<ul>
<li>Follow-up arrangements documented and shared with patient and carer</li>
<li>Treatment plan arranged for mental health and medical needs</li>
<li>Written Safety Plan given to patient and support person</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Develop a Safety Plan with the patient. It involves<sup>2,4</sup>:
<ul>
<li>Recognising warning signs and triggers</li>
<li>Making surroundings safe</li>
<li>Listing reminders of reasons to live</li>
<li>Identifying what helps them feel strong</li>
<li>Naming people and places to connect with</li>
<li>Listing family/friends they can talk to</li>
<li>Including professional supports</li>
<li>See: <a href="https://www.beyondblue.org.au/get-support/beyondnow-suicide-safety-planning" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.beyondblue.org.au/get-support/beyondnow-suicide-safety-planning</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
