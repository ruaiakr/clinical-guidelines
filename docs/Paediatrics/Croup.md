<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Croup - Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Croup usually develops over a few days with concurrent URTI like symptoms</li>
<li>It is a viral inflammation and swelling of the upper airway, which can lead to obstruction</li>
<li>Also see Croup - emergency management in children</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>sudden onset of Barking cough</li>
<li>Inspiratory stridor, hoarseness of voice</li>
<li>± respiratory distress:
<ul>
<li>accessory muscle use, abdominal breathing, inter/subcostal recession, tracheal tug</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>URTI like symptoms
<ul>
<li>they may have associated wide spread wheeze, fever but no signs of toxicity</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Always consider differential diagnosis<sup>1</sup> eg Foreign body inhalation, <a href="/docs/Emergency/Button-battery-ingestioninsertion">Button battery</a>, <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Pertussis">Pertussis</a>, <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Bronchiolitis">Bronchiolitis</a>, <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Epiglottitis">Epiglottitis</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>DO NOT EXAMINE THE THROAT.</strong> do not upset child further as distress may exacerbate symptoms</li>
<li>Assess for Risk factors for severe croup<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>Risk factors for severe croup<sup>1</sup></strong>
<ul class="pccm-content-list" style="margin-bottom:0;">
<li>&lt; 6 months of age</li>
<li>Structural upper airway condition eg tracheomalacia</li>
<li>Previous severe croup</li>
<li>Unplanned re-presentation &lt; 24 hours of first croup presentation</li>
<li>Down syndrome</li>
</ul>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Avoid distressing child as may increase symptoms. Nurse child upright on parent/carer’s lap</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-note-panel">
<p><strong>NOTE:</strong> loudness of the stridor is not a good indicator of the severity of the obstruction</p>
<p>Investigations: Routine blood tests/imaging/and nasopharyngeal swabs are not recommended</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If any risk factors, contact MO promptly</li>
<li>In all cases:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>minimize handling, keep the child calm eg sitting quietly, reading or watching TV</li>
<li>keep the child with parents/carers to ↓distress</li>
<li>allow the child to adopt a position of comfort that minimizes airway obstruction</li>
<li>reassure and educate parents/carers on the cause, usual course and management to ↓ anxiety</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<td><strong>Mild</strong></td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Home treatment: symptomatic care including antipyretics, mist and oral fluids</li>
<li>Outpatient treatment: Prednisolone 1 mg/kg/dose BD for 3 days</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Moderate</strong></td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Oral prednisolone 1 mg/kg/dose BD for 3 days</li>
<li>Nebulized adrenaline 0.5 mL/kg using the 1 mg/mL (1:1000 solution) diluted in 3 mL N/S; (max doses: less than or 4 years old: 2.4 mL/dose; more than 4 years old: 5 mL/dose); repeat dose of adrenaline can be given 20 mins – 1 hr after the 1st dose</li>
<li>Refer for admission if 2nd dose needed</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Severe</strong></td>
<td>
<ul>
<li>Give oxygen without causing distress. Consider using oxygen tubing held near nose/mouth - 10 L/minute</li>
<li>Contact Dr/MO urgently who may order:
<ul>
<li>adrenaline nebulizer</li>
<li>prednisolone 2 mg/kg (max. 50 mg)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</td>
</tr>
