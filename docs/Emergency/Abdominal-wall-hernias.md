<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Abdominal wall hernias</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>A hernia is defined as the protrusion of a viscus or its contents through a weakness in the abdominal wall. These are a common cause of abdominal pain and must be assessed during abdominal examination.</p>
<p><strong>Hernias can be classified as follows:</strong></p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommendations</h2>
<p><strong>All patients presenting with abdominal pain or swelling should be examined</strong></p>
<p>for hernia. Refer urgently if the hernia is irreducible, painful, or associated with symptoms of bowel obstruction. Avoid attempts to manually reduce the hernia.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. Anatomical type:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list"><li>Inguinal hernia</li><li>Umbilical hernia</li></ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Reducibility:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list"><li>Reducible hernia</li><li>Non-reducible (incarcerated or strangulated) hernia</li></ul>
<p>Hernias may present without symptoms or may become painful or obstructed.</p>
<p><strong>Bowel herniation into the hernia sac may lead to obstruction, strangulation, or</strong></p>
<p>ischaemia.</p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Abnormal swelling, especially in the umbilical or groin area</li>
<li>Swelling may reduce when lying flat (if reducible)</li>
<li>Pain or tenderness over the swelling</li>
<li>Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal distension if bowel obstruction present</li>
<li>Incarcerated or strangulated hernias may present with:<ul><li>Severe abdominal pain</li><li>Non-reducible tender mass</li><li>Constipation or no passage of flatus</li><li>Fever or signs of sepsis</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<p>If signs of intestinal involvement are present (severe abdominal pain, vomiting, distension):</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Admit the patient immediately</li>
<li>Keep nil by mouth (NBM)</li>
<li>Insert nasogastric tube for decompression and free drainage</li>
<li>Start IV fluid resuscitation</li>
<li>Start broad-spectrum antibiotics</li>
<li>Provide analgesia</li>
<li>Arrange urgent referral to the main hospital</li>
<li>Surgical consultation must occur within 6 hours to avoid strangulation and ischaemic bowel If no signs of intestinal involvement:</li>
<li>Provide pain relief</li>
<li>Advise rest and avoid lifting or strenuous activity</li>
<li>Avoid massage or manual reduction (may risk ischaemia)</li>
<li>Refer to surgeon for elective hernia repair</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Inspect for visible abdominal or groin swelling</li>
<li>Palpate swelling: assess for tenderness and reducibility</li>
<li>Ask the patient to cough or stand to assess impulse</li>
<li>Check for signs of bowel obstruction:<ul><li>Distension</li><li>Bowel sounds (high-pitched or absent)</li><li>Vomiting or failure to pass stool/gas</li></ul></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Monitor for pain, recurrence, or worsening signs</li>
<li>Reassess hernia regularly for changes in size, tenderness, or reducibility</li>
<li>For reducible hernias, refer for elective surgical repair</li>
<li>For non-reducible or symptomatic hernias, urgent referral is required</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral / consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Urgent referral to surgical team if:<ul><li>Painful, tender, or non-reducible hernia</li><li>Signs of bowel obstruction or sepsis</li></ul></li>
<li>Elective referral for reducible hernias without complications</li>
<li>Do not attempt manual reduction of incarcerated or strangulated hernias outside hospital setting</li>
</ul>
