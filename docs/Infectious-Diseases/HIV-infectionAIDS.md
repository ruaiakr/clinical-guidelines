HIV infection/AIDS
Background
Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) attacks the immune system, specifically the
CD4+ T cells, and can lead to Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (AIDS) if
untreated. HIV is transmitted through blood, sexual contact, and from mother to
child during childbirth or breastfeeding.
Recommendations
•	 Early testing and diagnosis are essential for effective treatment and
management.
•	 Antiretroviral therapy (ART) is the cornerstone of treatment to suppress viral
replication and prevent progression to AIDS.
•	 Educate patients on prevention methods, including safe sex practices and harm
reduction strategies.
1.
May present with
•	 Acute HIV infection (fever, rash, swollen lymph nodes)
•	 Weight loss and fatigue
•	 Recurrent infections
•	 Opportunistic infections like tuberculosis (TB) or candidiasis in advanced disease
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Begin ART as soon as HIV is diagnosed, unless contraindicated.
•	 Provide supportive care for opportunistic infections.
•	 Offer counseling and support services for patients and their families.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess the patient's CD4 count and viral load to determine disease progression.
•	 Monitor for symptoms of opportunistic infections.
•	 Screen for comorbid conditions like tuberculosis and hepatitis B/C.
4.	 Management
•	 Initiate ART, including a combination of drugs like tenofovir, emtricitabine, and
efavirenz.
•	 Offer prophylaxis for opportunistic infections.
•	 Provide mental health support and education on adherence to treatment.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Regular follow-up visits every 3-6 months to monitor viral load, CD4 count, and
adherence to ART.
•	 Monitor for side effects of medications.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
Refer to a specialist for advanced HIV care or treatment of opportunistic infections.
Consider referral for psychosocial support or counseling services.
