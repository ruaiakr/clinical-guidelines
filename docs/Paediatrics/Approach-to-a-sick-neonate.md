<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Approach to a sick neonate (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Neonates can become seriously ill soon after birth or at any time during the neonatal period.</li>
<li>Many neonatal illnesses present with non-specific signs.</li>
<li>Always check for WHO danger signs and other serious abnormal clinical signs.</li>
<li>If any are present, stabilise the neonate urgently and then investigate the possible cause.</li>
<li>Perform a complete neonatal examination once the neonate is stable.</li>
<li>If the neonate was born at home or another facility, examine thoroughly on arrival.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Newborns are especially vulnerable in the first weeks of life. Early recognition of serious signs and prompt emergency care are essential to improve outcomes. WHO danger signs should always be checked, and emergency care must be provided while the underlying cause is being assessed.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="who-danger-signs">WHO Danger Signs</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not feeding well</li>
<li>Convulsions</li>
<li>Drowsy or unconscious</li>
<li>Movement only when stimulated or no movement</li>
<li>Tachypnoea (RR &gt; 60/minute)</li>
<li>Grunting</li>
<li>Severe chest in-drawing</li>
<li>Temperature ≥ 37.5 °C or &lt; 35.5 °C</li>
<li>Central cyanosis</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="additional-serious-signs">Additional Serious Abnormal Signs</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Nasal flaring</li>
<li>Respiratory rate &lt; 30/min or apnoea</li>
<li>Heart rate &lt; 100 or &gt; 180 bpm</li>
<li>Capillary refill time &gt; 2 seconds</li>
<li>Cardiac murmur</li>
<li>Lethargy, irritability, jitteriness, floppy, bulging fontanelle</li>
<li>Subtle seizures or abnormal movements</li>
<li>Ineffective suck</li>
<li>Jaundice in first 24 hours of life or extending to hands and soles</li>
<li>Abdominal distension or mass</li>
<li>Birth trauma (e.g. skull or clavicle fracture, brachial plexus injury)</li>
<li>Neural tube defects (e.g. spina bifida)</li>
<li>Omphalitis — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Omphalitis">Omphalitis (Cord Sepsis)</a></li>
<li>Extreme pallor</li>
<li>Numerous or large skin pustules</li>
<li>Swollen, painful joint or reduced joint movement</li>
<li>Recurrent hypoglycaemia (BGL &lt; 2.5 mmol/L on more than 2 occasions)</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>Note:</strong> Subtle seizures may include chewing or sucking movements, eye blinking, disorganised limb movements, or pedalling.
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Call for help immediately</li>
<li><strong>Airway:</strong> Maintain head and neck in a neutral position to keep airway open.</li>
<li><strong>Breathing:</strong> Start oxygen via nasal prongs (aim SaO₂ 90–95%)
<ul>
<li>If apnoeic or RR &lt; 20/min → start bag and mask ventilation.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Circulation:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Insert IV line (or intraosseous line if IV is difficult)</li>
<li>If signs of shock or poor perfusion → give:
<ul>
<li>IV 0.9% NaCl or Ringer lactate 10 mL/kg over 20 minutes</li>
<li>Repeat up to 2 more times if needed (max 3 boluses)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Blood glucose:</strong>
<ul>
<li>If BGL &lt; 2.5 mmol/L → give 2 mL/kg of 10% glucose IV slowly</li>
<li>If BGL not available and signs of hypoglycaemia (e.g. jitteriness) → treat as above</li>
<li>If no IV access → give expressed breast milk or 10% glucose via NGT/OGT</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Convulsions:</strong> If seizing, manage with anticonvulsants (refer to Section 3.3)</li>
<li><strong>OGT/NGT:</strong>
<ul>
<li>If abdominal distension → aspirate and leave tube open to drain</li>
<li>If respiratory distress → may be used for feeding as appropriate</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Continue resuscitation and monitoring</li>
<li>Assess airway, breathing, circulation, and neurological status</li>
<li>Begin identifying possible cause of illness</li>
<li>Perform full neonatal physical examination (see neonatal exam form in <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-health-visits#neonate-exam-checklist">Child health visits</a>)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Stabilise first using ABCD approach</li>
<li>Monitor temperature, oxygen saturation, blood glucose, and signs of perfusion</li>
<li>Start antibiotics if sepsis is suspected — see <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Neonatal-sepsis">Neonatal sepsis</a></li>
<li>Provide warmth, oxygen, fluids, and nutrition as required</li>
<li>Keep baby and mother together whenever possible</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Observe closely for changes in vital signs or new danger signs</li>
<li>Continue feeding support and check weight daily</li>
<li>Repeat blood glucose if previously low</li>
<li>Monitor fluid balance and input/output</li>
<li>Reassess for underlying diagnosis</li>
<li>Ensure documentation of all observations and treatment</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / Consultation</h2>
<p>Refer or admit to neonatal care unit if:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Any WHO danger sign or serious abnormal sign present</li>
<li>Apgar &lt; 4 at 1 min or &lt; 7 at 5 min</li>
<li>More than 2 minutes of bag and mask ventilation at birth</li>
<li>Low birthweight &lt; 2000 g</li>
<li>Any seizure or suspected serious condition (e.g. sepsis, birth trauma, congenital anomaly)</li>
<li>Baby cannot be safely managed in the current facility</li>
</ul>

</div>
