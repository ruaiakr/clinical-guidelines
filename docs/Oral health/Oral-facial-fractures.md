<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Oral facial fractures (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Purpose</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>To assist Medical Assistants and Nurses in the primary care setting to recognize, assess, provide initial management, and appropriately refer patients presenting with oral facial fractures.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Facial fractures are increasingly common presentations in primary healthcare. Prompt recognition and appropriate management are critical to prevent permanent functional, cosmetic, or vision-threatening complications.</li>
<li>Note: the Oral health section divider also refers to Fractured Jaw / Oral Facial Fractures protocols in the Emergency chapter. See also <a href="/docs/Emergency/Fractured-jaw">Fractured jaw</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">General Principles</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Follow Advanced Trauma Life Support (ATLS) protocols for all trauma cases.</li>
<li>Conduct a full visual assessment in any facial trauma.</li>
<li>Identify red flags requiring immediate referral.</li>
<li>Provide early pain management and wound care as appropriate.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Nasal Fractures:</strong> Often resulting from interpersonal violence, nasal fractures are the most common facial fractures, accounting for up to 58.6% of all facial fractures. Symptoms include nasal deformity, epistaxis, and periorbital bruising.<br /><a href="https://www.racgp.org.au/afp/2012/april/maxillofacial-trauma/">www.racgp.org.au/afp/2012/april/maxillofacial-trauma/</a></li>
<li><strong>Mandibular Fractures:</strong> These may present with pain, swelling, malocclusion, and limited mouth opening. They can result from blunt or penetrating trauma and may destabilize the airway.<br /><a href="https://www.entnet.org/wp-content/uploads/files/Trauma-Chapter-5.pdf">www.entnet.org/wp-content/uploads/files/Trauma-Chapter-5.pdf</a></li>
<li><strong>Zygomatic Fractures:</strong> Symptoms include periorbital swelling, infraorbital nerve paraesthesia, and restricted eye movement.</li>
<li><strong>Orbital Fractures:</strong> Signs include diplopia, reduced visual acuity, and orbital rim tenderness.</li>
<li><strong>Frontal Bone Fractures:</strong> Present with forehead deformity, possible CSF rhinorrhoea, and periorbital bruising.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Airway Management:</strong> Ensure airway patency, especially in cases of mandibular fractures that may compromise the airway.<br /><a href="https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-1346-6_48">https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-15-1346-6_48</a></li>
<li><strong>Hemostasis:</strong> Control bleeding using direct pressure, nasal packing, or hemostatic agents as appropriate.</li>
<li><strong>Analgesia:</strong> Administer appropriate pain relief, considering the severity of the injury.</li>
<li><strong>Antibiotics:</strong> Prophylactic antibiotics may be indicated in cases of open fractures or significant soft tissue involvement.<br /><a href="https://tsaco.bmj.com/content/9/1/e001304">https://tsaco.bmj.com/content/9/1/e001304</a></li>
<li><strong>Imaging:</strong> Obtain appropriate imaging studies, such as X-rays or CT scans, to assess the extent of the fractures.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>History:</strong> Obtain details of the mechanism of injury, time of injury, and any associated symptoms.</li>
<li><strong>Examination:</strong> Perform a thorough head and neck examination, assessing for deformities, swelling, tenderness, and neurological deficits.</li>
<li><strong>Functional Assessment:</strong> Evaluate the patient’s ability to open and close the mouth, chew, and speak.</li>
<li><strong>Neurological Examination:</strong> Assess cranial nerve function, particularly the trigeminal nerve, for sensory deficits.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Conservative Management:</strong> Indicated for non-displaced fractures without significant functional impairment. Includes analgesia, soft diet, and follow-up.<br /><a href="https://www.rch.org.au/trauma-service/manual/maxillofacial-injury/">https://www.rch.org.au/trauma-service/manual/maxillofacial-injury/</a></li>
<li><strong>Surgical Intervention:</strong> Required for displaced fractures, fractures with airway compromise, or those causing significant functional impairment.<br /><a href="https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/maxillofacial-trauma-19339123/19339123">https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/maxillofacial-trauma-19339123/19339123</a></li>
<li><strong>Referral:</strong> Refer to oral and maxillofacial specialists for definitive management.<br /><a href="https://metronorth.health.qld.gov.au/specialist_service/refer-your-patient/oral-maxillo-facial/facial-fractures-lacerations">https://metronorth.health.qld.gov.au/specialist_service/refer-your-patient/oral-maxillo-facial/facial-fractures-lacerations</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Monitoring:</strong> Regular follow-up to assess healing, manage complications, and address any functional issues.</li>
<li><strong>Rehabilitation:</strong> May include physiotherapy for jaw mobility and speech therapy if necessary.</li>
<li><strong>Psychological Support:</strong> Provide support for patients dealing with the psychological impact of facial trauma.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><strong>Urgent Referral:</strong> For patients with airway compromise, significant bleeding, or complex fractures.</li>
<li><strong>Routine Referral:</strong> For non-displaced fractures without functional impairment.</li>
<li><strong>Specialist Consultation:</strong> Consult with oral and maxillofacial surgeons for definitive management.<br /><a href="https://www.westernhealth.org.au/HealthProfessionals/Referrals/Documents/ASC%20Access%20and%20Referral%20Guideline_Oral%20Maxillofacial%20Surgery.pdf">https://www.westernhealth.org.au/HealthProfessionals/Referrals/Documents/ASC%20Access%20and%20Referral%20Guideline_Oral%20Maxillofacial%20Surgery.pdf</a></li>
</ul>
