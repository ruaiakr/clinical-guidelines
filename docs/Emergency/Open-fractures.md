# Open (compound) fractures - Adult/Child

Open (compound) fractures - Adult/Child
Defined as a fractures with a cut through the skin. The overlying skin is wounded
and the broken bone may be exposed. Compound fractures are known as open
fractures. These fractures can become infected easily and need thorough cleansing
and debriding as soon as possible after the injury.
GENERAL CONSIDERATION:
1.
Always treat as a trauma patients : Primary survey / secondary survey and
admit
2.
Consult MA/Orthopedic surgeon – for urgent referral and treatment opinion
3.
Open fracture treatment = exposed fracture bone to reduce + thorough wound
wash + IV antibiotic (Reduce the risk of osteomyelitis) + TT injection  +  splint
and elevate
1.
May present with
•	 Open wound on same limb segment as an associated fracture2
•	 Bone has broken the skin or mucous membrane ± visible bone1
•	 Wound may be small eg puncture3
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Do primary + secondary survey as per Traumatic injuries.
•	 Assess against Criteria for early notification of trauma for interfacility transfer
(inside front cover)
•	 Stop any bleeding by putting pressure on wound, not on exposed bone1
•	 If signs of neurovascular compromise contact MO urgently +

–
thoroughly irrigate with sodium chloride 0.9%

–
give antibiotics + requires urgent reduction
•	 Immobilise/apply splint as per Simple fracture of limbs.

–
irrigate with sodium chloride 0.9% if severely contaminated wound and
remove all debris and foreign objects.2

–
apply a saline-soaked dressing covered with an occlusive layer 3,4

–
apply a splint to immobilise the limb

–
elevate the limb and advice to rest on the bed (not to mobilise)
•	 Insert IVC
•	 Give analgesia. See Acute pain.
3.	 Clinical assessment 1,3
•	 Assess as per Simple fracture of limbs.

–
ask if contaminated environment eg agricultural, sewage, fresh or salt water1

–
assess neurovascular status distal to the fracture
