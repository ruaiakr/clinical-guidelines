Upper respiratory tract infection (URTI) - Child
Common cold
Recommend
•	 Viral infection is the most likely cause and antibiotics are not recommended1
Background
•	 Primary bacterial infection is uncommon, however secondary bacterial infection
may develop1
1.
Clinical signs and assessment
•	 Runny/blocked nose
•	 Sneezing, cough
•	 Sore throat
•	 Headache
•	 Malaise ± low grade fever

–
Assess the general condition of the child, including taking his/her weight

–
Examine the tympanic membranes with an otoscope to check for infection

–
If the child is coughing or wheezing, listen to his or her breathing and chest
sounds with stethoscope
2.	 Differential diagnosis
•	 Consider differential diagnosis.  See:

–
Sore throat

–
Child with fever

–
Child with cough

–
Child with stridor
•	 Contact MO if symptoms are:

–
severe or respiratory distress
3.	 Treatment and Management
•	 There are no specific treatments for viral upper respiratory infections
Kiribati Primary Clinical Care Manual | 549

•	 Encourage fluids and stress the importance of ensuring that the child remains
well hydrated
•	 Caregivers can use saline nose drops to clear nasal secretions.
•	 Check the immunization status of the child. If he/she is incompletely immunized,
immunize the child during the visit.
•	 Remember that a cold is not a contraindication to vaccination
Possible complications of upper respiratory infections include the following:
•	 Otitis media
•	 Sinusitis (thick, purulent discharge for longer than 2 weeks)
•	 Pneumonia
•	 Asthma and wheezing
4.	 Follow up
•	 Advise to be reviewed if symptoms worsen or become severe:
