Poisoning and overdose – Adult/Child
Recommend
•	 Manage all suspected poisoning or overdose urgently.
•	 Contact the Medical Officer (MO) early.
Background
•	 Poisoning and overdose can result from accidental or deliberate ingestion,
inhalation, injection, or dermal exposure to a wide range of substances. Early
recognition and appropriate supportive care are essential. Avoid gastrointestinal
decontamination (e.g. activated charcoal) until toxicology assessment is
completed.
1.
May present with¹-⁴
•	 Confusion, drowsiness, loss of consciousness, fitting
•	 Respiratory failure
•	 Hyperthermia or hypothermia
•	 Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea
•	 Hypotension, hypertension, bradycardia, tachycardia, arrhythmias
•	 Conscious and fully orientated
•	 History suggestive of deliberate or accidental poisoning or drug taking
2.	 Immediate management
•	 DRSABCD. Resuscitate if required. See BLS
•	 Contact MA urgently
•	 Insert IVC × 2 if required
•	 If contamination suspected:

–
Remove all clothes

–
Shower with soap and water

–
Use PPE for staff safety
•	 If patient is confused, withdrawn, aggressive, or acutely disturbed, prioritise
safety. See Mental health emergency
•	 Do not undertake gastrointestinal decontamination until full toxicology
assessment has been completed
3.	 Clinical assessment¹-⁴
•	 Perform a full Toxicology assessment – see next table
•	 Monitor and record vital signs:

–
BP, HR, RR, T

–
SpO₂

–
BGL

–
GCS or AVPU scale,

–
Note: hyperthermia can be life-threatening
