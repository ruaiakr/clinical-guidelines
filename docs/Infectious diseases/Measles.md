Measles
Background
Measles is a highly contagious viral disease characterized by a high fever,
cough, conjunctivitis, and a characteristic red blotchy rash. It can lead
to severe complications, especially in young children and those who are
immunocompromised.
Recommendations
•	 Vaccination with the measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine is the best
prevention strategy.
•	 Early identification and isolation of measles cases are important to prevent
outbreaks.
•	 Supportive care is critical, especially for high-risk individuals (e.g., malnourished
children, immunocompromised patients).
1.
May present with
•	 High fever (often > 38.3°C)
•	 Cough, runny nose, and conjunctivitis
•	 Koplik spots (small white spots inside the mouth)
•	 Rash that begins on the face and spreads downward
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Isolate the patient to prevent transmission.
•	 Provide antipyretics for fever and pain relief.
•	 Hydration and nutritional support, especially if the patient is young or
malnourished.

3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Assess the presence of Koplik spots and confirm the diagnosis.
•	 Evaluate for complications such as pneumonia or encephalitis.
•	 Check immunization history and ensure the patient is isolated from non-immune
individuals.
4.	 Management
•	 Administer supportive care, including hydration, fever management, and rest.
•	 Monitor for secondary infections, especially pneumonia.
•	 In high-risk groups, consider vitamin A supplementation to reduce the risk of
complications.
5.	 Follow-up
•	 Monitor for secondary infections (e.g., pneumonia, otitis media) and other
complications.
•	 Follow-up visits within 2-3 weeks to ensure recovery and vaccination status.
6.	 Referral/Consultation
•	 Refer to a pediatrician or infectious disease specialist if complications develop.
•	 Consider referral for vitamin A supplementation if necessary.
