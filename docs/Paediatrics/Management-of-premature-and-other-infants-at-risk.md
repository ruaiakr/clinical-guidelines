Duration of antibiotics
•	 7–10 days in most cases
•	 Stop after 5 days if symptoms resolve within 24 hrs
•	 Adjust duration if blood culture results are known (de-escalate if possible)
•	 Refer to meningitis section for extended treatment durations
5.	 Follow up
•	 Continue to monitor while awaiting transfer
•	 Ensure hydration and temperature control
•	 Reassess response to antibiotics daily
•	 Update retrieval team or receiving hospital on clinical status
6.	 Referral / Consultation
•	 Refer immediately to TCH if neonatal sepsis is suspected
•	 Begin antibiotics and fluids before transfer
•	 Continue stabilisation while waiting for retrieval or transport

Management of premature and other infants
at risk (Kiribati Original Protocol)
(Because of Size and Age or High-Risk Pregnancy)
•	 Consult a doctor at the central hospital regarding transfer (and care pending
transfer) of all twins, premature, and low-birth weight infants. Also consult about
any infant who is having problems.
•	 The following summary provides information about problems certain high-risk
infants may have.
Infants at Risk Because of Size and Age
•	 A gestational age examination allows the health worker to assess neurologic and
physical maturity in order to classify the infant according to age, sex, and size.
Infants of Mothers with Multiple Pregnancy
•	 Higher risk of premature labour, abruptio placentae and intrapartum
haemorrhage.
•	 One or more infants may be compromised in fetal life.
•	 Infant risks include intrauterine growth retardation (IUGR), twin-to-twin
transfusion, and other complications.
Infants of Rh-Sensitised Mothers
•	 These mothers are Rh-negative with a prior Rh-positive infant and no Rhogam
administration.
•	 Infants need prompt oxygen and may require intubation.
•	 Risks include congestive heart failure, acidosis, hypoglycaemia, rapidly rising
bilirubin; often critical condition.
Infants of Diabetic Mothers
•	 Risk of birth trauma, intracranial bleeding, and/or asphyxia.
•	 Hypoglycaemia within first 6 hours or later.
•	 Higher risk of respiratory distress syndrome, congenital anomalies,
hyperbilirubinaemia, and hypocalcaemia.
Infants of Mothers with Polyhydramnios
•	 Often have fetal anomalies, including GI tract and CNS defects.
•	 Other complications: premature rupture of membranes, cord prolapse, premature
labour, and small for gestational age.
Infants of Mothers with Hypertension of Pregnancy
•	 Common complications include prematurity and IUGR.
•	 Increased risk of abruptio placentae.
•	 High magnesium levels can cause hypotonia.
•	 Infants may need resuscitation and close monitoring for respiratory distress,
hypoglycaemia, or CNS issues.

Infants of Mothers with Placental Bleeding in the Third Trimester
•	 Premature delivery is likely.
•	 Immediate treatment for shock/asphyxia may be needed.
•	 Resuscitation and blood volume restoration may be required.
Preterm Infants
•	 Born before 37 weeks' gestation.
•	 Higher risk of illness and death; the earlier the birth, the greater the risk.
•	 Common problems: respiratory and cardiac issues, hypothermia, hypoglycaemia,
hyperbilirubinaemia, infections, GI or brain bleeding.
Postterm Infants
•	 Risk of in utero death, asphyxia, meconium aspiration, CNS damage,
hypoglycaemia, and lung bleeding.
Large for Gestational Age Infants (LGA)
•	 Above the 90th percentile in size.
•	 May have congenital heart disease, bone fractures, cephalhaematoma, nerve
injuries, subdural haematomas.
•	 Prone to hypoglycaemia.
Small for Gestational Age Infants (SGA)
•	 Below 10th percentile in size.
•	 Causes: malnutrition, maternal infection, diabetes, hypertension, smoking.
•	 Often thin, wasted, with little subcutaneous tissue.
•	 Common issues: meconium aspiration, asphyxia, hypoglycaemia, congenital
anomalies.
Summary of Problems of Preterm or Low Birthweight Babies
•	 Irregular respirations, apnoea, and asphyxia
•	 Poor temperature control
•	 Difficulty with swallowing and sucking
•	 Higher infection risk
•	 Fragile capillaries leading to haemorrhage
•	 Common jaundice
•	 Anaemia and rickets due to low iron/vitamin D
•	 Kidney immaturity causing salt retention and oedema
•	 Slower weight gain: immature babies double weight in 1–3 months
Rules of Care for Preterm or Low Birthweight Babies
•	 Maintain utmost cleanliness
•	 Handle minimally
•	 Keep cot/crib warm and wrap in calico
•	 Place between mother’s breasts for warmth
•	 Feed carefully to avoid aspiration
•	 Keep room sunny to prevent jaundice
•	 Administer iron and multivitamin drops if transfer delayed

After Delivery
•	 Gently clear airway with mucus extractor
•	 Provide oxygen as needed (limit to 1 L/min)
•	 Delay cord clamping to allow placental blood transfer
•	 Wrap baby in warmed blanket; elevate head
•	 Use incubator if available (target temp: 33°C)
•	 Administer Vitamin K 1 mg IM
•	 If pethidine given to mother within 4 hrs, give 0.02 mg (1 mL) neonatal naloxone
IM
Conservation of Energy
•	 Cluster care every 3 hours (temp, feeds, nappy change, position)
•	 Treat cyanosis promptly
•	 Keep baby flat during feed; elevate head 30° after feeding for 15 mins
•	 Do not bathe
•	 Gently clean vernix with liquid paraffin
Feeding
•	 Start within 3 hours of birth to prevent hypoglycaemia
•	 Use NGT for expressed breast milk if unable to suck
•	 Feed every 4 hours (day and night)
•	 Aspirate stomach before feed and check residual volume
•	 Encourage breastfeeding as soon as baby can suck
