<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Complications of liver cirrhosis – Adult</h1>

<p>Cirrhosis of the liver can lead to a range of serious and potentially life-threatening complications. The four protocols below addresses common complications of liver cirrhosis encountered in primary care: <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Ascites">ascites</a>, <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Variceal-hemorrhage">variceal haemorrhage</a>, <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Spontaneous-Bacterial-Peritonitis">spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)</a>, and <a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Hepatic-encephalopathy">hepatic encephalopathy</a>. Early recognition, basic supportive care, and timely referral are critical.</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Liver cirrhosis is a chronic condition caused by long-term damage to the liver, commonly due to hepatitis B or alcohol use. As cirrhosis progresses, it leads to portal hypertension and impaired liver function, which in turn result in complications such as:</p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li><a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Ascites">Ascites</a> – accumulation of fluid in the abdomen</li>
<li><a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Variceal-hemorrhage">Variceal haemorrhage</a> – bleeding from dilated veins in the oesophagus or stomach</li>
<li><a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Spontaneous-Bacterial-Peritonitis">Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (SBP)</a> – infection of ascitic fluid</li>
<li><a href="/docs/Gastrointestinal/Hepatic-encephalopathy">Hepatic encephalopathy</a> – brain dysfunction caused by toxins normally cleared by the liver</li>
</ul>
