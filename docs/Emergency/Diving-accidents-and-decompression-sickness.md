–
do NOT pull/jerk back on the driver when releasing the trigger
•	 Hold the hub + pull the driver straight off needle set. Continue to hold hub in

•	 See instructions + video at www.teleflex.com/anz/en/product-areas/emergency-

Manual insertion
•	 Hold the needle set with the catheter hub and stylet as 1 piece. Ensure stylet +
hub stay screwed together
•	 Rotate clockwise/anticlockwise while applying gentle, moderate steady
downward pressure without rocking the needle set. Allow rotation + pressure to
penetrate the bone cortex, not excessive force
•	 Child - stop when change of pressure or resistance felt as a ‘pop’ or ‘give’
indicating entry to medullary space
•	 Adult - advance needle set approx. 1–2 cm after entry into medullary space,
which is felt as a change in resistance; in the proximal humerus for most adults
the needle set should be advanced 2cm or until hub is flush or against the skin

Diving accidents and decompression sickness
Recommend
•	 Emphasise diver safety.
•	 Advise all divers to attend certified safe diving training classes.
•	 Visitors should only dive if qualified.
•	 There is no hyperbaric chamber in the islands.
Background
Diving-related problems are increasingly common due to air travel and deeper
dives. As depth increases, pressure rises from air and water, leading to increased risk
of barotrauma and gas-related injury. Most underwater deaths are related to diver
inexperience, carelessness, or equipment failure. Key hazards include air embolism,
decompression sickness, and sinus-related injuries due to blocked passages in the
nose, ears, and sinuses.
1.
May present with
Air Embolism
•	 Symptoms appear within 2 minutes of ascent
•	 Personality changes
•	 Seizures
•	 Loss of consciousness
•	 Neurological deficits (motor or sensory)
•	 Pneumothorax
•	 Shock
Decompression Sickness
•	 Joint pain (“the bends”) – deep aching or throbbing
•	 Skin bubbles, mottling, rashes, itching, crawling sensation
•	 Neurological symptoms – mood change, altered consciousness, seizures
•	 Lung symptoms – chest pain, cough, dyspnoea, cyanosis, shock
Ear, Nose and Sinus Problems
•	 Severe ear or sinus pain
•	 Hearing difficulty
•	 Nosebleed or blood behind tympanic membrane
•	 Vertigo

2.	 Immediate management
Air Embolism
•	 Place patient on left side with head down (to keep bubbles in right side of heart)
•	 Protect cervical spine if injury suspected (use collar or stabilisation)
•	 Maintain airway
•	 Give 100% oxygen at 7–8 L/min (continue until exhausted; give air breaks only for
assessment or hydration)
•	 Insert IV line and run normal saline rapidly if hypotensive
•	 Give IV Valium 5–10 mg slowly (diluted to 10 mL with sterile water or saline)
•	 If not breathing, check central pulse — if absent or doubtful, begin BLS
•	 Apply direct pressure to stop any bleeding (do not use tourniquet)
•	 Transfer to hyperbaric chamber as soon as possible (delayed therapy can still
help)
•	 Use aircraft pressurised or fly at sea-level altitude
Decompression Sickness
•	 Manage as for air embolism:
•	 Left side, head down
•	 100% oxygen at 8–10 L/min
•	 IV fluids rapidly if BP low
•	 Valium IV slowly for seizures
•	 Transfer to hyperbaric chamber as soon as possible
Ear, Nose and Sinus Problems
•	 Beconase AQ nasal spray: 2 sprays per nostril, twice a day for 3–5 days
•	 Promethazine (Phenergan) 25 mg orally twice daily for 3–5 days
•	 If patient has had similar problems in the past:

–
Begin above therapy 1 week before diving and continue through diving period
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Full head-to-toe check for signs of gas embolism, sinus trauma, or hypoxia
•	 Neurological examination (motor, sensory, behaviour, speech)
•	 Chest and lung assessment
•	 Ear examination (including tympanic membrane)
•	 BP, HR, RR, oxygen saturation

5.	 Follow up
•	 Observe for delayed symptoms (joint pain, fatigue, confusion)
•	 Monitor vital signs closely
•	 Advise against further diving until cleared by a doctor
•	 Document dive history, including duration, depth, ascent pattern, and previous
symptoms
6.	 Referral / consultation
•	 Consult doctor all cases of:

–
Air embolism

–
Decompression sickness
•	 Also consult for:

–
Vertigo

–
Hearing loss

–
Blood in middle ear
Prevention and patient education
•	 Educate divers about:

–
Safe diving practices

–
Importance of certification

–
Lack of hyperbaric chamber in Kiribati
