<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Scabies – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Treat scabies promptly to prevent secondary infections, which can lead to serious complications such as <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-post-streptococcal-glomerulonephritis">acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (APSGN)</a> and <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Acute-rheumatic-fever">acute rheumatic fever (ARF)</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Scabies is caused by infestation with the Sarcoptes scabiei mite and spreads through close physical contact. It causes intense itching and skin lesions. Crusted (Norwegian) scabies is a highly contagious form with a large mite burden. Dog scabies does not affect humans.</li>
<li>For further details and photos, refer to the National Healthy Skin Guideline at: <a href="https://infectiousdiseases.telethonkids.org.au/resources/skin-guidelines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://infectiousdiseases.telethonkids.org.au/resources/skin-guidelines/</a></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="scabies">Scabies</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Small bumps/papules, blisters ± visible burrows</li>
<li>Very itchy, worse at night</li>
<li>Common sites: finger/toe webbing, wrists, elbows, armpits, waistline, abdomen, breasts, buttocks, thighs, genitals</li>
<li>Infants: pustules on palms and soles; may be widespread and include head/neck</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="crusted-norwegian-scabies">Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Scaling and crusting, may not be itchy</li>
<li>Creamy colour crusts, may affect large areas</li>
<li>Common sites: buttocks, elbows, arms, palms, soles</li>
<li>May resemble tinea, psoriasis, eczema, or dermatitis</li>
<li>Highly contagious</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Take history, including immunocompromised status</li>
<li>Check vital signs</li>
<li>Examine skin – see Skin assessment – adult, or child</li>
<li>Look for signs of impetigo (infected scabies):
<ul>
<li>Honey-coloured crusts, pus-filled lesions, redness or weeping</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If crusted scabies suspected:
<ul>
<li>Take skin scraping for microscopy</li>
<li>Ask about household contacts and living conditions</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="additional-management">Additional management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If infected scabies, treat at same time – see <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Impetigo-Child-Adult">Impetigo</a></li>
<li>MO may order topical corticosteroid cream if itch persists after treatment</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="scabies-advice">Scabies Advice</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Apply cream in clinic if needed</li>
<li>After shower, apply to whole body (except face), including:
<ul>
<li>Between fingers/toes, soles of feet, under nails (use nail brush), genitalia, behind ears</li>
<li>All creases (e.g. elbows, knees)</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Leave on overnight. Reapply if washed off.</li>
<li>Infants &lt;6 months: cover hands after application; leave on for 8 hours</li>
<li>Repeat treatment in 7 days</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="household-advice">Household advice</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Treat all close contacts at same time – even if no symptoms</li>
<li>Wash clothes/bedding the next morning; dry in the sun</li>
<li>Keep children home from school/childcare until day after first treatment</li>
<li>Itching can persist 2–4 weeks after treatment</li>
<li>If persistent or worsening, consult MO</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="crusted-scabies-management">Crusted (Norwegian) Scabies</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO – may require hospitalisation and infectious disease specialist input</li>
<li>Treat with:
<ul>
<li>Ivermectin (oral)</li>
<li>PLUS Benzyl benzoate or Lyclear®</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Wash crusts the next day with sponge before reapplying treatment</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="household-environmental-measures">Household/environmental measures</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Wash clothes, sheets, and towels in hot water daily or seal in plastic for 8 days</li>
<li>Vacuum furniture, floors, and cars</li>
<li>Treat all contacts and monitor for reinfestation</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="follow-up-scabies">Scabies</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If itch lasts &gt; 4 weeks after treatment:</li>
<li>Confirm correct application of Lyclear®</li>
<li>Confirm contacts were treated and household cleaned</li>
<li>Consider trying benzyl benzoate or consult MO for ivermectin or other diagnosis</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="follow-up-crusted-scabies">Crusted scabies</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If managed in community, support daily treatment until cured</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral / consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Contact Skin Doctor via Messenger or TCH Nawerewere switchboard</li>
<li>Refer all complicated or crusted scabies</li>
</ul>
