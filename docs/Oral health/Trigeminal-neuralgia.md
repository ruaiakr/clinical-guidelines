Background
Trigeminal neuralgia is a chronic pain condition involving the trigeminal nerve (5th
cranial nerve).
ΓÇó	 It causes sudden, severe, sharp, or electric shock-like pain on one side of the face.
ΓÇó	 Often triggered by light touch, chewing, talking, wind or brushing teeth.
ΓÇó	 May be idiopathic or due to compression, multiple sclerosis (MS), post-herpetic
neuralgia, or rarely a tumour.
ΓÇó	 In resource-limited settings, clinical diagnosis and first-line treatment should be
initiated before considering imaging.
