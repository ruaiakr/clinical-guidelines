<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Dry perforation - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Hole in eardrum with no discharge</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Perforated eardrum (hole) without any discharge</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history and examine ears as per <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Ear-assessment">Ear assessment</a>, including:
<ul>
<li>duration of perforation (if known)</li>
<li>recent trauma to ear/head eg slap</li>
<li>prior ear infections, when, treatment, is child under ENT specialist</li>
<li>concerns about hearing, speech and learning/behavioural problems, development</li>
<li>hearing test(s) - when, results</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Examine ears:
<ul>
<li>document size and position of perforation eg draw picture in notes</li>
<li>do valsalva, tympanometry or pneumatic otoscopy to confirm eardrum not moving to exclude healed perforation.</li>
<li>note any crusting/scab or granulation on the eardrum/near hole</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If related to trauma, see <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Traumatic-rupture-of-eardrum">Traumatic rupture of eardrum</a>.</li>
<li>Advise Ear Hygiene</li>
<li>Consult ENT team if hole could indicate Cholesteatoma, eg:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>is in Attic area, (upper area) of eardrum OR</li>
<li>borders rim of the eardrum OR</li>
<li>crusting/scab or granulation on the eardrum/near the hole</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Otherwise, advise parent/carer:
<ul>
<li>if a child, is at risk of developing chronic discharging ears</li>
<li>will often heal by itself in time, but need to monitor</li>
<li>water precautions (when swimming/bathing).<sup>1</sup> Use cotton wool in ear with Vaseline® over top, or earplugs with swimming cap</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Advise to return if ear starts discharging</li>
<li>Otherwise, review monthly until healed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If perforation persists for &gt; 3 months, refer for hearing test/audiology review + ENT review</li>
<li>If language, learning or behavioural problems, refer to speech pathology</li>
</ul>
