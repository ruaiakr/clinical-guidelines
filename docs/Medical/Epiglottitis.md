# Epiglottitis - Adult/Child

Epiglottitis - Adult/Child
 Background1
•	 A life-threatening condition caused by infection of the epiglottis + surrounding
area
•	 Since the Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib) vaccine, epiglottitis is rare
1.
May present with
•	 High fever, looks sick
•	 Hyperextension of neck, may not move neck
•	 Drooling saliva, difficulty swallowing
•	 Stridor, muffled voice
•	 Restless, unsettled
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Secure airway

–
Anticipate any airway obstruction but avoid distressing a child as may
exacerbate symptoms:

–
Avoid examining the oral cavity and throat

–
allow the child to settle in the position most comfortable.
•	 The patient should be kept in an upright position as supine positioning can
aggravate airway obstruction
•	 Start urgent IV antibiotic chloramphenicol 25mg/kg up 1g IV 6hrly

–
Oral antibiotic if can’t assess to iv: amoxicillin clavulanate 500+125 mg (child
15 mg/kg amoxicillin component up to 500 mg) orally, 8-hourly.
•	 Corticosteroid:

–
Dexamethasone 10 mg IV, as a single dose; repeat at 24 hours if required (not
recommended for children)
•	 IV Fluid to maintain hydration, especially if the patient is unable to swallow.
3.	 Clinical assessment1
•	 Assess for any upper airway obstruction (stridor, muffle voice, difficulty
breathing)
•	 Also looking for drooling, and dysphagia.
•	 Sore throat which is progressive rapidly.
•	 High grade fever, sick looking.
