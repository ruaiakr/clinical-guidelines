<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acute sinusitis - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Also commonly known as acute rhinosinusitis is a symptomatic inflammation of the mucosal lining of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>with purulent nasal drainage</li>
<li>accompanied with nasal obstruction, facial pain/pressure/fullness.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Suspect <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Foreign-body-in-ear-or-nose">Foreign body</a> in child with:
<ul>
<li>any nasal occlusion, facial swelling ± pain, or smelly, purulent or blood-stained nasal discharge</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Consult MA urgently if there are any signs of spreading infection:
<ul>
<li>acute onset confusion</li>
<li>Double or ↓vision</li>
<li>neck stiffness, severe headache, photophobia</li>
<li>swelling or cellulitis around eyes, forehead or face</li>
<li>bulging eyes, painful eye movements</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history, including:
<ul>
<li>onset, duration, severity of symptoms</li>
<li>facial pain/tenderness</li>
<li>fever, cough, nasal discharge - colour</li>
<li>immunocompromised</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:
<ul>
<li>any facial swelling + tenderness with gentle palpation</li>
<li>ENT</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Suspected Acute viral Sinusitis.
<ul>
<li>Adequate rest and hydration and warm facial packs may be useful.</li>
<li>paracetamol or ibuprofen if needed</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Suspected Acute Bacterial Sinusitis.
<ul>
<li>it is usually self-limiting, and antibiotics are not usually indicated</li>
<li>consider symptomatic treatment as per <a href="/docs/Common%20medical%20conditions/Upper-respiratory-tract-infection">URTI</a>.</li>
<li>Paracetamol or ibuprofen.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>May consider antibiotics, if:
<ul>
<li>severe symptoms persisting &gt; 3–4 days, or worsening after initial improvement, or persist for &gt; 7–10 days without improvement</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Amoxicillin/clavulanate:
<ul>
<li>children: 90 mg/kg/ day orally given in 2 divided doses; adults: 2000 mg orally (extended-release) twice daily Dose refers to amoxicillin component.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
