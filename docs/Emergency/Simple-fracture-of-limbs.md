# Simple fracture of limbs - Adult/Child

Simple fracture of limbs - Adult/Child
Definition
•	 Close fracture
•	 Deformed or fracture limb with no open wound
Signs and symptoms
•	 History of trauma or injury

–
Important to get a mechanism of injury

Important clinical assessment need to document in

•	  Examine the blood supply distal to the possible fracture.

–
Is the extremity warm or cold?

–
Is the extremity pale?

–
Is there a puise distal to the fracture'?

–
Check capillary filling (press the nails)-docs the pink colour return within 1-2
seconds aller releasing pressure?)
•	 Examine the nerve supply to the distal limb

–
Is there any feeling or sensation in the distal limb or hand (use a pin prick or
cotton wool)?

–
Test both sides of the extremity for feeling.

–
Have the patient perform specific movements.
•	 Assessing nerve function if forearm fracture

–
Are all finger movements distal to the fracture normal?

–
Can all the fingers move normally as if the patient is playing a piano (can the
patient spread the fingers)?

–
Can the patient make a fist and flex and extend the hand at the wrist?

–
Can the patient hyperextend the thumb?

–
Can the patient move his thumb to touch his little finger'

2.	 Immediate management
•	 Do primary + secondary survey as per Traumatic injuries
•	 Assess against Criteria for early notification of trauma for interfacility transfer
(inside front cover)
•	 Immobilise/apply splint on a fractured limb in neutral position. Severely
deformed limb from injury with highly suspicious of fracture or dislocation
•	 NEED Manupulation under sedation (Adult):

–
IV midazolam 2 - 3mg / IM Morphine 10mg  (dose 0.05mg – 0.1mg / kg / dose)

–
Reduction : Traction and counter-traction manouvre to achieve a straight and
neutral position

–
Apply a splint or a back-slap to hold the limb in neutral position

–
Elevate

–
Consult surgeon/MA for urgent referral
•	 Regular pain relief – Panadol and NSAIDs
•	 No need to give antibiotic

PLASTER BACKSLAB2,4
Positioning
of slab
•	 Discuss with MO + see Backslabs for guidance
What you
need
•	 Plaster bandages eg 7.5/10 cm - short arms/wrists, 15/20 cm -
long arm
•	 Undercast padding
•	 Bowl of cold water + trauma scissors
•	 Crepe bandages + sling
How to do
•	 Remove all rings, bracelets, watches, restrictive clothing, shoes2
•	 Apply 2-3 layers of padding + 50% overlap beyond margins of
plaster
•	 Measure a length of plaster 1 cm shorter than the padding/
stockinette at each end. Fold the roll in about ten layers to the
same length
•	 Submerge in water until bubbling stops, remove + squeeze out
excess
•	 Lightly mould to the contours of the limb in a neutral position
•	 Plaster must not fully encircle the limb
•	 Turn back padding over ends of plaster + apply crepe bandage
firmly over slab
•	 Elevate limb on pillows or sling for arm
•	 Advise patient it may take 4-6 weeks to heal + Plaster care ±
crutches if not evacuated
