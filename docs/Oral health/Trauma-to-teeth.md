<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Trauma to teeth - Adult/Child</h1>
<p class="pccm-page-subtitle">Knocked out tooth, displaced tooth, broken tooth</p>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Offer education on how to manage a knocked out ‘adult’ tooth. Prompt First aid may end up saving a tooth</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Knocked out, displaced, broken tooth/teeth</li>
<li>Bleeding in mouth</li>
<li>Injury/swelling to lips, tongue/face</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If a knocked out adult tooth:
<ul>
<li>replant within 15 minutes if possible – see how to do this under <a href="#management">Management</a>.<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If practical, find all missing teeth/tooth fragments</li>
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>circumstances of injury</li>
<li>current medications</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Inspect mouth, teeth, soft tissues/gums.</li>
<li>Assess for bleeding +
<ul>
<li>bite – suspect jaw or facial fracture if bite is abnormal. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Fractured-jaw">Fractured jaw</a>.</li>
<li>Check for other injuries, especially head and neck<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>If a tooth looks like it is missing, but not found at site of the accident, assess if:
<ul>
<li>patient has inhaled the tooth, OR</li>
<li>tooth has been forced up into the socket completely, appearing to be missing (covered in a clot)</li>
<li>if not sure, consult MO/dentist who may consider x-ray<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Control any bleeding with gentle pressure:
<ul>
<li>if bleeding continues, see Post tooth extraction bleeding.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Offer analgesia – ibuprofen preferred.<sup>2</sup> See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a>.
<ul>
<li>add paracetamol if needed</li>
<li>advise to take regularly, rather than as required, for continuous pain relief</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="knocked-out-tooth">Knocked out tooth<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Check if it is an adult or baby tooth:
<ul>
<li>baby teeth – smaller, lighter in colour</li>
<li>a child &gt; 5 years may have a mixture of adult and baby teeth, can be hard to tell</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Do NOT replant a knocked out baby tooth</strong> – can damage the developing adult tooth</li>
<li>Replant permanent (adult) tooth within <strong>15 minutes if possible</strong> – more likely to heal:
<ul>
<li>if done &gt; 1 hour, unlikely to succeed</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check tetanus vaccination status and give booster if indicated. See Tetanus immunisation.</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-note-panel" style="border-color:#ef6a4c; background:#fff7f0;">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="how-to-replant">How to replant a knocked out adult tooth<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hold tooth carefully by the top (crown), not the root</li>
<li>Check the tooth is intact – sometimes the root may have broken off:
<ul>
<li>if suspect it is not intact, consider urgent dental review</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If dirty, rinse tooth briefly with dairy milk or sodium chloride 0.9%:
<ul>
<li>do NOT use water. Do not rub or scrub</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Replace tooth in the socket with firm finger pressure:
<ul>
<li>be careful to ensure the tooth is placed the right way around. Take extra care if multiple teeth lost</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Use <a href="#temporary-splint">Temporary splint</a> to hold in place (see below)</li>
<li>Advise:
<ul>
<li>soft diet</li>
<li>use chlorhexidine gluconate 0.2% mouthwash. See <a href="/docs/Oral health/Gum-disease">Gum disease</a></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>ALL re-implanted teeth need urgent review and treatment by dentist:</strong>
<ul>
<li>arrange evacuation as needed</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="temporary-splint">Temporary splint<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fix the replanted tooth to the adjacent teeth:
<ul>
<li>use a small piece of folded aluminium foil or malleable material eg Blu Tack®, orthodontic wax</li>
<li>put firmly over replanted tooth and teeth either side, so adjacent teeth act as a splint. Ask the patient to bite down to hold in position</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Start antibiotics after replanting the tooth:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>doxycycline if not allergic and ≥ 8 years of age OR</li>
<li>amoxicillin if not allergic for child &lt; 8 years, or if doxycycline allergy in adult:
<ul>
<li>if allergic to amoxicillin, consult MO/dentist</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="unable-to-replant">If unable to replant a knocked out tooth<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do not let the tooth dry out</li>
<li>Store in dairy milk – cool or room temperature. Tooth ligament cells can survive in milk up to 6 hours</li>
<li>If milk not available, store in sodium chloride 0.9% or saliva + plastic wrap – get patient to spit saliva into wrap first. Ligament cells can survive up to 1 hour if stored this way</li>
<li>Do NOT store in water</li>
<li>Consult dentist urgently</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="displaced-tooth">Displaced tooth<sup>1</sup> – tooth is still in the socket, but moved position</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do not reposition a baby tooth</li>
<li>Reposition adult tooth to original position with firm finger pressure</li>
<li>Put Temporary splint on tooth</li>
<li>Consult dentist urgently – arrange evacuation as needed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="broken-tooth">Broken tooth/teeth<sup>3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Keep tooth fragments, as dentist may be able to re-bond onto the broken tooth</li>
<li>If no pain from broken tooth, treatment is not urgent – refer to the next dental clinic</li>
<li>If pain, dentine or pulp may have been exposed</li>
<li>Exposure of dentine:
<ul>
<li>intermittent pain when exposed to stimulus – hot, cold or sweet food or drinks</li>
<li>cover any obvious cavity with orthodontic wax or other inert material eg Blu Tack® or temporary filling eg Cavit®<sup>2</sup> – note: will not last very long</li>
<li>analgesia not normally needed</li>
<li>advise to see dentist as soon as possible</li>
<li>avoid stimulus eg hot/cold drinks</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Exposure of pulp:
<ul>
<li>red soft tissue is seen in the area of break or cavity</li>
<li>severe pain persisting as a dull throbbing ache – even after removal of the stimulus</li>
<li>very sensitive to touch, may bleed or have a blood clot over it</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>needs urgent dental treatment:
<ul>
<li>within 24 hours to avoid infection/more damage</li>
<li>arrange evacuation as needed</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if delay to treatment expected, consult dentist who may advise:
<ul>
<li>orthodontic wax or other inert material eg Blu Tack®, to cover the broken tooth to reduce pain</li>
<li>OR temporary filling eg Cavit®</li>
<li>note: these measures may still not be successful in reducing pain adequately. Consult dentist</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="doxycycline">DOXYCYCLINE</h2>
<div class="pccm-drug-box">
<div class="pccm-drug-header">
<span class="pccm-drug-name">DOXYCYCLINE</span>
<span class="pccm-drug-level">L3</span>
</div>
<div class="pccm-assessment-table-wrap" style="margin:0; overflow-x:auto;">
<table class="pccm-drug-table">
<tr><th>FORM</th><th>STRENGTH</th><th>ROUTE</th><th>DOSE</th><th>DURATION</th></tr>
<tr>
<td>Tablet</td>
<td>50 mg<br />100 mg</td>
<td>Oral</td>
<td><strong>Adult</strong><br />100 mg daily<br /><strong>Child ≥ 8 years</strong><br />&lt; 26 kg: 50 mg<br />26–35 kg: 75 mg<br />&gt; 35 kg: 100 mg</td>
<td><strong>7 days</strong></td>
</tr>
</table>
</div>
<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> Take with food or milk to reduce stomach upset. May cause nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, epigastric burning, tooth discolouration or photosensitivity. Take with a large glass of water. Do not lie down for an hour after taking. Do not take iron, calcium, zinc or antacids within 2 hours. Avoid sun exposure</p>
<p><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> Safe in the First 18 weeks</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Serious allergy to tetracyclines. Taking oral retinoids. After 18 weeks of pregnancy</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO/Dentist. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a>.</p>
</div>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As per dentist’s advice. Refer for next dentist clinic visit</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO/dentist as above. Consider telehealth consult with dentist</li>
</ul>
