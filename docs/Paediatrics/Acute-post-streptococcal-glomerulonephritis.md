<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Acute post streptococcal glomerulonephritis (APSGN) - Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Aim to prevent APSGN by promoting:
<ul>
<li>early treatment of <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Impetigo-Child-Adult">Impetigo</a>, (skin sores) and <a href="/docs/Ear,%20Nose%20and%20Throat/Sore-throat">Sore throat</a></li>
<li>community control of <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Scabies">scabies</a> and skin sores</li>
<li>regular washing + hand hygiene, especially children</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>APSGN is an inflammatory kidney disease which occurs 2–3 weeks after a skin or throat infection with Group A Streptococcus (GAS), or occasionally groups C or G</li>
<li>Most common 2–17 years of age, but can occur at any age</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Microscopic haematuria detected on urinalysis</li>
<li>Acute nephritis - typical presentation:
<ul>
<li>puffy face (facial oedema)</li>
<li>≥ moderate blood in urine on dipstick - urine can look smoky, tea/cola coloured</li>
<li>hypertension</li>
<li>peripheral oedema eg in legs/hands</li>
<li>proteinuria</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Skin sores/infected scabies OR recent history of skin sores or sore throat</li>
<li>In severe cases of nephritis:
<ul>
<li>respiratory distress due to pulmonary oedema</li>
<li>lethargy, general weakness, anorexia</li>
<li>uncommon - severe headache, convulsions, coma</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If severe symptoms, urgently consult MO</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get history + ask about:
<ul>
<li>puffiness of face or eyes, legs or arms</li>
<li>urine colour (red-brown)</li>
<li>urine output - has it decreased</li>
<li>any other symptoms eg SOB, feeling unwell, off food</li>
<li>do any close contacts have similar symptoms</li>
<li>recent history of skin sores/infected scabies or sore throat (in prior 2–3 weeks)</li>
<li>previous medical history, including APSGN or close contacts with APSGN</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do physical examination, including:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>vital signs - use the table <a href="#bp-requiring-further-evaluation">BP requiring further evaluation in children</a> to determine if child is hypertensive. Do not rely on CEWT BP ranges</li>
<li>urinalysis - check for blood and protein</li>
<li>weight - bare weight if &lt; 2 years. Assess against recent weights</li>
<li>skin for sores/infected scabies</li>
<li>face, hands and feet for oedema/puffiness</li>
<li>throat - any redness</li>
<li>listen to chest for crackles or wheeze - may indicate pulmonary oedema</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">BP in children</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Measure on right arm for consistency, with appropriately sized cuff</li>
<li>Can vary considerably during the same visit or across visits. If initial BP ↑, do 2 more BP at same visit and average them</li>
<li>Check against the BP requiring further evaluation in children table:
<ul>
<li>if BP is ≥ the values, also measure height</li>
<li>check BP against table 4 (boys) or table 5 (girls)</li>
<li>a BP &gt; 90th percentile is elevated</li>
<li>if unsure, consult MO</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management<sup>1,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If microscopic haematuria found on urinalysis but NO other symptoms:
<ul>
<li>if prior history of APSGN - haematuria can persist for up to 3–6 months post resolution<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>if there is no history of APSGN in last 6 months - advise to see MO at next clinic</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Consult MO promptly if:
<ul>
<li>any child with oedema or hypertension:
<ul>
<li>BP &gt; 90th percentile is elevated and requires further investigation</li>
<li>BP &gt; 95th percentile requires aggressive treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if hypertension ± heart failure, MO may order furosemide (frusemide) 1-2mg/kg 1-2 times/day; can repeat the dose in 6 hours if the child has not urinated, bed rest and salt restriction in diet</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If ‘clinical evidence’ suggests probable APSGN promptly consult MO:<sup>1</sup>
<ul>
<li>evacuation/hospitalisation required</li>
<li>give benzathine benzylpenicillin (Bicillin LA®):
<ul>
<li>if allergic to penicillin give oral trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole<sup>4</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>treat <a href="/docs/Skin%20problems/Scabies">Scabies</a>, if present</li>
<li>ask for close contacts from previous 2 weeks + adults and children staying in house</li>
<li>notify Public Health Unit for further advice on examining + management of contacts</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Follow up close contacts in collaboration with Public Health Unit</li>
<li>Children with a history of APSGN should be monitored through an individualised care plan developed in conjunction with a paediatrician. Check care plan is in place and follow up anything outstanding</li>
<li>Resolution of APSGN:
<ul>
<li>usually resolves quite rapidly assuming concurrent resolution of infection</li>
<li>haematuria can persist for up to 3–6 months</li>
<li>proteinuria may persist longer - mild increase sometimes up to 3 years or more</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult MO for all suspected cases</li>
</ul>
