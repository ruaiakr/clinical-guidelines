# Acutely swollen/painful joint - Child

Acutely swollen/painful joint - Child
Recommend1,2
•	 The causes can be difficult to diagnose. Be suspicious of septic arthritis
(orthopaedic emergency), acute oste0myelitis and ARF
Background
•	 Also see Limp - emergency management in children
www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/qpec-statewide-guidelines/
1.
May present with1,4-6
•	 Hot, swollen, tender joint(s) ± fever, malaise and fatigue
•	 Painful hip
•	 Joint pain on movement
•	 ↓ mobility, limp, problems with weight bearing
2.	 Immediate management2
•	 Vital signs
•	 Screen for Sepsis
3.	 Clinical assessment2-6
•	 Assess  for Red flags - may require urgent further assessment ± evacuation
Red flags1
•	 Systemic symptoms eg T ≥ 38.5, malaise, weight loss, night sweats
•	 &lt; 4 years of age
•	 Inability to weight bear or severe, localised joint pain
•	 Inflammation of 1 joint2
•	 Bony pain
•	 Possible unwitnessed trauma/non-accidental injury
•	 Overweight adolescent
•	 Get history, including:

–
pain - acute or gradual onset

–
ARF/RHD diagnosis or family history of2

–
recent injury/wound - can be minor1,3

–
recent joint surgery

–
other symptoms eg skin infections, sore throat, diarrhoea, fatigue, rash3

–
IV drug use6
