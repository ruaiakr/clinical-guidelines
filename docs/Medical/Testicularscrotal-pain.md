Testicular/scrotal pain - Adult/Child
Testicular torsion
Recommend1
•	 Testicular torsion is an emergency. Surgery is required ≤ 6 hours to avoid
permanent harm, even if the pain has been present > 6 hours
1.
May present with1,2
•	 Pain in the testes/scrotum ± swelling/lump, nausea + vomiting
•	 Abdominal pain only
•	 Note: if recent testicular injury assume testicular torsion until proven otherwise
2.	 Immediate management1
•	 Rapidly assess against table below:

–
the presence or absence of a single sign cannot exclude testicular torsion +

–
keep in mind patient may be reluctant to volunteer symptoms due to
embarrassment and reluctance to be examined3
•	 Contact MO urgently in all cases of testicular/scrotal pain
•	 Offer analgesia ± antiemetic. See Acute pain, Nausea and vomiting
•	 Do vital signs

Testicular torsion
Epididymo-orchitis (EDO)
Examination
•	 Absent cremasteric reflex*
•	 Testis - abnormal position,
horizontal lie on standing and
high riding
•	 Scrotal skin changes - red or
darkening
•	 Tender to palpate globally
•	 Thickened spermatic cord
•	 Intact cremasteric reflex*
•	 Red, hot, swollen testis in
normal position
•	 Tender epididymis (tubular
structure found at back of
testes, running in a sagittal
plane)
Also check
•	 Urinalysis - leucocytes does
not exclude testicular torsion
•	 Lump in groin of child with
acute abdominal or inguinal
pain can be torsion of
undescended testis but may
be mistaken for swollen lymph
node or abscess
•	 Urinalysis - NAD does not
exclude EDO
•	 Risk factors eg recent
urethral surgery, history of
viral infection eg mumps;
sexually active4
*Cremasteric reflex - pinch or stroke the skin of the upper thigh. The testis on the
same side should elevate via contraction of the muscle
