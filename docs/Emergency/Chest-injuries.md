<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Chest injuries - Adult</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background1,3</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Life-threatening emergencies include tension pneumothorax, massive haemothorax, open pneumothorax, flail chest, pulmonary contusions, great vessel injury, diaphragm rupture</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Chest Trauma</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management 1-6</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do primary survey DRSCABCD as per Traumatic injuries, + initiate any lifesaving measures</li>
<li>Assess against Criteria for early notification of trauma for interfacility transfer (inside front cover)</li>
<li>Use tables on following pages for possible chest injuries and for immediate management BLUNT TRAUMA TO CHEST EG FALL FROM HEIGHT, MOTOR VEHICLE ACCIDENT<sup>1-4</sup> SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS CONSIDER</li>
<li>Increasing SOB, ↑ HR, ↓ BP</li>
<li>Agitation, hypoxia</li>
<li>↓ air entry + hyper- resonance on percussion on affected side</li>
<li>↓ chest movement</li>
<li>Trachea deviation (late sign) Tension pneumothorax</li>
<li>Immediate needle decompression (next page)</li>
<li>↓ air entry/dull percussion on affected side</li>
<li>Hypotension/shock</li>
<li>Hypoxia Haemothorax can be massive</li>
<li>Treat hypovolaemia - IV sodium chloride 0.9% or Hartmann's 1 L (20 mL/kg for child)</li>
<li>If respiratory distress develops, treat as tension pneumothorax</li>
<li>Paradoxical movement<ul><li>segment of chest wall moves in when the patient breathes in and out when patient breathes out Flail chest</li></ul></li>
<li>Ventilation support as needed</li>
<li>MO may intubate prior to evacuation</li>
<li>Coughing up blood, crackles</li>
<li>↑ HR, hypoxia, ↑ WOB Pulmonary contusion</li>
<li>O<sup>2</sup> + ventilation support</li>
<li>↓ air entry and ↑ percussion on affected side</li>
<li>± unequal chest movement Simple pneumothorax</li>
<li>Be alert to ↑ SOB - may be tension pneumothorax</li>
<li>Tender sternum May have underlying lung or cardiac injury</li>
<li>ECG</li>
<li>MO will advise further</li>
<li>Chest wall tenderness/ swelling</li>
<li>Pain worse on inspiration/ cough Broken rib(s)</li>
<li>MO may advise review next day if minor/no other injury/concerns</li>
<li>↓ BP, asymmetric, diminished or absent peripheral pulses</li>
<li>Paraplegia Great vessel injury<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Urgent MO advice PENETRATING TRAUMA TO CHEST EG GUNSHOT OR STAB WOUND<sup>1,4</sup>,5 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS CONSIDER</li>
<li>Wound to the chest</li>
<li>± object sticking out Possible haemothorax or pneumothorax</li>
<li>Do NOT remove any object sticking out of wound eg knife. VERY CAREFULLY pack around with gauze soaked in sodium chloride 0.9% and secure</li>
<li>Cover open chest wound with:<ul><li>vented chest seal OR</li><li>sterile occlusive dressing large enough to overlap the wound edges, taped securely on 3 sides (opening at bottom) to provide a flutter-type valve effect - air can move out but not in</li></ul></li>
<li>Chest wall opening</li>
<li>Air sucking in Open/ sucking chest wound/open pneumothorax Other life-threatening complications: cardiac tamponade (can be detected with eFAST), aortic/ tracheo-bronchial/oesophageal injury, diaphragm injury, blunt cardiac injury<sup>2,3</sup> Needle decompression for tension pneumothorax<sup>5,6</sup></li>
<li>Insert 14 G needle/cannula (use longer needle) at 2nd intercostal space, mid- clavicular line<ul><li>air should escape with a rush + respiratory distress should decrease</li></ul></li>
<li>If not working:<ul><li>insert needle at 5th intercostal space, mid axillary line</li><li>check IVC has not kinked or tension pneumothorax has recurred/another on the other side</li></ul></li>
<li>Note: will require definitive treatment ie chest drain to be inserted</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment<sup>6</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do secondary survey EFGHIJ as per Traumatic injuries, including examination of chest</li>
<li>Do eFAST if trained/available</li>
<li>Be aware of risk factors for potential deterioration:2<ul><li>past history - smoker, COPD, asthma, obesity</li><li>age &gt; 55 years, uncontrolled painrespiratory compromise:↑ WOB, ↑ RR, ↓SpO<sup>2</sup></li><li>≥ 3 fractured ribs</li><li>shallow breathing, unable to cough</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4.	 Management 1,2,6</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Give O<sup>2</sup> to maintain SpO<sup>2</sup> ≥ 94%</li>
<li>Ensure adequate analgesia.</li>
<li>See Acute pain.</li>
<li>Consult MO in all cases, who may advise:<ul><li>urgent evacuation</li><li>close monitoring of vital signs + WOB</li><li>chest x-ray</li><li>ECG</li><li>nil by mouth</li><li>bloods - FBC, group and hold</li></ul></li>
<li>Note: if critical, MO may advise finger thoracostomy (if skilled). Usually only done if patient</li>
<li>ventilated</li>
<li>MO may insert chest tube prior to evacuation. Attach to Heimlich Valve® or similar ambulatory chest drainage system for evacuation</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>MO will advise ongoing management</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Require Surgical Team admission</li>
</ul>
