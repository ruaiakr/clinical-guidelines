# Acute appendicitis

Acute appendicitis
Background
Acute appendicitis is the most common abdominal surgical emergency in the
world, with a lifetime risk of 8.6% in males and 6.9% in females. It is caused by
inflammation of the appendix and requires prompt recognition and referral.
The classical history involves colicky central abdominal pain that localises to the
right iliac fossa (RIF) within 6–12 hours and becomes constant. However, this pattern
is only present in approximately half of patients, and clinical judgement must be
used to support diagnosis.
Recommendations
All suspected cases of acute appendicitis should be admitted immediately.
Keep nil by mouth (NBM), start triple IV antibiotics (Gentamicin, Ampicillin and
Metronidazole), hydrate with IV fluids, provide analgesia, and refer urgently to the
main hospital for surgical management.

3.	 Clinical assessment
Abdominal examination findings of severe appendicitis:
•	 Direct tenderness: Pain on pressing in the RIF
•	 Guarding: Voluntary or involuntary tightening of abdominal muscles in the RIF
•	 Rigidity: Board-like abdomen due to reflex spasm of abdominal wall
•	 Rebound tenderness: Pain when pressing firmly for 30 seconds in the RIF and
releasing suddenly
•	 Rovsing’s sign: Rebound pain in the RIF when pressing and releasing in the LIF
•	 Psoas sign: Pain in the RIF when patient lifts flexed thigh against resistance
•	 Fever or tachycardia may also be present
