<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Substance use disorders – Adult</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Alcohol withdrawal delirium (delirium tremens) is a medical emergency</li>
<li>Benzodiazepines are indicated for delirium tremens not antiepileptics</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Onset occurs 2–5 days after stopping last alcoholic drink</li>
<li>Progression from mild to moderate to severe withdrawal can occur quickly without treatment</li>
<li>For alcohol cessation resources, see the Chronic conditions manual: <a href="https://www.health.qld.gov.au/rrcsu/clinical-manuals/chronic-conditions-manual-ccm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">www.health.qld.gov.au/rrcsu/clinical-manuals/chronic-conditions-manual-ccm</a></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="mild-moderate-withdrawal">Mild to moderate withdrawal<sup>1,3</sup></h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Tremor</li>
<li>↑ HR and BP</li>
<li>↑ T</li>
<li>Anxiety, agitation, restlessness</li>
<li>Insomnia</li>
<li>Nausea and vomiting</li>
<li>Sweating</li>
<li>Headache</li>
<li>Palpitations</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="severe-withdrawal">Severe withdrawal (progressing to delirium tremens)<sup>1,2</sup></h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Altered mental status e.g. confusion and disorientation or restlessness</li>
<li>Heightened response to stimuli</li>
<li>Severe hyperactivity, tremor and agitation</li>
<li>Hallucinations, paranoid and delusional thoughts<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Seizures</li>
<li>Sympathetic overdrive e.g. fluctuation in BP or HR, disturbance of fluid balance and electrolytes, raised temperature</li>
<li>Conditions. Observe outstretched hands<sup>1,2</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If confused or withdrawn, strange, aggressive or acutely disturbed behaviour, see <a href="/docs/Mental%20health/Acute-severe-behavioral-disturbance">Mental health emergency</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment<sup>1,2</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If in a hyperstimulated state, assess in a quiet room with low light, in the company of a familiar person, friend or relative if possible</li>
<li>If patient is frightened, reassure and avoid confrontation</li>
<li>Get history:
<ul>
<li>from family members if patient unable to give a history</li>
<li>medications, including non-prescription and illicit drugs</li>
<li>alcohol intake:
<ul>
<li>amount, type and duration</li>
<li>other alcoholic compounds i.e. methanol, ethylene glycol, methylated spirits</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do vital signs +
<ul>
<li>GCS</li>
<li>BGL + SpO<sub>2</sub> to exclude glycaemia or hypoxia as cause of behaviour</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Assess for thiamine deficiency (Wernicke encephalopathy):
<ul>
<li>confusion and global memory impairment</li>
<li>slurred speech, stumbling, falling</li>
<li>involuntary eye movements</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li><strong>Wernicke encephalopathy is considered an emergency</strong></li>
<li>Assess using the Alcohol Withdrawal Scale (AWS)</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Scoring Key</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>&lt; 5 = mild</li>
<li>5–14 = moderate</li>
<li>&gt;14 = severe</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>The immediate aim is to modify the environment and increase the safety of the patient over the next 3–4 days.</li>
<li>Discuss inpatient, outpatient or evacuation options with MO.</li>
<li>Initiate diazepam early in the course of alcohol withdrawal to prevent progression to more severe withdrawal without over-sedation.<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Give all patients prophylactic thiamine for Wernicke encephalopathy.<sup>1,2</sup></li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>ALERT:</strong> Give thiamine before glucose in any form. Glucose may deplete thiamine stores, causing Wernicke encephalopathy
</div>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="mild-withdrawal-aws">Mild withdrawal AWS &lt; 5:</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Supportive care</li>
<li>Maintain hydration, antiemetics, paracetamol for headache, loperamide for diarrhoea</li>
<li>Sedation with diazepam if necessary as per drug box</li>
<li>4 hourly observations</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="moderate-withdrawal-aws">Moderate withdrawal AWS 5–14:</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Maintain hydration</li>
<li>Sedation with diazepam as per drug box</li>
<li>1 hourly observations</li>
<li>Continue to monitor AWS prior to administering diazepam</li>
<li>If not settling after 80 mg diazepam, MO may consider olanzapine 5–10 mg</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="severe-withdrawal-aws">Severe withdrawal AWS &gt; 14 or delirium tremens requires hospitalisation:</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Insert IVC and rehydrate with IV sodium chloride 0.9%</li>
<li>Urgently contact MO and arrange evacuation</li>
<li>Diazepam every 2 hours as per drug box (specialist may order midazolam infusion)</li>
<li>If not settling after 80 mg diazepam, consult MO</li>
<li>Continue to monitor AWS prior to administering diazepam</li>
</ul>
<p>MO will order ongoing diazepam and thiamine regimen for above</p>
<p>Offer antiemetic. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Nausea-and-vomiting">Nausea and vomiting</a></p>
<p>Attend to any underlying chronic conditions or infection</p>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="outpatient-diazepam">If being treated as an outpatient, the MO may order:</h3>
<p><strong>Supply of daily diazepam regimen:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Day 1: diazepam 10 mg 6 hourly</li>
<li>Days 2–3: diazepam 5–10 mg 8 hourly</li>
<li>Day 4: diazepam 5 mg morning and night</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="other-outpatient-considerations">Other outpatient considerations:</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>A reliable adult to regularly monitor progress</li>
<li>A safe, alcohol-free environment</li>
<li>Withholding medicines if alcohol use is resumed</li>
<li>A clear plan in case of deterioration or emergency</li>
<li>Child protection for children of parents or carers who are withdrawing. See <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-protection">Child protection</a>.</li>
<li>Domestic and family violence and safe transport needs</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>On the first morning, assess the patient for:
<ul>
<li>early withdrawal symptoms. See <a href="#alcohol-withdrawal-scale">Alcohol withdrawal scale (AWS)</a></li>
<li>intoxication or alcohol consumption in the past 8 hours. If any, cease treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>MO/drug and alcohol RN should see the patient daily for the first 3–4 days. Then continue daily or second daily contact until withdrawal is completed</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/consultation<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Mental Health, Alcohol and Other Drugs for ongoing follow-up, counselling and case management</li>
<li>Consider child health services or Child and Youth Mental Health Services if concerned about welfare of children in care of patient. See <a href="/docs/Paediatrics/Child-protection">Child protection</a></li>
</ul>
</div>
