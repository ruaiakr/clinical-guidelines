# Dyspnoea – Adult

Dyspnoea – Adult
Recommend
Dyspnoea is a serious clinical symptom that may be caused by cardiac or
respiratory conditions. Early identification of the underlying cause is important
for appropriate management. Sudden onset dyspnoea, especially in children, may
indicate foreign body aspiration and requires urgent attention.
Background
Dyspnoea is defined as an uncomfortable awareness of breathing or difficulty in
breathing. It can result from disease of the lungs, heart, or nervous system. Common
causes include asthma, pneumonia, congestive heart failure, pulmonary embolism,
anaemia, or pleural effusion. It is essential to distinguish between cardiac and
respiratory origins of breathlessness for appropriate management.
1.
May present with
•	 Shortness of breath during activity or at rest
•	 Chest tightness (may be mistaken for pain)
•	 Waking at night gasping for breath (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea)
•	 Needing multiple pillows to sleep (orthopnoea)
•	 Wheeze, cough (dry or productive)
•	 Fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance
•	 Anxiety, rapid breathing, fast heartbeat
2.	 Immediate management
•	 Assess airway, breathing, and circulation
•	 Provide oxygen if available (1–3 litres per minute)
•	 Support patient in a sitting position
•	 Ask about and document key symptoms and history:

–
Onset, duration, triggers (e.g. exercise, allergens)

–
History of heart disease or respiratory illness

–
Smoking or occupational exposures

–
Presence of cough, sputum (colour, amount)

–
Orthopnoea or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea
•	 Do full physical examination:

–
Vital signs: RR, SpO₂, HR, BP, temperature

–
Chest exam: wheeze, crackles, reduced breath sounds, dullness to percussion

–
Cardiac exam: murmur, elevated JVP, pedal oedema

–
General appearance and work of breathing
•	 Consult MO urgently if:

–
Acute severe distress

–
Hypoxia or cyanosis
	›
Suspected cardiac failure or pulmonary embolism
	›
No clear diagnosis after assessment
