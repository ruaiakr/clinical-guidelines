<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Foreign body in eye - Adult/Child</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with<sup>1-3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Report of something in the eye - (Foreign Body sensation)
<ul>
<li>Pain, photophobia - more if corneal FB</li>
<li>Redness, epiphora</li>
<li>may be worse when blinking or with eye movements</li>
<li>Poor vision</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>± red, watery eye(s), swollen eyelid(s), photophobia</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>See <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Eye-injury">Eye injury</a> if:<sup>4</sup>
<ul>
<li>obvious penetrating injury, or caused from high speed and force (high velocity)</li>
<li>protruding foreign body (FB) - do not remove</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise to not rub the eye</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Ask about:
<ul>
<li>time + how FB happened eg:<sup>4</sup>
<ul>
<li>hammering metal, angle grinding, operation of high speed machinery - high velocity</li>
<li>sand, dust, eyelash<sup>1,3</sup> - low velocity</li>
<li>if unsure, ask about all activities in preceding 24 hours, especially activities that can cause high velocity projectiles</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>use of protective eyewear<sup>1,3</sup></li>
<li>pain, changes in vision, photophobia<sup>1,3</sup></li>
<li>contact lenses - get patient to remove<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>previous eye conditions</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Examine both eyes as per <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Eye-assessment">Eye assessment</a>.
<ul>
<li>instil (or Plain Lignocaine 1%) if needed for pain<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>visual acuity (VA) - test unaffected eye first (Always Right Eye first)</li>
<li>evert eyelids:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>if FB seen under eyelid, use moistened cotton tip with sodium chloride 0.9% to sweep away<sup>1,2</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>stain with fluorescein - may help to see FB + show any <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Corneal-abrasion">Corneal abrasion</a>.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>If unable to tolerate examination, contact MO<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If no FB visible, but fluorescein shows abrasion on cornea, see <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Corneal-abrasion">Corneal abrasion</a>.</li>
<li>Consult MO if:
<ul>
<li>high velocity projectile - x-ray may be needed</li>
<li>event happened a few days prior + there is ↑ pain, worsening vision + diffuse redness<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>↓VA, pupils irregular or non-reactive</li>
<li>blood in anterior chamber (hyphaema) - suggests perforation of eyeball</li>
<li>FB ‘lodged’ in the cornea</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Only try to remove the FB if it:
<ul>
<li>looks superficial/not ‘lodged’ in the eye</li>
<li>is at least 4 mm from the pupil</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading" id="removal-of-foreign-body">Removal of foreign body<sup>2,3</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Lie patient down, with affected eye closest to you</li>
<li>Ask patient to look up, approach from side to ↓ blinking. Hold eyelid open if needed<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>First try to irrigate with sodium chloride 0.9% (use a syringe without needle) FB may wash off<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Or, try to dislodge with a moistened cotton tip applicator using ‘dab’ or 'nudging' motion<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>If still not dislodged, MO may consider removal from cornea with 25 G needle (best done with slit lamp)<sup>4</sup></li>
<li>Consider instilling oxybuprocaine in both eyes to stop blinking to aid removal</li>
<li>If unable to remove the FB, or there is a 'rust ring' after removal - consult MO</li>
</ul>
</div>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If FB removed from cornea give chloramphenicol as per <a href="/docs/Eye%20problems/Corneal-abrasion">Corneal abrasion</a>.</li>
<li>Advise to wear protective eyewear during risky activities<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If FB removed from cornea, advise to return the next day for review + stain with fluorescein:
<ul>
<li>consult MO if VA ↓, pain or redness ↑, or defect on fluorescein staining</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>For any other FB, advise to return if the sensation of FB in eye worsens or persists overnight:
<ul>
<li>consult MO</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>As above</li>
</ul>
