<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Orbital/periorbital cellulitis - Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Orbital cellulitis is an infection of the eye socket + surrounding tissue. It is an emergency + can cause blindness + intracranial infection<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Periorbital cellulitis is an infection of the eyelid<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>Overlapping clinical features makes it difficult to differentiate<sup>2</sup></li>
<li>Children &lt; 4 years are at ↑risk of both due to a undeveloped orbital septum<sup>1</sup></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Single eye with:<sup>2</sup>
<ul>
<li>swelling + redness of eyelid</li>
<li>eye pain or tenderness</li>
<li>Low grade fever</li>
<li>Ptosis (Drooping eyelid)</li>
<li>Usually follows trauma around the eye or
<ul>
<li>Facial Infection</li>
<li>Hordeolum</li>
<li>Following upper-respiratory tract infection</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do vital signs</li>
<li>Screen for <a href="/docs/Emergency/Sepsisseptic-shock">Sepsis</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Get rapid history:<sup>1,2</sup>
<ul>
<li>pain - eye, ear, facial</li>
<li>fever, malaise</li>
<li>headache - severe or persistent</li>
<li>↓ vision, double vision</li>
<li>recent stye, trauma or insect bite to eye/eyelid</li>
<li>Bacterial sinusitis, tooth or ear infection<sup>3</sup></li>
<li>Hib immunisation history</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Check head + neck, look for obvious signs of infection eg:
<ul>
<li>insect bite to the eye, wound, stye, infected tooth, enlarged lymph nodes, tender sinuses<sup>3</sup></li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Examine both eyes. Check for:<sup>1-3</sup>
<ul>
<li>↓ VA, pupil reaction to light not equal</li>
<li>degree of swelling, reduced eye opening - does this limit your ability to examine the eye</li>
<li>painful/limited eye movements - unable to look in different directions</li>
<li>protrusion of the eyeball</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If both eyes are swollen, or non-tender (painless) swelling in a well looking patient, more likely to be an allergic reaction.<sup>2</sup> Consult MO</li>
<li>Contact MO urgently in all other cases, who may advise:</li>
<li>if periorbital cellulitis likely AND patient otherwise well eg no fever, malaise:
<ul>
<li>oral antibiotics</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>if orbital cellulitis OR severe periorbital cellulitis suspected:
<ul>
<li>insert IVC</li>
<li>blood cultures, FBC<sup>1,2</sup></li>
<li>Pus swab (For culture)</li>
<li>IV antibiotics</li>
<li>urgent evacuation ± CT scan<sup>1</sup></li>
<li>urgent ENT + ophthalmology advice</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Offer analgesia. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="antibiotics">Antibiotics<sup>1</sup> MO may order:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>oral trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole (PLUS IV cefotaxime if severe)</li>
<li>If non remote community and NO prior history of MRSA infection:
<ul>
<li>oral flucloxacillin (cefalexin if mild allergy, clindamycin if immediate sensitivity) <strong>OR</strong></li>
<li>oral amoxicillin + clavulanic acid (if features of sinusitis, cefalexin if mild allergy)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> Take with food to reduce stomach upset. May cause fever, nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, itch, rash or sore mouth. Avoid sun exposure. Report straight away if sore throat, fever, rash, cough, breathing difficulties, joint pain, dark urine or pale stools</p>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> If renal impairment, taking ACE inhibitor or potassium, HIV or SLE seek MO advice</p>
<p><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> Do not use in the 1st trimester or in late pregnancy</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Severe or immediate allergic reaction to sulfonamides, megaloblastic anaemia, severe hepatic impairment, elderly</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a></p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Offer CMI:</strong> May cause diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, pain at injection site, rash, headache or dizziness. Can cause severe diarrhoea (colitis) due to C. difficile</p>
<p><strong>Note:</strong> Rapid injection &lt; 1 minute can cause life-threatening arrhythmias. If renal impairment advise MO</p>
<p><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> Do not use in the 1st trimester or in late pregnancy</p>
<p><strong>Contraindication:</strong> Severe hypersensitivity to penicillins, carbapenems and cephalosporins. Do not mix with aminoglycosides eg gentamicin</p>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong> Consult MO. See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a></p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>If not evacuated, advise to be reviewed daily until improved or sooner if symptoms worsen:
<ul>
<li>if not improving after 24 hours, contact MO</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Advise to see MO at next clinic</li>
</ul>
