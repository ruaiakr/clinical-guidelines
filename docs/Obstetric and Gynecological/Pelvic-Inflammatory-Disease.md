<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) (Kiribati Original Protocol)</h1>

<div class="pccm-alert-panel">
<strong>Note:</strong> The following guidelines are to be supplemented by Kiribati Obstetrics and Gynaecological: Clincial Practice Guildelines' 2021
</div>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>PID is a potentially serious reproductive tract infection that can lead to infertility, ectopic pregnancy, or chronic pelvic pain. Early recognition and treatment are essential to reduce complications.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) is an infection of the upper female genital tract, including the uterus, fallopian tubes, and ovaries. It is usually caused by ascending sexually transmitted infections (STIs), most commonly <em>Neisseria gonorrhoeae</em>, <em>Chlamydia trachomatis</em>, and <em>Mycoplasma genitalium</em>.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="may-present-with">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Pelvic or lower abdominal pain with no other identifiable cause</li>
<li>Cervical motion tenderness</li>
<li>Uterine tenderness</li>
<li>Adnexal tenderness</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Additional supportive features (not required but supportive):</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Fever &gt;38.3°C</li>
<li>Abnormal cervical or vaginal discharge</li>
<li>Elevated ESR or CRP</li>
<li>NAAT positive for Chlamydia or Gonorrhoea</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="immediate-management">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Begin empiric treatment if clinical suspicion is high do not delay for test results</li>
<li>Rule out ectopic pregnancy with urine pregnancy test</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="clinical-assessment">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>History: sexual activity, contraception use, past STIs or PID</li>
<li>Examination: abdominal exam, pelvic exam if trained</li>
<li>Investigations:
<ul>
<li>Urine pregnancy test</li>
<li>NAAT for Chlamydia, Gonorrhoea, and <em>M. genitalium</em></li>
<li>HIV and syphilis serology</li>
<li>Vaginal swab for microscopy and culture (if available)</li>
<li>CBC and CRP</li>
<li>Pelvic ultrasound if tubo-ovarian abscess suspected</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="management">4. Management</h2>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="outpatient">A. Outpatient (mild to moderate PID)</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Start treatment immediately:
<ul>
<li>Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose (1 g if &gt;150 kg body weight)<br />PLUS</li>
<li>Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 14 days<br />PLUS</li>
<li>Metronidazole 500 mg orally twice daily for 14 days<br />(Metronidazole covers anaerobes and should be included unless contraindicated)</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="inpatient">B. Inpatient (severe PID or complications)</h2>
<p><strong>Admit if:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Surgical emergency not excluded (e.g. <a href="/docs/Emergency/Acute-appendicitis">appendicitis</a>)</li>
<li>Tubo-ovarian abscess</li>
<li>Severe illness (fever, vomiting, significant pain)</li>
<li>Pregnancy</li>
<li>No improvement after 48–72 hours of outpatient therapy</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Recommended regimen:</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>IV Ceftriaxone 1–2 g daily</li>
<li>PLUS</li>
<li>Doxycycline 100 mg orally or IV every 12 hours</li>
<li>± Metronidazole 500 mg IV every 8 hours</li>
</ul>
<p>Continue IV antibiotics until 24–48 hours after improvement, then complete 14 days with oral doxycycline ± metronidazole.</p>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="follow-up">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Reassess clinically within 48–72 hours to confirm response to treatment</li>
<li>Counsel patient to abstain from sex until treatment completed and partner(s) treated</li>
<li>Test of cure not routinely needed unless symptoms persist or <em>M. genitalium</em> involved</li>
<li>Retest for STIs in 3 months</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="referral-consultation">6. Referral/Consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to doctor or hospital if suspected abscess, pregnancy, or no response to treatment</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="partner-notification">7. Partner Notification and Treatment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>All sexual partners from the last 60 days should be evaluated and treated for Chlamydia and Gonorrhoea, regardless of symptoms</li>
<li>Advise abstinence until all partners have been treated</li>
</ul>
</div>
