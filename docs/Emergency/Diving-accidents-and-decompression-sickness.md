<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Diving accidents and decompression sickness</h1>
<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Emphasise diver safety.</li>
<li>Advise all divers to attend certified safe diving training classes.</li>
<li>Visitors should only dive if qualified.</li>
<li>There is no hyperbaric chamber in the islands.</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p><strong>Diving-related problems are increasingly common due to air travel and deeper</strong></p>
<p>dives. As depth increases, pressure rises from air and water, leading to increased risk of barotrauma and gas-related injury. Most underwater deaths are related to diver inexperience, carelessness, or equipment failure. Key hazards include air embolism, decompression sickness, and sinus-related injuries due to blocked passages in the nose, ears, and sinuses.</p>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<p><strong>Air Embolism</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Symptoms appear within 2 minutes of ascent</li>
<li>Personality changes</li>
<li>Seizures</li>
<li>Loss of consciousness</li>
<li>Neurological deficits (motor or sensory)</li>
<li>Pneumothorax</li>
<li>Shock Decompression Sickness</li>
<li>Joint pain (“the bends”) – deep aching or throbbing</li>
<li>Skin bubbles, mottling, rashes, itching, crawling sensation</li>
<li>Neurological symptoms – mood change, altered consciousness, seizures</li>
<li>Lung symptoms – chest pain, cough, dyspnoea, cyanosis, shock Ear, Nose and Sinus Problems</li>
<li>Severe ear or sinus pain</li>
<li>Hearing difficulty</li>
<li>Nosebleed or blood behind tympanic membrane</li>
<li>Vertigo</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2.	 Immediate management</h2>
<p><strong>Air Embolism</strong></p>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Place patient on left side with head down (to keep bubbles in right side of heart)</li>
<li>Protect cervical spine if injury suspected (use collar or stabilisation)</li>
<li>Maintain airway</li>
<li>Give 100% oxygen at 7–8 L/min (continue until exhausted; give air breaks only for assessment or hydration)</li>
<li>Insert IV line and run normal saline rapidly if hypotensive</li>
<li>Give IV Valium 5–10 mg slowly (diluted to 10 mL with sterile water or saline)</li>
<li>If not breathing, check central pulse — if absent or doubtful, begin BLS</li>
<li>Apply direct pressure to stop any bleeding (do not use tourniquet)</li>
<li>Transfer to hyperbaric chamber as soon as possible (delayed therapy can still help)</li>
<li>Use aircraft pressurised or fly at sea-level altitude Decompression Sickness</li>
<li>Manage as for air embolism:</li>
<li>Left side, head down</li>
<li>100% oxygen at 8–10 L/min</li>
<li>IV fluids rapidly if BP low</li>
<li>Valium IV slowly for seizures</li>
<li>Transfer to hyperbaric chamber as soon as possible Ear, Nose and Sinus Problems</li>
<li>Beconase AQ nasal spray: 2 sprays per nostril, twice a day for 3–5 days</li>
<li>Promethazine (Phenergan) 25 mg orally twice daily for 3–5 days</li>
<li>If patient has had similar problems in the past:<ul><li>Begin above therapy 1 week before diving and continue through diving period</li></ul></li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3.	 Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Full head-to-toe check for signs of gas embolism, sinus trauma, or hypoxia</li>
<li>Neurological examination (motor, sensory, behaviour, speech)</li>
<li>Chest and lung assessment</li>
<li>Ear examination (including tympanic membrane)</li>
<li>BP, HR, RR, oxygen saturation</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5.	 Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Observe for delayed symptoms (joint pain, fatigue, confusion)</li>
<li>Monitor vital signs closely</li>
<li>Advise against further diving until cleared by a doctor</li>
<li>Document dive history, including duration, depth, ascent pattern, and previous symptoms</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6.	 Referral / consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Consult doctor all cases of:<ul><li>Air embolism</li><li>Decompression sickness</li></ul></li>
<li>Also consult for:<ul><li>Vertigo</li><li>Hearing loss</li><li>Blood in middle ear Prevention and patient education</li></ul></li>
<li>Educate divers about:<ul><li>Safe diving practices</li><li>Importance of certification</li><li>Lack of hyperbaric chamber in Kiribati</li></ul></li>
<li>Advise against diving for people with:<ul><li>Lung disease or asthma</li><li>History of pneumothorax</li><li>Epilepsy or seizures</li><li>Migraine</li><li>Stroke</li><li>Heart disease or failure</li><li>Chronic ear problems or surgery</li><li>Vertigo</li><li>Pregnancy</li><li>Diabetes</li><li>Bleeding disorders Additional diving precautions</li></ul></li>
<li>Do not dive:<ul><li>If flown within last 24 hours</li><li>After non-decompression dive or dive longer than 2 hours in past 48 hours</li></ul></li>
</ul>
