<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Generalised weakness – Adult/Child</h1>

<div class="pccm-guideline-panel">
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Background</h2>
<p>Generalised weakness is a common complaint and may result from physical illness, poor nutrition, infection, mental health disorders, or chronic disease. It is important to distinguish between true muscular weakness and fatigue. Chronic or progressive symptoms must be evaluated thoroughly to identify the underlying cause.</p>
<h2 class="pccm-section-heading">Recommend</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Assess for systemic diseases such as anaemia, diabetes, heart failure, infection, or neurological disorders</li>
<li>Rule out psychiatric causes such as depression or grief</li>
<li>Review nutritional status and medication use</li>
<li>Chronic or unexplained weakness should be referred to a medical officer</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">1. May present with</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Tiredness, reduced strength, inability to perform usual tasks</li>
<li>Difficulty walking, climbing stairs, combing hair</li>
<li>Associated symptoms: fever, palpitations, tremors, polyuria, night breathlessness</li>
<li>History of chronic illness or recent infection</li>
<li>Poor appetite or weight loss</li>
<li>Emotional distress, recent bereavement, anxiety</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">2. Immediate management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Not applicable</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">3. Clinical assessment</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Take a full history:
<ul>
<li>Onset, duration, pattern (general or local)</li>
<li>Triggers or relieving factors</li>
<li>Impact on work/daily life</li>
<li>Recent illnesses, sleep, diet, stress</li>
<li>Medication or substance use</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Physical exam including:
<ul>
<li>Vitals and general appearance</li>
<li>Neurological assessment</li>
<li>Musculoskeletal exam</li>
<li>Signs of anaemia, infection, or thyroid disease</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">4. Management</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Address reversible causes:
<ul>
<li>Poor sleep or nutrition: improve rest and diet</li>
<li>Overwork: advise rest and gradual return to activity</li>
<li>Mild infection: symptomatic treatment</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Treat specific conditions if identified:
<ul>
<li>Anaemia: treat per anaemia protocol</li>
<li>Diabetes or hypothyroidism: refer to MO</li>
<li>Cardiac or respiratory issues: assess and refer as needed</li>
<li>Mental health issues: provide support, consider MO review</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Reassure if no serious cause found and symptoms are mild</li>
<li>Encourage gradual increase in activity and good sleep hygiene</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">5. Follow up</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Reassess within 1–2 weeks or sooner if deterioration</li>
<li>Monitor weight, activity tolerance, mood and energy levels</li>
<li>Repeat full assessment if new symptoms emerge</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-blue">6. Referral/consultation</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Refer to MO if:
<ul>
<li>Weakness persists or worsens without clear cause</li>
<li>Associated with serious signs (fever, weight loss, breathlessness, neurological symptoms)</li>
<li>Suspected depression or anxiety requiring more support</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
