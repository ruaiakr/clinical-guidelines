<h1 class="pccm-page-title">Managing injection pain – Adult/Child</h1>

<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Do not rush or force injection on patient. Staff or family must not restrain patient to give injection.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="non-pharmacological-strategies">Non-pharmacological strategies<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Patients of all ages should have control over how and where they receive their injection.</li>
<li>Short wait time for injection.</li>
<li>Pain-blocking techniques applied to site prior to injection, e.g.:
<ul>
<li>Ice pack for 5 minutes.</li>
<li>Firm pressure for 10 seconds.</li>
<li>Ice and vibration:
<ul>
<li>Buzzy® – apply ice pack for 5 minutes, then Buzzy® for 60 seconds. Move Buzzy® directly above the site of insertion during injection, keeping in contact with skin.</li>
<li>Exocool®/CoolSense® – use Buzzy® for 60 seconds first, then Coolsense® for 10 seconds, then Buzzy® as above.</li>
<li>Other device, e.g. Shot Blocker® – C-shaped plastic piece that fits around the injection site. Presses skin with multiple small blunt bumps to ‘saturate sensory nerves.’ Injection given through U-shaped opening.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Distraction techniques, e.g. electronic games, videos.</li>
<li>Refrigerate needle prior to injection.</li>
<li>Allow syringe/barrel to reach room temperature before use.</li>
<li>Inject slowly (e.g. over 2–3 minutes) – be guided by the patient.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="pharmacological-strategies">Pharmacological strategies<sup>1</sup></h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Paracetamol before injection and at appropriate intervals after. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
<li>Anaesthetic spray/cream before injection, e.g. Emla®.
<ul>
<li>Note: only anaesthetises skin, not deeper layers.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Nitrous oxide + oxygen (Entonox®) during injection. See <a href="/docs/Pain,%20nausea%20and%20vomiting/Acute-pain">Acute pain</a></li>
<li>If Bicillin L-A®:
<ul>
<li>May be given with lidocaine to reduce pain of injection (see next page)<sup>2</sup>.</li>
<li>If still highly distressed, consider consulting MO</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<div class="pccm-drug-notes">
<p><strong>Preparation:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Transfer the Bicillin L-A® dose to a 3 mL syringe.</li>
<li>Then draw up 0.5 mL of lidocaine 1%.</li>
<li>Do not mix, to allow the lidocaine to be injected first.</li>
<li>For detailed instructions, see ARF and RHD guidelines. <a href="https://rhdaustralia.org.au/arf-rhd-guidelines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">https://rhdaustralia.org.au/arf-rhd-guidelines/</a></li>
</ul>
<p><strong>CMI:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>It will hurt as it goes in.</li>
<li>Report drowsiness, dizziness, blurred vision, vomiting, or tremors.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Management of associated emergency:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Ensure resuscitation equipment is readily available.</li>
<li>Consult MO.</li>
<li>See <a href="/docs/Emergency/Anaphylaxis">Anaphylaxis</a></li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="ventrogluteal-injection">Ventrogluteal injection</h2>

<p class="pccm-flowchart-caption">Injection site — surface view and anatomical landmarks (iliac crest, greater trochanter)</p>
</figure>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="technique">Technique:</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Place patient in a side-lying position.</li>
<li>Use your right hand on the patient’s left hip, or left hand on the patient’s right hip:
<ul>
<li>Place palm of your hand on the greater trochanter, thumb pointing towards the pelvis/abdomen.</li>
<li>Place your index finger towards the front (anterior superior iliac spine).</li>
<li>Fan your middle finger as far along the iliac crest as you can reach.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>The injection site is in the middle of the triangle formed between the middle and index fingers.</li>
<li>See video: <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BlO_hojT5ik" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Ventrogluteal injection technique</a></li>
</ul>
