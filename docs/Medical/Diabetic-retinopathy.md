# Diabetic retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy
Background
•	 Damage to the retina and blood vessels due to diabetic mellitus (DM)
•	 Can affect any patient with DM (type 1 or 2)- risk increases with years since
diagnosis and poor or unstable blood sugar control.
•	 Progresses over months/years from non-proliferative to proliferative retinopathy

–
Blindness from damage to the macula and/or retinal detachment and scarring
•	 Damage is usually progressive and irreversible
1.
May present with
•	 Asymptomatic at the beginning of retinopathy
•	 May have decreased VA
•	 Sudden or Gradual
•	 Flashes of light
•	 Floaters
•	 History of DM, but can be in patients without diagnosis of DM (presenting feature
for DM)

–
Blood sugar levels are often poorly controlled (high or fluctuating)
2.	 Immediate management Not applicable
3.	 Clinical assessment
•	 Get rapid history/assessment as per Eye assessment plus Red Reflex
•	 Also ask:
	›
how quick did the vision go - sudden, over several minutes or hours
	›
1 or both eyes
	›
other symptoms - any:

–
preceding flashes ± floaters- may indicate retinal detachment

–
double vision, ‘dark shadow’ in vision of affected eyes
	›
prior episodes
•	 Check Blood sugar levels and blood sugar
•	 Careful, dilated ophthalmoscopy in both eyes with red-free light
•	 Assess, if possible, grade of retinopathy (if skilled)

4.	 Management
•	 Consult/Refer with Ophthalmologist
•	 Treatments aims to stop progression of the disease- it cannot reverse the loss of
vision

–
Laser treatment

–
Monthly Intravitreal Injection
•	 All Diabetic patients should have dilated fundus exam annually or as per advice
accordingly.
•	 Strict control of blood of sugar levels and blood pressure
•	 Prognosis:

–
Good if early stage and good glycaemic/blood sugar control.

–
Prognosis decreases with level of retinopathy, glycaemic/blood sugar control
and lack of treatment options.
5.	 Follow up
•	 As per Eye Nurses/ Ophthalmologist
6.	 Referral/consultation
•	 As above
