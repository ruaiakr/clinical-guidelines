<h1 class="pccm-page-title">IV Therapy / IV Flow Rates</h1>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="body-fluids-and-electrolytes">BODY FLUIDS AND ELECTROLYTES</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Our survival depends on the balance of fluids and electrolytes (salts or minerals with an electrical charge—sodium, potassium, chloride etc) in our bodies. Our total body weight is made up of between 50% to 70% water (dependent on age, sex and amount of body fat). Intracellular fluid (ICF) is located inside the cells and accounts for 2/3 of the body fluid. The other 1/3 is extracellular fluid (ECF)—found in the blood vessels (plasma) and in the spaces between cells in the body.</li>
<li>Illness, trauma, surgery all change the balance of fluids and electrolytes in the body. We treat these imbalances with IV fluids. IV therapy however, can be dangerous to the patient—especially if the patient gets too much or too little fluid.</li>
<li>IV fluids vary in their concentration of dissolved particles—their TONICITY. That is why we give different fluids for different conditions.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="isotonic">ISOTONIC</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Isotonic fluids have the same concentration of dissolved particles (tonicity) as the fluid inside the cells of our body. When we give these fluids, water doesn’t move across cell membranes. 0.9% Normal Saline (Na 154, Cl 154) is an ISOTONIC fluid.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="hypertonic">HYPERTONIC</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hypertonic fluids have a concentration greater than that in cells. When we give it, water comes out of the cells and cells shrink. These fluids usually cause diuresis (increased urine output). 10% and 50% Dextrose and Mannitol are HYPERTONIC fluids. Note that 10% Dextrose is often used in babies.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="hypotonic">HYPOTONIC</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Hypotonic fluid has a concentration less than that of the fluid in cells. When it is given to a patient, cells swell. Sterile or distilled water is an example of very HYPOTONIC FLUID. That is why we only use it for diluting medicines (&amp; then dilute it further with IV fluid in the chamber/burette), we never administer it as IV fluid.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="electrolyte-solutions">ELECTROLYTE SOLUTIONS</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Electrolyte solutions are used to hydrate, prevent dehydration, or to correct fluid and electrolyte imbalance. Electrolyte solutions are 0.9% Sodium Chloride, Hartmann’s (Na 129, K 5, Ca 2, Cl 109, Bicarb(HCO<sub>3</sub>) 29) and Barts (0.18% Saline in 4% Dextrose) solutions.</li>
<li>Hartmann’s solution (Lactated Ringers) solution contains multiple electrolytes and bicarbonate (as lactate). It doesn’t contain glucose, calories and can’t replace potassium losses. It should <strong>NOT</strong> be given to patients with liver disease or lactic acidosis because of the lactate.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="blood-volume-expanders">BLOOD VOLUME EXPANDERS</h2>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Plasma and HAEMACCEL are examples of blood volume expanders. Plasma is blood with the red blood cells taken out. Haemaccel has big molecules and stays in the circulation for 4–6 hours (therefore fluids and volume expanders or blood must be continued).</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="pccm-section-heading-orange" id="iv-fluids-prescription-in-common-practice">IV FLUIDS PRESCRIPTION IN COMMON PRACTICE:</h2>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="iv-fluids-for-maintenance">1. IV FLUIDS FOR MAINTENANCE</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>Isotonic fluids (Normal Saline or Hartman’s): 4-2-1 rule for hourly fluid rate</li>
<li>A guide to estimating IV fluid maintenance, hourly basis, is calculated on body weight as follows:
<ul>
<li>First 10 kg = 4mls/hr plus</li>
<li>Second 10 kg = 2mls/hr plus</li>
<li>Subsequent 10kgs = 1 ml/hr</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h3 class="pccm-section-heading-blue" id="iv-fluids-for-resuscitation">2. IV FLUIDS FOR RESUSCITATION</h3>
<ul class="pccm-content-list">
<li>During acute dehydration, severe blood loss or hypovolemic states, adequate and prompt IV fluids resuscitation with Normal Saline or Hartman’s is critical, via 2 IV lines if possible, is critical to prevent shock states that may cause kidney and other organs failure.</li>
<li>Boluses of 10-20 mls/kg IV Fluids Normal Saline or Hartman’s can be repeatedly given (2-4 times) or more until signs of improved volume status in patient, are back, ie, dry sunken eyes resolve, capillary refill time improves to &lt; 2 seconds, good pulse volume, Urine output improving to 1-2mls/kg/hour, Blood pressure stabilized.</li>
</ul>
